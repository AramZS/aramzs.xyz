<p>Sept. 15, 2026</p>
<p>With so much attention on protein lately, you’d be forgiven for forgetting about fiber.</p>
<p>But if you’re like most adults in the United States, you’re probably falling short of the recommended minimum of <strong>21 to 38 grams per day</strong>, depending on your age and sex. The average U.S. woman consumes about 15 grams of fiber daily, and men consume about 18 grams per day.</p>
<p>Closing that gap may be one of the <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/14/well/eat/fiber-diet.html">best strategies for improving your health</a>. Fiber nourishes gut microbes, lowers blood pressure and cholesterol, improves digestion and reduces the risks of heart disease, Type 2 diabetes and certain cancers.</p>
<p>It can seem like a big challenge to eat enough fiber, but nutrition experts say it doesn’t have to be. You just have to know where to look.</p>

<h2>Breakfast</h2>
<p>The first meal of the day is an excellent area to focus on when boosting your fiber consumption, said Dalia Perelman, a research dietitian at Stanford.</p>
<p>Popular breakfast foods like eggs, bacon, white toast and many muffins, bagels and pastries have little to no fiber. Even some fruits, like melon and grapes, are surprisingly low in the nutrient.</p>

<p>When increasing your fiber consumption, it’s best to do it slowly and drink plenty of water throughout the day, said Dr. Wendi LeBrett, a gastroenterologist in Los Angeles. Those strategies can reduce your chances of having unpleasant symptoms like bloating and gas, she said.</p>
<p>Try adding five grams of fiber to your daily total every day for a week, then bump it up by another five grams per day for the next week and so on until you hit at least <strong>21 to 38 grams per day</strong>, Ms. Perelman suggested.</p>

<h2>Dinner</h2>

<p>You might notice that there were no fiber supplements among these meals and snacks. That’s because experts generally recommend getting fiber from whole foods.</p>
<p>Most fiber supplements contain just one or two types of fiber, as opposed to the many types in fiber-rich foods that help nourish a <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/01/08/well/eat/gut-health-tips.html">healthier, more diverse microbiome</a>.</p>
<p>That said, if you’re still struggling to reach your fiber goal after eating more of these foods, it’s OK to add a supplement like <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/07/well/eat/health-benefits-psyllium-husk.html">psyllium husk</a>, Dr. LeBrett said.</p>
