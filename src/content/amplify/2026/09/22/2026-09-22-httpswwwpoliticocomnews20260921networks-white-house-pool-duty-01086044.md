<p>The nation’s major television networks are declining to participate in White House pool duties moving forward in response to the Trump administration’s decision to bar CNN, MS NOW and POLITICO from the White House, Fox News Washington bureau chief Bryan Boughton wrote in an email to TV pool subscribers.</p>
<p>“This is to advise that, effective today, the TV pool will not be covering events designated as pool coverage of the President,” Boughton wrote in the email, obtained by POLITICO. “This follows the White House’s position preventing CNN from fulfilling its assigned pool duties. There will be no replacement pool put in place.”</p>
<p>The email, which was not sent to POLITICO in its official capacity, was designated “off the record” and “for planning purposes only.”</p>
<p>The “pool” refers to the press corps that travels with the president.</p>
<p>The move could make it more difficult for images and video of President Donald Trump’s activities at the United Nations General Assembly this week to get to the American public. The television pool provides video from events where space constraints prevent every outlet from sending its own camera crew, with footage then made available broadly to news organizations.</p>
<p>CNN was scheduled to serve as the television pool Monday. But a White House “daily guidance” email sent to reporters Sunday night listed no TV pool — which consists of the five major networks, ABC, CBC, CNN, NBC and Fox News — at all.</p>
<p>Instead, it listed Hearst, which operates local TV stations across the country, as secondary TV crew in Washington, and the pro-Trump Real America’s Voice as secondary TV crew for the president’s trip to New York.</p>
<p>It was not immediately clear to what extent those outlets, which are outside the traditional five-network TV pool arrangement, would disseminate their footage more broadly.</p>
<p>Spokespeople for the five networks did not immediately respond to requests for comment.</p>
<p>Trump announced Friday that he was banning the three outlets from the White House “effective immediately,” accusing the outlets of reporting “FAKE NEWS.” He also threatened to extend the restrictions to other news organizations, including The New York Times and the Washington Post.</p>
