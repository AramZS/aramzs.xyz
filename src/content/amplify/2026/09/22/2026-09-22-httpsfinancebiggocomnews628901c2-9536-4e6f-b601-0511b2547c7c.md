<p>South Korea's Kakao Entertainment is terminating service for Tapas, the North American webtoon platform it acquired for approximately ₩1.1 trillion (approximately $809.6 million). The ambitious 2021 investment, intended as a beachhead for the North American market, is effectively being written off just five years later.</p>
<p>According to content industry sources on the 22nd, Kakao Entertainment plans to send advance notices regarding the Tapas service termination to publishers and rights holders on the same day. The notice is expected to include the service shutdown timeline, plans for managing existing titles, settlement procedures, and other follow-up measures.</p>
<p>Tapas was a platform Kakao Entertainment acquired to penetrate the North American webtoon market. In 2021, Kakao Entertainment finalized the acquisitions of both Tapas and the web novel platform Radish. At the time, Tapas was valued at approximately ₩600 billion (approximately $441.6 million) and Radish at approximately ₩500 billion (approximately $368.0 million). The combined investment in the two companies reached approximately ₩1.1 trillion (approximately $809.6 million).</p>
<p>Kakao Entertainment had positioned Tapas as the first webtoon platform in North America and a forward base for expanding its global content business. However, the North American platform strategy has been progressively scaled back. Last year, the company terminated the Radish web novel service, stating at the time that it would "end Radish in North America and concentrate capabilities on Tapas to run a more efficient story business."</p>
<p>Tapas and Radish merged in 2022 and were reorganized under the Tapas Entertainment umbrella. Kakao Entertainment had outlined a vision to grow its North American story IP business by bundling Tapas, Radish, and Wuxiaworld, but with Radish already shuttered and Tapas now entering termination procedures, the strategy of directly operating platforms in North America has effectively been abandoned.</p>
<p>According to data released by Tapas Entertainment, the combined catalog of Tapas, Radish, and Wuxiaworld includes more than 115,000 titles, over 80,000 creators, and more than 19 million registered readers and downloads. Tapas alone, which launched in the United States in March 2012, has 75,000 creators, 100,000 titles, and 10 million cumulative registered users.</p>
<p>This is not the first time Kakao Entertainment has wound down overseas platforms. Kakao Webtoon announced the termination of its Taiwan service in October 2024 and ended operations there on November 27, 2025. At the time, the company said the decision was made to concentrate overseas business capabilities on North America and Japan.</p>

<h3>Accounting Burden Already Reflected</h3>
<p>The North American platform investments have already weighed on Kakao Entertainment's financial results. Kakao recognized a goodwill impairment of ₩88.9 billion (approximately $65.4 million) related to Kakao Entertainment in its Q4 2023 results. Of this, the Tapas impairment amounted to ₩46 billion (approximately $33.9 million). At the time, Kakao explained that Tapas's remaining goodwill balance was only ₩11.4 billion (approximately $8.4 million) after the significant impairment.</p>
<p>Restructuring related to Tapas had already begun earlier. In 2023, Kakao Entertainment initiated liquidation procedures for Tapas Korea, the Korean subsidiary of Tapas Entertainment. The company described the move at the time as a management efficiency measure. The Tapas service termination is interpreted as a business-level cleanup of a North American platform investment that had already been substantially written down on the books.</p>
<h3>Consolidation of Webtoon and Web Novel Platforms</h3>
<p>The decision is part of Kakao Entertainment's restructuring to consolidate its webtoon and web novel platforms under KakaoPage by year-end. Kakao Entertainment has set a policy to integrate services around the KakaoPage content platform by the end of the year. Currently, Kakao Entertainment operates story platforms—webtoons and web novels—across three separate services: KakaoPage, Kakao Webtoon, and Tapas (U.S.).</p>
<p>The integration will focus on enhancing the content, user data, AI technology, and operational capabilities accumulated by each platform. Authors affiliated with the platforms are expected to be notified of the service consolidation imminently.</p>
<p>KakaoPage and Kakao Webtoon have long coexisted as "two families under one roof." KakaoPage is a webtoon and web novel platform launched by Kakao in 2013 under the name Podotree, while Kakao Webtoon traces its origins to a service that began on Daum in 2003. When Kakao merged with Daum in 2014, two webtoon and web novel platforms came to coexist under the Kakao umbrella.</p>
<p>Internally, calls for platform consolidation have surfaced periodically, but the effort has repeatedly fallen through due to differences in operating methods and organizational culture between the two teams. The separate operation of the U.S. platform Tapas added to criticism that services were excessively fragmented within a single company.</p>
<p>This stands in contrast to rival Naver Webtoon, which unified web novel (Series) and webtoon users through a single app and went straight to a Nasdaq listing.</p>
<p>Naver Webtoon's U.S. subsidiary, Webtoon Entertainment, listed on the Nasdaq on June 27, 2024. The IPO price was $21 per share, and the company issued 15 million shares to raise approximately $315 million. A webtoon industry insider said, "KakaoPage built platform competitiveness by pioneering the 'wait-to-read-free' business model, while Kakao Webtoon has strengths in webtoon planning and production capabilities. Because each organization has different competitive advantages, their organizational cultures differ, which appears to have made integration difficult."</p>
<h3>Business Restructuring Tied to Kakao's Spin-off</h3>
<p>The consolidation is drawing attention because it is taking place as parent company Kakao pursues a spin-off into "Kakao AI" and "Kakao X." Business areas centered on KakaoTalk will be handled by Kakao AI, while entertainment, finance, mobility, and other businesses will fall under Kakao X.</p>

<p>Kakao Entertainment, which will be placed under Kakao X, plans to first pursue business efficiency improvements to position itself as a future growth engine for Kakao X. Kakao X is slated to launch as a future-value investment company overseeing content affiliates including Kakao Entertainment, SM Entertainment, and Kakao Piccoma.</p>
<p>Kakao Entertainment's management efficiency drive is accelerating. On the 14th of last month, the company announced the merger of subsidiaries Samyang C&amp;C, InTime, and Pilyeon Management. The three companies are regarded as having strong capabilities in producing webtoons and web novels in genres such as romance fantasy and traditional martial arts.</p>
<p>Industry observers interpret the moves as Kakao Entertainment reducing direct operation of low-margin overseas platforms and restructuring its content strategy around IP distribution, video adaptations, and proven businesses such as Japan's Piccoma. Having shut down a platform it acquired for over ₩1 trillion (approximately $736.0 million) as a North American beachhead just five years later, Kakao Entertainment's global content strategy is visibly shifting from direct platform operation to IP utilization.</p>
<p>Piccoma, the Japanese platform that Kakao Entertainment is anchoring its restructuring around, ranked first overall in Japan's domestic mobile app revenue in 2025, combining both games and non-games. According to Sensor Tower data, it has held the top spot for three consecutive years since 2023. Piccoma launched in Japan in April 2016 and surpassed 50 million cumulative downloads as of March 2025.</p>
<p>Parent company Kakao's financial performance points in a different direction. Kakao posted record results in 2025 with consolidated revenue of ₩8.0991 trillion (approximately $6.0 billion) and operating profit of ₩732 billion (approximately $538.8 million). Q4 2025 operating profit was ₩203.4 billion (approximately $149.7 million), up 136% year-over-year.
