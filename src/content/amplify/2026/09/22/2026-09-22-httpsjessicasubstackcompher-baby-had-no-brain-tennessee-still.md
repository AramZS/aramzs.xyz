<h4><strong>Tennessee Woman Denied Abortion Despite Fatal Fetal Diagnosis</strong></h4>
<p>A <strong>Tennessee</strong> woman was forced to leave the state to end her doomed and dangerous pregnancy, <em><a href="https://www.wbir.com/article/news/local/knoxville/east-tennessee-couple-forced-travel-life-saving-procedure-states-abortion-law-restrictions/51-f733d29b-3454-43e7-8054-aae0bb8a80b3">10 News</a></em><a href="https://www.wbir.com/article/news/local/knoxville/east-tennessee-couple-forced-travel-life-saving-procedure-states-abortion-law-restrictions/51-f733d29b-3454-43e7-8054-aae0bb8a80b3"> reports</a>. This one is hard to read, but it’s important.</p>
<p>Amanda Jablonski was pregnant with her second child when she and her husband, Brandon Chancey, got unthinkable news. At her 22-week ultrasound, they learned their daughter had anencephaly: she’d developed without a skull or a brain. <strong>There was no chance for survival, and every day Amanda stayed pregnant was a threat to her health and life.</strong></p>
<p>“The brain and eyeball, all everything was just floating around inside my wife, which you can get very sick from,” Brandon said. But because of Tennessee’s abortion ban—which has no allowances for fatal fetal abnormalities—doctors couldn’t legally end Amanda’s pregnancy. They couldn’t even induce labor until her life was actively in danger.</p>
<p>I want to pause on that for a moment, as horrible as it is. Tennessee law requires women to walk around with fetal parts floating in their bodies until they’re on the brink of death. What part of this is even nominally “pro-life?” It reminds me of the young <strong>Texas </strong>woman I spoke to in 2023 who was denied an abortion <a href="https://jessica.substack.com/p/an-american-nightmare-young-pregnant">even though her fetus was developing </a><em><a href="https://jessica.substack.com/p/an-american-nightmare-young-pregnant">without a head</a></em><a href="https://jessica.substack.com/p/an-american-nightmare-young-pregnant">.</a> The cruelty is mind-boggling.</p>
<p>In the end, Amanda and Brandon were forced to travel to Chicago to get the care she needed. And because of security measures to protect patients and providers from anti-abortion protesters, Brandon wasn’t allowed to go into the clinic with his wife.</p>
<p>“I walked her down the hall at the hotel into an Uber. I sat in a hotel room by myself—hoping my wife will show back up,” he said.</p>
<p>The couple reached out to Republican lawmakers with their story, but haven’t heard back.</p>

<p>Remember: when <em>Roe</em> was first overturned, Tennessee’s trigger ban <strong>didn’t even have an exception for women’s lives.</strong> When legislators tried to add one, Tennessee Right to Life and Susan B. It took months of back-and-forth before legislators watered the language down enough to satisfy them. A useful reminder of just how much power these extremists hold in some states.</p>
<h4><strong>Mormon Church Tells Followers to Oppose Abortion Amendments</strong></h4>
<p>The Mormon church has come out to publicly oppose abortion rights ballot measures in <strong>Idaho </strong>and <strong>Nevada</strong>—and is urging its members to do the same. Members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints (LDS) in both states received <a href="https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/09/read-letter-from-church-of-jesus-christ-of-latter-day-saint-leaders-on-idaho-abortion-prop-1-initiative/">letters</a> encouraging them to “voice opposition” to the measures; the statements were also read from the pulpit this past Sunday.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.axios.com/local/salt-lake-city/2026/09/18/mormon-church-idaho-abortion-ban-prop-1">As </a><em><a href="https://www.axios.com/local/salt-lake-city/2026/09/18/mormon-church-idaho-abortion-ban-prop-1">Axios</a></em><a href="https://www.axios.com/local/salt-lake-city/2026/09/18/mormon-church-idaho-abortion-ban-prop-1"> notes</a>, while the church has long been anti-abortion, it’s unusual for them to speak out on public policy.</p>
<p>In Idaho, Proposition 1 would repeal the state’s ban and allow abortion until ‘viability’, after which women would need to face a medical emergency in order to access care. Idaho’s abortion ban is one of the strictest in the country; the state has lost about a third of its OBGYNs as a result of the law.</p>
<p>In Nevada, <a href="https://ballotpedia.org/Nevada_Question_6,_Right_to_Abortion_Initiative_(2026)">Question 6</a> would codify a right to abortion up until ‘viability’ in the state constitution. The measure was already approved once in 2024—it needs to be approved a second time this November before it’s adopted as an amendment.</p>
<h4><strong>Ballot Measure Updates: Virginia, Nevada, Massachusetts &amp; Missouri</strong></h4>
<p>Speaking of ballot measures: early voting has begun in <strong>Virginia</strong>, where a pro-choice measure could codify abortion rights in the state constitution. <a href="https://www.13newsnow.com/article/news/politics/elections/virginia-early-voting-numbers-trend-november-midterm-election/291-73422d83-007a-48b3-8ee1-899f4cf92b0f">As of yesterday</a>, over 34,000 voters have cast their ballots since in-person early voting began.</p>
<p><strong>That’s </strong><em><strong>triple the turnout </strong></em><strong>Virginia saw in 2022.</strong></p>
<p>Remember: Virginia is the sole state in the South without a post-<em>Dobbs</em> abortion ban, and has become a regional access hub over the past four years. (<strong>North Carolina</strong> has also seen a massive uptick in out-of-state patients, but the state only offers abortion until 12 weeks of pregnancy.)</p>
<p>Now, Virginia voters get to decide whether to codify abortion protections via <a href="https://ballotpedia.org/Virginia_Question_1,_Right_to_Reproductive_Freedom_Amendment_(2026)">Question 1</a>. If the measure passes, the state constitution will guarantee a right to reproductive freedom throughout pregnancy—including abortion—while still letting lawmakers regulate the procedure in the third trimester. (Unless a doctor says continuing the pregnancy threatens the patient's life or health, or if the fetus isn't viable.)</p>
<p>Pro-choice activists are keen to get protections in place so reproductive rights are secure regardless of who controls the legislature and governor’s office.</p>
<p>The proposed amendment had to clear Virginia’s General Assembly twice before going on the ballot; now voters just need to approve the measure with a simple majority.</p>

<p>Conservatives’ response to the ballot initiative has been simple: they’re lying their asses off. <a href="https://www.nationalreview.com/2026/09/virginias-amendment-1-is-extreme-and-deceptive/">The </a><em><a href="https://www.nationalreview.com/2026/09/virginias-amendment-1-is-extreme-and-deceptive/">National Review</a></em> says the measure will make abortion “freely available…all the way up to birth,” while the <em><a href="https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/in_focus/4734499/virginias-extremist-abortion-vote/">Washington Examiner</a></em> claims “they want to strip nearly all abortion regulations for all nine months of pregnancy.” Those on the right also insist that the amendment will eradicate parental consent for abortion and gender affirming care.</p>

Question 6 provides for an abortion being performed by a ‘health care professional,’ a term Nevada law defines broadly to include dentists, chiropractors, nurses, optometrists, psychologists, and lab technicians.”</p></blockquote>
<p>If you’ve been reading <em>Abortion, Every Day</em> for a while, you may recall that Republicans used this strategy in <strong>Florida</strong>, too. <p>I can’t imagine Nevada voters will fall for this lie. After all, 64% of voters approved the measure in 2024—that’s not a small number! (Remember, the measure needs to pass twice before it can be adopted into the Nevada constitution.)</p>

<p>That’s right, Democrats are “lunatics” for wanting to check in on new mothers <em>with a completely voluntary program. </em><a href="https://www.mass.gov/news/governor-healey-announces-new-actions-to-strengthen-maternal-mental-health-and-postpartum-care#&amp;_intcmp=fnc_politics_article_main-content_article-body_6_10">According to a press release from Healey’s office,</a> the move is part of a broader state effort to "strengthen postpartum care and mental health support for mothers and families across Massachusetts, with a focus on reaching people earlier [and] making support easier to access.”</p>

<p>Blasting Democrats for government interference in motherhood while forcing women into pregnancy is crazy work, but that seems par for the course these days with conservatives.</p>
<p>You can always count on <strong>Illinois </strong>for <a href="https://gov-pritzker-newsroom.prezly.com/gov-pritzker-launches-illinois-reproductive-health-care-hub">good news</a>: last week, Gov. The new online resource is a one-stop-shop for those seeking all kinds of reproductive care—from abortion and birth control to pregnancy and postpartum support.</p>
<p>The hub connects patients with providers, but it also shows you how to find funding and low-cost care. That matters because Illinois is seeing a <em>massive</em> influx of out-of-state patients: about 1 in 4 people who leave their state for abortion care go there.</p>
<p>Also important: the site keeps your information private, explains Illinois law (including protections for patients and providers), and tells you exactly who to contact if you think your rights were violated.</p>
<p>Gov. Pritzker says, “patients deserve accessibility and clarity.”</p>
<blockquote><p>“Illinois has always protected women’s right to choose, and the State’s new hub will help inform and support Illinoisans as they make important and personal decisions for their health.”</p></blockquote>

In response, Attorney General Ken Paxton sued San Antonio—claiming the city council was “forcing Texas taxpayers to subsidize abortion tourism.”</p>
<p>Then Gov. <p>The good news? The city council has found a way to operate a fund for reproductive rights. The bad news? It can’t be for abortion. Instead, the $500k set aside in the city’s budget will pay for STI testing, doula training, and reproductive healthcare workshops. That’s terrific, obviously, but so frustrating that city leaders have had to jump through so many hoops just <em>to help women</em>.</p>
