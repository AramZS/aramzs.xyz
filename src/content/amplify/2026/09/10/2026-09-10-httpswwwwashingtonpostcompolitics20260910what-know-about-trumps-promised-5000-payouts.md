<html><body><figure><img alt="" src="https://cloudfront-us-east-1.images.arcpublishing.com/wapo/EBIDE3PTOMK7KZQFEGHUMWZQFE.JPG"/><figcaption>President Donald Trump speaks at the Republican midterm convention in Dallas on Wednesday. (Evan Vucci/Reuters)</figcaption></figure><p>President Donald Trump has pledged a $5,000 dividend to every American adult if Republicans retain control of Congress in November’s midterm elections, prompting questions about how the benefit would work, whether the plan is legal and where the money would come from. </p><p><a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/09/oil-price-spike-iran-attacks-intrudes-gops-midterm-convention/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/09/oil-price-spike-iran-attacks-intrudes-gops-midterm-convention/">Payments from the “Trump dividend” pledge</a> would have to be spent in the United States, he told the Republican Party’s midterm convention in Dallas, without offering further detail. </p><p>Trump has proposed payouts before, though not tied to an election result, and two previous nationwide payouts never materialized.</p><p>Here’s what we know. </p><h3>What Trump is proposing </h3><p>Trump said he “will issue a dividend to every adult citizen in the United States of America for $5,000” if Republicans win both the House of Representatives and the Senate, “very much like a successful company will do a cash distribution to its shareholders.”</p><p>He said the “only condition” is that it would need to be spent in the United States. “We don’t want you going to Canada to spend the money. We don’t want you going to China, to Germany,” Trump said.</p><p>He compared the payment to <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/12/18/trump-military-housing-warrior-dividend/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/12/18/trump-military-housing-warrior-dividend/">last year’s $1,776 “warrior dividend</a>” to service members. <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/07/04/trump-accounts-launch-with-1000-eligible-newborns/" rel="">He also lauded his “Trump accounts,”</a> an investment account under which children born under Trump’s second term are eligible for a $1,000 deposit.</p><p>The White House did not immediately elaborate on how the plan would work, and after the speech, Vice President JD Vance appeared to begin walking it back.</p><p>“What the president is talking about is a dividend for American workers,” Vance told Fox News, appearing to shrink the pool of those potentially eligible from all American adults. The White House has yet to respond a request for more details.</p><h3>Payouts could increase deficits</h3><p>The payments, which could cost in excess of $1 trillion, would require congressional approval. <a href="https://x.com/berniemoreno/status/2097872890582446418" rel="" target="_self" title="https://x.com/berniemoreno/status/2097872890582446418">Sen Bernie Moreno (R-Ohio) said on X</a> that he will “get a bill ready” to get the dividend “passed immediately” after the election.</p><p>But the plan quickly drew opposition over its likely effect on the national debt, <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/18/us-debt-set-hit-40-trillion-months-earlier-than-expected/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/18/us-debt-set-hit-40-trillion-months-earlier-than-expected/">which surpassed $40 trillion last month</a>. </p><p>“If the goal is to increase borrowing, drive up interest rates, weaken the longer-run economy, explode the debt, push up inflation and leave our children further indebted — this will do it,” Maya MacGuineas, president of the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget, a nonpartisan think tank, said in an email Thursday.</p><p>“With Americans grappling with real affordability concerns and bond markets all but pleading for fiscal restraint, now is not the time to make outlandish promises that blow up the budget,” MacGuineas said.</p><h3>Is it a campaign promise or buying votes?</h3><p>Critics immediately pounced on the plan as an attempt to buy votes, while others suggested it is more akin to a campaign promise.</p><p>“Donald Trump now wants to buy your vote with $5,000 in taxpayer-funded blood money,” California Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) <a href="https://x.com/GavinNewsom/status/2097897505552052485?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2097897505552052485%7Ctwgr%5E60024569d607e0e89fa2edab23401ec17d61de1c%7Ctwcon%5Es1_&amp;ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.foxnews.com%2Flive-news%2Fgop-trump-convention-midterm-election-dallas-september-9" rel="" target="_self" title="https://x.com/GavinNewsom/status/2097897505552052485?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2097897505552052485%7Ctwgr%5E60024569d607e0e89fa2edab23401ec17d61de1c%7Ctwcon%5Es1_&amp;ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.foxnews.com%2Flive-news%2Fgop-trump-convention-midterm-election-dallas-september-9">wrote on X</a>.</p><p>Rep. Dan Goldman (D-New York) <a href="https://x.com/danielsgoldman/status/2097998346526818679" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://x.com/danielsgoldman/status/2097998346526818679">said</a> only Congress “can make that promise” and called Trump’s proposal “corrupt and blatantly illegal.”</p><p>Asked in his interview with Fox News about criticism that the proposal was a bribe for votes or would add to the national debt, Vance said he didn’t think it was a “controversial idea.” </p><h3>Checks could worsen inflation </h3><p>Trump campaigned for office by blaming inflation on President Joe Biden’s stimulus package passed to address the coronavirus pandemic. </p><p>This year, prices have been rising because of the ongoing conflict in Iran, which has curtailed oil supplies globally. The effects of the energy crunch have trickled out beyond gas pumps, sending up prices for groceries, airline tickets and more.</p><p>Sending out $5,000 checks would push up inflation just as the Trump administration has struggled to tame it, experts say.</p><p>“That’s just a huge amount of stimulus in the economy,” said Douglas Holtz-Eakin president of the American Action Forum, a conservative-leaning think tank.</p><p>“You create enormous demand for goods and services,” Holtz-Eakin said. “The only way to offset the resulting price increases would be to undo that demand by raising interest rates a lot.” </p><h3>Trump has promised similar payments before</h3><p>Previous broad, nationwide rebates floated by Trump have not panned out. In February 2025, he touted the idea of sending Americans <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/02/19/trump-doge-dividend-skepticism/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/02/19/trump-doge-dividend-skepticism/">a “dividend” based on the savings he expected the U.S. DOGE Service to achieve</a>, and in November, he proposed a $2,000 dividend funded by tariffs the U.S. imposed on nearly every country. Neither materialized.</p><p>Trump launched the “Trump accounts” for eligible children in July and the $1,776 “warrior dividend” bonus for service members in December. </p><p>Previous administrations have also come up with similar payouts. Trump in 2020, and then the Biden administration in 2021, offered individual payments as part of stimulus packages during the coronavirus pandemic. Neither was contingent on voter action.</p><h3></h3></body></html>
