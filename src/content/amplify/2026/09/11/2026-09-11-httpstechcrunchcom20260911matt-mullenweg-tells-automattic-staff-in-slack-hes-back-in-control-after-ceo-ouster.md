<p>Matt Mullenweg has posted a message to Automattic’s Slack, claiming he is back in control as CEO of the WordPress.com parent company, according to a screenshot of the message seen by TechCrunch, which was confirmed by two sources. The post arrives only days after Automattic’s board voted to oust Mullenweg as CEO for unknown reasons.</p>
<p>Automattic has been asked to confirm if Mullenweg’s statement is true, but has not yet responded to a request for comment. Mullenweg responded to our inquiry say a blog post is forthcoming. <em>This story is still developing.</em></p>
<p>On Wednesday, the WordPress founder and a key advocate for the open source community told his team via Slack that Automattic’s board members had “conspired” behind his back by voting to put him on a paid leave of absence. The company’s chief financial officer, Mark Davies, was made interim CEO in the process, he said.</p>
<p>A spokesperson from Automattic confirmed to TechCrunch that Mullenweg was on leave and Davies was interim CEO.</p>
<p>Early Friday morning, Mullenweg shared the following new message on Automattic’s Slack:</p>
<blockquote> <p><em>“(Don’t call it a comeback…) Howdy everybody! Happy to announce the board is back in agreement, and I’m in control of Automattic. A lot happened in the past 48 hours that we need to sort out, and I hope much of it was a misunderstanding, because I have huge respect and regard for those involved.” </em></p> </blockquote>
<p>He also posted a link to the YouTube video for LL Cool J’s “Make Said Knock You Out.”</p>
<p>As of yet, no one at Automattic has heard a confirmation from the board. Mark Davies’ Slack account was also deactivated, sources said.</p>
<p>However, TechCrunch heard yesterday that Mullenweg had removed all Slack admins from the Automattic Slack and made a bizarre post about being a “pirate” in which he cursed. (Mullenweg famously <a href="https://dexa.ai/s/YVKKGO2C">does not curse</a>). Given this situation, it’s hard to know if he’s now trolling or if legitimate changes have been made.</p>
<p>After his surprise removal as CEO this week, Mullenweg’s public and private social media posts suggested he wasn’t done fighting the decision. On Reddit, he <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/WPDrama/comments/1wbyxcm/comment/p8xbss4/">told</a> those speculating what this meant for the open source community that they didn’t know what they were talking about after initially replying, “Hey y’all. I’m here, just fine,” to a Reddit post linking to the <a href="https://www.404media.co/wordpress-automattic-ceo-matt-mullenweg-put-on-leave-of-absence/">404 Media article</a> that first broke the news.</p>
<p>On X, Mullenweg also <a href="https://x.com/photomatt/status/2097940298060194096">posted</a> that “Things are different than they appear,” and <a href="https://x.com/photomatt/status/2097904586950685094">said</a> he was in need of sysadmins and security researchers to help him move some of his stuff hosted at Automattic elsewhere, even though he supported Davies as CEO.</p>
<p>In both places, he suggested that Silver Lake, the PE firm that’s the majority owner of the hosting company WP Engine, which is currently embroiled in a lawsuit with Mullenweg and Automattic, was <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Wordpress/comments/1wbyxw2/comment/p8x53yy/?context=3">involved</a> in this plan <a href="https://x.com/photomatt/status/2097904641258528791">to “destroy” his life</a>.</p>
<p>On Friday morning, Mullenweg <a href="https://x.com/photomatt/status/2098331987161592207">posted on X</a>, “Boards are never boring,” followed by a laughing/crying emoji.</p>
