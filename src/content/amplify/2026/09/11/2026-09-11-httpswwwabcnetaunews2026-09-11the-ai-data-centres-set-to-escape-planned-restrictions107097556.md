<p>Dozens of already-approved but yet-to-be-built AI data centre projects will escape the federal government's proposed restrictions around energy and water use because the new rules are not expected to be retrospective.</p>
<p>ABC analysis of planning records and company documents has revealed this new generation of data centres will be significantly bigger and power hungrier than existing ones.</p>
<p>Earlier this year, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced the federal government <a data-component="Link" data-uri="coremedia://article/106918380" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-15/labor-to-set-standards-for-ai-data-centres-and-copyright/106918380">would create Australian standards</a> to quell community concerns <a data-component="Link" data-uri="coremedia://article/106722390" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-27/ai-data-centres-pressuring-energy-transition-greenpeace-says/106722390">about the environment</a>, while attracting the investment of major tech companies.</p>
<p>With the government aiming to pass a law to establish these rules by early 2027, the uncertainty has not slowed down the breakneck speed of development.</p>
<p>Business has welcomed the tens of billions of dollars being invested in building the physical infrastructure powering the generative AI boom, although not all of the approved data centres will likely be built.</p>

<h2 data-component="Heading">Bigger, hungrier</h2>
<p>ABC analysis of upcoming projects includes developments from Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, AirTrunk, CDC and NEXTDC, as well as property groups Goodman and Stockland, each of which were contacted for confirmation.</p>
<p>The list has been verified by analysts from commercial renewable energy project mapper RenewMap and real estate construction analytics firm CBRE.</p>
<figure><p class="rw-outer-content"><span>Some content could not be imported from the original document.</span> <a href="https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/044AN/1/?abcnewsembedheight=400&amp;dark=false">View content ↗ </a></p></figure>
<p>Advocacy group Data Centre Australia published a report saying Australia had 162 data centres with a combined IT capacity of 1.5 gigawatts as of March of this year.</p>
<p>The combined capacity of the approved, but unbuilt, 25 data centres is at least 2.9 gigawatts, based on each company's statements of computing power and the state planning-approved energy capacity.</p>
<p>One project approved last week is property developer Goodman's five-storey data centre Project Apollo in Sydney's north.</p>
<p>It will take two years to build and be one of Australia's biggest data centres by computing capacity if it existed today, according to the facility's proposal.</p>
<p>While not subject to the federal government's rules, its approval came with conditions from the NSW government relating to water and energy use.</p>
<figure data-component="Figure" data-print="inline-media" data-uri="coremedia://imageproxy/107138370"><img alt="A grey map of Australia with small red dots showing existing data centres and larger yellow dots showing proposed centres." data-component="Image" data-lazy="true" sizes="100vw" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/39993b5b0e4fe98158aadb65782541f1?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=1080&amp;cropW=1620&amp;xPos=45&amp;yPos=0&amp;width=862&amp;height=575"/><figcaption>Australia has more than 160 existing data centres and dozens more in development. (ABC News)</figcaption></figure>
<p>RenewMap co-founder and director Alex Thompson said the development pipeline had accelerated in recent months, with proposed centres generally much larger than those already operating.</p>
<p>He said NSW and Victoria were the main growth areas, particularly western Sydney and Melbourne's outskirts, while developers were also pursuing regional locations alongside wind, solar and battery projects.</p>
<p>Mr Thompson said even though they have been approved, "not all of these data centres will get built".</p>
<h2 data-component="Heading">Environmental concerns</h2>
<p>For software engineer and western Sydney resident Jack Tollefsen, the expansion is happening close to home.</p>
<p>He lives in Horsley Park and works down the road from NEXTDC's S4 development, which is under construction and expected to provide 365MW of capacity.</p>
<figure data-component="Figure" data-print="inline-media" data-uri="coremedia://imageproxy/107124078"><img alt="A man wearing a v-neck black shirt with his hair tied back squats next to a garden bed with green seedlings in it." data-component="Image" data-lazy="true" sizes="100vw" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/f060e98247eb0bcf14870963ab0f6bd1?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=3072&amp;cropW=4608&amp;xPos=0&amp;yPos=0&amp;width=862&amp;height=575"/><figcaption>Jack Tollefsen takes pride in his community garden on Sydney's western fringe and is worried about a massive data centre being built in the area. (ABC News: Billy Cooper)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Mr Tollefsen has started a petition opposing the data centre and others being built in suburban areas.</p>
<p>An environmentalist who opposes AI, Mr Tollefsen said he had raised his concerns with local and state representatives.</p>
<blockquote>"My main concerns with this are largely the environmental impact, the impact it has on the ecosystem, the native wildlife in the area,"</blockquote>
<p> he said.</p>
<p>Mr Tollefsen said he was disappointed the federal government had not already introduced minimum standards for data centres.</p>
<p>He said he hoped NEXTDC would at least reduce its water usage by designing it with a closed-loop system, a more water-efficient approach that requires more energy.</p>
<figure data-component="Figure" data-print="inline-media" data-uri="coremedia://imageproxy/107123774"><img alt="cranes and bulldozers on a building site there is a worker in high vis in the foreground standing next to piles of dirt" data-component="Image" data-lazy="true" sizes="100vw" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/9309aa5017308cd92fc4590a2c0df65b?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=2945&amp;cropW=4417&amp;xPos=0&amp;yPos=0&amp;width=862&amp;height=575"/><figcaption>NEXTDC's data centre, being built on Sydney's western fringe, is expected to be bigger than existing projects. (ABC News: Billy Cooper)</figcaption></figure>
<p>The proposed national standards are intended to address energy, water and land-use impacts of large data centres, but state and territory governments will retain their planning responsibilities.</p>
<p>The exact details of the proposed legislated standards have not yet been released.</p>
<p>So far, the federal government has said they will require data centres to underwrite new renewable energy to cover their consumption, cover the connection costs, be as water-efficient as possible, and be built in appropriate locations.</p>
<p>Some of the approved data centres may meet these standards, even if they are not legally required to comply.</p>
<p>In July, Mr Albanese delivered a landmark speech detailing how Australia would respond to the opportunity and challenges presented by AI.</p>
<p>The prime minister said Australia had both the desire and capacity to attract investment from major AI companies, including data centres, as long as they met certain requirements.</p>
<figure data-component="Figure" data-print="inline-media" data-uri="coremedia://imageproxy/107097604"><img alt="Anthony Albanese sitting at a table with state premiers" data-component="Image" data-lazy="true" sizes="100vw" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/a71c18d0de22bca4339c2964b38b922b?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=2688&amp;cropW=4032&amp;xPos=0&amp;yPos=168&amp;width=862&amp;height=575"/><figcaption>Anthony Albanese attended National Cabinet last month, discussing rules for AI data centres. (ABC News)</figcaption></figure>
<p>He said the government would pass a law in early 2027 setting rules to force data centres to minimise water use and maximise energy efficiency.</p>
<p>"We can set the terms, we can determine AI's social licence," he said, claiming the standards would create certainty for investors, too.</p>
<p>But the prime minister <a data-component="Link" href="https://www.pm.gov.au/media/radio-interview-abc-sydney-24">later told ABC Radio Sydney</a> the rules would not apply to projects that have been already approved or are in construction.</p>
<p>"You can't retrofit. But as well, for many of the centres which are under construction, tend to be smaller ones," he said.</p>
<p>A spokesperson for the Department of Industry, Science and Resources said Australia currently had far fewer large data centres built and in construction than the United States, citing research from S&amp;P Global Ratings.</p>
<p>"Current investment trends suggest the number of large data centres will increase. The Australian government wants this investment to be sustainable and aligned with our national interests," they said.</p>
<h2 data-component="Heading">Call to stop building until rules in place</h2>
<p>Greens senator Sarah Hanson-Young has been leading calls for a ban on data centre construction until the government establishes minimum standards.</p>
<p>She said communities were already facing pressure from developments being proposed and built, and argued standards introduced after projects were approved would not provide the protections the government was promising.</p>
<p>"In the absence of a moratorium on building and approving new data centres, then these rules must be retrospective because otherwise they're worthless," she said.</p>
<p>Senator Hanson-Young said the government risked losing community support for AI if it did not take concerns about energy, water and local benefits seriously.</p>
<aside data-component="RelatedCard" data-uri="coremedia://article/107113524"><figure><img alt="a large, black, factory-like building with water tanks and infrastructure" data-component="Image" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/f137592aebb2dd29f5cfde674c4a8be5?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=2268&amp;cropW=4032&amp;xPos=0&amp;yPos=0&amp;width=862&amp;height=485"/><figcaption>a large, black, factory-like building with water tanks and infrastructure</figcaption></figure><p data-component="Typography">As data centres rapidly come online to meet demand, governments are urged to ensure development does not outpace adequate planning.</p></aside>
<p>Data Centres Australia chief Belinda Dennett said her industry was already taking steps to meet the government's expectations around power and water use.</p>
<p>"Members will continue to meet those requirements until the new rules are in force — but they cannot comply with something that isn't yet established or even articulated."</p>
<h2 data-component="Heading">Investment continues</h2>
<p>Kate Muller, a partner in Ashurst Perkins Coie's projects and energy transition practice, said the delay and uncertainty around national standards was not dampening investor interest.</p>
<p>Ms Muller said Australia was attractive to major technology companies because of its security, and a government and policy environment similar to US-headquartered businesses.</p>
<p>She said developers were used to dealing with some regulatory uncertainty.</p>
<figure data-component="Figure" data-print="inline-media" data-uri="coremedia://imageproxy/107113412"><img alt="A woman wearing glasses with her hair pulled back smiles with her mouth closed she is wearing a black shirt and green blazer." data-component="Image" data-lazy="true" sizes="100vw" src="https://live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/cc6c70c37ea95012422ded224370ab7b?impolicy=wcms_crop_resize&amp;cropH=707&amp;cropW=1061&amp;xPos=0&amp;yPos=0&amp;width=862&amp;height=575"/><figcaption>Kate Muller's work includes advising on data centre projects. (ABC News: Nickoles Coleman)</figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>"The degree of debate, guideline and regulation is unlikely to deter that investment and we certainly haven't seen that in our practice and with the people that we're speaking to around industry,"</blockquote>
<p> she said.</p>
<p>Ms Muller said clear expectations could help ensure projects delivered benefits for Australians, while public debate could help address their potential detrimental effects.</p>
<p>"Indeed, we expect to see a genuine drive toward more data centre development in Australia," she said.</p>
<p>Two federal parliamentary inquiries are examining data centres and AI respectively, and will report back later this year.</p>
