<h3>From the desk of Mother Jones CEO, Monika Bauerlein...</h3>
<p>Newsrooms can be funded in many ways. One of the most controversial (and volatile) ways is by a for-profit corporation or a billionaire owner. We see it across the headlines on a weekly basis: the claw backs in public media, the gutting of <em>The Washington Post</em>, the bending over backwards to appeal to Trump and his allies.</p>
<p><strong>But not here</strong>.</p>
<p>When <em>Mother Jones</em> first started publishing 50 years ago, our founders made a critical decision: to be a reader-supported nonprofit. They knew that no corporate owner would be interested in a muckraking newsroom; they also knew that no muckraking newsroom would be interested in following the agenda of a corporate owner.</p>
<p>And so, we’ve been reader-funded for half a century. We rely on contributions from our readers—<strong>readers like you</strong>—whether it’s $50, or $15 a month, or whatever fits your budget. People give what they can, and every donation makes a difference for our newsroom, which has grown tremendously—in size and reach and renown—since its inception in 1976.</p>
<p>You may be wondering: <em>What does it take to publish an investigation? And what does my donation actually fund?</em> The answers are one and the same: It takes people, resources, and time. And that’s what your donation funds directly.</p>
<p><strong>Every donation <em>Mother Jones</em> receives from readers fortifies our newsroom</strong>, whether we’re covering underreported scandals out of Washington, DC, or the most important news of the day. And right now, <strong>each donation will be doubled because of our $50,000 match</strong>. So when you make a donation—$5, $50, any amount—it’ll go twice as far.</p>
<p>Your support keeps us going. If you’re able, donate today.</p>
