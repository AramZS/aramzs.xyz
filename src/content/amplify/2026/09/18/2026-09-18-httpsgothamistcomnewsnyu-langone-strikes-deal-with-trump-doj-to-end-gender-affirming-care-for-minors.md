<p>NYU Langone Health joined a list of hospitals that have struck deals with the Trump administration to end gender-affirming care services for minors, the U.S. Department of Justice announced on Friday.</p>
<p>NYU Langone will pay $8.5 million, similar to the $2 million “monetary penalty” paid by Mount Sinai Health System, according to the announcement.</p>
<p>The DOJ is announcing the deal with NYU Langone, which serves the New York City region and Long Island, to shutter its services mere weeks after Mount Sinai <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/mount-sinai-reaches-deal-with-trump-doj-to-end-gender-affirming-care-for-minors">reached a similar deal</a> with the White House earlier this month. Gothamist <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/trans-teens-across-new-york-city-scramble-after-nyu-langone-shutters-care">previously reported</a> that NYU Langone and <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/nyc-parents-say-mount-sinai-has-cut-off-services-for-trans-kids-ahead-of-federal-rule">Mount Sinai</a> ended their trans youth health programs earlier this year.</p>
<p>A handful of other major hospital systems across the U.S. have also recently reached agreements with the White House to stop providing gender affirming care services to minors, including the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center, which reached a deal with the DOJ on Friday.</p>
<p>NYU Langone has been cooperative in the federal agency’s investigation of “entities potentially violating federal law,” according to a Trump administration official.</p>
<p>“I want to be clear: our efforts and investigations into other providers are ongoing,” Brett Shumate, assistant attorney general for the Civil Division, said in a statement. “Those that have broken the law and not resolved their potential liability will be prosecuted to the fullest extent possible.”</p>
<p>NYU Langone initially ended its services for trans youth earlier this year after the Trump administration threatened to pull federal funding from hospitals, including proposing stripping access to Medicaid and Medicare, which are major sources of revenue.</p>
<p>An NYU Langone spokesperson said the hospital reached the agreement to get the DOJ to stop its ongoing investigations, therefore preventing the hospital system from having to turn over provide “confidential information” on any patient who was treated for gender dysphoria as a minor.</p>
<p>The hospital had been <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/nyu-langone-hit-with-us-subpoena-seeking-info-on-minors-receiving-gender-affirming-care">subpoenaed</a> by the Trump administration to turn over proximate health information on minors who had received gender-affirming care in May.</p>
<p>“NYU Langone has not entered into this settlement because of any wrongdoing and has affirmatively denied any wrongdoing or liability in the agreement,” said spokesperson Steve Ritea. “We believe this resolution is in the best interests of our patients and their families, protecting them as well as our providers and the institution from prolonged litigation.”</p>
<p>State and city officials, including U.S. Rep Jerrold Nadler, railed against NYU Langone for reaching an agreement with the Trump administration.</p>
<p>“I am disappointed in NYU Langone for acquiescing to the pressure from the Trump administration to violate the constitutional rights and dignity of patients seeking gender affirming healthcare,” Nadler said in a statement.</p>
<p>The New York attorney general’s office said it is reviewing the settlement.</p>
