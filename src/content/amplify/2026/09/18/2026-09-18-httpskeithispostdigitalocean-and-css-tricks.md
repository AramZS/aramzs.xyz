<h2>What they’re doing to CSS-Tricks</h2>
<p>If you don’t know the history: Chris Coyier started CSS-Tricks in 2007, and DigitalOcean bought it in 2022. In February 2023 they laid off the people working on it and the site <a href="https://chriscoyier.net/2024/02/28/where-im-at-on-the-whole-css-tricks-thing/">sat there for a year</a>. In June 2024 they brought Geoff Graham back as editor. He got it publishing again, two to five articles a week, mostly by himself.</p>
<p>The last article went up on August 31. On Monday, Geoff <a href="https://geoffgraham.me/why-css-tricks-has-been-quiet/">explained why it’s been quiet</a>. His last few invoices haven’t been paid, he can’t get an answer out of DigitalOcean, and he can’t afford to work for free for months.</p>
<p>Geoff has been doing this for 12-ish years. He says he isn’t angry, and that he’s more hopeful than anything else. In the comments he describes it as “talking to a wall.”</p>
<p>I’m not Geoff, so I get to be angry 😡.</p>
<p><a href="https://vale.rocks/micros/20260915-0135">Declan Chidlow put the two stories next to each other</a> . This is DHH, in <a href="https://omarchy.org/news/2026/09/digitalocean-joins-as-founding-corporate-patron/">the post announcing DigitalOcean’s money</a>:</p>
<blockquote> <p>But the part of this patronage that really made me smile was how quickly it all came together. I reached out to Paddy Srinivasan, DigitalOcean’s CEO, on X on Wednesday. We had a call that same night. I sent a proposal on Saturday. By Sunday, we’d finalized everything.</p> </blockquote>
<p>Wednesday to Sunday. DHH sends the CEO a message on X and has $3 million four days later. The editor of a site DigitalOcean owns can’t get an invoice paid or an email answered. Declan says Geoff has been trying to raise this with them for months.</p>
<h2>And the $50 😡😡😡😡😡</h2>
<p>Also in Declan’s post: Bart Piotrowski, who works on Flathub’s infrastructure, <a href="https://social.treehouse.systems/@barthalion/117241534134006167">said DigitalOcean cancelled its sponsorship of the GNOME and Flathub VMs</a> in August. It was $50 a month.</p>
<p>$50 a month is $600 a year. The Omarchy pledge is $1 million a year. One year of it would cover those VMs for <strong>1,666 years</strong>.</p>
<p>So there’s $3 million for a guy who <a href="https://keith.is/post/why-im-canceling-1password">writes that you should deport Romani people like you’d shoot wolves</a>, and there isn’t $50 a month for GNOME and Flathub, and there isn’t whatever it costs to pay the one person running CSS-Tricks.</p>
<h2>What CSS-Tricks is to me</h2>
<p>CSS-Tricks is where I’ve learned so many cool new techniques. When I search for how to do something in CSS and there’s a CSS-Tricks link in the results, that’s the one getting clicked. I don’t open three tabs to compare. There are very few sites I trust like that.</p>
<p>If I ever post about CSS here, like <a href="https://keith.is/post/css-has-selector"><code>:has()</code></a>, <a href="https://keith.is/post/css-grid-techniques">grid</a>, <a href="https://keith.is/post/css-scope-at-rule"><code>@scope</code></a>, or <a href="https://keith.is/post/using-property-for-custom-css-properties"><code>@property</code></a>, I’m trying to bring the energy of a CSS-Tricks tutorial.</p>
<p>The flexbox guide has probably been open in a tab on every web developer’s computer at some point. The Almanac has a page for just about every property and selector. And for the last two years the new stuff has kept coming, two to five articles a week, because Geoff kept it coming.</p>
<p>There aren’t many places like this left. Andy Bell wrote <a href="https://bell.bz/youll-miss-publishers-when-theyre-gone/">You’ll miss publishers when they’re gone</a> this week, and Declan ended his post with “there are very few quality publications about the web left, and it would be a major blow to lose another.”</p>
<h2>Anyway</h2>
<p>Geoff posted again today: <a href="https://geoffgraham.me/the-front-end-community-is-still-alive-and-beautiful/">The front-end community is still alive and beautiful</a>. People have been loud about this all week. Kevin Powell made a video, Eric Bailey thinks <a href="https://ericwbailey.website/published/css-tricks-could-be-a-co-op/">CSS-Tricks could be a co-op</a>, and Geoff says “there has been a little bit of movement with all your help. People are listening.”</p>
<p>So keep being loud! When someone in his comments asked how to help, Geoff said nice comments are the best support, “that and tagging DO where they pay attention.”</p>
