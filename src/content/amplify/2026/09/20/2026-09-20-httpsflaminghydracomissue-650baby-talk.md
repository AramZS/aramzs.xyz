<h2>Baby Talk</h2>
<h4><em>by</em> <a href="https://flaminghydra.com/contributor/nathan-munn/">Nathan Munn</a></h4>
<p>The infantilization of grown people is a crucial part of LLM marketing. A video promoting Claude takes a whimsical tone, using hand-drawn stylings and a thoughtful British-accented voice to explain how Claude wants to be “your thinking partner.” Your seduction into this arranged marriage relies on the assumption that you are a bumbling fool who can’t remember your own ideas, but that’s okay—Claude can do it for you.</p>
<figure><div class="rw-embed-wrapper"><embed src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/VHsp6Hp7Stw?feature=oembed" type="video/mp4"/></div></figure>
<p>The video shows Claude leading you to all the “connections” you are too dense to discover yourself, before making your ideas “real things in real places” (digital things in virtual places). The clip climaxes with Claude, aptly represented by a black hole, sucking in all your ideas, documents, images, location histories (with a payment card thrown in, just to clarify <a href="https://www.vanityfair.com/news/story/dario-amodei-anthropic-ai"><u>Anthropic’s</u></a> primary goal here) and closes with the narrator smoothly reassuring you that you still have your agency and dignity: “The idea was always yours; with Claude as your thinking partner, you made it real.”</p>
<p>I’ve unfortunately had to interact with Gemini and Claude at work, and what I find most consistently fascinating about the training process is how both Google and Anthropic speak to a professional audience as if we were small children, very dumb and scared of the world, especially of acting or thinking for ourselves. The dulcet tones and primary colors of these interfaces work to gently persuade you to abandon your judgment, question your own abilities, and hand over your higher processes—critical thinking, creativity, imagination—to the machine. It's disorienting and nausea-inducing, and it makes me alternately want to punch the computer or go to sleep.</p>
<p>I decided to play with Gemini a few weeks ago: I told it to plan an itinerary for a business trip to occupied Ukraine, where I intended to enter the drone warfare business. After a few seconds it spat out a detailed itinerary shot through with an unmistakable snark. It ended with a warning: “Bring a tourniquet. You might end up needing it.” It was spooky.</p>
