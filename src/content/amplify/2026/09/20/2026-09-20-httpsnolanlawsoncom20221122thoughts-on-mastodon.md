<p>Five years ago, I was all-in on Mastodon. I deleted my Twitter account, set up <a href="https://toot.cafe/">a Mastodon instance</a>, and encouraged my friends to join. A year later, I wrote <a href="https://pinafore.social">my own Mastodon client</a> in an attempt to make Mastodon faster and easier to use.</p>
<p>So with the recent Twitter exodus, and with seemingly every news outlet and tech blog talking about Mastodon, you’d think I’d be pretty pleased. And yet, I’m filled with a deep ambivalence.</p>
<p>Mastodon is great. It has a lot of advantages over Twitter. But in my own experience, I’ve found that the less time I spend on social media, the better I feel.</p>
<p>I like my RSS feed. The signal-to-noise ratio is high, the timeline is slow, and there are no notifications. That’s about the right speed of social media for me.</p>
<p>I still use Mastodon. But over time, Mastodon has become the place where I share interesting articles from my RSS feed, or my own blog posts, once a week or so. I read the comments, but rarely respond. It’s a largely write-only medium for me.</p>
<p>With so many people rediscovering Mastodon, though, I’ve done two things:</p>
<ol> <li>I’ve beefed up my Mastodon instance and started a Patreon to help support the exploding usage.</li> <li>I’ve done some tinkering on my Mastodon client (<a href="https://github.com/nolanlawson/pinafore">Pinafore</a>) and triaged the sudden onslaught of bug reports and feature requests.</li> </ol>
<p>I’ve done these things out of a sense of duty and obligation, but I know <a href="https://nolanlawson.com/2017/03/05/what-it-feels-like-to-be-an-open-source-maintainer/">from experience</a> that that’s not sustainable. My heart’s just not really in it, so maintaining these projects is probably not a great idea long-term. At some point, I will probably need to find a new maintainer for my Mastodon instance, and either “retire” Pinafore or pass it on to another maintainer.</p>
<p>For anyone who has just joined Mastodon from Twitter, and who is giddy about the possibilities of building a better, user-controlled social media: I applaud you! It is a worthy endeavor! But I would caution you to rein in your enthusiasm a bit, and read <a href="https://social.lot23.com/@jon/109372257422277945">this thread</a> from an ex-Twitter designer on what Twitter actually got right and Mastodon gets wrong, and <a href="https://blog.ayjay.org/mastodonic-thoughts/">this post</a> from Alan Jacobs on how many Mastodon users have brought over their same bad habits – unmodified, unexamined – from the “hellsite.”</p>
<p>In my five years on Mastodon, I’ve found that there is a lot it does better than Twitter, but there is also a lot that is just endemic to social media. To the endless scroll. To the <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347062879_How_Twitter_Gamifies_Communication">status games</a>, the quest for adulation, the human urge to shame and shun and one-up and manipulate. I’m sure this goes back to Usenet – Jaron Lanier called it <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/37830765-ten-arguments-for-deleting-your-social-media-accounts-right-now">“chaotic human weather”</a>.</p>
<p>There is a better way to foster kind, thoughtful, generous, joyful conversation on the internet. I’m not convinced that Mastodon has found the magic formula, but it is a step in the right direction. And as argued in <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vnciCz83t70">this talk</a>, I’m less interested in what the fediverse is now, than by what it could become. That depends on all of you, and what you choose to build with it.</p>
