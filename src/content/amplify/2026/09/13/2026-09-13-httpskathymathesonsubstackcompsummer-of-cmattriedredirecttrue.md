<p><em><strong>Note from Kathy:</strong> This is the first Substack post from my fellow new Corkonian (and husband!) Jim MacMillan.</em></p>
<p>When Olivia Rodrigo says “<a href="https://www.instagram.com/reels/DZGf1_AB4aW/">nobody writes songs like her right now</a>,” people might notice. When Brandi Carlile calls someone “<a href="https://www.hotpress.com/music/brandi-carlile-praises-cmats-baptising-performance-at-just-wanna-weekend-festival-23126825">an every-500-years kind of star,</a>” it’s hard to ignore.</p>
<p>Last year, Rolling Stone described her Glastonbury set as “<a href="https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/cmat-live-at-glastonbury-2025-review-photos-51392/">the arrival of a superstar</a>.” And Booker Prize-winning author Douglas Stuart (“Shuggie Bain”) has <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reels/DYb9vfhNGnM/">compared her songwriting</a> to Bob Dylan and Leonard Cohen.</p>
<p>So why have you probably never heard of CMAT?</p>
<p>I’ll come back to that. First, I should explain why I’m so caught up in telling you this.</p>
<p>I had the great fortune to come of age during the golden era of Irish rock and pop. From their earliest days, U2, Sinéad O’Connor, The Cranberries and The Pogues have been the soundtrack of my life.</p>
<p>When I first moved to Ireland a few months ago, I really wanted to assimilate. I started watching how people dressed and how they moved. I listened to RTÉ (which is sort of like their NPR) to keep up with the news but also to learn how they talk.</p>
<p>And I started listening to as much Irish music as I could, which sent me down one rabbit hole after another. Every artist seemed to connect songs with history, politics or other current events. Every interview sent me searching for another story, another song, or another reference I didn’t yet understand.</p>
<p>The contemporary music surprised me most. Suddenly I was listening to Fontaines D.C., Kneecap, Kingfishr and Orla Gartland. And it felt like I had been missing a whole new golden age.</p>
<p>Then one morning CMAT showed up — in my Irish news alerts. She had just won the prestigious <a href="https://www.thejournal.ie/cmat-wins-ivor-novello-award-for-best-album-7047449-May2026/">Ivor Novello Award</a> for Best Album (“Euro Country”) and used her acceptance speech to call out fellow artists, saying now is “<a href="https://www.instagram.com/reels/DYnTKR3AEOZ/">not the time to sit on the fence</a>” because “fascism is on the rise.”</p>
<p>That led me to discover she had also previously <a href="https://www.hotpress.com/opinion/cmat-interview-bertie-ahern-22990453">vowed to stop</a> Irish presidential candidate and former Taoiseach (or Prime Minister) Bertie Ahern, and rebuked him <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_SVNTv44C4g">in the title track</a> of the album <a href="https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2025/09/05/the-cmat-song-and-the-claim-about-suicide-during-the-economic-crash-is-it-accurate/">for the wave of suicides</a> that followed Ireland’s 2008 financial crash. Irish journalists <a href="https://www.irishexaminer.com/opinion/commentanalysis/arid-41682500.html">took her challenge seriously</a>. Even Ahern vaguely <a href="https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsidewest/articles/2025/09/05/ahern-president/">acknowledged her impact</a> when he pulled out.</p>
<p>She is <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/may/29/cmat-shares-deep-sadness-over-body-shaming-bbc-radio-1-big-weekend">also a vocal survivor</a> of social media body-shaming trolls, and <a href="https://youtu.be/Unltjg1wmXQ?si=ZeNsADoaZFvyrQQ3&amp;t=4">remains boldly defiant</a>, saying: “Every environment I am placed in becomes more hostile.”</p>
<p>But I came here to talk about the music.</p>
<p>CMAT, short for Ciara Mary Alice Thompson, is a singer, songwriter and performer who somehow combines absurd humor with devastating emotional honesty. She writes songs that are sarcastic, self-deprecating and laugh-out-loud funny before suddenly becoming heartbreaking. And her lyrics keep revealing themselves more and more over repeated listens.</p>
<p>Her perspective feels unmistakably Irish, even when she’s <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4eO2tqFOVZU&amp;t=73s">borrowing an American country twang</a>. She has written seemingly silly and oddly specific songs about celebrity chef Jamie Oliver, filmmaker Peter Bogdanovich and soccer legend Vincent Kompany, but then you find out they are about much more.</p>
<p>When she sings “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LqIgN-uvxRU">When a Good Man Cries</a>,” she calls herself “Dunboyne Diana,” a nod to her hometown outside Dublin and Diana, Princess of Wales — but she also calls herself “The People’s Mess” instead of “The People’s Princess.”</p>
<p>She is backed by the Very Sexy CMAT Band, whose pedal steel, fiddle, keyboards and everything else bring country, pop, rock and Irish sounds <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nX3rGtIoII">together in amazing ways</a>. Sometimes the whole band wears coordinated dresses, including the guys with beards.</p>
<p>I am not much of a music critic, but I have a huge library in my head at this age and I swear I hear notes of Cher, Dolly, Janis, Linda, Patsy, Patti, Sinéad and many others.</p>
<p>Her live performances are hard to describe. Audiences sing and dance along <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nPSe3PuvBBU">at huge festivals</a> and <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zKlA8OvN_mE&amp;t=1406s">little record stores</a>. She dives into the crowd with her wireless mic like she is partying with best friends.</p>
<p>And then she’ll sing about <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2qFzeS9E9Zg">holiday stress</a>, <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2SOdl24SKrY">suicide</a> or grief: “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z5B5ZGuZz2I">Lord, Let That Tesla Crash</a>” is so devastating that <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x-t1Xn_LBB8">she can’t even get through it live</a>.</p>
<p>Her vocal crescendos are something else. Check out her New Year’s Eve cover of Harry Nilsson’s “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G0DTzK8eDdk">Without You</a>” with Jools Holland on piano.</p>
<p>So why haven’t you heard of her? Maybe the live shows overwhelm the songwriting. Maybe it’s because she constantly mocks celebrity culture or because <a href="https://jimmacmillan.com/me/">she speaks her mind</a>. Or maybe she’s simply still arriving.</p>
<p>In June, <a href="https://www.thejournal.ie/cmat-olivia-rodrigo-cover-bbc-live-lounge-7058945-Jun2026/">Olivia Rodrigo covered one of CMAT’s songs</a> on BBC Radio 1’s Live Lounge. Last month, <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/@tuneswithtaylor/video/7666932544373263647">Brandi Carlile joined her for a duet</a> at the Newport Folk Festival, and CMAT will soon be opening for her on the West Coast.</p>
<p>If you get <a href="https://cmatbaby.com/live/">the chance to see her</a>, I think you should, especially in case she really <a href="https://youtu.be/jx4BMAr-MFA?si=Ev4kYofH0NPwfpxH&amp;t=176">takes next year off</a>. If not, start with a YouTube search for “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=CMAT+live">CMAT live</a>.”</p>
<p>Does she belong in a class with Bob Dylan and Leonard Cohen? Well, they never inspired an international TikTok dance trend like “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/MzohdDQrJPo">The Woke Macarena</a>.”</p>
