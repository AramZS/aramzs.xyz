<p>WASHINGTON, Sept 20 (Reuters) - One of the world's best-known cybercrime ‌groups said on Sunday it had hijacked one of its chief rivals' dark web site, bringing what it said was a long-simmering feud out into the open.</p>
<p>ShinyHunters, a digital extortion group known for aggressive data theft campaigns, said it broke into the dark web site of cybercrime gang cl0p on Friday after ​discovering a vulnerability in cl0p's software and using it to establish wide-ranging control over the group's infrastructure.</p>
<p>"We basically own them ​now," ShinyHunters said in an online chat with Reuters.</p>
<p>Cl0p did not respond to repeated messages seeking comment. ⁠The group's dark web site was unreachable when Reuters tried to visit it on Sunday, but on Saturday it displayed the words, "Domain ​Seized By ShinyHunters," according to a screenshot preserved by cybercrime research platform eCrime.ch.</p>
<p>Two cybersecurity experts told Reuters the clash appeared to be ​genuine.</p>
<p>"Street beefs on the dark web are a real thing," said Brandon Parsons, a threat intelligence manager with Minnesota-based Ascent Solutions.</p>
<p>Joe Roosen, the senior director of security research at Texas-based SpyCloud, said that he had never seen one cybercrime group take another head-on quite so openly. "This was a twist for sure," he ​said. "It is rare I get to see these criminals fight each other."</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">ORACLE SOFTWARE EXPLOIT CLASH</h2>
<p>ShinyHunters said it had been at loggerheads with ​cl0p over the alleged theft last year of a software exploit taking advantage of what was a then-unknown flaw in the E-Business Suite (EBS) made by ‌Oracle <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/ORCL.N">(ORCL.N)</a>.</p>
<p>Such "zero days" — ⁠so-called because cybersecurity teams have had zero days to fix them — are particularly treasured by hackers because they can be used to gain sweeping access to vulnerable networks.</p>
<p>Cl0p, a Russian-speaking cybercrime gang, used the EBS vulnerability to steal data from what a Google analyst estimated was <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/google-says-dozens-organizations-affected-by-oracle-linked-hacking-campaign-2025-10-09/">more than 100 companies</a>, but ShinyHunters told Reuters it had discovered the zero day first.</p>
<p>As the dispute festered, cl0p threatened to reveal the ​identities of several of its rival's ​members, according to ShinyHunters.</p>
<p>ShinyHunters in ⁠turn threatened to reveal the details of cl0p's internal workings.</p>
<p>Reuters could not immediately establish the veracity of ShinyHunters' account of the feud.</p>
<p>Cl0p is counted as one of the most prolific and innovative <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/who-is-behind-sweeping-moveit-hack-2023-06-27/">cybercrime groups</a>, largely ​because of its skill in finding and exploiting vulnerabilities in enterprise software.</p>
<p>In 2023, cl0p leveraged a ​bug in MOVEit ⁠file management software to steal data on tens of millions of people from <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/moveit-hack-spawned-around-600-breaches-isnt-done-yet-cyber-analysts-2023-08-08/">more than 600 companies</a>. Just last month it claimed to have <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/philips-shell-targeted-by-hacking-group-2026-08-13/">stolen large volumes of data</a> from nearly 50 companies worldwide, including Philips <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/PHG.AS">(PHG.AS)</a>, Shell <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/SHEL.L">(SHEL.L)</a>, Fiserv <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/FISV.O">(FISV.O)</a> and GE <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/GE.N">(GE.N)</a>.</p>
<p>ShinyHunters is similarly prolific. It drew media attention in April for ⁠its claim ​to have <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/millions-rockstar-games-business-records-stolen-hacking-group-says-2026-04-13/">stolen millions of business records</a> from video game developer Rockstar Games, the ​maker of "Grand Theft Auto", and for a hack in May centered on <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/canvas-parent-company-reaches-agreement-with-hacking-group-behind-recent-breach-2026-05-12/">education tool Canvas</a> that caused widespread disruption <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/schools-reach-out-hackers-canvas-breach-hits-us-classrooms-source-says-2026-05-08/">across U.S. schools</a>.</p>
<p>This month, AI company Anthropic said it had caught ​ShinyHunters-linked hackers <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/anthropic-disrupts-russian-chinese-ai-campaigns-targeting-its-claude-models-2026-09-10/">trying to use its tools</a>.</p>
