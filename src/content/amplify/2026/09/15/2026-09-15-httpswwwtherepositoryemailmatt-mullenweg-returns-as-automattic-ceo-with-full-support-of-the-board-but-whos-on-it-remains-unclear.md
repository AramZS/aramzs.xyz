<p>Automattic has confirmed Matt Mullenweg is back as CEO, two days after the board voted to <a href="https://www.therepository.email/automattic-board-forces-matt-mullenweg-on-leave-names-cfo-mark-davies-as-interim-ceo">force him on paid leave and install CFO Mark Davies as interim chief executive</a>. But the return has come with significant fallout — according to sources, Davies, Chief Legal Officer Andy Missan, and board members Toni Schneider and Sue Decker are all gone</p>
<p>“Matt Mullenweg is the chairman and CEO of Automattic, with full support of the board and if you search online you can see many top executives and Automatticians supporting him as well,” a company spokesperson told <em>The Repository</em> in an email sent at 5:04pm ET on Saturday. The same statement was also provided to other media outlets, more than 24 hours after Mullenweg reportedly told staff he was back.</p>
<p>Asked about the status of its CFO, its CLO, and its board composition, a different spokesperson side-stepped the questions in a follow-up statement on Sunday ET: “Matt and Automattic’s leadership team have great respect for everyone involved. While we can’t comment on specific individuals, we’re thankful for their contributions to Automattic and its mission, and we remain excited about what’s ahead with Matt at the helm. Matt was away for only 33 hours and 20 minutes — we’re now back to work.”</p>
<h2>“Don’t call it a comeback”</h2>
<p>Mullenweg announced his return in Automattic’s Slack early Friday morning ET, according to sources, and first reported by <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/matt-mullenweg-tells-automattic-staff-in-slack-hes-back-in-control-after-ceo-ouster/">TechCrunch</a>.</p>
<p>“(Don’t call it a comeback…) Howdy everybody! Happy to announce the board is back in agreement, and I’m in control of Automattic. A lot happened in the past 48 hours that we need to sort out, and I hope much of it was a misunderstanding, because I have huge respect and regard for those involved,” he wrote, also posting a link to the YouTube video for LL Cool J’s “Mama Said Knock You Out.”</p>
<p>According to sources, no one at the company had heard a confirmation from the board, and Mullenweg had removed all Slack admins from the company workspace. Given Mullenweg’s behavior after being put on leave, including reportedly <a href="https://www.therepository.email/matt-mullenweg-swears-for-the-first-time-in-42-years-and-posts-hiring-call-to-move-my-stuff-off-automattic">swearing for the first time in 42 years and calling himself a pirate</a>, sources said no one knew whether to believe him.</p>
<p>Shortly after announcing his return in Automattic Slack, he published a blog post titled ‘Major Life Announcement’ and shared that he had <a href="https://ma.tt/2026/09/mirene/">bought a houseboat</a>.</p>
<figure><a data-bid="1" data-no-instant="1" href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/8724"><img alt="WP Engine ad." sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 600px" src="https://www.therepository.email/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WPE-AD-Newsroom_300x250.png" srcset="https://www.therepository.email/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WPE-AD-Newsroom_300x250.png 600w, https://www.therepository.email/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WPE-AD-Newsroom_300x250-300x250.png 300w"/></a><figcaption><a href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/8724">WP Engine ad.</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>On X, he appeared to shrug the whole thing off. “Founder advice: If you don’t have a coup attempt every few years, you’re not hiring strong enough leaders,” he posted, adding, “I think this was my fifth.”</p>

<h2>Four departures</h2>
<p>Whatever happened behind the scenes, sources say Davies, Missan, Schneider, and Decker are no longer at Automattic.</p>
<p>The board named Davies interim CEO on Wednesday. In a Slack message to staff, according to 404 Media, he said he’d lead “with transparency, communication, and teamwork.” But by Friday, his Slack account had been deactivated, according to multiple sources, and it’s understood he’s no longer CFO.</p>
<p>Andy Missan, Automattic’s Chief Legal Officer, is also out, according to sources. His LinkedIn now shows his role ended in September 2026.</p>
<p>Toni Schneider, who <a href="https://www.therepository.email/automattic-board-forces-matt-mullenweg-on-leave-names-cfo-mark-davies-as-interim-ceo">confirmed the board’s action in Automattic’s Slack</a>, is no longer on the board, according to sources. Schneider is CEO of Bluesky and a partner at True Ventures, which invested early in Automattic during its Series B. He didn’t respond to <em>The Repository’s</em> requests for comment.</p>
<p>Mullenweg and Schneider go back 20 years. Mullenweg recruited Schneider as CEO in January 2006, about six months after founding the company. In a <a href="https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2013_07_27_Schneider.html">2013 interview for the WordPress history book, <em>Milestones: The Story of WordPress</em></a>, Schneider described how it happened: “Matt decided that he sort of felt ready to start a company. He approached me and said I’d love to do this. I don’t know anything about running a company, do you want to be CEO?” Schneider ran Automattic for eight years before Mullenweg took over as CEO in January 2014. On <a href="https://fs.blog/knowledge-project-podcast/matt-mullenweg/">The Knowledge Project podcast</a>, Mullenweg called Schneider “like a co-founder” and “my business soulmate.”</p>
<figure><a data-bid="1" data-no-instant="1" href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/6715"><img alt="20i advertisement" src="https://www.therepository.email/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/V3-WP-text-350x250-1.png"/></a><figcaption><a href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/6715">20i advertisement</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>It’s not clear whether Davies, Missan, Schneider, and Decker were fired or resigned, and whether others at the company also left or were let go. Automattic’s follow-up statement thanked unnamed individuals for “their contributions” but didn’t address the departures directly. Mullenweg, Davies, and Schneider didn’t respond to questions from <em>The Repository</em>.</p>
<p>According to sources, the board’s composition has also changed. Automattic hasn’t confirmed or denied reports that there’s a new board. In the original Slack message announcing his removal, Mullenweg accused Davies, Schneider, and board members Ann Dunwoody and Sue Decker of “conspiring” against him. Dunwoody’s position on the Automattic board remain unclear, while Decker’s LinkedIn profile has been updated to show that her six years on the board finished this month.</p>
<p>At TechCrunch Disrupt in October 2024, Mullenweg said he held 84% of Automattic’s voting power. On Reddit last week, responding to speculation about the board’s authority, he wrote: “I can fill seats on the board whenever I want, but that’s not the point.”</p>
<h2>The departures and the lawsuit</h2>
<p>All three departures are tied to the dispute with WP Engine, which is headed back to court for a hearing on September 30 over <a href="https://www.therepository.email/automattic-fires-back-at-wp-engines-sanctions-motion-says-hosting-company-cant-identify-a-single-missing-message">allegations that Mullenweg destroyed messages related to the case</a></p>
<p>WP Engine’s <a href="https://wpengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Cease-and-Desist-Letter-to-Automattic-and-Request-to-Preserve-Documents-Sent.pdf">cease and desist letter</a>, sent in September 2024, alleged that Davies told a WP Engine board member that Automattic would “go to war” if the hosting company didn’t agree to pay for a trademark license. According to <a href="https://automattic.com/2024/10/01/wpe-terms/">Automattic’s own account</a> of the negotiations, published in October 2024, trademark licensing discussions with WP Engine began in February 2023 and were continued by Schneider during Mullenweg’s sabbatical.</p>
<p>Last month, Missan published a post on Automattic’s blog under the headline “<a href="https://automattic.com/2026/08/12/wrong-again/">WRONG AGAIN</a>,” pushing back against WP Engine’s spoliation allegations.</p>
<h2>Executives line up on X</h2>
<p>Within hours of Mullenweg’s reported Slack post that he was back in charge, a wave of Automattic executives posted public statements of support on X — enough that the company’s official statement referenced them, telling reporters to “search online” to see “many top executives and Automatticians supporting him as well.”</p>

<h2>Inside the company, a different picture</h2>
<p>On Blind, an anonymous professional network where verified employees can talk honestly about their workplaces, current and former Automattic employees have been processing the events of the past week — and filling in the gaps where the company hasn’t shared information with them.</p>
<p>In messages views by <em>The Repository</em>, one employee wrote that senior leadership was “being guilted into congratulatory posts on X lest they find themselves axed.” Another said they were updating their resume, writing: “[Mullenweg’s] vague posting and flippant attitude is clear that he is in control and worse, with no guardrails.” Another confirmed what sources had separately told <em>The Repository</em>: “The CFO and CLO are already gone.”</p>
<p>Others debated whether the board’s original action was justified. “They should have showed their hands when they could. But they chose not to,” one wrote. Another pushed back: “How else were they supposed to do it? Serious question. Matt will never willingly leave and the board has a responsibility and a fiduciary duty to act in the company’s best interests.”</p>
<p>A poll asking what would happen next drew 33 votes: 57.6% expected an internal purge, 36.4% expected nothing to change, and 6.1% thought an alignment offer — similar to the <a href="https://www.therepository.email/159-people-leave-automattic-wordpress-executive-director-steps-down">October 2024 buyout</a> that saw 159 people leave — was coming.</p>
<figure><a data-bid="1" data-no-instant="1" href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/10511"><img alt="Metorik advertisement." src="https://www.therepository.email/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MTK-MREC-300x250-Goals.png"/></a><figcaption><a href="https://www.therepository.email/linkout/10511">Metorik advertisement.</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Automattic hasn’t explained why the board removed Mullenweg in the first place, how he regained control, or what changed in the 33 hours and 20 minutes that Mullenweg wasn’t CEO.</p>
