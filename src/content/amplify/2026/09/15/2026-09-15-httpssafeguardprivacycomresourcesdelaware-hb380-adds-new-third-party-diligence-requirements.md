<p>If your company sells personal data to third parties or shares it for targeted advertising, Delaware just became the state to watch. The same still holds true for processors.</p>
<p><a href="https://legiscan.com/DE/text/HB380/id/3448790/Delaware-2025-HB380-Engrossed.html">Delaware House Bill 380</a>, effective January 1, 2027, amends the state’s existing privacy law with the most prescriptive third-party due diligence requirements of any U.S. state privacy law to date. Compliance will take real lead time to build, so the smart move is to start now, not in Q4 2026.</p>
<p>From Monday, September 21st, you have 70 business days to get this right.</p>
<h2 data-fontsize="32" data-lineheight="40px">What HB380 actually requires</h2>
<p>If you’re a data controller, HB380 requires “reasonable due diligence” on any third party you disclose personal data to, including sales and targeted advertising disclosures.</p>
<p>Most state laws leave “reasonable” vague. Delaware doesn’t. At minimum, HB380 requires:</p>
<ul> <li>Questionnaires assessing the third party’s privacy policies and technical/organizational safeguards</li> <li>Documents to verify those answers</li> </ul>
<p>That’s the floor, not the ceiling. More sensitive data disclosures may require additional measures beyond questionnaires and documents.</p>
<p>HB380 also obligates third parties and processors to cooperate: they must provide the information you (or your auditor) need to complete your diligence. If a vendor won’t engage with your questionnaire or document requests, that’s now a compliance gap.</p>
