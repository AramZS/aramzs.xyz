<p>New Mexico’s signature crop is having a rough year, and that means chile lovers and the businesses that sell it should brace for tighter supply and higher prices this season.</p>
<p>The New Mexico Chile Association said Tuesday that unusually warm nighttime temperatures during the summer harvest stressed plants across the state. Fields produced a normal first pick, but many never recovered enough for the expected second harvest.</p>
<p>The association estimates supply shortfalls between 20% and 50%, depending on the product and supplier, with reduced availability hitting chile products “across the board.”</p>
<p>Suppliers are now combing through what’s left in the fields to figure out how much product they can actually deliver. The association is urging customers — from processors to restaurants — to talk directly with suppliers now, so shortages don’t catch anyone off guard later in the season.</p>
