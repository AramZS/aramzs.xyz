<p>X Money, a core part of Elon Musk’s mission to turn X into an “<a href="https://www.theverge.com/23940924/elon-musk-x-twitter-all-hands-linda-yaccarino-super-app">everything app</a>,” is rolling out starting today, <a href="https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/27/x-money-apple-wallet/"><em>9to5Mac</em></a> reports. The <a href="https://money.x.com/en">payment platform</a> offers a digital wallet and peer-to-peer payments similar to Venmo, along with a metal Visa card users can emblazon with their X username. Other perks include Apple Wallet support and free money transfers on X. It also advertises “up to” a 6 percent APY, but only Premium Plus subscribers are <a href="https://money-support.x.com/en/i/interest-faq">eligible for it</a> — standard Premium subscribers have to deposit a certain amount of money to get a “boosted” 6 percent APY.</p>
<p>X Money was previously limited to an invite-only beta, but will now be available to X Premium and Premium Plus subscribers in the US. The launch comes a few months after Elon Musk <a href="https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2031363107839438939?s=20">said</a> “early public access” for the payment platform was launching in April.</p>
<p>Earlier this year, Senator Elizabeth Warren <a href="https://www.theverge.com/policy/911702/elon-musk-x-money-senator-warren">raised safety concerns</a> about X Money, writing that it could pose risks to “consumers, our national security, and the stability of the financial system.” Launching a payment platform on X has been a mission of Musk’s for a while now — in 2023, he said <a href="https://www.theverge.com/2023/10/26/23934216/x-twitter-bank-elon-musk-2024">during an X staff meeting</a> that, “If it involves money, it’ll be on our platform.”</p>
