<p>Apple TV has just released the first trailer for their upcoming adaptation of William Gibson’s revolutionary <a href="https://reactormag.com/a-transformative-classic-neuromancer-by-william-gibson/">1984 Cyberpunk novel, <em>Neuromancer</em></a>.</p>
<p>The preview is a teaser in the true sense of the word, meaning that it doesn’t really give us much to go off of. It largely consists of a retro monitor that displays the words “The sky above the port was the color of television, tuned to a dead channel” before cutting to a blurry image of an interior setting that we can’t quite make out. The text is simply the opening line of the <em>Neuromancer</em> novel, and the description for the teaser offers little additional information:</p>
<figure><blockquote> <p><em>42 years ago, William Gibson introduced the world to Neuromancer. Now, the next chapter is loading</em>.</p> </blockquote></figure>
<p>The phrase “the next chapter” is quite interesting as it suggests that the show will, or is prepared to, go beyond the events of the novel. That particular detail has not otherwise been hinted at or confirmed at this time, and the show’s official description (“A hacker and an assassin are thrust into a web of high-stakes crime as they take aim at a corporate dynasty”) seemingly sticks fairly close to the major events of the book. One would assume that the other two novels in the Sprawl trilogy (<em>Count Zero </em>and<em> Mona Lisa Overdrive</em>) will eventually be adapted if this first season goes well, though that has also not been confirmed at this time.</p>
<p>Beyond that, the only other notable takeaway from the teaser is the apparent confirmation that the show will feature the kind of rough, retro tech that the novel famously utilized. That’s probably a good decision given that the sometimes crude nature of the story’s technology ended up being one of the defining elements of both the narrative and the popular notion of modern cyberpunk worldbuilding that<em> Neuromancer</em> helped to shape and popularize.</p>
<p>We previously learned that Graham Roland (<em>Dark Winds</em>) and J.D. Dillard (<em>Sweetheart</em>) will serve as co-creators on Apple TV’s <em>Neuromancer</em> adaptation with Roland operating as the showrunner and Dillard set to direct the series’ pilot. It’s also been confirmed that Gibson is involved with the series as an executive producer and creative consultant. So far, the show’s cast is headline by Callum Turner as Case, Briana Middleton as Molly, Mark Strong as Armitage, and Clémence Poésy as Lady 3Jane. The most prized detail about the series so far is its release date, which this teaser sadly doesn’t even hint at. However, the release of this teaser and reports that the show is currently in post-production all suggest that it may still be on track for its rumored late 2026 debut.</p>
<p>Regardless of its eventual premiere date, <em>Neuromancer</em> will almost certainly be one of the year’s most talked-about shows. For quite some time, the <em>Neuromancer</em> novel was considered to be one of the most-requested, yet most-daunting adaptations in the genre canon. Some went so far as to refer to it as an unfilmable novel due to its experimental storytelling style and once-daunting technical requirements. Yet, as Apple TV continues to carve a surprising niche for itself as the destination streaming service for critically acclaimed sci-fi and fantasy series, hope remains that they just might get this right.</p>
