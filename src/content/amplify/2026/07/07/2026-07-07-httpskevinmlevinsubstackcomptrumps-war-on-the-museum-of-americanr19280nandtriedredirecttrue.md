<p>Late on Independence Day, <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/05/arts/design/white-house-smithsonian-american-history-museum.html">the White House Domestic Policy Council released</a> a 162-page report titled “<a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/saving-americas-story/">Saving America’s Story</a>,” accusing the <a href="https://americanhistory.si.edu/">National Museum of American History</a> of “institutional capture by a radical, activist ideology.” The timing was by no means accidental.</p>
<p>The administration chose the nation’s birthday, on the eve of the Semiquincentennial, to declare that the country’s flagship history museum cannot be trusted to tell the American story.</p>
<p>I have not read every word of this report. My instinct as a historian, and as someone deeply invested in public history debates, was to evaluate its claims about specific exhibits, and weigh its characterizations against the interpretive choices actually on display.</p>
<p>I started to do just this, but quickly realized that this would be a mistake. To engage the report on those terms is to grant it a status it does not deserve.</p>
<p>Simply put: this is not a work of historical assessment.</p>
<p>Consider what the document does not contain. <strong>There is no named author. There is no methodology. There are no acknowledgments, no advisory panel, no external reviewers, and no indication anywhere in its pages that a single trained historian or public historian evaluated the museum’s work.</strong></p>
<p>A few footnotes cite internal documents as “on file with author,” an unnamed figure the report never identifies. The individuals publicly associated with the review include a former presidential speechwriter, an attorney, and a budget director.</p>
<p>Its five hundred footnotes follow the conventions of a legal brief, built from screenshots, including YouTube timestamps and executive orders. It engages no historiography. Not one curator from the NMAH is interviewed.</p>

<p>Compare that to the exhibits it condemns. <a href="https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/many-voices">Many Voices, One Nation</a>, the report’s chief target, was developed over roughly a decade by professional curators and accompanied by a scholarly companion volume from Smithsonian Institution Scholarly Press featuring sixteen essays by curators and affiliated scholars.</p>
<p>I visited the exhibit last summer and thoroughly enjoyed my experience. What I remember most was being surrounded by children, who were deeply engaged throughout the room’s interactive exhibits and by the conversations they were having with their adult chaperones. It was wonderful to see.</p>
<p>The reviewers claim none of the training held by the people they presume to review. When the report does encounter the standards of the museum profession, it dismisses them outright by declaring the entire field captured by ideological dogma.</p>
<p>The report is nothing more than the latest tool in this administration’s sustained campaign to politicize the Smithsonian and seize control over how history is interpreted across the institution.</p>

<p>The pattern is by now familiar to all of you. An executive order promising to <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/restoring-truth-and-sanity-to-american-history/">restore “truth and sanity” to American history</a>. A takeover of the Kennedy Center. Interpretive panels <a href="https://abcnews.com/Politics/court-clears-trump-admin-immediately-replace-slavery-exhibit/story?id=134443527">forced back into place in Philadelphia</a> by court order. National Park Service <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/27/climate/park-service-fees-washington-trump.html">funds redirected to Trump’s cosmetic projects</a> while the substance of public history is starved and surveilled.</p>
<p>The report concludes that the museum’s leadership “cannot be trusted,” language that reads less like a finding than a pretext for installing Trump’s sycophantic loyalists.</p>
<p>We have seen this before. The 1776 Commission, which this report cites approvingly, <a href="https://www.historians.org/news/aha-statement-condemning-report-of-advisory-1776-commission/">included no professional American historians</a> and was dismissed by the field within days of its release. This document belongs to the same genre. It is oppositional research dressed in the costume of scholarship, produced by political staff, timed for maximum symbolic effect, and aimed not at improving the museum but at delegitimizing the people who run it.</p>
<p>Let me be clear about what I am not arguing. The Smithsonian is a public institution, and its work should be assessed. I suspect that Smithsonian director Lonnie Bunch and his staff would welcome scrutiny if it arrived in the form of a good faith and academically informed assessment, the kind that historians and museum professionals conduct as a matter of course.</p>
<p>That is not what we see here, because the Trump administration has never been interested in a serious discussion about history.</p>
<p>It has no interest in history at all beyond how it can be manipulated to serve a political agenda. This report saves nothing. It is the Trump agenda, footnoted.</p>
