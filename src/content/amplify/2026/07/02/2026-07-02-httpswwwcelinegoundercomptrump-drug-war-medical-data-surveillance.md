<p>The Trump administration’s <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/National-Drug-Control-Strategy-2026-1.pdf">2026 National Drug Control Strategy</a> reads like a war on fentanyl. The 195-page plan is also a blueprint for a national surveillance system built from Americans’ most intimate data: prescription records, toxicology labs, sewage, your medical chart, license-plate scans, police intelligence — all run through AI.</p>
<p>It details the architecture and skips the limits. No mention of HIPAA. Nothing to stop a system built for fentanyl from tracking abortion, pregnancy, or gender transition. Fewer guardrails than counterterrorism surveillance gets under the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act of 1978 (FISA).</p>
<p>Most of the machinery already exists. Every state runs a <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11042">prescription database HIPAA doesn’t cover</a>, and a federal agent can pull the records with a subpoena no judge sees. The strategy would run AI on your health records, too.</p>
<p>This is not theoretical. In December 2025, a Georgia woman went to the ER after taking abortion pills. Staff called police. By March she faced <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/24/georgia-woman-bail-abortion-pills-murder-charge">a murder charge</a> built on her medical records. In that case, no surveillance tech was required.</p>
<p>Add wastewater that detects medications for abortion, plate readers that log cars to clinics, and Palantir inside HHS. The question stops being whether the system can find you, and becomes whether anything is off-limits.</p>
