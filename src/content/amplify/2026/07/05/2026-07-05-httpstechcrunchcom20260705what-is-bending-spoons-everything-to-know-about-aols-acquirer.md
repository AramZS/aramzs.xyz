<p><a href="https://bendingspoons.com/">Bending Spoons</a>, the Milan-based tech conglomerate that made headlines for acquiring the likes of AOL and Vimeo, went public on the Nasdaq this week <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/bending-spoons-defies-saas-slump-surges-40-on-first-day-of-trading/">with a pop</a>, briefly reaching a market capitalization over $25 billion.</p>
<p>While Bending Spoons stock has slightly slumped since then, its market cap remains twice double its previous private valuation of $11 billion, confirming investor appetite for its playbook and portfolio, which includes digital brands such as Meetup, Eventbrite, and WeTransfer.</p>
<p>Bending Spoons’ strategy shares similarities with private equity, with the difference that it holds onto the brands it acquires. Its focus is on making them more financially successful — with tech and AI, but also often through price hikes and layoffs that have caused controversy.</p>
<p>Speaking to TechCrunch, co-founder and chief product officer Matteo Danieli said some of the scrutiny was due to the fact that products such as Evernote were genuinely loved by their users. But he said that despite all the changes, customer retention has been “remarkably stable.”</p>
<p>The user base of Bending Spoons itself has grown significantly in its 13 years of existence, and particularly in the last couple of years. As of March 2026, its portfolio served over 500 million monthly active users and more than 9 million monthly paying customers, according to its <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2004711/000110465926076487/tm2613674-10_f1.htm#tLETT">filing</a>.</p>
<p>This also goes against the idea that Bending Spoons acquires dead companies, a narrative that entrepreneur Joe Hyrkin has been battling since selling digital publishing platform <a href="https://techcrunch.com/tag/issuu/">Issuu</a> to the Italians in <a href="https://www.eu-startups.com/2024/07/milan-based-bending-spoons-announces-the-acquisition-of-digital-publishing-platform-issuu/">2024</a>.</p>
<p>“’Old internet brands’ is the wrong frame,” Hyrkin <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7478194258311749633/">wrote</a> on LinkedIn after the IPO. “They acquire products with real customer behavior, then integrate them into a centralized system of product, engineering, data, monetization, AI, and operating discipline.” This seems to be working: Bending Spoons reported $1.31 billion revenue in 2025; but its market capitalization indicates that investors anticipate even more.</p>

<h2><strong>How did Bending Spoons start?</strong></h2>
<p>The little-known backstory is that Bending Spoons was born out of the remains of Evertale, a Copenhagen-based startup that <a href="https://techcrunch.com/events/disrupt-sf-2011/startup-alley/">participated in Disrupt SF 2011’s Startup Alley</a> and raised <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2012/02/24/evertale-a-social-network-for-the-less-proactive-gets-seed-funding-from-mangrove/">seed funding</a> for its<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2012/12/11/so-its-like-color-evertale-pivots-launches-real-time-photo-sharing-app-wink/">photo-sharing app</a>, Wink.</p>
<p>Evertale failed not long after, and investors were able to exit, but its founders and a couple of employees kept working together, initially on in-house apps. Soon enough, the team made its first acquisition, followed by many others, CEO and co-founder Luca Ferrari told the venture podcast 20VC <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rJyHmyjG204">in one of his rare interviews</a> before the company decided to go public.</p>
<p>In 2020, Bending Spoons made an exception to its policy of no longer building its own products when it created and donated Immuni, Italy’s official COVID-19 contact-tracing app. But other than that, it has mostly been honing a formula: identifying a popular product it thinks it can improve inside and out, and buying it from owners who have reached their limits in some way.</p>
<p>This approach was long orthogonal to VC, and Bending Spoons remained bootstrapped for years. But it eventually raised equity financing <a href="https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/bending-spoons">several times</a>, including <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220927005732/en/Bending-Spoons-Raises-%24340-Million-in-Financing-as-Global-Demand-for-Mobile-First-Creator-Tools-Soars">in 2022</a>, <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/15/evernote-and-meetup-owner-bending-spoons-raises-155m-in-equity-financing/">2024</a> and <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251030216227/en/Bending-Spoons-Raises-%24710M-for-Continued-Investment-and-Growth">2025</a>. Pre-IPO, it also had VIP backers like tech industry bigs Eric Schmidt, Mike Krieger, and Xavier Niel; and stars Andre Agassi, Bradley Cooper, Maluma, The Weeknd, and The Chainsmokers.</p>
<h2><strong>What happens after a Bending Spoons acquisition?</strong></h2>
<p>After the acquisition, Bending Spoons is anything but a passive owner, making changes to the products’ user experience and features, as well as to the underlying tech; monetization strategy, including pricing; and team organization, including headcount.</p>
<p>While this focus on efficiency and revenue overlaps with private equity strategies, Bending Spoons claims a key difference: It “aims to hold forever, and has never sold an acquired business.” It is building a live portfolio, not presiding over a tech graveyard.</p>
<h2><strong>What companies has Bending Spoons acquired?</strong></h2>
<p>While Bending Spoons acquired several companies between 2014 and 2021, including the AI-powered photo enhancer Remini, its most notable acquisitions happened more recently.</p>
<p>In 2022, it acquired Filmic, known for its popular video- and photo-editing apps, and <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/04/bending-spoons-lays-off-entire-staff-of-flimic/">laid off the entire staff</a> in December 2023.</p>
<p>In a deal also announced in 2022 and finalized in early 2023, Bending Spoons also acquired Evernote, the note-taking app that had reportedly reached a $1 billion valuation before hitting trouble. Layoffs <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/27/bending-spoons-lays-off-129-evernote-staffers/">followed the acquisition</a>, as well as <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/29/its-official-evernote-will-restrict-free-users-to-50-notes/">cuts to Evernote’s free offering</a>.</p>
<p>The first half of the following year, 2024, was particularly active, with the <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240124551711/en/Bending-Spoons-finalizes-acquisition-of-U.S.-based-community-building-platform-Meetup-and-announces-%2450000-Community-Fund">acquisition of Meetup</a>, app maker <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240110239723/en/Bending-Spoons-Acquires-Mosaic-Group-Digital-Assets-From-IAC">Mosaic Group</a>, and <a href="https://www.northstarmeetingsgroup.com/Planning-Tips-and-Trends/Event-Planning/Event-Technology/Hopin-StreamYard-to-be-acquired-by-Bending-Spoons">Hopin’s StreamYard</a> all happening within six months.</p>
<p>In July 2024, it went on to acquire the publishing platform<a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240719725742/en/Digital-Publishing-Platform-Issuu-Acquired-by-Bending-Spoons">Issuu</a> and the file transfer service<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/31/bending-spoons-acquires-file-transfer-service-wetransfer/">WeTransfer</a>, where it later <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/08/bending-spoons-plans-to-lay-off-75-of-wetransfer-staff-after-acquisition/">cut staff</a> and <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/18/wetransfers-free-plan-now-has-a-monthly-limit-of-10-transfers/">made changes to its free plan</a>, introducing stricter limits. In December 2025, WeTransfer’s cofounder Nalden criticized Bending Spoons’ decisions and said he was <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/28/why-wetransfer-co-founder-is-building-another-file-transfer-service/">building another file transfer service</a>.</p>
<p>In November 2024, Bending Spoons announced it would spend <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/25/bending-spoons-is-taking-video-platform-brightcove-private-in-233m-acquisition/">$233 million</a> on an all-cash take-private deal to acquire video platform Brightcove. The acquisitions continued apace in early 2025, with <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250320568940/en/Bending-Spoons-Acquires-komoot">route planner Komoot</a> and management software maker <a href="https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/225798-22">Harvest</a>.</p>
<p>Bending Spoons also announced its intention to <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/vimeo-to-be-acquired-by-bending-spoons-in-1-38b-all-cash-deal/">acquire Vimeo</a> in a $1.38 billion all-cash deal, and soon after, to <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/bending-spoons-to-acquire-aol/">acquire AOL</a> from Yahoo for an undisclosed amount. (Disclosure: Both AOL and Yahoo are former owners of TechCrunch, and Yahoo retains a small interest.)</p>
<p>In December 2025, Bending Spoons announced it would acquire yet another well-known brand: <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/02/bending-spoons-agrees-to-buy-eventbrite-for-500m-to-revive-stalled-brand/">Eventbrite</a> — and for only some $500 million, a far cry from the company’s <a href="https://www.wsj.com/articles/eventbrite-valued-at-1-76-billion-in-ipo-1537443074">$1.76 billion</a> valuation when it went public in 2018.</p>
<p>The Vimeo deal closed in the latter half of 2025, and was followed by <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/vimeo-starts-layoffs-after-acquisition-by-bending-spoons/">massive layoffs</a> impacting most of the workforce including the entire video team. The acquisitions of AOL, Eventbrite and <a href="https://tractive.com/blog/it/stampa/tractive-and-bending-spoons-announce-strategic-acquisition-to-accelerate-innovation-in-pet-technology?srsltid=AfmBOoqDVfrh-aHsvqiBrQmieYpcrYCHHqMOwB4pLOI1AdTA9GTON_dd">Tractive</a> were also completed this year.</p>
<h2><strong>What’s next for Bending Spoons?</strong></h2>
<p>Four of Bending Spoons’ cofounders have remained at its helm over the years: Matteo Danieli, Luca Ferrari, Francesco Patarnello, and Luca Querella. The IPO made them billionaires, at least on paper, while retaining control of the company, with more than 80% of the voting power.</p>
<p>Some of their decisions will affect workers. According to the company, it added “1,830 full-time equivalent team members through the acquisitions of AOL, Eventbrite, and Vimeo” but has already “parted ways” with many, and will continue. “Once the transformations of the three businesses are substantially complete later in 2026, we expect only a few hundred to remain.”</p>
<p>This headcount reduction presumably won’t affect the number of “Spooners” — the term Bending Spoons reserves to some core team members that have gone through its highly selective hiring process. There are currently some 620 of them, but that number hasn’t grown fast: in 2025, it only made 286 hires out of some 800,000 job applications.</p>
<p>Core headcount may not have increased by much, but productivity has. “In part helped by progress in AI, revenue per full-time equivalent Spooner increased from $1.12 million in 2023 to $2.57 million in 2025, and was $0.97 million in Q1 2026,” the company said. It helped it escape the SaaS reckoning it now also hopes to benefit from.</p>
<p>“As many businesses struggle to adapt, our ability to expand the earnings of an acquired business may improve,” Bending Spoons observed. In addition, “an environment of greater uncertainty could provide opportunities for us to acquire businesses at more favorable valuations.”</p>
<p>Despite what it sees as a favorable moment, Bending Spoons has remained selective in its acquisitions, but keeps on a wide net. By its own reporting, it sourced over 2,500 acquisition opportunities in 2025, conducted in-depth analyses of approximately 200 of them, and completed six acquisitions. More will certainly follow — that’s the playbook.</p>
<p>“We’ve identified more than 1,000 digital businesses (both private and public) that could be attractive acquisition targets in the future, representing nearly $400 billion in aggregate estimated revenue in 2025,” Ferrari wrote in a letter on behalf of the Bending Spoons team.</p>
<p>The playbook hasn’t changed, but the hint at take-privates is a reminder that the company has gone from paying “$10,000 for our first acquisition” to now “pursuing acquisitions in the billions of dollars.”</p>
<p>What follows may be even more intense. “As AI enables us to accomplish more with fewer people, the scalability of our acquisition and transformation model should improve as well,” Ferrari predicted.</p>
