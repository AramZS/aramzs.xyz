<p class="rw-outer-content"><span>Some content could not be imported from the original document.</span> <a href="https://www.msn.com/en-us/health/nutrition/taco-bells-pulls-ingredients-from-menu-amid-explosive-diarrhea-outbreak/ar-AA27EZV4">View content ↗ </a></p><p>Stomachs are turning across the country as fast food chain Taco Bell pulls ingredients from its menu, citing breakouts of a foodborne illness that can cause “explosive” diarrhea.</p> <p>In a post on <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/reddit">Reddit</a>, user <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/tacobell/comments/1upgl19/it_looks_like_the_recall_is_more_than_just/">The_Sludge</a> shared a photo from their local <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/taco-bell">Taco Bell</a> drive-thru, which specifies that many ingredients have been temporarily put aside, affecting menu items.</p> <p>“We are currently unable to sell lettuce, cilantro, onion, pico de gallo and guacamole due to a nationwide recall,” reads the sign. “Any items ordered that normally come with these items will not contain them.”</p> <p>This recall will affect popular menu items like the Crunchwrap Supreme and the Cantina Chicken Bowl.</p> <figure><img data-document-id="cms/api/amp/image/AA1QPBgj" data-reference="image" src="https://img-s-msn-com.akamaized.net/tenant/amp/entityid/AA1QPBgj.img"/><figcaption>File photo: A Taco Bell logo is displayed outside their restaurants on August 2, 2025 in San Diego, California. (Photo by Kevin Carter/Getty Images)</figcaption></figure> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-confirmed-cases">Confirmed Cases</h2> <p>The ingredient recall is seemingly linked to a recent outbreak of cyclosporiasis being monitored by the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/cyclosporiasis/php/surveillance/index.html">Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)</a>. The illness is caused by several species of the Cyclospora parasite.</p> <p>According to a previous <em><a href="https://www.newsweek.com/map-shows-states-facing-surge-in-explosive-diarrhea-outbreak-12172481">Newsweek</a></em> report, the <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/illness">illness</a> can cause watery diarrhea that may lead to frequent, and sometimes explosive, bowel movements.</p> <p>Cases of the outbreak have been confirmed in at least the following states:</p> <ul class="wp-block-list"> <li>Alaska</li> <li>Colorado</li> <li>Connecticut</li> <li>Florida</li> <li>Georgia</li> <li>Illinois</li> <li>Louisiana</li> <li>Massachusetts</li> <li>Michigan</li> <li>New Jersey</li> <li>New York</li> <li>North Carolina</li> <li>Ohio</li> <li>Pennsylvania</li> <li>Tennessee</li> <li>Texas</li> <li>Virginia</li> <li>Wisconsin</li> </ul> <p>An unusually high number of cases have been reported in <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/michigan">Michigan</a>, where the original poster (OP) is from, which may indicate why this specific Taco Bell chose to exercise particular caution.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-potentially-not-a-nationwide-change">Potentially Not a Nationwide Change</h2> <p>Whether the recall is actually occurring at all <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/restaurant">restaurant</a> locations nationwide, however, is up for debate.</p> <p>“As a manager at a Taco Bell restaurant, there hasn’t been any company-wide emails regarding any [recalls],” one self-identified manager said in the Reddit comment section.</p> <p>“Just went to [Taco Bell] tonight and got items with lettuce and onions. No such notice posted at my [Taco Bell]. Must have to do with certain lot numbers?” one customer theorized.</p> <p>Whether local Taco Bell stores are holding off on adding these ingredients to menu items or not, it may be best for customers to try to avoid them if possible, especially as the illness continues to spread across the country.</p> <p>While no one fully knows how cyclosporiasis gets into food and water, the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/cyclosporiasis/prevention/index.html">CDC</a> advises that people avoid any <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/food-drink">food</a> or <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/drinking-water">water</a> that may be contaminated.</p> <p>“If you are traveling to cyclosporiasis-endemic (regularly occurring within an area or region) areas (such as tropical and subtropical regions), it’s important to know that routine chemical disinfection or sanitization of food or water is unlikely to kill <em>cyclospora</em>,” the CDC writes.</p> <figure><img data-document-id="cms/api/amp/image/AA27F59Z" data-reference="image" src="https://img-s-msn-com.akamaized.net/tenant/amp/entityid/AA27F59Z.img"/><figcaption></figcaption></figure> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-avoid-contaminated-foods">How to Avoid Contaminated Foods</h2> <p>The organization advises washing hands with soap and water, washing all fruits and vegetables thoroughly before eating, cutting away damaged or bruised areas of fruit and storing peeled and cut fruits and vegetables in the refrigerator immediately.</p> <p>While it is unpredictable, no one wants to spend the next few days befriending their toilet. For those looking to pick up a taco in the near future, maybe stay away from some of the recalled ingredients on the menu, whether the restaurant itself has recalled them or not.</p> <p><em>Newsweek </em>has reached out to @The_Sludge for comment via <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/reddit">Reddit</a>.
