<p>Something I always like to do when I listen to albums is put on a TV show or a movie to see how well they go together. In my opinion, soundtracking a TV show or a movie with an album you like listening to is more entertaining than just listening to a record on its own, and it personally helps me get more immersed in the album—as long as you have your program muted of course. I always make sure that whatever album I’m listening to and whatever show I’m setting the music to connect tonally and thematically. Sometimes it works great, sometimes it doesn’t so much, sometimes it works even better than expected! But it’s a thrilling way to listen to music for me, and over time I’ve come to connect songs to shows that I think connect the most together. I plan on making this the first in a series on how a TV show I love fits perfectly with some certain songs I also love, because it’s fun to show people who would have thought of the connections themselves. I’m going to start with the recently departed indie web cartoon hit, <i>The Amazing Digital Circus</i>.</p>
<p><i>The Amazing Digital Circus</i> is about six players—Pomni, Ragatha, Jax, Zooble, Gangle, and Kinger—who find themselves trapped in a surreal virtual world spearheaded by a kooky ringmaster AI named Caine. He puts the unwilling participants through traumatizing adventures to distract them from their unpleasant fate of permanently staying in the circus with no known way to leave so they wouldn’t lose their sanity, or else they will abstract into glitchy monstrosities. I got to see the series finale in theaters recently with a bunch of fans and cosplayers and it was an awesome experience; the show had become popular enough to be a phenomenon all around the world, which is crazy considering how small its animation company, Glitch, was just three years earlier. I made a playlist comprising 10 songs that I think fit the finale best thematically; I structured it like a concept album so it’s easier to follow the events in a natural and cohesive way.</p>

<p>Note: This will be discussing the events of the series finale of <i>The Amazing Digital Circus </i>thoroughly, as I talk about how these songs relate to it, so if you haven’t seen the show or the finale yet, go watch and then come back here, or else read at your own caution of being spoiled. Thank you!</p>
<p>Note 2: Since one of the main characters, Jax, is canonically a closeted trans woman, I will be referring to her with she/her pronouns for consistency.</p>
<p><strong>Ratboys – “Open Up”</strong></p>
<p>The opening song from Ratboys’ newest and best album <a href="https://www.getalternative.com/ratboys/"><i>Singin’ to an Empty Chair</i></a> is about trying to go the extra mile to help a close friend or loved one confide about their problems, problems that are clearly suffocating them. In the show, Jax hides behind the persona of being a jerk to prevent talking about the issues that had been plaguing her the entire show, including a dark past that made her wind up in the circus in the first place, losing two of her closest friends Ribbit and Kaufmo to abstraction, and most pressing of all, her secret transfem identity. Holding in all these deep-rooted issues and isolating herself from the other players is what ultimately causes her to abstract. Basically: she has a hard time opening up to people. Lyrics like “<i>Live in reverse, go back in time / What’s it gonna take to open up? / What does it take to lie?</i>” and “<i>Pick all the locks inside our heads / It takes a while in your defense</i>” relate to how Pomni had to literally get into Jax’s mind (through her abstracted state) to find out her backstory and how it led her to becoming the callous yet repressed person she was, having to go through several doors of intrusive thoughts and versions of Jax to reach the real version of her, and “<i>Bending my back to break the ice / Everything’s good as advertised</i>” connects to how Pomni cares for Jax so much, she’ll stop at nothing to help her no matter how much she rejects it. “<i>If you wanna tell me everything/I won’t go, I’m listening</i>” reflects how when Jax felt like it was too late to talk to Pomni about her issues due to bringing abstraction on herself, Pomni says she could still talk to her then. And I feel like the song as a whole connects with Jax mustering up the courage to come out as trans, especially in an intimate scene where Jax talks to Ribbit about a confession to her mother gone wrong and Ribbit (who goes by she/they pronouns) instinctively understands what she means by putting her bowtie on Jax’s head and telling her “Your secret’s safe with me.”</p>
<p><strong>Low – “Quorum”</strong></p>
<p>The opening track off the slowcore pioneers’ experimental noise album <i>Double Negative </i>musically evokes a feeling of being pulled back and under a glitchy black-and-white wave with such strong force and distance it can come across as jolting. I think this is how the gang in the circus feels when Caine gets deleted by Kinger (spoilers for episode 8) and the circus is left as nothing more than a downtrodden husk of itself, and it channels their hopelessness and depression when they find out they’re just digital copies of the human beings they were scanned from, rather than the human beings themselves. Interestingly, Ribbit mentions in the flashback with Jax that she cut off ties with their parents due to conflicts over not sharing their Mormon beliefs, and the song is about members Alan Sparhawk and Mimi Parker (who were a Mormon couple) leaving their church and disavowing their homophobic and exclusionary policy that had just been put in place. I’m guessing Ribbit blamed it on the Quorum for her parents not being understanding.</p>
<p><strong>Empath – “Passing Stranger”</strong></p>
<p>This song has a really mournful tone to it that I think applies to the best friend trio of Jax, Ribbit and Kaufmo and how so much tension and misunderstandings led to them all abstracting. When you abstract, it’s a fate similar to death, as while you’re still alive in a way you can no longer be the version of yourself you once were. And it’s preceded by the memories of them all hanging out and getting along with each other, which makes this all the more heartbreaking. “<i>When you go back you can’t unsee/And remembering is like pulling up weeds.</i>” Jax tried pushing these memories so deep in the back of her mind that not talking about them and reaching out to anybody only made her mental state dissolve completely. In a just timeline, she would not only be healthy, but fully embrace her true trans self and have friends who loved and cared about her.</p>
<p><strong>The Hotelier – “Life in Drag”</strong></p>
<p>The most aggressive song on this playlist is also the lone screamo song on The Hotelier’s groundbreaking masterpiece <i>Home, Like Noplace Is There</i>. “Life in Drag” is about a trans man who is mourning the suicide of a fellow transmasc friend, brought on by people who made him feel like an outcast with their rampant transphobia. In <i>The Amazing Digital Circus</i>, abstraction is often used as a metaphor for suicide when players are at their limit, and Jax’s backstory strongly hints that when she came out to her mother, she didn’t take it well at all. Being closeted is ultimately a major factor in leading her to abstract, and this song conveys her entire character perfectly. “<i>Who taught you how to hate yourself? / Who forced you to confide in spell? / Mistook ‘pathetic’ for empathy / Cast a stone at the foe, and the stone hit me.</i>” Jax was never comfortable with how she looked and always did her best to look “manly,” calling her rabbit form when she first got in the circus stupid. Jax never sought help from the other players and felt that any semblance of empathy towards her would be weak, and instead of confronting her mother’s transphobia, she concluded that she was right and ended up hating herself for not committing to be a man. People like Jax’s mom wear “<i>binary like a badge of fucking honor,</i>” as the song goes. Jax may disagree with this notion, but regardless of her staying closeted, she truly was performing her gender, and a gender that was never hers—a life in drag.</p>
<p><strong>Shamir – “Cloudy”</strong></p>
<p>Accidental pop star turned lo-fi indie rocker Shamir released this motivational song on the second of two albums in 2017, titled <i>Revelations</i>. A lot of lyrics on this album talk about his mental health struggles at the time, and this song still stands to me as his most powerful song, especially on this topic (though he said he wrote it in 2012). It’s about pulling through in life in spite of stressing over even the most minuscule things. A reoccurring line in the last two episodes is “we’re all in this together,” which in the gang’s context could also roughly mean “<i>We all have pain a time or two / But don’t let it get to you.</i>” In the beginning, they’re broken over finding out that there’s no escape from the circus due to only being brain scans of the people they came from, as they had dreams in their former lives or were at least simply content with them (“<i>I used to carry the weight of the world on my shoulder</i>”). Jax kept stressing over how she treated her friends to the point of abstraction rather than talking about her problems (“<i>I can’t keep stressing over all this petty shit / The stress will kill you, if you allow it</i>”). And after the players grieve the loss of Jax, they refurbish the circus into a less traumatizing and more freeing place where they can do whatever they want (“<i>‘Cause when we die we carry the same old problems / So in this life let’s try to resolve them</i>”). Creator Gooseworx once said that the show is ultimately about finding meaning in a stagnant life, and the song could apply to that message as beautifully as the show did. Against all odds, the circus found the bright side to everything.</p>
<p><strong>Sour Widows – “Cherish”</strong></p>
<p>Look no further than the opening lines of this song from grungy indie rockers Sour Widows to sum up Jax’s situation: “<i>Fuck everything I did / To feel good for a moment, nothing past / Nothing past / Fuck what I never said / And every door that I couldn’t open in the past / Fuck the past.</i>” Jax constantly hurt others for cheap thrills, yet her truest regrets are not opening the doors for her deepest insecurities she would keep shoving down. And by the time Pomni was able to finally understand what she had been going through in her abstracted state, Jax rejects her kindness as she doesn’t feel worthy of her love. “<i>Can you leave me out? / Tell me I’m a sore loser / With a bitter heart / I crawl inside and let it take me.</i>” But at the end of the day, after the strong friendships she forged and then tossed away (especially with Ribbit), she just wants to experience that joy and be loved by someone again. “<i>There is no fix, I’m just tender like this / I watch you take me in / Will you love me like this? / ‘Cause I wanna be cherished by you.</i>” Pomni was the first real friend Jax had made since the abstractions of Ribbit and Kaufmo, and Jax felt enough of a bond with her to feel cherished again. When Pomni hugs Jax in her abstraction, she made sure Jax knew she had a genuine friend in her-someone to be cherished by. Michael Kovach (the voice of Jax) said at the show’s Momocon panel recently that he pictured Jax doing a rock song; given how moody and regretful she was, I can imagine it sounding a lot like this.</p>
<p><strong>Low – “Don’t Walk Away” &amp; “More”</strong></p>
<p>Both these songs are off of Low’s swan song <i>HEY WHAT</i>. “Don’t Walk Away” thematically connects with when Pomni hugs Jax and wants her to stay with her. She can’t play the game anymore of letting Jax repress her problems while she witnesses idly by. “<i>I have slept beside you now / For what seems a thousand years</i>” relates to how Pomni has spent enough time in the circus to see through Jax’s facade, so she’s doing the best she could to help her before it’s too late. And then with “More,” Jax is looking back on her life with serious regret in her final moments, telling Pomni that she doesn’t want to go. “<i>I gave more than what I should’ve lost / I paid more than what it should’ve cost.</i>” Jax wants all that she didn’t have, as the song goes, but she knows it’s too late for her as she doesn’t have much time left. She regrets all the good she rejected in her life so she can become the pushy and shallow jerk that she was, and wishes she could’ve come for help sooner: “<i>I should have asked for more than what I got</i>.”</p>
<p><strong> glass beach – “yoshi’s island”</strong></p>
<p>Of all the bands represented here, this is the band and song I know the <i>Digital Circus</i> fandom is familiar with the most—I’ve seen fanart for Jax with the lyrics of this one. So it was a no-brainer to include, and it really emphasizes the unique crossover glass beach had with the animation community and <a href="https://www.getalternative.com/interview-glass-beach-break-down-their-restless-debut/">the DIY emo scene</a>. It’s definitely the most <i>Digital Circus</i>-coded song on here, because it talks about the exact situation Jax had found herself in: coming out as trans to her mom and not being understood about it, and then finding someone who she at least could had been a true companion with (Ribbit) and spending the rest of her life with her. Throughout the show, we see Jax try to reinforce the idea of manliness on herself, because that’s what society had taught her to be like. “<i>When they try to change your shape until your sense of self dies.</i>” As we would later find out, this is a result of Jax going through a coming out gone wrong with her mom that had her face degrading laughter and scrutiny. “<i>Does it hurt to say you love her like a son? / The world could kill her, just cause she can’t fit in right.</i>” We then see in her memories that Jax finds Ribbit, a non-binary girl who she eventually had romantic feelings for. Jax confides in Ribbit about her backstory, and having gone through a similar coming out journey herself, Ribbit immediately understands and puts her bowtie on her head, and then the two give each other a loving gaze. “<i>But you make me feel like I’m out of my body / We ran out of words to say we’re hopelessly lonely / So don’t leave the city, please don’t leave the covers too / Just stay right here, let’s live life like lovers do</i>.” Of course, once Kaufmo interrupted this intimate moment between the two, this tale couldn’t last, but if Jax had pursued this future further, her life not only would be drastically different, but would exactly mirror the brighter events in this song. Jax would’ve promised to Ribbit that she would think about nothing about loving her. But instead, she pushed her away and kept this identity hidden, repressing it enough where she’d look in the mirror and bash herself for crying because it’s a sign of weakness. “<i>I can’t take the pressure / Cry on the bathroom floor when you look in the mirror</i>.” All in all, this is the most applicable song to Jax, because it describes her gender dysphoria in a very real and authentic way. And on top of that, line by line, she went through the same basic pattern of events this song’s protagonist had experienced. (It should worth noting that Gooseworx, the show’s creator, is trans). “yoshi’s island” is nothing short of being Jax’s ultimate trans anthem, and I’m glad that other fans see it the same way. (RIP to glass beach, by the way).</p>
<p><strong>The Notwist – “Consequence”</strong></p>
<p>The final song on this list by the German indie rock/electronic band The Notwist ultimately spells the fate of Jax, and lays out the repercussions of the strife she had put herself through. Along with all her other insecurities, she wasn’t able to truly embrace the fact that she loved having Pomni as a friend by telling her. “<i>You’re the color / You’re the movement and the spin / Never could it stay with me the whole day long</i>.” When Pomni finds Jax in her abstracted form, Jax tells her that “you’re not supposed to miss me, you’re not supposed to love me,” really showing she doesn’t feel capable of being loved for and cared about at all and would rather have Pomni leave her to be the deformed monster she became (“<i>Leave me paralyzed, love</i>”). She also tries to refuse any further emotional connection with her because of how much she let her down (“<i>I’m not in this movie, I’m not in this song</i>”). Despite this, Pomni runs over to hug and console Jax over all the bad she had ever endured, and Jax finally reaches a point of catharsis from this long-needed embrace. Unfortunately, some nearby flash grenades startles Jax’s abstracted form and she is rendered as a body of light from the inside, leaving her to be “paralyzed” in a way. Pomni is rescued by the other circus members before she is absorbed by the abstraction, but she made sure if all else failed, that Jax knew she was truly loved in the end-despite presenting herself as a jerk, or repressing a version of herself that could’ve been a lot more admirable. Jax had failed with consequence, major consequence at that, but after everything she had been through, she had left a positive impact on someone who cared about her after all.</p>
