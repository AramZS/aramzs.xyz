<p>Poor President Donald Trump. He loves filing lawsuits. So fun, so satisfying, so revenge-y!</p>
<p>But he often seems to forget that real lawsuits require more than just filing a complaint and demanding a bribe to back down.</p>
<p>Why can’t he just sue the BBC for defamation, demand $10 billion and have them write him a check for $10 billion? Who needs all this “discovery” and “proof” anyway?</p>
<p>In a very unfun—for Trump, that is—turn of events, the magistrate judge overseeing Trump’s lawsuit <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c93487x97kyo">just ruled</a> that yes, the BBC can subpoena Trump’s inner circle to talk about Jan. 6, and yes, they can get his financial records.</p>
<p>See, actual lawsuits lead to actual discovery, where the plaintiff actually has to provide things that prove their allegations. To be fair, the cavalcade of U.S. media outlets buckling <a href="https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2024/12/16/2292198/news/ABC-gives-Trump-what-he-wants-and-he-s-not-going-to-stop/">immediately</a> after Trump sued perhaps gave him the false impression that a lawsuit is simply a demand for a bribe, which is promptly paid.</p>
<p>So, when Trump <a href="https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2025/11/11/2353343/news/Trump-s-war-on-the-media-goes-global/">sued</a> the BBC for $10 billion for defamation, alleging the BBC edited his speech at the Ellipse on Jan. 6, 2021, in a way that gave the false impression he was directly calling for violence, he seemed to think that was it, a done deal, give him all the money.</p>

<p>A couple of tiny problems there, just that Trump has to prove, in a defamation lawsuit, that the BBC acted with <a href="https://news.syr.edu/2025/11/11/syracuse-law-professor-analyzes-trumps-threatened-1b-lawsuit-against-bbc/">actual malice</a>, showing that they knew their reporting was false or that they recklessly disregarded the truth.</p>
<p>There’s also the little matter of showing damages. You can’t just say “I was hurt—$10 billion, gimme,” which is what Trump seems to think happens here. Indeed, Trump’s lawyer, Alejandro Brito, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-judge-orders-trump-lawyers-explain-missed-deadline-10-billion-bbc-defamation-2026-06-08/">didn’t even bother</a> to file a timely response to the BBC’s motion to dismiss, which is kind of a big thing to miss. If that name seems familiar, it’s because Brito is also the attorney who a federal judge just referred to the Florida bar for <a href="https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2026/7/14/800069843/courts/trumps-failed-slush-fund-deal-everyones-a-loser/">disciplinary</a> proceedings in the slush fund case for his role in that collusive sham.</p>
<p>Trump and his legal team have also been stonewalling in discovery. BBC lawyers, in a hearing over the discovery issues, told the court they’ve turned over 87,000 pages of discovery to Trump and, in return, received 735 pages that were mostly news articles and material “pulled from the internet.”</p>
<p>Yeah, that’s not really how this works. You don’t get to just turn in some Truth Social posts and a few articles or something and call it a day.</p>
<p>So, the court <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/trump-loses-bid-to-block-bbc-subpoenas-in-10-billion-lawsuit">ruled</a> that the BBC can subpoena some of Trump’s inner circle to ask about Trump’s state of mind on Jan. 6. Trump’s allegation is that the BBC defamed him by making it look like he was directing the mob to the Capitol and calling for violence, but how very dare they, because his heart is as pure as snow. But of course, if Trump really was intending to get his mob to wreak violence, then it isn’t defamation to say that at all, now is it? So, the BBC gets to chat with Trump officials and minions about his state of mind that day, much to his eternal chagrin.</p>

<p>Trump also doesn’t want to show his financial records, because of course he doesn’t. However, you can’t get damages without showing you were damaged. Trump’s lawsuit says his reputation and that of the Trump Organization were damaged to the tune of $10 billion by the BBC’s broadcast—a broadcast that, mind you, did not air in the United States.</p>
<p>But neither the BBC nor the court is just supposed to take Trump’s word for that. Proving damages requires coughing up financial data that shows you lost money, missed out on business deals, and the like.</p>
<p>Now, Brito is trying to <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/07/21/trum-bbc-lawsuit-finances">walk back</a> the damages claim, saying he only wants reputational damages and therefore no longer has to show that Trump’s companies were damaged to the tune of $10 billion. This also didn’t fly with the court, which said that Trump has to provide discovery based on the complaint Brito brought, not some future complaint he might bring.</p>
<p>Trump can’t rely on his usual thuggery here. The BBC has no broadcast licenses in America that Federal Communications Commission Chair Brendan Carr can threaten. The BBC is not seeking government approval to <a href="https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2025/7/25/2335135/news/Bribery-is-still-illegal-Paramount-merger-reeks-of-Trump-payoff/">merge</a> with another giant company, approval that could only be achieved with a bribe. The BBC is a giant media company in another country and can afford to defend itself.</p>
<p>So Trump is trying a different tactic. Now, the Department of Justice is <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.703382/gov.uscourts.flsd.703382.91.0.pdf">seeking to participate</a> in the case on behalf of the United States. How, exactly, is the DOJ getting involved in one of Trump’s lawsuits that he brought in his personal capacity? The DOJ’s contention seems to be that because some of the people subpoenaed are or were in the government, the DOJ has to step in, and oh, by the way, it will take several weeks for them to decide officially what to do.</p>
<p>The BBC’s <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.703382/gov.uscourts.flsd.703382.99.0.pdf">response</a> to this nonsense highlights how this is exactly what happened in the slush fund sham settlement case. Here, Trump is saying he’s a private widdle guy bringing a lawsuit on his own, and the DOJ is saying they just happened to get interested in this case on behalf of the United States, and totally not at all because they just do what Trump tells them.</p>
<p>Except there was just a big court <a href="https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2026/7/14/800069843/courts/trumps-failed-slush-fund-deal-everyones-a-loser/">ruling</a> in the slush fund case that pointed out that Trump has issued executive orders and argued in multiple courts, including the Supreme Court, that he controls everything in the executive branch and they must do exactly what he says. Turning around and pretending, then, that the DOJ is somehow acting independent of Trump is ridiculous.</p>
<p>The court hasn’t yet ruled on whether the DOJ can step in, and you can also expect that Trump is going to run to the appellate court to whine about how mean it is that he is required to participate in his own lawsuit, but that likely only delays the inevitable.</p>
<p>Buckle up, Trump. You and your inner circle are not gonna enjoy this ride.</p>
