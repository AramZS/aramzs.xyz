<p>Boox’s new Tile lineup will take on Xteink’s popular e-readers that are small enough to <a href="https://www.theverge.com/news/838885/xteink-x4-e-reader-magnetic-apple-iphone-google-pixel">stick on the back of an iPhone</a> with MagSafe. As spotted by <a href="https://goodereader.com/blog/onyx-boox/new-onyx-boox-picco-wants-to-compete-against-xteink"><em>Good E-Reader</em></a>, the first one is the Picco, which will feature a 3.97-inch front-lit, black-and-white e-paper display and expandable storage with an SD card slot. Boox confirmed to <em>The Verge</em> that the Picco is in the works, noting that it’s part of a new Tile lineup and will have an “accessible price point,” but without any other details about its plans, like specs, the specific price, or a release date.</p>
<p>The front-lit display could put it on par with Xteink’s new $99 <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/966560/xteink-e-ink-e-reader-x4-pro-touchscreen-light-hands-on">X4 Pro e-reader</a>, which <em>The Verge</em>’s Andrew Liszewski calls “the best tiny e-reader you can buy.” However, part of the appeal of Xteink’s little e-readers is their ability to magnetically stick to the back of a phone, and it’s not clear yet if the Boox Picco will have a similar feature.</p>

<p>It appears to have buttons in a promotional image, but it’s also not clear yet if it will rely solely on buttons, like the <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/870583/xteink-x4-e-ink-reader-books-hands-on">original Xteink X4</a>, or also include a touchscreen. According to <em>Good E-Reader</em>, the Picco will likely have a minimal, tiled UI limited to basic apps like Neoreader, a music player, and a podcast player, putting it in a very different category than the Android-powered <a href="https://www.theverge.com/24184777/boox-palma-e-ink-smartphone-reader">Boox Palma</a>.</p>
