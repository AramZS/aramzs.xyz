<figure><figcaption>Bethesda </figcaption></figure>
<p>Microsoft is in full damage control mode following <a href="https://aftermath.site/xbox-layoffs-product-teams-fan-criticism/"><u>last week’s catastrophic layoffs</u></a> of 1,600 game workers—<a href="https://aftermath.site/xbox-layoffs-july-2026/"><u>with another 1,600 to come</u></a>—and if you observe its efforts (not very) closely, you’ll spot a theme: <em>Fallout</em>. Last week, it was <a href="https://aftermath.site/xbox-layoffs-obsidian-fallout-bethesda-memorial/"><u>Obsidian pivoting back to <em>Fallout</em></u></a> all these years after fan favorite <em>New Vegas</em>. This week, it’s Bethesda uttering words gamers have been waiting a decade to hear: <em>Fallout 5</em>.</p>
<p>In <a href="https://bethesda.net/en/article/7wrffyXajE4BmCLJVpOkcN/a-note-from-bethesda-game-studios?ref=aftermath.site"><u>a lengthy note</u></a>, Bethesda talked about how it’s “investing more deeply in the worlds players love” despite having just laid off hundreds of employees, touching on <em>Elder Scrolls VI</em> and <em>Fallout 5</em> without really saying much about them beyond what we already knew: They’re in development, they’re using custom Bethesda tech, and <em>Elder Scrolls VI </em>will be the first of the two to release… <a href="https://aftermath.site/elder-scrolls-vi-trailer-six-years-later/"><u>at some point</u></a>.</p>
<p>The note does not mention layoffs nor the many extremely talented people who contributed to the aforementioned projects—as well as <em>Starfield</em>, <em>Fallout 76</em>, and <em>Elder Scrolls Online</em>, <a href="https://aftermath.site/zenimax-save-our-devs-microsoft-protest-layoffs/"><u>not to mention classics like <em>Morrowind</em>, <em>Oblivion</em>, and <em>Skyrim</em></u></a>. It takes on the same tone as notes published by other recently eviscerated studios like <a href="https://x.com/idSoftware/status/2075638499412189455?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2075638499412189455%7Ctwgr%5E1fcf19ec17dad186aeb7f2bc6738276c6ff1941f%7Ctwcon%5Es1_&amp;ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fkotaku.com%2Fdoom-maker-id-software-pushes-back-against-concerns-about-its-future-we-still-have-the-crew-we-need-to-build-the-games-and-tech-were-known-for-2000714747&amp;ref=aftermath.site"><u><em>Doom </em>and <em>Quake </em>institution id Software</u></a>: Despite layoffs that, in many cases, <a href="https://aftermath.site/xbox-layoffs-id-software-frankfurt-doom/"><u>cut teams in half</u></a>, everything is Fine. Better than fine, in fact; studios are working on sequels to things you’ve heard of! Yaaaaaay.</p>
<p>The implicit message? All the bloodletting was—and will continue to be—worth it. This is just the price other people have to pay for your favorite games. Some self-proclaimed fans of these series have even declared the layoffs just punishment after developers didn’t give them exactly what they wanted sooner. Ameliorative non-announcements like these <a href="https://aftermath.site/podcasts/aftermath-hours-podcast-xbox-layoffs/"><u>do not disabuse them of that notion</u></a>.</p>
<p>But what really got me wasn’t Bethesda’s post about its thrilling future in the franchise mines, but what Xbox CEO and <a href="https://aftermath.site/asha-sharma-microsofts-xbox-instacart-meta-pain-sponge-succesion/"><u>pain sponge</u></a> Asha Sharma <a href="https://x.com/asha_shar/status/2078119482204270711?ref=aftermath.site"><u>had to say</u></a> in response:</p>
<blockquote>Todd Howard just shared plans for the <em>Fallout</em>, <em>The Elder Scrolls</em>, and <em>Starfield </em>franchises. These worlds have earned a special place with hundreds of millions of people over many years. I'm looking forward to what's next, including the partnership with Obsidian on a new <em>Fallout </em>game and closer collaboration with ZeniMax Online Studios on <em>The Elder Scrolls</em>. The achievements of these franchises are remarkable: <em>The Elder Scrolls V</em>: <em>Skyrim </em>has crossed 65 million units sold, becoming one of the 10 best-selling games of all time,<em> Fallout 4</em> has also crossed 35 million units sold, joining the top 25, and <em>Starfield</em> will soon reach over 1 billion hours played.</blockquote>
<p>This statement sucks so bad that it makes my blood boil. Look what the franchises did, all by themselves! Look what they’ve accomplished, of their own volition, thanks to their own intrinsic goodness! So many big numbers, which translate to giant heaps of cash in the bank accounts of execs and shareholders! Never mind the people who spent decades painstakingly crafting the many games, stories, and other creative endeavors that corporate ghouls now so happily flatten into “franchises,” erasing themes, meaning, and human beings in favor of empty iconography.</p>
<p>This is how the people running the show at Xbox—and countless companies besides—view games, not to mention everything else in their robotic, colorless lives. Numbers and lines trending ever upward. Fuel for platitudes and faux-inspirational nonsense directed at the LinkedIn hivemind, of which they all paradoxically believe they’re the main character. Another stepping stone toward the next bigger, better thing. No ultimate destination, no camaraderie, no soul. Just an endless ladder that leads to the void regardless of which way you climb.</p>
<p>Of course someone like this would survey the current landscape of justified rage and fear—after she, a glorified crony, buoyed by other cronies beneath her, tore crucial organs out of the games industry—and brainlessly burp up a bunch of names and numbers. Sharma is as hollow as her statements, and if things continue at their current pace, Xbox will be too.</p>
