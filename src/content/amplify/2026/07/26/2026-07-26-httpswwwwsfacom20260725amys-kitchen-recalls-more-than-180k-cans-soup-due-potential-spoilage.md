<p>(Gray News) - Amy’s Kitchen is recalling more than 180,000 cans of its lentil soup because the soup could spoil before use.</p>
<p>The company recalled one lot of Amy’s Organics Lentil – Light in Sodium, according to an <a href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/ires/?Event=99306">FDA enforcement report</a>. The report said 15,350 cases were recalled, with 12 cans in each case, resulting in 184,200 cans.</p>

<p>The report listed the reason for the recall as “potential for spoilage.” The <a href="https://www.amys.com/recall">company’s website</a> said it was due to a “manufacturer defect” in the can’s easy-open tab.</p>
<p>Recalled cans can be identified by lot number 60D0924 and best-by date April 2027.</p>
<p>The soup was sold in 28 states, including Arizona, Georgia, Florida, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Tennessee and Texas. The soup was also sold in Canada.</p>
<p>The recall was initiated on June 22. The FDA classified it as a Class II recall on July 14. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/industry-guidance-recalls/recalls-background-and-definitions">According to the FDA</a>, a Class II recall means consuming the recalled product may cause “temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences.”</p>
<p>Amy’s Kitchen said customers can return the recalled cans to the place of purchase for a refund.</p>
