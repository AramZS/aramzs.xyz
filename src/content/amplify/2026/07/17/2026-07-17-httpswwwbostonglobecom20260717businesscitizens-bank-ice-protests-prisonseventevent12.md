title: Citizens Bank parts ways with two prison operators amid ICE protests
---
<p>After enduring months of <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/10/business/citizens-bank-gbio-protest-ice/?p1=Article_Inline_Text_Link">anti-ICE protests at its bank offices</a>, Citizens Financial Group is parting ways with two for-profit prison operators that oversee detention facilities for the federal immigration enforcement agency.</p>
<p>Citizens, in a statement on Friday, said it will end its <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/03/06/business/citizens-bank-ice-protests-prison-companies/?p1=Article_Inline_Text_Link">credit facilities with GEO Group and CoreCivic</a> — a decision the bank said was driven by changing financial needs of the two clients, not any public relations impacts of the protests.</p>
<p>CoreCivic has been a Citizens client since 2011, and GEO has worked with Citizens since 2018. But CoreCivic has sold several properties to the US government, and GEO appears poised to do so as well. As a result of these changing capital needs and financial structures, Citizens said, the two companies have less of a need for a bank of its size. That prompted Citizens to declare that it’s time to exit the credit facilities for the two clients.</p>

<p>The bank added: “This is a business decision based on changed commercial circumstances and does not reflect any change in our view regarding these companies’ business models or operations.”</p>
<p>Throughout the protests, Citizens has refrained from commenting directly on the client relationships, citing the importance of protecting client privacy. But given the decision to sever ties with the two prison operators, Citizens said it’s now an appropriate time to comment.</p>

<p>Among other things, Citizens said it has been disappointed “that the activists have dragged it into what is largely a political matter” and that the groups’ characterizations of the bank, including about its commitment to immigrants, “do not reflect who we are or the record we have built.”</p>
<p>Fresh off a victory in which Avelo Airlines announced in January that it would end its deportation flights for the federal government, activists <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/05/02/metro/clergy-ice-citizens-bank-protest/?p1=Article_Inline_Text_Link">stepped up their pressure on Citizens</a> with waves of protests as well as by highlighting customer withdrawals. (As with Citizens, Avelo did not cite the protests as the reason it severed ties with ICE.) Several other major banks had already ended their relationships with private prison operators in 2019.</p>
<p>On Friday, the De-ICE Citizens Bank Coalition issued a statement calling the bank’s decision to end lending relationships with CoreCivic and GEO Group as “an important victory for the people who refused to let a major bank finance human suffering brought on by ICE detention activities.”</p>
<p>The coalition expressed skepticism about the bank’s framing that its exit is dictated by changing commercial circumstances, adding “the reality is clear: public conscience and community resistance matter and have paid off.”</p>
<p>However, the coalition is also seeking an assurance in writing from the bank that it’s terminating all banking relationships with CoreCivic and GEO Group. The stated reason for this: The bank, in its statement, referred to a federal requirement preventing it from denying services to businesses based on political considerations.</p>
<p>Citizens is one of the 20 largest banks in the US, with around $186 billion in deposits. The activist group says individuals, organizations, and municipalities have agreed to withdraw a combined total of more than $350 million in recent months because of the bank’s ties with the private prison industry.</p>
<p>Among the prominent local critics of Citizens was the Greater Boston Interfaith Organization. A spokesperson says the GBIO and its member congregations committed to withdrawing a combined $13 million in deposits from Citizens in protest, while nearly 100 individuals associated with these churches pledged to pull another $7 million.</p>
<p>Prominent GBIO leader Ray Hammond, a pastor at the Bethel AME Church in Jamaica Plain, said he was “cautiously elated” to hear the news about Citizens. Hammond, who once served on the bank’s board more than two decades ago, said he’s happy the bank is doing the right thing by severing ties to the prison companies, even if it isn’t admitting to being motivated by the protests.</p>
<p>“I have to believe that banks care about their reputation and their brand,” Hammond said. “I have to believe that being connected to an agency which not only terrorizes immigrant neighbors but kills American citizens isn’t great for your brand. ... I do hope this encourages other people to really ask, rather than just complain about the direction your country is going, what can I do to change that direction?”</p>
