<h2>Poetic License</h2>

<p>In her recent controversial review for the <em>London Review of Books</em>, translator and classicist Emily Wilson excoriates director Christopher Nolan for what she sees as his flat and humorless take on <em>The Odyssey</em>. Far from being the witty, flirtatious trickster that Wilson had hoped to see, Matt Damon’s Odysseus instead trudges across the screen for three hours watching his friends die face down in the shit. True enough. She shouts out a number of “creative responses” to the poem that she prefers, like Margaret Atwood’s <em>Penelopiad</em>, and Lisa Peterson’s play, which sets the story in a contemporary migrant camp. But considering how much Wilson yearns specifically for the humor and the godliness of the source material, I was surprised that she never mentioned the Coen Brothers’ 2000 musical romp, <em>O Brother, Where Art Thou?</em></p>
<figure><img alt="" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px" src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/11/a3/11a34ed2-0d03-4467-9051-6142cc358694/content/images/2026/07/OB-foursome.png" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/11/a3/11a34ed2-0d03-4467-9051-6142cc358694/content/images/size/w600/2026/07/OB-foursome.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/11/a3/11a34ed2-0d03-4467-9051-6142cc358694/content/images/size/w1000/2026/07/OB-foursome.png 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/11/a3/11a34ed2-0d03-4467-9051-6142cc358694/content/images/2026/07/OB-foursome.png 1152w"/><figcaption><a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001806/?ref_=mv_desc">John Turturro</a>, <a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000123/?ref_=mv_desc">George Clooney</a>, <a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0625789/?ref_=mv_desc">Tim Blake Nelson</a>, and <a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0454584/?ref_=mv_desc">Chris Thomas King</a> in <a href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0190590/?ref_=mv_desc"><i><em>O Brother, Where Art Thou?</em></i> (2000)</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Wilson spends a good deal of her review pointing out places where Nolan’s script fails to include this or that favorite tidbit from the poem, so it’s possible that the Coens’ adaptation just doesn’t rate. Their script is more like a gesture drawing someone might do of the story after having heard the gist of it thirdhand. In fact neither Joel nor Ethan Coen had even read <em>The Odyssey</em> when they wrote it. Then again, Wilson notes that the original epic was a mishmash of many dialects and traditions handed down through generations, originally recorded back when the alphabet was still a hot new technology. <em>The Odyssey</em> has never been anything but pastiche.</p>
