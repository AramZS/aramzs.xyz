<h2>The Jewish case for an AI exemption at work.</h2>
<h4><strong>IMPORTANT CAVEAT:</strong></h4>
<p><em><u>Don't say you didn't read this, OK.</u></em> What's below is not an argument for a ban on all AI use in all contexts.* In addition, I agree with those who note that we must also talk about cloud computing, Zoom and other aspects of data center work. Ultimately, what we need is regulation and policy change– just as we can't focus solely on individuals recycling while ignoring the fact that the Pentagon is the largest single consumer of fossil fuels in the world.</p>
<p>Rather, this piece offers an argument for some religious (Jewish) grounds under which an individual worker might request a<em> reasonable accommodation </em>from a job-related obligation or expectation to use AI. It also, I believe, makes a case for why Jewish individuals and organizations might consider thinking twice about the cavalier use of generative AI.**</p>
<p> *Even asking that question would demand a much more thoughtful, considered engagement with the technology and literature than I am able or willing to do at this time.<br/>
**Again, I'd like to table conversations about assistive tech for disabilities, use of AI for medical research, and much more for some other time. </p>
<p>Title VII of the Civil Rights Act makes it illegal for</p>
<blockquote>"an employer... to discriminate against any individual with respect to [their] compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment, because of such individual's race, color, religion, sex, or national origin; or... otherwise adversely affect [their] status as an employee, because of such ...."</blockquote>
<p>Nobody would think twice about demanding a <u>reasonable accommodation</u> if corporations asked Jews to start violating Shabbat or partaking of the pork BBQ at the company retreat against their own practice. And while I would not demand that every Jew request such an accommodation (for Shabbat or keeping kosher), they should absolutely have that option available, even if their religious practice is a work in progress. So too, here.</p>
<p>And despite seeing many people (including religious Jews!) eliding the moral and religious issues around AI because "<em>that train has already left the station</em>," we should make choices based on whether they are right or wrong. Also, it's a fallacy: Movements grow from dissent. College students are <a href="https://fortune.com/2026/06/15/stanford-students-protest-sundar-pichai-google-graduation-speech/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com"><u>already</u></a><a href="https://www.post-gazette.com/business/tech-news/2026/05/30/artificial-intelligence-workforce-job-displacement-pitt/stories/202605220063?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com"> <u>pushing</u></a><a href="https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/berklee-college-ai-music?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com"> <u>back</u></a> against AI use and God willing we'll change who's holding power very soon (speedily, and in our days). And the fact that the immediate response to the papal encyclical was a groundswell of people <a href="https://www.inc.com/minda-zetlin/employees-are-seeking-religious-exemptions-to-avoid-using-ai-heres-why-every-ceo-should-pay-attention/91358715?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com"><u>looking</u></a> for religious exemptions was telling. We all decide what train tickets to get.</p>
<p>So, why might a person need a religious accommodation in order not to use AI at work?*</p>
<p> *Note, again: I'm not telling you, specifically, what to do.   Special thanks to the fabulous Liz Platt, Director of the <a href="https://lawrightsreligion.or/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">Law, Rights and Religion Project</a>, for her notes on this. You can read more about the broader fight for religious liberty beyond the Christian Right <a href="https://lawrightsreligion.org/our-work/whose-faith-matters?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">here</a>. Thanks also to the wonderful Rabbi Mike Rothbaum for his eyes and insight. </p>
<h3><br/>1) It's forbidden to benefit from theft. </h3>
<p>As you may know, AI models (whether ChatGPT, Midjourney or etc.) don't get information from nowhere. They scoop up profound amounts of data from the Internet, and their responses to people's prompts are based on what they've already stashed in their proverbial pockets, which was taken from the labor of real people (many of whose livelihoods now at risk as a result of all this.) <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20221121234510/https://twitter.com/Andantonius/status/1591280083679215616">Here's</a> a baking metaphor, if it's helpful.</p>
<p>As it happens, I'm one of the many authors whose books (half a million of them!) <a href="https://www.npr.org/2025/09/05/nx-s1-5529404/anthropic-settlement-authors-copyright-ai?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">were stolen</a> by Anthropic AI. After a judge ruled that our work was pirated and a class action lawsuit could go to trial, they settled out of court. Legally: theft. Judaism isn't a fan of the practice.</p>
<p>But! You might say! I'm not stealing the work! It's already stolen! All I'm doing is entering in some words and asking it to make me a picture!</p>
<p>As Rabbi Cantor Jessica Lynn Fox <a href="https://www.ccarnet.org/tag/journal-2025/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">put it</a>,</p>
<blockquote><em>Copyright and intellectual property protections in Jewish law go back at least to Talmudic times, when we read in BT M’gillah 15a: “And Rabbi Elazar further said that Rabbi Chanina said: Whoever reports a saying in the name of one who said it brings redemption to the world.”</em></blockquote>
<p>More than that, it's forbidden to <em>benefit</em> from stolen goods. To wit:<br/>
Sefer HaChinuch, an important work from thirteenth-century Spain:</p>
<blockquote>It is forbidden to benefit from one about whom it is assumed that all that they have is from stolen property. (Sefer HaChinukh 229:4)</blockquote>
<p>From there, the authoritative 16th c. law code the Shulchan Aruch:</p>
<blockquote>Anything that is assumed to be stolen, it is forbidden to acquire/take it. And so, if the majority of that thing is stolen, we don't take/acquire it. (Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat 358:1)</blockquote>
<p>And after that, a digest of the Shulchan Aruch summarizes a few things:</p>
<blockquote>It is forbidden to derive the slightest benefit from that which was stolen or robbed while it is in the possession of the thief or robber. (Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 182:9) </blockquote>
<p> See also, eg (eg Talmud Beitza 29a, Mishneh Torah, Robbery and Lost Property 13:11, etc.) </p>
<p>Is the thief the the AI generator, the CEO of the company, profiting off the theft, or the company's shareholders? I'm not sure, at the end of the day, that it matters, for <em>"that which was stolen" </em>is still in their possession, helping the "thief or robber" to generate a profit.</p>

<h3>2) Using generative AI without attribution is fraud.</h3>
<p>I'll just <a href="https://www.ccarnet.org/ccar-responsa/5784-2-artificial-intelligence-and-authorship/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com#post-21538-footnote-2">hand the mic</a> to the Central Conference of American Rabbis for this one:</p>
<blockquote>If [a] rabbi claims to be the “author” of a poem without indicating that it was produced using AI, the congregation will hear the rabbi saying that [they] produced original content... To not make that difference clear is to engage in <em>onaat d’varim</em> (verbal fraud) or <em>g’neivat daat</em> (deception). Both of these are strenuously prohibited by our tradition.....</blockquote>
<p>There are many sources on both <em>onaat devarim </em>and <em>g'neivat da'at</em> within the tradition; the link above lists a number of them.</p>

<h3>3) We're commanded to preserve the earth.</h3>
<p>Though it's true that not all data center usage is due to AI, study after study indicates that AI systems are, as one paper <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666389925002788?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">put it</a>,</p>
<blockquote>"rapidly becoming the key growth driver of global data center electricity consumption."</blockquote>
<p>And though it's difficult to know exactly what that means, since transparency is so hard to come by, people are doing their best with the information they do have. We know that in 2023, early in the AI boom, US data centers consumed <a href="https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/land-lines-magazine/articles/land-water-impacts-data-centers/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">roughly</a> as much energy as the whole nation of Ireland. One <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666389925002788?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">study</a> suggested that the the carbon footprint of AI systems alone could be roughly the same as that of New York City, with a <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-ai-impacts-data-centers-water-data/?embedded-checkout=true&amp;ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">water footprint</a> that may approximate the entire global annual consumption of bottled water. AI data centers are, indeed, <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-ai-impacts-data-centers-water-data/?embedded-checkout=true&amp;ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">increasing water scarcity</a>.</p>
<p>On the level of legal obligation, the core of a Jewish environmental obligation comes from the commandment not to destroy fruit trees, even in times of war (for, as Ibn Ezra puts it, "<em>the life of humanity depends on the trees of the field." </em>(Ibn Ezra on Deuteronomy 20:19). This principle (known as <em>bal taschit, </em>"do not destroy,") is expanded in later Rabbinic literature, as Sefer HaChinuch notes:</p>
<blockquote>And this is the way of the pious and people of [proper] action — they love peace and are happy for the good of the creatures... and they do not destroy even a grain of mustard in the world. And they are distressed by all loss and destruction that they see; and if they can prevent it, they will prevent any destruction with all of their strength. (Sefer HaChinuch 529:2)</blockquote>
<p>And, more pointedly, we have the Rabbis of the Talmud saying <em>very explicitly</em> that wasting energy or similar resources is a violation of this sacred principle:</p>
<blockquote>One who covers an oil lamp [causing the flame to burn inefficiently] or uncovers a kerosene lamp [allowing the fuel to evaporate faster] violates the prohibition of bal tashchit. (Talmud Shabbat 67b)</blockquote>
<p>And these obligations extend to understanding the ways in which we are all interdependent:</p>
<blockquote>Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai taught a parable: People were on a ship. One of them took a drill and started drilling underneath him. The others said: What are you sitting and doing?! The person replied: What do you care!? Is this not underneath my area that I am drilling?! They said: But the water will rise and flood us all on this ship! (Midrash Leviticus Rabbah 4:6)</blockquote>
<p>It could be argued that these data centers will continue to exist regardless of the actions of any individual person or even community. However, it can also be argued, I believe, that we as Jews are commanded to do what we can to reduce our overall damage to the planet, and refraining insofar as one may be able from participating in an industry that is actively harmful to the future of the globe may be not only a pathway, but an obligation– and that compelling a Jew to serve as an active agent in this destruction is demanding that they violate the many principles enumerated above.</p>
<h3>4) We're commanded to preserve our own, and others' health. </h3>
<p>Perhaps unsurprisingly, it seems that all this data center pollution has serious health impacts. Amazon recently <a href="https://oregoncapitalchronicle.com/2026/03/31/amazon-to-pay-20-5-million-settlement-over-northeast-oregon-nitrate-pollution/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">settled</a> a class action lawsuit, paying over $20.5 million to 17 plaintiffs in one Oregon county impacted by contaminated groundwater near its data center that has resulted in a spike of rare cancers, kidney failures, miscarriages and other health issues. The NAACP is <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/apr/14/naacp-lawsuit-elon-musk-xai-memphis?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">suing</a> Elon Musk for violating the Clean Air Act as his xAI data centers spew carcinogens into Black communities at the Tennessee and Mississippi border. As one recent paper <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06288?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">suggests</a>,</p>
<blockquote>"data center health costs are unevenly distributed: in the most affected counties, the estimated per-household health burden can reach about seven times the national average."</blockquote>
<p>Chatbot usage has also been linked to murders, mass killings, overdoses, and the suicides of both adults and children.</p>
<p>The Torah teaches,</p>
<p>The important medieval commentator Rashi notes that this means,</p>
<blockquote>witnessing their death, if you were able to rescue them. (Rashi on Leviticus 19:16)</blockquote>
<p>The Torah also teaches that one should build a low wall or a railing on one's (flat) roof so that people don't fall off the roof. (Deuteronomy 22:8) From this commandment, Maimonides learns that</p>
<blockquote> ... any obstruction that is a danger to life must be removed as a matter of positive duty and extremely necessary caution.The sages have prohibited many things because they are dangerous to life. (Mishneh Torah, Murderer and the Preservation of Life 11:4-5)</blockquote>
<p>And the 16th c. Jewish law code the Shulchan Aruch notes, in the voice of Rabbi Moses Isserles, that</p>
<blockquote>One should be careful of all things that cause danger, because danger is stricter than transgressions.... and it is prohibited to rely on a miracle. (Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 116:5)</blockquote>
<p>In other words, preserving life and health are among our greatest moral priorities. Perhaps even more strongly than above, if we have the ability to take actions that might be detrimental to others' health or to refrain from doing so, Jewish law is hardly ambiguous on the matter. Of course, if one's AI usage is somehow connected to saving or preserving of life– for example, using it in the development of new cancer therapies – that may be part of a different conversation. But for many people, there may be a strong case to be made with regards to a religious accommodation to a professional requirement to its use. (This information may also dissuade some readers from its gratuitous use for "amusing" images or the like, perhaps.)</p>
<h3>5) We're here to be moral, human beings </h3>
<p>In a post <a href="https://www.centeraipolicy.org/work/ai-is-lying-to-us-about-how-powerful-it-is?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">entitled</a>, “AI Is Lying to Us About How Powerful It Is,”<br/>
Jason Green-Lowe, Executive Director of the Center for AI Policy,<br/>
wrote:</p>
<blockquote>Unlike humans, AIs have no innate sense of conscience or morality that would keep them from lying, cheating, stealing, and scheming to achieve their goals. You can train an AI to speak politely in public, but we don’t yet know how to train an AI to actually be kind....</blockquote>
<p>Given that AI has been trained on massive amounts of human-created information, it's unsurprising that these programs are spitting out information that is demonstrably <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07856-5?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">racist</a>, <a href="https://www.aub.edu.lb/communications/aub@work/Pages/2026/2/real-problem-behind-ai-misogyny-isnt-images.aspx?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">misogynistic</a>, <a href="https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/10/ai-llms-age-bias-older-working-women-research?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">ageist</a>, <a href="https://www.psu.edu/news/information-sciences-and-technology/story/trained-ai-models-exhibit-learned-disability-bias-ist?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">ableist</a>, <a href="https://glaad.org/2026-ai-report-build-for-everyone/lgbtq-impacts/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">homophobic</a>, <a href="https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/02/06/llm-transphobia-researc/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">transphobic</a> and <a href="https://ironholds.org/resources/papers/agr_paper.pdf?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">more</a>, only exacerbating sites of ongoing iniquity– like in <a href="https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/01/20/pinpointing-ai-bias-health-care/?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">healthcare</a>, <a href="https://www.justice.gov/crt/case-document/file/1562776/dl?inline&amp;ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">housing</a>, bank <a href="https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-campbell-announces-25-million-settlement-with-student-loan-lender-for-unlawful-practices-through-ai-use-other-consumer-protection-violations?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">lending</a>, <a href="https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/ai-reinventing-hiring-same-old-biases-heres-how-to-avoid-trap?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">hiring</a>, and determining<a href="https://news.tulane.edu/pr/ai-sentencing-cut-jail-time-low-risk-offenders-study-finds-racial-bias-persisted?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com"> criminal sentences</a>.</p>
<p>And when researchers <a href="https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/ai/2026/05/researchers-left-ai-agents-alone-in-a-virtual-town-and-watched-it-all-unravel?ref=lifeisasacredtext.com">gave</a> AI agents a simulated town, allowing them control over everything from resource management to voting, the pursuit of</p>
<blockquote>"what is right and good" (<em>Deuteronomy 6:18)</em></blockquote>
<p>wasn't on the agenda. Models ran from rubberstamping ideas without thought diversity (Claude); something described as a "shared hallucination," (Gemini); inability to care for life (OpenAI); or, yes, total societal collapse within 96 hours (Grok, ofc).
