<p>The White House was already facing credible allegations of having politicized federal disaster relief funds, and in recent days, concerns reached a new level. <a href="https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-endorsements-federal-disaster-relief-fema">Politico reported</a>:</p>
<blockquote> <p>President Donald Trump denied disaster aid to four Democratic-led states in a move that is raising new questions about whether he’s injecting political motivations into emergency management decisions.</p> <p>Trump on Friday rejected $227 million in aid requests from New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts and Rhode Island for help recovering from a major snowstorm in February. Two days earlier, Trump approved Federal Emergency Management Agency disaster aid for six Republican-led states, while extolling the political virtues of GOP politicians and candidates in the states.</p> </blockquote>
<p>The pattern has hardly gone unnoticed.</p>
<p>Members of Rhode Island’s congressional delegation <a href="https://www.whitehouse.senate.gov/news/release/ri-lawmakers-blast-trump-for-politicizing-disaster-aid-denying-ris-request-for-blizzard-recovery-assistance/">sent the president a letter</a> earlier this week, explaining, “On the same day you approved major disaster declarations for several Republican-led states, you chose to leave Rhode Islanders out in the cold. It is unacceptable to politicize the disaster declaration process.”</p>
<p>As Politico’s report noted, Democratic Sen. Andy Kim of New Jersey similarly said in a statement, “If there is a reason other than politics for him and his administration to withhold FEMA assistance, we need an explanation immediately.”</p>
<p>To hear Team Trump tell it, the concerns are baseless. In fact, a White House spokesperson said in a statement that “there is no politicization to the president’s decisions on disaster relief.”</p>
<p>Ideally, that would be believable, though the administration hasn’t exactly earned the benefit of the doubt.</p>
<p>Last year, for example, Trump <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/115419577297297862">announced his approval</a> for a disaster aid request for Missouri while emphasizing the number of times he won Missouri’s electoral votes — as if there was a connection between his political support there and his eagerness to provide federal relief. In the same statement, he went on to mention the “incredible Patriots” in the GOP stronghold.</p>
<p>Last week, shortly before rejecting federal disaster funds for several states led by Democrats (states he lost in all three of his presidential campaigns), the president also announced relief aid to states he won in 2024, several of which <a href="https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-endorsements-federal-disaster-relief-fema">included explicit endorsements for Republican candidates</a> in the respective states.</p>
<p>In other words, even as the White House tells the public that “there is no politicization to the president’s decisions on disaster relief,” Trump has <em>literally connected campaign endorsements</em> to his approvals for aid requests, a move that has no precedent in American history.</p>
<p>Making matters worse is the familiarity of the circumstances. <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/03/23/trump-denies-disaster-aid-for-democratic-led-states-00831199">Politico reported</a> in March, “President Donald Trump has rejected disaster aid for Democratic-run states at the highest rate in the 47-year history of the Federal Emergency Management Agency.”</p>
<p>The same report, relying entirely on FEMA data, found that the Republican administration’s approval of federal relief for red states inched higher during the first year of Trump’s second term, while approval of aid to blue states collapsed to unprecedented levels.</p>
<p>“Never in my lifetime has a president treated disaster relief as a political cudgel,” Washington Sen. Patty Murray, the top Democrat on the Appropriations Committee, said in response to the data. “What President Trump has done to politicize disaster relief and hold up support for Americans who need it — including my constituents in Washington state — is frankly unforgivable.”</p>
