<h2> <p>‘Decision again highlights the extremely tragic situation of the universal Church’</p></h2>
<figure><img src="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/media/5707579/3eymdy3-20260708102819440_web.jpg?width=410"/></figure>
<p>THE Society of St Pius X (SSPX) has rejected a <a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/topics/vatican">Vatican</a> excommunication order after its unauthorised consecration of bishops last week (<a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2026/3-july/news/world/world-news-in-brief">News, 3 July</a>), insisting that <a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/topics/pope-leo-xiv">Pope Leo</a>, or his successors, will one day adopt its programme of “restoring all things in Christ”.</p>
<p>“This decision again highlights the extremely tragic situation of the universal Church,” the Superior General SSPX, Fr Davide Pagliari, told the Pope in a letter.</p>
<p>“Our Society has taken and will continue to take a far-reaching initiative to help souls, amid the doctrinal and moral confusion into which the Church has fallen. We in no way claim to be replacing the Church and have no other ambition than faithfulness to her.”</p>
<p>The SSPX was reacting to the ruling by the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith that the society had acted schismatically by consecrating four bishops “without a pontifical mandate” during a mass festival in Switzerland, and that “clerics and lay faithful” also faced excommunication if they continued to follow the movement.</p>
<p>The letter said that the “objectively unjust and invalid” sanctions would one day be removed, when the Vatican realised that the SSPX was not a “nest of serpents and scorpions”, but “a small army of loyal sons” and “authentically Catholic souls”.</p>
<p>Founded by the French Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre, who was excommunicated in 1988, the SSPX uses the pre-Second Vatican Council missal and rejects the Council’s approaches to ecumenism and religious freedom.</p>
<p>It claims about 200,000 followers, with more than 700 priests, and published a “profession of faith” two weeks ago, listing its 154 doctrines.</p>
<p>The chairman of the London-based Latin Mass Society, Dr Joseph Shaw, branded the SSPX’s move unjustified, but also urged bishops to make better arrangements for traditionalists who remained loyal to the Pope.</p>
<p>In a statement on Monday, the SSPX accused Rome of “acting inconsistently” towards dissenting groups, referring to its relations with “Orthodox schismatics” and the recognition of bishops approved by the <a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/topics/china">Chinese </a>Communist Party.</p>
<p>The Pope, it complained, had “rolled out his red carpet” for the <a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/topics/archbishop-of-canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> in April (<a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2026/1-may/news/uk/a-joy-and-privilege-to-be-received-by-the-pope-says-archbishop-of-canterbury">News, 1 May</a>), when <a href="https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/topics/roman-catholic-church">Catholic</a> doctrine did not “recognise the validity of Anglican consecrations or the ordination of women”.</p>
