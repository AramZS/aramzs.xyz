<div class="rw-embed-wrapper"><embed src="https://cdn.embedly.com/widgets/media.html?src=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fembed%2FdInnbQrYVhQ%3Ffeature%3Doembed&amp;display_name=YouTube&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DdInnbQrYVhQ&amp;image=https%3A%2F%2Fi.ytimg.com%2Fvi%2FdInnbQrYVhQ%2Fhqdefault.jpg&amp;type=text%2Fhtml&amp;schema=youtube"/></div><p><strong>Girls Can Tell </strong>is a perfect album. It was a comeback album from a band I did not care about till they came back. After two disappointing 90s albums their label dropped them. They went on hiatus and somewhere in there Britt Daniel studied The Kinks and Elvis Costello and became a first-rate songwriter.</p><p>Daniel’s got an instantly recognizable rock and roll voice. He emotes like a champ. He’s got throaty raspyness with an underlying sweetness. He makes interesting choices in his vocal melodies and line delivery. He can Beatles when he wants to–<strong>John Lennon specifically.</strong></p><p>Spoon aren’t full on retro time travelers like The Lilys or Apples in Stereo (bands I also love). This may be why Spoon are more popular. Yet I often find other bands that aim to modernize 60s &amp; 70s sounds end up with bland results or incorporate only the aspects of contemporary music I don’t like. Spoon perfects the balance.</p><p>Spoon was co-founded by drummer Jim Eno. <strong>Spoon sounds like a band co-founded by a drummer.</strong> The piano? Percussive. Vibraphone going bing bong? Percussive. Daniel’s vocals on songs like “<a href="https://youtu.be/dT2bvgv4pNA?si=SuqTGa8MiqpA6O5c">Believing Is Art</a>” are EXTREMELY percussive. Loads of syncopation. Apparently they are fellow fans of Jamaican Ska.</p><p>There’s a lot going into these songs: yes Power Pop but also a Steely Dan chord here, a Duane Eddy guitar there. A Marquis Moon bass moment. And bless them for having vocal harmonies even if it’s just Daniel multitracked.</p><ul><li><a href="https://youtu.be/dInnbQrYVhQ?si=cBk9gqPtPJbJ1LNb"><strong>Everything Hits at Once</strong></a> is an all time great breakup song. The lyrics have the broadly relatable feelings that will connect with anyone who’s ever fallen out of love. At the same time, the lyrics have a specificity in their vacillation between confidence and doubt that make it vulnerable and interesting.</li><li>That song’s rhythm creeps up on you. Try singing the word “sneak” along with those little lightly played keyboard chords.</li><li>How was <a href="https://youtu.be/Amg2CzkWYQo?si=j7-ThAgwcg9cFYPA"><strong>Me and the Bean</strong></a> not a single?! (Probably because it is a cover)</li><li><strong><a href="https://youtu.be/nOH6BMPCetE?si=X_mfXk9T_IFUNvDr">Lines in the Suit</a> </strong>is my favorite Elvis Costello song of 2001.</li><li><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NU3qhr-NBk0&amp;list=RDNU3qhr-NBk0&amp;start_radio=1"><strong>The Fitted Shirt</strong></a> borrows Kashmir’s groove, but its about the challenge of finding slim fit shirts in the 90s. It’s also about complicated Dad feelings and nostalgia.</li><li>In retrospect <strong><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qCJ3SxUGr58&amp;list=RDqCJ3SxUGr58&amp;start_radio=1">Anything You Want</a> </strong>sounds 90s by way of Todd Rundgren. Great vocals.</li><li><strong><a href="https://youtu.be/lh9a37q7J5g?si=6fHXSjC6NXczYxUr">Take A Walk</a> </strong>goes hard. A real rocker that presages 2022’s Lucifer on the Sofa.</li><li><strong><a href="https://youtu.be/CLZZhUvWG28?si=RC0anp68bw44zjxk">10 20 am</a> </strong>is like the chamber pop songs The Kinks put on b-sides. Imagine if Spoon had a Dave Davies for backing vocals. I love Daniel’s voice and yet, what if he had a younger brother with even more rasp and nasality!?! IMAGINE!</li><li><a href="https://youtu.be/uMkqwM83M5k?si=XAhNszC76bPKq7BJ"><strong>Take the Fifth</strong></a> has become an all time favorite song. It reminds me of<a href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOJWuc3CN303NCSavTHEPc2R_JREBHr9Y"> Lilys’ perfect distillation of 1966</a>. The song is tight as fuck till a key few moments when it deliberately sounds like it’s about to fall apart. Like t<a href="https://youtu.be/uMkqwM83M5k?si=6mANjRoFkv81zd5f&amp;t=96">he tension of the off-key piano notes they hit at 1:46</a>. That’s drama!</li><li><a href="https://youtu.be/UwdfvcykQ0o?si=a-LpSzq0N-aFWkwz"><strong>This Book is a Movie</strong></a> is an early exploration to a cinematic style they’ll expand in the future. Bit of a Steely Dan vibe?</li><li><a href="https://youtu.be/pKi3DPIwoO0?si=1wo8Pp_L_a9PpFxI"><strong>Chicago At Night</strong> </a>is a modern take on The Kinks’ character study songs but loaded with self recognition too.</li></ul><p>The album is over too soon and I still don’t know what exactly it is that Girls Can Tell. Perhaps the title is a play on <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sa3l3Ei0tdg&amp;list=RDsa3l3Ei0tdg&amp;start_radio=1">Dave Edmund’s song Girls Talk</a> (written by Elvis Costello)?</p><p>I’m usually not into the bands Spoon get’s filled away with. I find that era of indie band full of dullness and dudes that can’t sing. But Spoon is MY indie band. Maybe yours too?</p><figure><img alt="the cover of spoon’s album girls can tell. fetaures a record spinning quickly" height="600" src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*P0ySQEr-eGkirraEw5poOQ.jpeg" width="600"/></figure>
