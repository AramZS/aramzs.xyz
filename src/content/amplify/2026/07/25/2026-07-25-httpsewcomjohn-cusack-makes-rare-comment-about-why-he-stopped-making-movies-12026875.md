<h1>John Cusack makes rare comment about why he pivoted away from film career</h1>
<p>The star of “High Fidelity” and “Say Anything” was promoting his new graphic novel that required “no committee.”</p>
<p>By</p>
<p><a href="https://ew.com/raechal-shewfelt-8642216">Raechal Shewfelt</a></p>

<ul>
<li>While promoting his upcoming graphic novel, John Cusack explained what’s keeping him away from Hollywood in recent years.</li>
<li>The star of movies such as <em>Say Anything</em> said his book was the work of two people, himself and the illustrator.</li>
<li>Now 60, Cusack appears in fewer movies annually than he once did.</li>
</ul>
<p><a href="https://ew.com/person/john-cusack/">John Cusack</a> is proud that his latest project is the work of a bare-bones crew.</p>
<p>“This was just like I wrote it. And I said, ‘I like this story,’” Cusack told <a href="https://x.com/Variety/status/2080721412663263270?s=20"><em>Variety</em></a> before appearing at <a href="https://ew.com/comic-cons/">San Diego Comic-Con 2026</a> to promote his upcoming graphic novel, <em>Momo</em>.</p>
<p>The <em>Say Anything</em> alum, who teamed up with artist Ignacio Noé for the book, has appeared in fewer films in the past few years than he once did.</p>
<p>The reason, on his end at least, became clear as he explained his latest creation.</p>
<p>“Nobody edited anything I wrote or that he drew. So it’s really a piece of work that was just done by two people,” Cusack said of himself and Noé. “It was just me and Ignacio’s story, and that doesn’t happen today.”</p>
<p>He said he appreciated that there was “no committee.”</p>

<p>The star of films such as <em>High Fidelity, Being John Malkovich,</em> and <em>Grosse Pointe Blank</em> had been looking for that kind of freedom in his work.</p>
<p>“I didn’t want to negotiate telling a story really,” said Cusack, 60. “When you ask somebody to put like $20 to $30 million into a bank account [for a film budget], they want to have an opinion, and they’re afraid and all that stuff.”</p>
<p>To Cusack, graphic novels seemed to be “the closest thing to film.”</p>
<p><img alt="John Cusack stars in 'High Fidelity' in 2000" src="https://ew.com/thmb/nhJwJeRCIwiwLVyo1K1sKvuDNw8=/2000x0/filters:no_upscale():max_bytes(150000):strip_icc():format(webp)/john-cusack-high-fidelity-072426-8e040d872c2741a79f47f653a5e3f77e.jpg"/></p>
<p>John Cusack stars in ‘High Fidelity’ in 2000.<br/>

<p>Cusack appeared in two films in 2025, the thriller <em>Fog of War</em> and <em>Detective Chinatown 1900</em>, a Mandarin-language film directed by Sicheng Chen and Mo Dai. Chen also directed his only movie in 2024, war drama <em>Decoded</em>. Before that, he did a single movie in 2022 — the action flick <em>Pursuit</em>— and the 2020 series <em>Utopia</em> for Amazon.</p>
<p>He complained as long ago as September 2014 about movie-making committees.</p>

<p>“My friend Joe Roth ran Disney [until 2000],” Cusack told the <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/sep/25/john-cusack-hollywood-maps-to-the-stars-interview"><em>Guardian</em></a>. “He made things like <em>The Rock</em> and <em>Con Air</em> to make shareholders happy, but then he also gave six or seven slots to people he liked. I got to make <em>High Fidelity</em> and <em>Grosse Point Blank</em>. Spike Lee got to make <em>Summer of Sam</em>. Wes Anderson got to make <em>Rushmore</em>. I had that memory of film and that’s gone.”</p>
<p>He added that there was once a “one for you, one for them” system that had ended.</p>
<p>“Now it’s six for them – with a committee cutting the film who weren’t part of making it – and maybe one for you,” Cusack said.</p>
<p>Cusack’s graphic novel <em>Momo</em> is out Sept. 26.</p>
