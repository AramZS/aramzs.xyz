<p>There are many things that Maria, who just graduated from high school, stressed about over the past four years: grades, her varsity-basketball team practice and game schedules, the usual friend drama, crushes, college applications. Not on that list: a forced genital exam to determine whether she was “really” female. Maria grew up in Washington State and has been playing basketball since she was 5 years old, often on all-girls teams, and she never had to prove anything beyond her skill to do so. But if she were still a senior come November, she along with her teammates and every other school-sports-playing girl across the state, could be required to do exactly that.</p>
<p>This fall, voters will weigh in on <a href="https://www2.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/finaltext_3276.pdf">IL26-638</a>, a ballot initiative seeking to amend Washington state law to bar transgender athletes from participating on girls’ sports teams specifically. If it passes, the measure would impose special eligibility requirements on athletes from kindergarten through high school, mandating that a health-care provider <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/2024-olympics-gender-test-imane-khelif-rose-eveleth.html">certify the players’ sex assigned at birth</a> through genetic screening, a testosterone-levels test, or an examination of their reproductive anatomy at their yearly sports physical.</p>
<p>Those physicals, Maria explains, are usually perfunctory: “Like, <em>Are you walking normally? Are you lightheaded?</em> I have never had to take any clothes off. I’ve never felt uncomfortable or violated.” If IL26-638 were to pass and Maria were still in school, she says she would likely drop out of her team. (We’re withholding Maria’s real name to protect her privacy.) “I honestly don’t know if I would be able to do these tests,” she adds. “It would also make me question, If I do it, are all my teammates going to too? Or would I just play with the three or four girls who are willing to or have access to do it?”</p>
<p>IL26-638 comes amid a furious backlash against transgender rights. Sports specifically have been a field of contentious debate. Just recently, WNBA star Sophie Cunningham sparked controversy when <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/sophie-cunningham-wants-you-to-know-she-isnt-maga.html">she echoed</a> conservative talking points framing trans female athletes as a threat to their cisgender counterparts, claiming she wants to “protect women’s sports” from “biological men.” Since 2020, <a href="https://mapresearch.org/equality-map/bans-on-transgender-youth-participation-in-sports/">27 states</a> have restricted trans athletes’ ability to participate in girls’ and women’s sports, citing similar concerns around fairness and safety. In June, <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/supreme-court-transgender-girls-sports-decision.html">the Supreme Court ruled</a> in <em>West Virginia </em>v.<em> B.P.J.</em> that such bans are constitutional. In addition to Washington State, <a href="https://19thnews.org/2026/07/trans-athletes-sports-ballot-measures/">Arizona and Colorado</a> will have voters weigh in on whether to implement this type of ban. But IL26-638 is “the most extreme measure of its kind in the country in the mechanisms by which it forces all girls to have their sex assigned at birth verified by a health-care provider,” says Libby Watson, the campaign manager for No Hate in WA State.</p>
<p>Voters I spoke to, like Maria and Watson, are frustrated that this type of attack is coming to Washington of all places, a state with a track record as <a href="https://www.hrc.org/resources/state-scorecards/washington">one of the most progressive</a> in the country when it comes to LGBTQ+ rights. “It’s not reflective of who we are. It’s unbelievable to think we would be subjecting kids to these kinds of invasive medical exams that have no basis in health or safety,” says Lauren Hipp, a mother of three and the national director of early education at the advocacy group MomsRising. “One of my daughters is a rising fourth-grader, and she played a sport last year. Are you going to ask a fourth-grader to undergo this?”</p>
<p>The text of IL26-638 doesn’t establish who would conduct the sex-verification tests, who would pay for them, when they should take place, or even how the policy would be enforced across schools. That has left voters like Hipp with many questions: Will the verification of a student’s reproductive anatomy happen visually, or will it require an internal pelvic exam, which is rarely clinically indicated for children? Many <a href="https://nohateinwastate.org/about-i-638">schools in the state</a> offer free sports physicals, which are conducted in group settings, says Watson. So will girls be required to disrobe in front of their peers, then? And in the scenario where a parent refuses to let their child undergo a genital exam but can’t afford to pay for a genetic or hormone test, then what? Will their kid have to forgo playing sports altogether?</p>
<p>IL26-638’s supporters say passing it is necessary <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/sophie-cunningham-wants-you-to-know-she-isnt-maga.html">to protect girls</a> across the state. Since 2007, kids in Washington <a href="https://assets-rst7.rschooltoday.com/rst7files/uploads/sites/652/2024/05/29125133/Gender-Diversity-Toolkit_2024.pdf">have been allowed</a> to join sports teams that align with their gender identity. That is unacceptable to <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/01/09/us/politics/ballot-measures-washington-state-wealthy.html">Brian Heywood</a>, a right-wing hedge-fund manager <a href="https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2025/09/08/new-wa-initiatives-seek-to-undo-rewrite-of-parental-rights-law-block-trans-girls-in-sports/">who is backing</a> the ballot initiative through his political action committee, Let’s Go Washington. (Heywood is also funding a <a href="https://letsgowashington.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/IL26-001-TEXT-.pdf">separate ballot measure</a> that would repeal <a href="https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2025/05/20/wa-governor-signs-parental-bill-of-rights-rewrite/">recent pro-LGBTQ+ amendments</a> to a parental-rights law.)</p>
<p>The two-decade-old inclusive policy operates “at the expense of female athletes and their safety,” Heywood <a href="https://www.kuow.org/politics/2026-06-04/transgender-ballot-initiative-could-require-genital-exams-for-wa-secondary-school-students">recently told local radio station KUOW</a>. “This is not okay. Boys are welcome to play in whatever sport they want within the confines of their biological teams.”</p>
<p>Maria, one of the cisgender female athletes Heywood seems to be so worried about, tells me these concerns are overblown. According to the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association, there are only <a href="https://www.kuow.org/politics/2026-06-04/transgender-ballot-initiative-could-require-genital-exams-for-wa-secondary-school-students">about ten trans athletes</a> — including boys and girls — out of the more than 200,000 children currently participating in middle- and high-school sports in the entire state. In the 13 years Maria has played basketball and other sports, she’s never had a teammate who was openly trans and she can recall facing off against a single trans girl on another team only <em>once</em> — not that any of that should even matter. “If you’re coming to play a sport, then you’re coming to play a sport and that’s it,” Maria says. “There are already <a href="https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/in-focus/women-and-girls-in-sport">so many</a> <a href="https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167863">injustices</a> and <a href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/sexualorientation/iesogi/2023-10-31-stm-sogi-policy-en.pdf">inequalities</a> in sports. This is not one of them.” She adds that <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/trump-trans-athletes-executive-order-threats-to-women.html">fearmongering around trans athletes</a> transitioning to “steal” cis athletes’ spots or accomplishments is misogynistic. “They’re implying that a person could wake up one day, decide to transition, pop into this sport, and all of a sudden be amazing at it,” she says. “That really discredits women’s sports and the skill, strength, and dedication girls have put into their whole lives to become good at this.”</p>
<p><a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/60/3/198">A recent study</a> published in the <em>British Journal of Sports Medicine</em> found that the physical performance of trans female athletes tends to match that of their cisgender counterparts after they have undergone hormone therapy for a year or longer. “This refutes the logic behind blanket bans on transgender women in sports,” Bruno Gualano, a co-author of the study, told <a href="https://english.elpais.com/health/2026-02-04/groundbreaking-study-finds-no-evidence-that-trans-athletes-are-a-threat-to-womens-sports.html"><em>El País</em></a>. “Most of these policies are based on the assumption that transgender women retain inherent physical advantages and would therefore dominate women’s competitions. The data does not support this idea.”</p>
<p>“This is a witch hunt to find the five-to-ten trans girls who just want to play a sport they love with their friends and their community,” says Hipp. “This is absolutely not about girls’ safety. If they were interested in keeping girls safe, they wouldn’t have men like Brian Heywood forcing themselves into my daughters’ exam rooms.”</p>
<p>The text of the measure incorrectly suggests that children in Washington State already undergo sex-verification tests before they can play sports. Neither the state’s recommended <a href="https://wiaa-dna4aga5arc0gyeb.westus2-01.azurewebsites.net/results/Forms/PPE-Physical-Examination-Form.pdf">evaluation forms</a> nor those <a href="https://www.seattleschools.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Student-Physical-Participation-Exam-History-ADA.pdf">used</a> by Seattle Public Schools — Washington’s largest school district — require health providers to perform exams specifically to confirm a student athlete’s sex at birth.</p>
<p>Dr. Gina Martin, a family-medicine physician who offers sports physicals to her underage patients, agrees with the critics. “This measure was clearly not written by somebody who practices medicine,” she says. “Physicals focus on the two most important aspects of sports. First, musculoskeletal health — thinking about kids’ joints, bones, muscles, and tendons — and then cardiac health, looking into their heart health and finding out if we need to screen for additional cardiac risk factors.” She also screens for other medical problems, from asthma to rashes or lumps, that may need to be addressed before a child can participate in sports.</p>
<p>“What is certainly <em>not</em> medically necessary is for me to do an exam and confirm somebody’s sex, either through hormone testing, genetic testing, or a non-indicated genital exam,” Martin says, adding that she won’t do it if the measure passes. “I don’t even know how it would work because I’ve never run those tests on a patient.” A lot of colleagues have told Martin they are in a similar position and are horrified that the state may dictate how they practice medicine.</p>
<p>The fact that the interventions outlined in the measure are not medically necessary is something Paul, a high-school wrestling coach who trains both boys and girls, bristles at. “I’ve coached the sport for over eight years now, and I haven’t even encountered a trans athlete yet,” he says. “This whole ballot initiative is a waste of everyone’s time. It’s just people trying to push an agenda that I don’t think helps any kids.” (Paul asked to be identified only by his first name due to privacy concerns.)</p>
<p>If anything, Paul fears the measure could harm his female students. All his wrestlers are already required to undergo hydration and body-composition tests to help prevent them from engaging in unhealthy weight-cutting practices. To Paul, it already feels a bit intrusive to ask his teenage students to pee in a cup and have the girls wear only a sports bra and athletic shorts for their weigh-ins. “Asking them to dress to a certain level can be a lot,” he says. “So asking them to comply with even more invasive procedures is disgusting to me, quite honestly.”</p>
<p>And of course, should the measure pass, Paul would be forced not just to <em>ask</em> but to demand it. “There is <a href="https://www.hrw.org/report/2020/12/04/theyre-chasing-us-away-sport/human-rights-violations-sex-testing-elite-women">no consent that can be given</a> because the ‘choice’ athletes face is ‘Either you allow us to sex-test you and you can compete in your sport if you pass our test — or you can choose not to undergo the sex testing but then you can’t compete,’” explains Dr. Anna Posbergh, an assistant professor at the University of Illinois Department of Recreation, Sport, and Tourism who has studied this topic. “It is saying you don’t <em>have</em> to do it but then you won’t be able to do your job. You will lose your sponsorships. You will lose everything you’ve worked for. That’s not true consent. That’s coercion.”</p>
<p>“Sex testing is, in its nature, an extreme and unworkable concept,” says Chris Erchull, a senior staff attorney at GLAD Law. The practice in sports <a href="https://www.cbc.ca/listen/cbc-podcasts/1733-tested">dates back</a> nearly a century and has historically been used only at the elite level — think the Olympics or, as of July, <a href="https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/articles/cn4nvwl9550o">the Women’s Tennis Association</a>. But it has never been used for children and teens in this country. “The language in the measure is quite alarming because it quite literally would codify sex testing for youth athletes into the law,” Posbergh tells me. The initiative’s reference to verifying a “student’s reproductive anatomy” echoes the sex-testing policies of the 1960s, when female Olympians <a href="https://kpe.utoronto.ca/sites/default/files/donnelly-donnelly---sex-testing-naked-inspections-the-olympic-games.pdf">were required to walk naked</a> in front of (often male) doctors to prove their sex. This type of policy was short-lived owing to obvious ethical concerns.</p>
<p>The most enduring type of sex testing has been the genetic variety. Typically, cisgender women have XX chromosomes, while cisgender men have XY chromosomes. “One of the tests detects the presence of a second X chromosome; the other kind will detect the absence of a Y chromosome,” says Posbergh. This is how athletes can “prove” they are women. But the problem, Posbergh adds, is that biology doesn’t perfectly adhere to our definitions of female and male: “There are athletes who have a single X chromosome. There are athletes who have XXX chromosomes. We have so many <a href="https://www.thecut.com/2023/10/caster-semenya-race-to-be-myself-memoir-interview.html">stories of athletes</a> whose lives have been literally ruined because of a ‘failed’ sex test even though they had no performance advantage.”</p>
<p>Chromosome tests were stopped in the late 1990s due to these nuances but have seen a resurgence in this decade. Even geneticist Andrew Sinclair, <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/bookseries/abs/pii/S1067570196800040">who helped discover</a> the gene that indicates the presence of the Y chromosome, opposes these tests. “The gene’s presence only indicates that it exists, not whether it functions,” he <a href="https://hgsa.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/Position%20Statements/HGSA%20Position%20statement%20SRY%20gene%20testing%20in%20female%20athletes%2029%20August%202025.pdf">has said</a>, emphasizing that its presence alone “does not define biological sex.”</p>
<p>Testosterone tests have similarly become more common in recent years. “Testosterone was viewed as an adequate form of testing for sex because of the premise that men are inherently better athletes and that must be because they have higher testosterone,” Posbergh says. But this hormone exists on a spectrum — someone’s levels even <a href="https://www.trted.org/articles/do-testosterone-levels-change-throughout-the-day">rise and fall</a> throughout the day — and <a href="https://www.health.com/high-testosterone-in-women-8729788">there is a slew of medical reasons</a> outside of transitioning that could explain why a female athlete’s testosterone levels may be higher than the exams’ threshold.</p>
<p>So where does that leave girls in Washington State if voters approve the ballot measure? That remains unclear. Posbergh says genetic testing is generally expensive and requires specialized personnel that school districts simply don’t have. She’s also concerned that predators may take advantage of the genital exams in <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/justice-department-settles-with-larry-nassar-victims.html">a similar way to Larry Nassar</a>, the disgraced USA Gymnastics team doctor who <a href="https://www.thecut.com/2022/01/survivor-danielle-moore-larry-nassar.html">sexually abused</a> <a href="https://www.thecut.com/2021/12/larry-nassar-abuse-survivors-settle-for-usd380-million.html">more than 500 girls</a>.</p>
<p>“I haven’t met a single person who has thought this was a good initiative,” Maria, the student athlete, says. “And I think for good reason because it just has no place in school sports.” She has been organizing against the ballot measure, talking to other teenagers and tagging along with her parents to campaign rallies. “It breaks my heart. For me to encourage young girls to play sports and become passionate about it, just for more obstacles and barriers to be put in their way — it just feels so wrong,” she says. “This measure is rooted in such hate. It is villainizing children. It is controlling both girls’ autonomy and anatomy.”</p>
<p>“The sad and cynical truth is that people are funding these ballot initiatives because they want to drive Republican voter turnout for a midterm election,” says Erchull, the GLAD Law attorney. “It is just like <a href="https://ballotpedia.org/2006_ballot_measures">in 2006</a>, when Republicans poured money into ballot initiatives to oppose marriage equality.”</p>
<p>Watson, the No Hate in WA State campaign manager, warns that the measure could pass even in this deep-blue state. “Brian Heywood and Let’s Go Washington have millions of dollars, mostly given by Brian Heywood himself,” she says. “It’s a really expensive misinformation campaign that we’re up against. We know that both IL26-638 and the initiative around the state’s parental-rights law are beatable, but we need the resources to do that messaging.” As of July, it is a David-vs.-Goliath scenario: Campaign-finance records show that Let’s Go Washington <a href="https://www.pdc.wa.gov/political-disclosure-reporting-data/browse-search-data/committees/co-2026-30644#overview">has raised nearly $3.3 million</a>, while No Hate in WA <a href="https://www.pdc.wa.gov/political-disclosure-reporting-data/browse-search-data/committees/co-2026-38851">has raised a little more</a> than $1.3 million.</p>
<p>If voters end up passing IL26-638, overturning it won’t be simple. “I don’t want to pull any punches,” Erchull says. “The <a href="https://www.thecut.com/article/becky-pepper-jackson-supreme-court-transgender-sports-case.html"><em>West Virginia </em>v.<em> B.P.J.</em></a> decision just made it much more difficult to challenge a measure like this in court.” There are still some potential litigation avenues, including bringing forward privacy claims or arguing that, because the special eligibility requirements apply only to girls and not to boys, they constitute class-based discrimination. But those legal fights would be costly and lengthy, which means an untold number of girls across the state would have to submit to testing or forgo playing sports altogether.</p>
<p>Maria is frustrated that part of her experience as a first-time voter is in fighting a measure that seeks to create a solution for a problem that simply doesn’t exist. Don’t the adults pushing for these tests have anything better to do? “We’re forgetting this is about girls’ <em>school sports</em>,” she says with a typical teenage scoff. “This isn’t life-or-death.”</p>
