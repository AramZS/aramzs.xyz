<h1>Trump Orders Navy to Restore Older Tech on Aircraft Carriers, Costing Billions</h1>
<h2>The Navy spent years fighting the president’s push to go back to steam catapults</h2>
<p>By</p>
<p><a href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/marcus-weisgerber">Marcus Weisgerber</a></p>
<p>Updated Aug. 13, 2026 8:50 pm ET</p>
<figure>
<img alt="The USS Gerald R. Ford aircraft carrier approaching Naval Station Norfolk with sailors standing on deck, viewed from a pier where people in life vests are watching." src="https://images.wsj.net/im-47517692?width=700&amp;height=467"/>
<figcaption>The USS Gerald R. Ford pulling into Norfolk, Va., in May.Kendall Warner/The Virginian-Pilot/ZUMA Press</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>President Trump has directed the Navy to tear out a complex system for launching jet fighters from its aircraft carriers and instead go back to using steam catapults, a move Navy officials have fought for years and will likely cost billions of dollars.</p>
<p>U.S. officials say Trump signed a national security memorandum Thursday directing the change, which would involve removing the electromagnetic catapult system used by the ships in the Gerald R. Ford class of carriers.</p>
