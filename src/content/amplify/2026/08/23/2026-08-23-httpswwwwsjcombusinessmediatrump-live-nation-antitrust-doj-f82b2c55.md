<h2 data-testid="dek-block">Antitrust case started out aiming to force concert giant to sell Ticketmaster. After two White House meetings, a deal kept the company intact.</h2>
<p>Live Nation signs in Manhattan this spring. RICHARD B. LEVINE/ZUMA PRESS</p>
<p>By <a data-testid="author-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/rebecca-ballhaus">Rebecca Ballhaus</a> , <a data-testid="author-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/joe-palazzolo">Joe Palazzolo</a> , <a data-testid="author-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/dana-mattioli">Dana Mattioli</a> , <a data-testid="author-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/josh-dawsey">Josh Dawsey</a> and <a data-testid="author-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/dave-michaels">Dave Michaels</a> Aug. 23, 2026 9:00 pm ET</p>
<p>The Justice Department was preparing to wage a legal battle more than a decade in the making against the world’s largest concert promoter. Then President Trump called.</p>
<p>Settle it, the president told a senior DOJ official, the official later told associates.</p>
<p>The intervention was bizarre. The antitrust trial against <a data-type="company" href="https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/LYV">Live Nation</a>—which also owns Ticketmaster, a combination that draws relentless consumer criticism—was set to begin March 2, just days away. Trump had vowed to crack down on the industry just a year earlier when he signed an executive order targeting ticket-scalping and exploitative pricing in live entertainment. DOJ officials and the company had spent months exchanging settlement proposals but had little to show for it, and some in the government had been pushing to break up the giant.</p>
<p>White House involvement in law-enforcement matters, once taboo, is a regular feature of Trump’s second term. But many DOJ officials regarded the political interference in the Live Nation case as extreme even for an administration in which the president has openly pressured law-enforcement officials to pursue perceived enemies and pardoned allies.</p>
<p>Weeks before Trump’s call, Live Nation told DOJ officials that the company had brought in a fresh set of lawyers for settlement talks. They were from Sullivan &amp; Cromwell, the New York-based firm that represents Trump in his personal legal troubles.</p>
<div>DOJ officials also learned that   <a data-type="link" href="https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/boris-epshteyn-indian-billionaire-adani-592e9ede?mod=article_inline">Boris Epshteyn, Trump’s private legal coordinator</a>, had taken a keen interest in resolving the case, according to people familiar with the matter. The officials didn’t know whether he was working for Trump or Live Nation, or both. </div><p></p>
