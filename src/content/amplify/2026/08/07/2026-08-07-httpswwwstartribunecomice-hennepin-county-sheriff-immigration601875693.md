<p>Federal officials have warned Hennepin County Sheriff Dawanna Witt that failure to provide immigration agents access to inmates eligible for deportation could result in criminal charges, the latest attack by the Trump administration over the sheriff’s immigration policies.</p>
<p>U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has issued warnings to Witt that not handing over inmates eligible for deportation could result in federal witness tampering charges, according to an immigration enforcement subpoena obtained by the Minnesota Star Tribune. Similar memos were given to Sheriff Tom Dart of Cook County, Ill., which oversees Chicago, as first <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/05/us/ice-minnesota-sheriff-prosecution.html">reported by the New York Times</a>.</p>
<p>Detainer requests by ICE have remained a significant point of contention between cities and the Trump administration, including in Minnesota. Sheriffs have raised concerns that they risk civil rights violations if they hold someone in custody beyond their criminal case and do not comply with the request – unless agents have a signed judicial warrant.</p>
<p>White House border czar Tom Homan attributed the end of <a href="https://www.startribune.com/reckoning-retribution-and-resistance-the-behind-the-scenes-story-of-operation-metro-surge/601583420">Operation Metro Surge</a> — the increased immigration enforcement in Minnesota last winter — in part to increased cooperation with Minnesota jails, though Witt and many other sheriffs denied changing their policies.</p>
<p>The Department of Homeland Security said in an email the subpoenas are part of an effort to compel “sanctuary politicians” to cooperate with federal immigration authorities and detainers. The department added in a statement that authorities should “stop harboring” and releasing immigrants with a criminal background from custody, or work with ICE.</p>
<p>In a statement, Witt said her office will not change its policies as a result of the letters and that some of the subpoenas were served after the inmates in question were already released from custody.</p>
<p>“Threats of prosecution will not cause me to abandon my legal responsibilities or disregard constitutional protections. My focus remains on doing my job lawfully and not responding to political pressure or intimidation,” Witt said in the statement.</p>
<p>The Trump administration has repeatedly targeted Minnesota over immigration policies, but the moves have so far been unsuccessful.</p>
<p>A federal judge recently <a href="https://www.startribune.com/federal-judge-throws-out-justice-departments-lawsuit-targeting-minnesota-immigration-laws/601870223">dismissed a Justice Department lawsuit</a> against Minnesota and the Twin Cities over policies limiting how much officials cooperate with immigration agents. In his 56-page order, U.S. District Judge Eric Tostrud said forcing the state to assist with civil federal immigration law would violate the anti-commandeering doctrine under the Tenth Amendment, which prevents the U.S. government from compelling states to carry out its orders.</p>
<p>In another case, a judge temporarily blocked the administration’s attempt to withhold federal grants from communities to comply with its immigration policies.</p>
<p>One expert said a sheriff’s refusal to hand over inmates for deportation does not rise to the level of witness tampering as claimed in the subpoenas.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.startribune.com/is-minnesota-cooperating-with-ice-after-operation-metro-surge/601583745"><figure><img alt="" data-chromatic="ignore" data-nimg="1" src="https://arc.stimg.co/startribunemedia/AGPJ7V3RGRGITKTEBQ3GGUSLAM.JPG?&amp;w=80&amp;ar=1:1&amp;fit=crop"/></figure></a></p>
<p>“It’s an offensive reading of the statute,” said Bradford Colbert, a professor at the Mitchell Hamline School of Law. “There is no way anyone could possibly read this as tampering with a witness.”</p>
<p>Hennepin County oversees one of Minnesota’s largest jails and for roughly a decade has not abided by requests from immigration agencies to hold inmates on their behalf. Hennepin County also does not require inmates to reveal their immigration status or notify ICE when someone wanted by the agency is due to be released.</p>
<p>Richard Hodsdon, attorney for the Minnesota Sheriffs’ Association, was not aware of any other Minnesota sheriff who had received a letter like the one sent to Witt. Hodsdon said all state law enforcement officials honor federal warrants and court orders to turn over prisoners.</p>
<p>“The federal government has acknowledged that these detainers are requests,” he said.</p>
<p>During Operation Metro Surge, Witt and other county sheriffs faced pressure from Homan to change their policies and hold immigrants that ICE wanted until agents could detain them.</p>
<p>ICE detainer requests do not carry the same legal weight as a judicial warrant, and sheriffs risk civil rights violations if they hold people beyond the end of their criminal cases. Nobles and Anoka counties were successfully sued by immigrants who were held after their cases were concluded.</p>
<p>Last winter, a <a href="https://www.startribune.com/mn-jails-cooperation-ice/601581324">Star Tribune survey of all 87 sheriffs</a> in the state found that, of the more than half who responded, none said they made changes to their policies about how they cooperate with ICE. Law enforcement officials did say communication with immigration officials improved.</p>
<p>ICE has issued nearly 3,000 detainer requests to Minnesota law enforcement since Trump returned to office, according to the Deportation Data Project. The majority were issued to sheriff’s offices in the Twin Cities metro.</p>
<p>More than 1,300 detainers were declined by local agencies and Hennepin County turned down the most, denying 564 detainer requests.</p>
