<h1>Apple’s Next AirPods May Have Cameras. Your Office Isn’t Ready for the Privacy Implications</h1>
<p>A new video suggests AirPods with cameras in them are coming soon. Could they open the door for IP theft?</p>
<p>BY <a href="https://www.inc.com/author/kit-eaton">KIT EATON</a> <a href="https://x.com/kiteaton">@KITEATON</a></p>
<p>Aug 18, 2026</p>

<p>Apple’s big fall hardware release is just around the corner, and we suspect one of the hottest new devices we’ll see are <a href="https://www.inc.com/kevin-haynes/apples-secret-new-headphones-just-leaked-and-theyre-not-airpods/91349700">AirPods</a> with built-in cameras. A rumor about this innovation has been circulating for months, but the volume on those rumors just turned way up: Tech site <a href="https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/17/camera-equipped-airpods-macos-26-7/">MacRumors</a> dug into beta test code that Apple just released and found hard evidence that these new <a href="https://www.inc.com/jason-aten/live-translation-on-airpods-pro-3-is-the-breakthrough-apple-intelligence-has-been-waiting-for/91237635">AirPods</a> are on the way.</p>
<p>Because the cameras will be worn at ear height, they’ll probably have roughly the same viewpoint as the person wearing them will have—perfect for a sort of real time “what am I looking at?” query. A video clip MacRumors found gives an excellent example of one use. It explains that with “Apple Intelligence your world becomes saveable” so you merely have to, for example, look at the cover of a book you find interesting and ask it to save the data for you for later access.</p>
<p>MacRumors says that the system is even smart enough to detect if a users’ hair is obstructing the AirPod’s lenses, and will alert users that to “get the most accurate information about things in your environment” they should ensure they’re not covered in some way. According to an earlier <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-02/apple-subscriptions-macbook-air-shortages-apple-to-make-glasses-health-device-msbvajjp">leak</a> from Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman, these devices will have a codename of B790 and will be released as soon as next month, during an event that will also include new iPhone 18 units and probably Apple’s first foldable screen device—the iPhone Ultra.</p>
<p>It may sound bizarre to put a tiny camera system in devices that you shove into your ear. But it makes sense when you remember that Apple’s big bid to join the <a href="https://www.inc.com/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> revolution is coming in the form of <a href="https://www.inc.com/jason-aten/the-most-important-thing-apple-got-right-with-siri-ai/91365629">Apple Intelligence</a>. For the California-based tech giant, this AI push goes far beyond an update to the Siri chatbot. Instead, it’s threaded throughout the operating system of Macs, iPhones, and iPads. In this way, Apple Intelligence helps in multiple tasks, from simplifying emails to summarizing news stories, and it can also describe to users what it sees through, say, an iPhone’s cameras in real time. And this is where the new AirPods come in.</p>

<p>So far this may seem merely like an interesting piece of new hardware from a tech giant. And you can almost immediately imagine a host of uses for this kind of vision-enabled AI device, from helping you navigate through an unfamiliar city (“No, turn right here, not left!”) to logging the food you had for lunch. Instant translation of signs in a foreign language would also be handy. There are likely numerous health applications too, perhaps including fitness uses, and some to detect if the user has had an incident, similar to fall alerts from Apple Watches.</p>
<p>But if this rumor proves to be true (and the video evidence suggests it is) then Apple is entering somewhat risky territory. Meta has made a splash in the wearable tech market with its Ray Ban smart glasses. They’ve proved popular, and Meta recently upgraded them with a display. But in recent months the wearables have faced an aggressive public backlash, triggered by a Meta plan to build in <a href="https://www.inc.com/kevin-haynes/meta-wants-to-add-facial-recognition-to-its-smart-glasses-but-1-major-concern-is-holding-it-back/91303260">facial recognition</a>. Subsequently, the public has seized on the idea that users could use the cameras to film people without their consent in private locations. These glasses have even been called <a href="https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/meta-ai-pervert-glasses-smartglasses-b3032418.html">“pervert” smartglasses.</a></p>
<p>Privacy concerns may complicate Apple’s plans for the AirPods. For example, it may be hard to detect if someone is wearing older versions or newer ones with cameras, which could cause public conflict. Similarly, the public may worry exactly what Apple is using the images captured by the cameras for. To counter this, at the launch event Apple will likely lean hard into its history of preserving user privacy.</p>
