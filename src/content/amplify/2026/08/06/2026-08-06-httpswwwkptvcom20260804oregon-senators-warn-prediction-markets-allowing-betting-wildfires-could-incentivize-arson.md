<p>(KPTV) - A public coalition, including Oregon’s senators, has formally urged the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) to block prediction market platforms from offering bets on destructive wildfires, raising alarms over public safety, potential arson, and the ethics of profiting from natural disasters.</p>
<p>The demand follows recent reports that Polymarket, the world’s largest prediction market platform, <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/17/climate/betting-wildfires-polymarket-prediction-market-platforms">accepted more than $1.2 million in wagers on the devastating Palisades and Eaton fires</a> in January 2025. Those Southern California blazes killed 31 people and destroyed more than 16,000 structures.</p>
<p>“Offering bets on destructive wildfires threatens to minimize communities’ suffering all so the rich and powerful can profit,” the senators wrote in a letter to CFTC Chair Michael Selig.“There’s also the heightened risk—according to state and local fire officials—that individuals could be tempted to commit arson in order to make sure their bets are successful. By offering contracts on fires, prediction market sites run the risk of encouraging people to influence fires that have already started, creating additional concerns around public safety and insider trading.”</p>
<p>The pushback also targets a newly launched prediction platform that allows users to place simulated bets on California wildfires under the slogan, “You can’t predict fire, but you can trade on it.”</p>
<p>Critics and fire officials warn that allowing financial stakes on active disasters could incentivize bad actors to commit arson to ensure their bets succeed, or to influence the containment efforts of ongoing fires.</p>
<p>As the United States enters another severe fire season, advocates are calling on the CFTC to establish common-sense guardrails on both domestic Designated Contract Markets (DCMs) and offshore platforms before wildfire-related contracts proliferate.</p>
<p>The CFTC has been requested to respond to the following questions by Aug. 14, 2026:</p>
<ul><li><b>Rulemaking and Prohibitions:</b> Is the CFTC considering prohibiting DCMs from offering event contracts on wildfires as part of its current rulemaking process, given the potential conflict with public interest?</li><li><b>Market Curbing:</b> Does the agency plan to curb wildfire betting on prediction markets within the United States, and does it have strategies to address offshore platforms offering these contracts?</li><li><b>Public Interest Assessment:</b> Does the Commission believe event contracts tracking a wildfire’s duration, destruction, or growth rate serve the public interest?</li><li><b>Enforcement and Guidance:</b> Does the Commission have guidance or planned enforcement actions ready for wildfire-related event contracts?</li></ul>
