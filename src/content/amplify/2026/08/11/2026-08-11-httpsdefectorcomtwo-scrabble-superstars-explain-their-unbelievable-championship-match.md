<p><strong>CEDAR RAPIDS, Iowa — </strong>In the quarter-century that I’ve been playing and writing about Scrabble—a game in which greatness requires that rare matrix of biological gift, yeoman preparation, preternatural vision, marathon endurance, and blind luck—I’ve never witnessed anything as shocking and painful as the end of the 2026 North American championship here in July. The moment was right up there with any agony-of-defeat ending in any sport: After 3,700 games, thousands of moves, and millions of points scored by more than 200 players, the title was decided by a word that wasn’t a word at all.</p>
<p>On one side of the board: No. 1 seed <a href="https://www.mackmeller.com/">Mack Meller</a> of Lexington, Ky., a 26-year-old onetime child prodigy and current Scrabble grandmaster, content creator, teacher, and puzzle constructor. On the other: No. 5 seed Orry Swift of Winnie, Texas, a 39-year-old accounting professor and five-tool gamer—he’s been a top player in <em>World of Warcraft</em> and <em>Magic: The Gathering</em>; he’s about halfway through a quest to <a href="https://orryssnesreviews.wordpress.com/">play and review</a> every Super Nintendo game (about 880 in all); and, under his username Sheex, he recently completed a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iDz9IyXkX4k">charity speedrun</a> of <em>Ogre Battle: The March of the Black Queen</em>. “Coming off a high like that into making the finals at Scrabble nationals was crazytown,” he told me.</p>
<p>Swift entered his first Scrabble tournament in 2004 and has been among the highest-rated players for more than 15 years. Meller began playing competitively at age 10. He became the youngest player to achieve an expert rating, at 11, and cracked the Top 10 at 13. At the <a href="https://event.scrabbleplayers.org/2026/nasc/build/">Scrabble Players Championship</a>, held over five days in a football field-sized convention-center ballroom here, they topped a field of 51 players in the top North American-lexicon division (out of 242 overall) with identical 20-8 records to qualify for a best-of-five finals and a shot at the $10,000 first prize. (An additional 38 players competed using an international-English word list; <a href="https://stefanfatsis.substack.com/p/separated-by-a-common-language-part">it’s complicated</a>.)</p>
<p>It was Meller’s third straight finals appearance (he won in 2024 and lost last year) and Swift’s second (he lost in 2022). In a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@wanderer15">recap video</a> on his popular YouTube channel, former champ Will Anderson labeled the showdown “one of the best and most compelling matches we’ve seen yet in Scrabble.” I’ll put it another way: There’s a reason the subtitle of my <a href="https://www.bystefanfatsis.com/word-freak">book</a> about competitive Scrabble includes the words heartbreak, triumph, and genius.</p>
<p>I interviewed Meller and Swift about what went down. Our written conversation has been edited for length and clarity.</p>
<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Mack takes <a href="https://woogles.io/anno/8kMFevPGyb5TaV9VSSLEnY?turn=1">Game 1</a> of the finals, 383-326, and opens <a href="https://woogles.io/anno/RyScTMZgnyBuJ6q4C84eSi?turn=1">Game 2</a> with the delightful three-B, rack-clearing “bingo” <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/abubble">ABUbbLE</a> (lowercase denotes a blank) en route to a 528-321 blowout. Advantage, Mack.</p>

<p><strong>Swift</strong>: After losing the first two, I was pretty down. It just didn’t seem like I would ever draw any good letters in the finals. I went out for lunch with three other top players—Jason Li, Carl Johnson, and Rafi Stern—and they talked me through it. I think it helped to have people at the table who have deserved a championship but haven’t quite pulled it off. I suddenly didn’t mind the outcome, win or lose.</p>
<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Mack, we ate together and you seemed pretty chill, even encouraging the counter staff at Snack Wellness Cafe to tune into the <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hC-e2HikEZU">tournament livestream</a> for the afternoon games. But the tiles don’t care about momentum.</p>
<p><strong>Meller</strong>: I was trying extremely hard to not feel overconfident. Having come back from down 0-2 to win in 2024, I knew the match was far from over. Like you said, Stefan, how I drew in Games 1 and 2 would have no bearing on how the tiles shook out in the afternoon, and I knew Orry was more than capable of making the comeback.</p>
<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Orry, after the break, the tile gods smile down on you. You “bag” Mack twice in a row, that is, draw synergistic tiles allowing high-scoring plays turn after turn. In <a href="https://woogles.io/anno/QedLSkf2SspcMwGn4dxmLd?turn=1">Game 3</a>, you drop four bingos—<a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/evert">EVERTING</a>, OVERTAKE, <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/outsert">OUTSERT</a>, and <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/anneal">ANNEALS</a>—plus a 61-point ZEDA, a Yiddish-derived word meaning grandfather, for a 536-364 win. And in <a href="https://woogles.io/anno/Y6247vzrPA32xT5GCwP6PK?turn=1">Game 4</a>, you post an even bigger win, 584-367, slapping down <em>five</em> bingos—INVENTS, INVADER, GAMBLER, PICTURED, and FLECTION (an alt spelling of flexion).</p>

<p>You’re definitely having a good time. At one point you nearly draw the Scrabble meme OLEICAT—from a <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8opLB1D_RYY">video</a> by Will Anderson in which ChatGPT insists that OLEICAT is a valid word—and line it up for the rack camera.</p>

<p>According to the analyzer <a href="https://domino14.github.io/macondo/">Macondo</a>, Orry, in Game 3 you throw a perfect game, posting an Average Mistake Index of 0.0. (Scrabble, like every other sport, has an array of acronymic analytics.) Mack is close behind at 0.6. I’m a low expert, and my MiAV is around 3.5 per game. Your numbers reflect your skill and, in Mack’s case here, the fact that in Scrabble you can play nearly flawlessly and still get whomped.</p>
<p><strong>Swift</strong>: But I almost play the nonword VERGENT, falling into a trap where something scores a kazillion points so you talk yourself into it. And I would have let Mack play OVERTAKER, which also isn’t a word, because he’s Mack and I have easily drained 40 percent of my word knowledge by playing on the livestream. I definitely get nervous when the cameras are rolling.</p>
<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Like patrons at Centre Court at Wimbledon in England, the 100 of us players and staff in a nearby room at the Alliant Energy PowerHouse Convention Center attached to the DoubleTree Hotel in Cedar Rapids—and hundreds more watching online—are psyched the match is going the distance. And it does not disappoint. <a href="https://woogles.io/anno/9XGx8t6bbXxNZ9Wqc6i6zg?turn=1">Game 5</a> will go down as an all-timer, for both its nonstop action and bonkers ending.</p>
<p>After Orry’s opening play of EX for 18 points, four straight bingos hit the board in rapid succession: PAGEANT for 76 (Mack), RELIERS for 85 (Orry), PRESIDIO for 94 (Mack), and WINDABLE for 82 (Orry). To give a sense of how quickly your brains are processing, before Mack even straightens his new tiles—an R and S are sideways, a D and P upside down—he picks them up and lays down PRESIDIO, covering two double-word-score squares. Incredible.</p>

<p>You’ve played four bingos in the first five moves, the score is 185-170, and you’ve each used less than two of the allotted 25 minutes on your clocks. I love it when a game goes off the rails.</p>
<p><strong>Swift: </strong>This is one of those openers where you <em>really </em>want to go second. With my opening rack of EEEIRSX, EXERCISE through a C or <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/hexereis">HEXEREIS</a> from an H could generate a massive lead, whereas EX usually gives back more points than it scores. Luckily, I draw a bingo immediately. I’m trying to play fast on turns that have an obvious play. On the WINDABLE turn, after hitting my clock, my heart skips a beat. Is WINDABLE actually a word? Did I just miss <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/windslab">WINDSLAB</a> somewhere for more points?</p>
<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Like every top expert, you’ve drilled more than 25,000 seven-letter words and 31,000 eight-letter words over and over for years. But it’s one thing to solve a flashcard anagram and another to find a word over the board under time pressure, let alone with hundreds of people watching and commenting.</p>
<p>Mack, holding a rack of ACGNTV and a blank, you’ve got a <em>fifth </em>straight playable bingo, VaCATING through the second I in PRESIDIO for 63 points. Instead you play VAT atop the first two letters of WINDABLE for 35, keeping C, G, N, and the blank. Why?</p>
<p><strong>Meller: </strong>Continuing the bingo train with VaCATING is tempting, but I have to recognize the danger of the hotspot Orry’s last bingo created. He’ll score 50-plus points there very often, so I decide to take 35 for VAT, blocking the spot and saving the blank to give myself great chances at bingoing with a seven-letter word ending in G under PE in the middle of the board, or an eight through the open vowels in PRESIDIO. If I didn’t have a blank, I’d slam down the bingo and hope for the best. But with blank in hand, sometimes you can forgo weak bingos in favor of a strong non-bingo.</p>

<p><strong>Fatsis: </strong>Computer simulations back up your decision, and in fact Orry would have played INVOKE for 62 points on top. Instead, he has to settle for the same word for 42 two rows below. Orry, on your next turn you have three places for HOTLY—through the O in INVOKE; to the left of PRESIDIO, simultaneously forming HI, <a href="https://scrabble.merriam.com/finder/od">OD</a>, <a href="https://scrabble.merriam.com/finder/ti">TI</a>, and LO; and next to EX, making HEX. Each score well but are strategically different. You choose the one that keeps the board the most “open”—leaving access to potential horizontal bingos through or hooking PRESIDIO and creating a new vertical lane parallel to the Y in HOTLY.</p>

<p><strong>Swift: </strong>I wasn’t confident in my ability to keep the board shut down. I felt like the “leave” of L, O, and T on my rack and the positioning of HOTLY was going to give me a better chance to race. As it turns out, this is just objectively wrong and starts a skid of nervousness for me.
