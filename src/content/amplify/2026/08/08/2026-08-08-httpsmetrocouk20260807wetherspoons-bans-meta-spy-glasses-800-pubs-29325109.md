<p><a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/wetherspoons/">Wetherspoons</a> has taken a stand against the <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/meta/">Meta</a> eyewear described as ‘creepy spy glasses’.</p>
<p>Following increased privacy concerns, <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" href="https://metro.co.uk/2025/09/18/new-ray-ban-smart-glasses-launched-meta-superintelligence-move-24197550/">the smart glasses</a>, which allow wearers to capture photos and videos of their surroundings, have been banned by the pub giant.</p>
<p>Should punters wish to enter any of the UK’s 800+ Spoons, they will be ordered to ‘turn the cameras off’.</p>
<p>Wetherspoons explained that recording of customers or employees without consent is not permitted, adding that ‘Meta glasses seem to breach this code, and common sense, by enabling surreptitious surveillance.’</p>
<p>A spokesperson said: ‘Like many hospitality companies, Wetherspoon has CCTV cameras for security reasons, but their use is strictly controlled by data legislation.</p>
<p>‘Apart from that, the general code that applies in our pubs, and most pubs, is that you can’t film customers or employees without their permission.’</p>

<p>They went on to say that bosses’ ‘instinct’ to ban the techy specs is akin to their efforts to ‘stop audible playing of videos in [their] pubs, which also invades people’s space’.</p>

<p>Wetherspoons isn’t the first to ban them – those who wear the glasses in Soho House’s private members’ clubs can also be asked to take them off, with a spokesperson confirming their ‘no filming’ rule has no exceptions.</p>
<p>Last month, Monopoly Events, which is behind some of the biggest comic and entertainment events, announced that they, too, wished to put a stop to ‘secret filming’ via any ‘wearable recording devices’.</p>
<p>The decision was made after several Comic-Con guests said they would be reluctant to attend again after cases of people being unknowingly videoed.</p>
<p>ATG Theatres is among other companies prohibiting the glasses. It owns venues across Bristol, Edinburgh, and London.</p>

<p>Meta launched its first generation of smart glasses in a Ray-Ban collaboration in September 2021. The second generation, Ray-Ban Meta, dropped in 2023 with Meta AI integration before an additional line of Meta Glasses landed in June 2026.</p>
<p>These were met with widespread criticism and ethical objections, leading to them being referred to colloquially as ‘pervert glasses’.</p>
<p>As for their functions, the frames have a built-in POV (point of view) camera, open-ear audio, and Bluetooth connectivity.</p>
<p>By pairing them with the Meta app, users can ask questions, whether it be about the <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/weather/">weather</a> or a food item in a store, and receive answers.</p>
<p>They have been praised for their features, easily allowing people to stream music and <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/podcasts/">podcasts</a>, take <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/smartphones/">phone</a> calls, and send text messages on the go.</p>

<p>A spokesperson for the company has said they’re helpful for allowing wearers to ‘stay in the moment’, while also providing ‘critical new technology for people who are low-sighted or hard of hearing’.</p>
<p>Because of their sleek designs, the glasses are easy to mistake for regular Ray-Bans; however, their Meta powers can be detected by their thicker temples and a small camera lens in the upper right corner.</p>
<p>If someone is recording, the opposite corner’s LED light will turn on and begin pulsing.</p>
<p>As for the price tag, Meta glasses start at £269, but some far exceed the £400 mark, like the Kylie Jenner edition, which the reality star and entrepreneur designed.</p>
<p>Jenner has come under fire for promoting the accessory, particularly as women have expressed fears of them being misused to harass them and film them without their knowledge.</p>
<p>Nevertheless, Meta’s chief executive <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/mark-zuckerberg/">Mark Zuckerberg</a> has proudly boasted about the glasses’ success, as they continue flying off the market and account for more than 80% of all AI or smart glasses sales.</p>

<p>‘They’re some of the fastest-growing consumer electronics in history,’ declared the <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/facebook/">Facebook</a> founder.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton previously told the BBC that people should behave responsibly with the technology.</p>
<p>‘We have teams dedicated to limiting and combating misuse, but as with any technology, the onus is ultimately on individual people to not actively exploit it.’</p>
<p>Irrespective of the questions surrounding the Black Mirror-esque technology, it looks like they’re here to stay, as <a data-ico="hyperlink-article" data-track="inline-tag-auto-link_article" href="https://metro.co.uk/tag/apple/">Apple</a> is also developing its own version, reportedly set for release next year.</p>
<p>Researchers have predicted that as many as 100 million people will purchase a pair in the next few years.</p>
