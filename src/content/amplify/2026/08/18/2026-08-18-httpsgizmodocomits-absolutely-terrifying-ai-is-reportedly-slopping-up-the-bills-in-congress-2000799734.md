<p>Four anonymous congressional staffers who apparently help draft bills <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/17/ai-slop-lawmakers-congress-01008376">spoke to Politico</a> for a story published Monday. They say AI-generated text is seeping into bills at an alarming rate—alarming, because when they pass, bills turn into the federal statutes that are part of the bedrock of American life.</p>
<p>“It’s absolutely terrifying,” one of them told that publication.</p>
<p>If the report is true, AI in the legislative process sounds like it does something similar to <a href="https://gizmodo.com/random-people-armed-with-ai-and-no-lawyer-are-reportedly-filling-judicial-dockets-with-lawsuits-2000763269">AI in the lawsuit-writing process</a>: gives more people confidence to try and generate documents in jargon-heavy language that needs to be precise, while also making more work for the people who eventually have to read those documents.</p>
<p>Last year <a href="https://docs.house.gov/meetings/AP/AP24/20250402/118080/HHRG-119-AP24-Wstate-BurkeW-20250402.pdf">while addressing the House</a>, Warren Burke, who leads congress’ Office of Legislative Counsel—a collection of mostly lawyers who format and draft laws, request for legislation received by the office were up 72% in 2025 compared to two years earlier.</p>
<p>One of Politico’s anonymous staffers said people and organizations outside congress are a big part of the issue. They’re “going to Claude or ChatGPT to draft the legislative language itself,” the person said. They added that the OLC spends “more time trying to fix AI-drafted legislation than it would take for them to draft it from scratch.”</p>
<p>In a puzzling and somewhat ambiguous incident in June, Representative Anna Paulina Luna, a Republican from Florida, posted apparent evidence online that Anthropic’s Claude chatbot may have been used by a staffer to draft legislation. <a href="https://gizmodo.com/maga-congresswoman-denies-using-ai-to-write-bill-love-claude-but-grok-is-way-more-savage-2000777136">Later, she seemed to confirm that it had been used to at least edit a bill,</a> but the tweet in which she said that was deleted and replaced with a tweet saying the chatbot was only used for a summary of the bill.</p>
