<p>Intended to lock in serums that smooth, depuff, and tighten the under-eye area, these patches are historically single-use items. And because they’re a few bucks a pop (with a recommendation to use them a few times a week), they can be quite terrible for your wallet (as well as for the environment). For those reasons, we don’t recommend them.</p>
<p>Yet the <a data-affiliate-link="true" data-gtm-context="lede" data-gtm-element="product_link_impression" data-gtm-vis-first-on-screen30776727_938="8569" data-gtm-vis-first-on-screen30776727_940="8568" data-gtm-vis-has-fired30776727_938="1" data-gtm-vis-has-fired30776727_940="1" data-gtm-vis-recent-on-screen30776727_938="8569" data-gtm-vis-recent-on-screen30776727_940="8568" data-gtm-vis-total-visible-time30776727_938="100" data-gtm-vis-total-visible-time30776727_940="100" data-link-type="link" data-merchant="Sephora" data-payload-expected="false" data-pid="78133" data-url="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/78133/232481/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/" data-vars-outbound-link="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/78133/232481/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/" data-vars-store="Sephora" href="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/78133/232481/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/">Dieux Forever Eye Mask</a> stickers are somewhat different. The 100% silicone patches are designed to be reusable, and they work with your go-to eye cream, rather than being serum-soaked themselves.</p>
<p>You could argue that these are the original status-symbol under-eye masks. They rose to prominence <a href="https://www.gq.com/story/dieux-eye-mask-pandemic-skincare">during the pandemic</a> and were worn by celebrities like <a href="https://www.glossy.co/beauty/how-dieux-emerged-as-the-cool-kid-eye-mask-brand/">Hailey Bieber</a> (before she started making her single-use eye masks with Rhode).</p>
<p>The Dieux masks are simple to use: Apply an eye cream to your under-eye area, lay the patches on top, wait 10 to 20 minutes, and peel them off to reveal “plump” under-eyes, as the brand claims. Then use hand soap to gently cleanse the patches, leave them out to dry, and use them again whenever you want.</p>
<p>With the rise of single-use eye patches, we wanted to give this reusable option a closer look, and we were hoping they’d be a sustainable solution to a wasteful trend. After testing them with five Wirecutter writers (of various ages and with a variety of skin types), we’ve come to the conclusion that, like single-use masks, these patches aren’t worth the money for most people.</p>
<p>Here’s why and what to use instead.</p>

<p>We started our testing by following the brand’s instructions. We applied our usual daily eye creams, pressed the patches on top, and sat back for 10 to 20 minutes. During this first round of testing, we focused on assessing the vibe: Were the patches comfortable? Did they block our line of sight? Did they give us that coveted “self-care” feeling? And, of course, we noted how our skin looked after we peeled them off.</p>
<p>Unfortunately, the sensorial experience left most of our testers underwhelmed. Beauty writer Amanda Rosenthal missed the cooling, awakening feeling that some single-use under-eye patches deliver. And she said the Dieux patches felt comparably bland, “like I was sticking a Band-Aid on the skin.” If you place the patches in the freezer before using them, they do have a momentary cooling effect. But they don’t hold onto the chill and instead go back to room temperature within a minute.</p>

<p>These eye masks are also thicker than many single-use patches, and some testers said that made them feel uncomfortable. I personally found them to be so beefy that they ate into my field of vision, which made walking around feel trippy, so I couldn’t multi-task. But, to their credit, the masks did stay put, and they never slid down the face; the same can’t be said of many single-use, serum-heavy patches I’ve tried.</p>
<p>Panelist Joshua Lyon was similarly frustrated by their bulk, and he didn’t find the patches enjoyable to use. He described them as the type of product he’d be excited to try, use a few times, and then disregard. “Then it would end up in the back of a drawer for the next four years until I had to clean out that drawer and finally toss them,” he said.</p>
<p>After we had a feel for these patches, we also tried a split-eye test. We applied eye cream to the under-eye area of both eyes, but we covered only one side with a silicone patch. By checking the results at the 10- and 20-minute marks, we were able to directly compare the masked eye to the unmasked eye (side by side) to see whether the silicone was actually making a difference.</p>
<p>Nearly every tester said these reusable patches actually made their under-eye area feel drier than when they used eye cream alone. “I saw a difference, but in a bad way,” beauty writer Neha Tandon said. And Amanda said the minute she took off the mask, the skin under the masked eye felt noticeably drier than the skin under the other eye.</p>
<p>Two testers also tried the patches with Dieux’s <a data-affiliate-link="true" data-gtm-context="reviewIntro" data-gtm-element="product_link_impression" data-link-type="link" data-merchant="Sephora" data-payload-expected="false" data-pid="83402" data-url="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/83402/242541/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/" data-vars-outbound-link="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/83402/242541/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/" data-vars-store="Sephora" href="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/out/affiliate/v1/Sephora/83402/242541/4/link/ee6b0ab8b2b146f38cfd5b6689e174d8/">Auracle Eye Serum</a> to see whether that would change the outcome. It didn’t. After they used the patches, the testers said the masked under-eye area were still dryer than the unmasked ones, even though the cream was pleasantly silky and hydrating on its own.</p>
<p>We reached out to Dieux to share our results, and we asked for more testing tips. The company suggested that we try wetting the skin before applying eye cream and the patch. When I did a split-eye test with and without damp skin, the wetter side was indeed more hydrated post-patch, but my under-eye area still didn’t look as plump or juicy as I’d hoped.</p>
<p>We were surprised that these masks were ineffective for our panelists — primarily because the science says they should work. The dermatologists I interviewed told me that silicone is often used in medical settings to foster a healing environment for scars by acting as an occlusive layer that holds in moisture. For the under-eye area, the silicone layer is intended to prevent eye cream from evaporating, giving the skin more time to absorb it, dermatologist Nicole Weiler said.</p>
<p>Yet by the time we removed the patches, almost all of that eye cream had disappeared from the surface. When we asked Dieux about our results, the company told us that perceived dryness is due to all of the moisture being absorbed into the skin, leaving no product behind. In theory, this is true. But we found that the patches left behind a dry and tacky feeling, instead of the moisturized plumpness we’d been seeking.</p>
<p>Some people will want to buy these patches for the vibes alone, and we’re not here to judge. If you do choose to buy them, be sure to pair them with the right eye cream. The masks work best with “hydrating formulas that contain humectants, as these provide water for the mask to help retain vs. waxy, rich creams,” the company told us.</p>
<p>Still, in our testing we found that you may actually want some eye cream left behind in the end. That residual product keeps the under-eye area feeling hydrated to the touch, preps the skin for a smooth concealer application, and keeps it feeling plump and smooth. We’ve had better experiences with single-use patches, partly because they come with so much product on them. But we don’t recommend them because there are better, more sustainable paths to dewy, plump under-eyes.</p>
<p>Ultimately, our recommendation is comparably boring and perhaps anti-trendy, and it certainly lacks any real social status. Yet it’s the most practical and effective recommendation: Use a really good eye cream every single day.</p>
<p>The truth is that most eye creams are not designed to need silicone covers to work properly. These products often already contain occlusive ingredients — like petrolatum, silicones, or plant oils — to help lock in moisture.</p>
<p>If your eye cream isn’t working, applying more product could be an effective tactic (but proceed with caution with retinol or exfoliating creams, since too much product can cause irritation). If that doesn’t work, you could try another product that’s better. We have <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-eye-cream/">six favorites</a>, all of which truly changed our under-eye areas for the better during testing.</p>
<p>Being a passionate beauty consumer myself, I do understand the temptation to be part of something you’re seeing online. But this time, I’d recommend resisting the urge. Save yourself some money, and just get the eye cream — no patches needed.</p>
