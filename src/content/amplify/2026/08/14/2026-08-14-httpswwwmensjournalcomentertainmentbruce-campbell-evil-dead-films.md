<h1>Bruce Campbell Reveals His One ‘Problem’ with Recent ‘Evil Dead’ Films (Exclusive)</h1>
<p>The actor, who played Ash Williams in the original three movies, has served as executive producer on the recent films.</p>

<p>After three movies and three seasons of TV playing Ash Williams, Bruce Campbell has all but hung up his chainsaw.</p>
<p>That being said, he hasn’t totally left the <em>Evil Dead</em> franchise behind.</p>
<p>Now 68, the horror icon has not only made cameos in the three most recent films—2013’s <em>Evil Dead</em>, 2023’s <em>Evil Dead Rise</em>, and <em>Evil Dead Burn</em>—but he’s also been on board as a producer or executive producer on them, as well as the upcoming <em>Evil Dead Wrath</em>.</p>
<p>While Campbell admitted earlier this year that he took a step back from producing duties on Burn—so he could focus on his new film, <em>Ernie &amp; Emma</em>, <a href="https://www.mensjournal.com/entertainment/bruce-campbell-ernie-emma-movie">an indie dramedy he wrote, directed, and stars in</a>—he hyped up <em>Wrath</em> during a recent chat with Men’s Journal.</p>
<p>“ <em>Wrath</em> will be good, because I saw that filmmaker’s first movie, and it was really cool, and really smart, and really well thought out,” Campbell said of Francis Galluppi’s thriller <em>The Last Stop in Yuma County</em>.</p>
<p>“That’s half the battle, is you go, ‘Okay, we got a filmmaker who has a clue,’” he shared.</p>
<p>When asked how it’s been for him to see the series transform since its indie beginnings way back in 1981 with director Sam Raimi, Campbell revealed his one issue with the recent installments.</p>
<p>“I’d like more humor back in. The three features have been mostly humorless, and I personally have a problem with that,” he said, echoing a sentiment shared among some of the franchise’s fans. “There’s no point for these to get too grim. You can be scary without being grim.”</p>
<p>“A little more humor wouldn’t kill you,” he added. “And one hero that you really can root for. Ash is an idiot, but you want him to survive.”</p>
<p>“Modern filmmakers love to color their movies like a David Fincher movie, like a hospital room, you know?” Campbell continued. “Gray-green, like a garage. Garage lighting. And so, okay, it’s a choice. It’s not mine. I think even a horror movie can look pretty. Why not throw them off? Make it all look pretty and wonderful, and then let them have it.”</p>

<p>For any directors stepping into the <em>Evil Dead</em> universe for future films, Campbell also offered a piece of advice about the blood that will inevitably cover its protagonist by the end of the movie.</p>
<p>“The blood—we’ve tried to enforce this—the blood has to look red. It’s the oldest story in the book, but your blood has to look red,” he warned.</p>
<p>“We’ll see what happens [going forward], but I have high hopes for the next one, because I’m familiar with that guy’s work,” Campbell concluded.</p>
<p>Speaking with the actor about his transition from work in horror and a TV series like <em>Burn Notice</em> to his current movie—about a man trying to overcome grief following the sudden death of his wife—Campbell also explained why he’s stepped back from playing Ash.</p>
<p>“Because I can’t sling the chainsaws anymore, you know what I mean?” he shared. “I’ve seen enough of these movies where they bring these geezers back, and you go, ‘Oh, dude, stay on the ranch, stay on the ranch.’ So I didn’t want to be that guy.”</p>
<p>He added that, over time, one’s “sensibilities” also change.</p>
<p>While he was once excited about going “nuts” with special effects, monsters, makeup, stunts, and long shoots in his 20s, scripts became more important to him in his 40s.</p>
<p>“I think I started doing <em>Burn Notice</em> when I was 50. So, no demons, just chasing bad guys and s–t blowing up and all that,” he continued, “and my sensibilities just have changed to the point where I care more about people.”</p>
