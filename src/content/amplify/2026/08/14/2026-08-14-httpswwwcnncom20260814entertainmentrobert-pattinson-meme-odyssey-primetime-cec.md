<p>There are a few stars who have been all over our movie screens in 2026 but just one seems to be dominating our computer screens, too: <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2020/02/13/entertainment/robert-pattinson-the-batman">Robert Pattinson</a>.</p>
<p>Through no campaigning on his part, Pattinson has managed to pull off what’s been described as an <a href="https://x.com/eustacesilly/status/2085530485996855580">“absolutely generational run”</a> in meme culture this year. Clips of the British actor from “<a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/23/entertainment/word-of-week-odyssey-cec">The Odyssey</a>” and “<a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/07/entertainment/video/entertainment-showbiz-hollywood-minute-primetime-robert-pattinson-trailer-phoebe-bridgers-lost-weekend-album-planetariums">Primetime</a>” (set to be released on September 25) have been impossible to miss on social media in recent weeks.</p>
<p>Pattinson’s ascent to the status of summer meme champion carries with it real cultural cachet. In addition to providing free publicity for the films themselves, memes help to solidify an actor’s star power, influence and ubiquity in popular culture. Whereas a movie may only have a limited run in theaters, a meme can last forever. In Pattinson’s case, his latest entries in the internet hall of fame also reflect how his online status has changed across his career, from a position of mockery to the widespread adoration on <a href="https://x.com/xxxrept/status/2086078440264900615">display</a> today in numerous viral <a href="https://x.com/IvyAscent/status/2086431028781146407">fan edits</a>.</p>
<p>“Pattinson is definitely one of the more interesting actors with a big meme presence because he’s actually managed to survive multiple generations of internet culture,” said Zach Sweat, managing editor of the internet database <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/">Know Your Meme</a>. “He’s become this really funny figure on the internet.”</p>
<p>In just the past month, Pattinson has been responsible for a pair of wildly viral memes pulled from two of the four movies in his 2026 slate. In the first, taken from his role as the sniveling suitor Antinous in “The Odyssey,” a cocky Pattinson yells for somebody to “<a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/robert-pattinson-somebody-get-these-beggars-out-of-here">get these <em>beggars </em>out of here!”</a> A bootleg clip of the scene has spread across social media, used to mock cheap people who might mooch <a href="https://x.com/europemaxxed/status/2079952380066500639">cigarettes</a>, <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DbJC6XplPkT/?utm_source=ig_embed&amp;ig_rid=Anwk9EjzqJ6P5ux1l8krl_3&amp;img_index=5">gum</a> or even <a href="https://x.com/thedimitri/status/2080772537886142716">drugs</a>. It’s also been weaponized to denigrate everything from <a href="https://x.com/jiratickets/status/2080653963842523506">Microsoft apps</a> to the <a href="https://x.com/capeshitsuccs/status/2079233154510987411">DC Extended Universe.</a></p>

<p>The second of Pattinson’s meme contributions this summer arose from a movie that’s not even been released. When the trailer for “<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fHXyqQOKL8">Primetime</a>” dropped last week, a clip of Pattinson playing “To Catch a Predator” host Chris Hansen almost immediately went viral. “<a href="https://x.com/roryofffilm/status/2085383738226073905">You see how this looks, right?”</a> the actor states smugly in the meme that’s since been used to <a href="https://x.com/chunktrunk2/status/2086237059287568738?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2086237059287568738%7Ctwgr%5E047e9bc70faca9e07909a51f0709f148bc2167b7%7Ctwcon%5Es1_c10&amp;ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fiframely.forbes.com%2FwIvGYnuf%3Fapp%3D1theme%3Dlight">question</a> people’s <a href="https://x.com/thewittyblessed/status/2085861063724024088">movie choices</a> and <a href="https://x.com/corporatedudes/status/2085976868675555521">corporate nepotism</a>.</p>

<p>Both memes are so prevalent that they were <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/robert-pattinson-somebody-get-these-beggars-out-of-here">recently</a> <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/you-see-how-this-looks-right">added</a> as entries on Know Your Meme, which also hosts a <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/people/robert-pattinson">separate entry for Pattinson</a> himself, given his <a href="https://www.gq.com/story/robert-pattinson-viral-moments-ranked">history of going viral</a>. Sweat said the memes have wide appeal because of their adaptability to different contexts and the uniqueness of the actor’s delivery. “Pattinson has a talent for these incredibly expressive, slightly strange deliveries of his lines and his facial expressions that become really funny once they’re stripped out of that original context,” Sweat said.</p>
<p>Pattinson is also catnip for social media because of his quirky personality, which feels unconventional for a Hollywood star once declared <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2020/02/05/entertainment/robert-pattinson-most-handsome-trnd">“the most handsome man in the world.”</a> The actor’s penchant for idiosyncratic comments — such as when he said he used an <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2023/11/16/entertainment/robert-pattinson-inflatable-boat-intl-scli">inflatable boat as his sole piece of furniture</a> for six months — makes him a curiosity and avatar for internet weirdness.</p>
<p>“He looks like a statue. His jawline is etched to perfection from stone, and he’s so gorgeous,” said Kelsey Weekman, a reporter covering internet culture for <a href="https://www.yahoo.com/author/kelsey-weekman-276/">Yahoo News</a> who once dressed as a 2017 meme of <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/sensitive/memes/tracksuit-robert-pattinson-standing-in-the-kitchen">Pattinson wearing a tracksuit</a> for Halloween. “But he’s so strange, and that’s really fodder for virality.”</p>
<h2 data-article-gutter="true" data-component-name="subheader" data-editable="text" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/subheader/instances/cmsrn5s90000r3b6rfoqnor3r@published"> Teen meme dream </h2>
<p>From almost the beginning of his career, Pattinson has been a favorite subject for meme users. Right as social media was taking off in the mid-to-late 2000s, Pattinson became globally famous for playing Cedric Diggory in “Harry Potter” and Edward Cullen in “Twilight,” the vampire film series which gave rise to countless <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/twilight-comics/">mostly snarky image memes</a> featuring the actor. As recently as five years ago, meme users were still <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/this-is-the-skin-of-a-killer-bella">mocking Cullen’s sparkling appearance</a>. To the terminally online, Pattinson was mostly dismissed as a vanilla teen idol.</p>
<p>But as memes evolved from images to reaction GIFs and videos, Pattinson’s talents were also on display in more diverse movies than the franchises he’d come up with. He appeared in films like Robert Eggers’ “<a href="https://www.cnn.com/2019/07/30/entertainment/lighthouse-trailer-dafoe-pattinson-trnd">The Lighthouse</a>” in 2019 and the Safdie brothers’ 2017 film “Good Time.” Even his version of the Caped Crusader in 2022’s “<a href="https://www.cnn.com/2020/02/13/entertainment/robert-pattinson-the-batman">The Batman</a>” is an oddly gothic one.</p>

<p>“He’s this figure that people grew up with, and now he’s kind of branching out into weird character roles, and he’s finding a lot of success,” said Ryan Broderick, the founder of the <a href="https://www.garbageday.email/">Garbage Day</a> newsletter about online culture. “His roles are so clippable and can easily be condensed into animated GIFs or memes.”</p>
<p>Alison Willmore, a <a href="https://www.vulture.com/author/alison-willmore/">movie critic for New York magazine</a>, compared Pattinson to other actors big in meme culture who also starred in career-defining franchises at young ages before making bold, creative choices in their careers: Elijah Wood of “The Lord of the Rings,” Daniel Radcliffe of “Harry Potter” and Pattinson’s “Twilight” costar and ex-girlfriend Kristen Stewart. “He’s so memeable because he seems so willing to just go wherever,” Willmore said. “He’s willing to be a leading man and a character actor. There’s a bit of edge and friction to him that I think’s very appealing.”</p>
<h2 data-article-gutter="true" data-component-name="subheader" data-editable="text" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/subheader/instances/cmsrw9p0g00023b6rffjz3xx5@published"> A <s>millennial</s> Gen Z-ish sensation </h2>
<p>Certainly, part of Pattinson’s ubiquity in internet culture this summer is due to the sheer number of movies he’s in this year — “The Drama,” “The Odyssey,” “Primetime” and “Dune: Part 3,” a range that has left some joking that he might nab <a href="https://x.com/EvanAboutCinema/status/2086119014711918641">every spot in the best actor category</a> at the next Academy Awards. (“Dune: Part 3,” like “The Batman” and “Harry Potter,” is produced by Warner Bros. Pictures, which is owned by CNN’s parent company.)</p>

<p>But the actor’s kooky personality — Willmore, the film critic, described him as a “genuine eccentric” and “weirdo” — is also central to his appeal to people on the internet, a space which can favor the outlandish.</p>
<p>Indeed, several bits of internet lore associated with Pattinson have been drawn from bizarre comments he’s made on press tours to promote films, which are typically highly stage-managed affairs and thus make his behavior stand out even more. In a 2020 <a href="https://www.gq.com/story/robert-pattinson-on-batman-tenet-isolation-june-cover">interview</a> with GQ, for instance, the actor discussed a strange recipe for a microwaved pasta dish he claimed to invent that sparked a <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/robert-pattinsons-pasta">slew of jokes</a> and was later dubbed by other outlets that tried to recreate it as <a href="https://www.pedestrian.tv/bites/robert-pattinson-gq-microwave-pasta-dish-roadtest/">“chaotic,”</a> <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/food/2020/may/13/robert-pattinson-pasta-recipe-recreate">“ungodly”</a> and <a href="https://www.glamour.com/story/i-made-robert-pattinsons-terrifying-quarantine-pasta">“terrifying.”</a></p>
<p>He also has a penchant for outright <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DWF7pTODfTS/">pathologically lying</a> during interviews, such as when he admitted he’d fabricated a story he told on the “Today” show in 2011 about <a href="https://www.menshealth.com/entertainment/a29786443/robert-pattinson-weird-interview-quotes/">watching a clown die in an explosion</a> when he was a young boy visiting a circus. More recently, the decidedly millennial actor jokingly insisted to his “Die My Love” costar Jennifer Lawrence that he was a <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DRDn6FhEh-C/?hl=en">member of Gen Z</a>. (In a <a href="https://www.garbageday.email/p/don-t-worry-about-privacy-just-make-more-content-it-s-fine">recent edition</a> of his newsletter, Broderick noted that despite being 40, Pattinson “has the personality of someone who was Zoom-schooled”).</p>

<p>Male actors seem to be given more leeway than female stars to display bigger personalities without the public turning on them. Weekman, the Yahoo reporter, pointed to Lawrence, who was <a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/people/jennifer-lawrence">often a meme</a> in the mid-2010s for her goofy, relatable persona, before <a href="https://variety.com/2025/film/news/jennifer-lawrence-hates-press-old-interviews-annoying-1236561923/">encountering pushback</a> as an example of what Weekman described as “millennial cringe.”</p>
<p>But the internet can also chafe at male stars on a <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/2025/12/timothee-chalamet-marty-supreme-press-tour/685390/">headline-grabbing press tour</a>, as evidenced by some <a href="https://parade.com/news/timothee-chalamet-addresses-controversial-marty-supreme-press-tour-behavior">reactions</a> to Timothée Chalamet’s “Marty Supreme” <a href="https://variety.com/2025/film/news/timothee-chalamet-defends-marty-supreme-press-tour-backlash-1236613045/">promotional efforts</a> ahead of this year’s Oscars. Compared to Chalamet, another leading man with great range but <a href="https://variety.com/2025/film/news/timothee-chalamet-sag-speech-defending-great-actor-1236319180/">more naked ambition</a> than Pattinson, the British star’s self-effacing personality comes across as more endearing, Weekman believes.</p>
<p>“He doesn’t take himself seriously, but he takes his craft very seriously,” she said, “and that’s why I think we’ll never get rid of him.”</p>
