<p>A wildfire driven by high winds has jumped the Spokane River and is threatening neighborhoods near and within the city of Spokane.</p>
<p>New evacuation orders were issued just before 3:30 p.m., affecting thousands of people within the Spokane city limits. They were later expanded to include all the city north of Francis Avenue and west of Division Street.</p>
<p>The evacuation includes the entire South Indian Trail neighborhood.</p>

<p>Spokane City Councilwoman Kitty Klitzke, who represents northwest Spokane, urged residents in north Spokane to stay off the roads unless they are evacuating. Streets are reportedly packed as folks flee the evacuation zone.</p>
<p>Spokane Mayor Lisa Brown said she is scheduled to talk to Gov. Bob Ferguson in the 4 o’clock hour.</p>
<p>The Mann-Grandstaff VA Medical Center is under an evacuation order. Some patients have been transferred, including seven to Providence Sacred Heart Medical Center and another seven to Holy Family Hospital.</p>
<p>Earlier, residents at Spokane Health and Rehabilitation Home at 6025 N. Assembly St. was being evacuated as residents waited in the shade and grass for evacuation busses.</p>
<p>The fire has reported to have grown to 2,500 acres by 4:30 p.m., pushed by wind gusts of 35 to 45 mph.</p>
<p>Cars full of evacuating residents could be seen stretching south along Northwest Boulevard as the wind-driven Old Trails fire spreads. Smoke is so thick it’s blotting out the sun on North Driscoll Boulevard and West Francis Avenue.</p>
<p>Flames were visible as trees in the dry Riverside State Park erupted in towering flames.</p>
<p>Firefighters assisted by aircraft were attempted to protect neighborhoods.</p>
<p>Wind picked up around noon and Spokane, with 25 to 35 mph gusts blowing toward the east and northeast, said Antoinette Serrate, National Weather Service meteorologist. Those will start to died down around 8 p.m.</p>
<p>The fire quickly spread near Nine Mile Road. Residents near Nine Mile on North Greenwood Boulevard are spraying down their homes and their neighbor’s homes. Some are wetting down firewood outside their house. Theresa Sullivan, who was spraying down her home with a house and her neighbor’s home, said “It’s never been this close before.”</p>
<p>“I never thought it would be up here,” she said. “I’m in shock.”</p>
<p>Those living along Northwest Boulevard congregated along the ledge by the road. Across the Spokane River, flames covered a hillside just miles away.</p>
<p>As cars fled the flames bumper to bumper along for miles, those out of danger watched from picnic blankets.</p>
<p>In 30 years living in Spokane, Michael Keller said he had never seen fire get so close to the city’s core.</p>
<p>“I’ve never seen it this bad. It has never in my life been this close,” he said.</p>
<p>Keller’s wife and adult son had just left to go towards the evacuation zone, hoping to recover items from the empty home of a relative.</p>
<p>“I don’t know if they’ll even get in. They’re trying save we stuff, but I’m really worried about them if they do make it in,” he said.</p>
<p>The fire quickly tore through 350 to 400 acres in the area around West Euclid Road and North Old Trails Road within about an hour and a half of starting, according to the Watch Duty website. Not long after, it spread into Riverside State Park and jumped the Spokane River into the city. Gusts in Airway Heights are forecasted to reach up to 45 mph Saturday.</p>
<p>“Multiple structures are imminently threatened,” according to the website. Firefighters could be seen on the roofs of houses trying to save the homes from burning.</p>
<p>Mandatory evacuations have been expanded from Marchand Road on the north and Trails on the south, and from Lyons on the West and the Spokane River on the east.</p>
<p>Police are barricading the evacuation zone as some residents push back, hoping to be let in to collect their things.</p>
<p>Bob Moore raced up North Government Way Saturday but was intercepted by Spokane Police.</p>
<p>“You got to let me go up there, man,” Moore called from his truck window.</p>
<p>“I can’t let you up there right now,” the officer told him.</p>
<p>“My shop, my house. I need to protect my house,” Moore pleaded.</p>
<p>The officer called around, made an attempt to try and get Moore up to his home. “I’m worried about my stuff.”</p>
<p>“I’m sorry,” the officer told him.</p>
<p>Moore told The Spokesman-Review his wife grabbed their “go-bags” and their dogs and was waiting at the bottom of the hill.</p>
<p>“She called and said ‘You got to come home right now.,’” Moore said as he watched the plume of smoke erupt past Riverside State Park.</p>
<p>Others who were intercepted at the roadblock on North Government Way were crying and pleading with police to let them up the hill. There was nothing police could do, they said.</p>
<p>As of about 1:30 p.m., the blaze had spread into Riverside State Park on the north, according to Watch Duty.</p>
<p>The blaze comes as the National Weather Service on Friday issued a historic first warning for Eastern Washington of “particularly dangerous” weather, which is a step up from the typical red flag warnings the agency issues on hot, dry and windy days.</p>
<p>“This means that new fires will grow rapidly and out of control,” the warning reads. “Be on alert for fire starts and be prepared to evacuate immediately for all fires.”</p>
<p>The weather warning, however, was only meant to be in place from 2 p.m. to 8 p.m. The less severe red flag warning is in effect from 11 a.m. to 11 p.m.</p>
