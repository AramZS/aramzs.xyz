I made wonderful friends, became the most authentic version of myself, went internationally viral various times, and just had a ton of fun.</p>

<p>I believe one should always be mischiefmaxxing. This is a introductory course on what that means and how to do it.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-anchor-1"><sup> [1] </sup></a></p>
<p><strong>I define mischief as an amusing endeavor that exists for no purpose but to spark delight.</strong> It’s a defiance against the status quo that everything must be a means to an end, and a resistance to the monotony and passiveness of everyday life.</p>
<p>Welcome to: a beginner’s guide to mischief.</p>
<h2><strong>seeding ideas</strong></h2>
<p>Take that off-hand joke you made, and actually make it real.</p>
<p>“Wouldn’t it be funny if…” Do it. Do it. Do it. Don’t just hypothesize. Don’t overthink it.</p>
<p><em>Wouldn’t it be funny if we made an anti-run club, <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/fck-run-club-join-sit-club">a “sit club,”</a> since everyone’s joining run clubs these days to meet people, but most of them don’t even enjoy running?</em></p>
<p><em>Wouldn’t it be funny to throw <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/stupid-party-ideas">a party with a hundred Alexes</a>, to help my roommate Alex find friends? </em></p>
<p><em>Wouldn’t it be funny to <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/hunk-o-mania-a-tale-of-romance-fortitude">find my friend a girlfriend via flyers on the street</a>, as if he’s a lost dog?</em></p>
<p><em>Wouldn’t it be funny to list <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/cooking-up-mehrans-steak-house">my house on google maps as a restaurant</a>, because of my roommate’s infamous steak dinners? </em></p>
<p>“Ha ha, good one!” You could leave it at that. Or you could yank it into reality, and craft a scheme that takes on a life of its own. A joke among your friends could become entertainment for millions of people across the world. It could lead to meeting your now-closest friends. Funnily enough, it could even result in serious business people trying to hire you.</p>
<p>If this sounds preposterous to you, well, it sounds preposterous to everyone else. So most people don’t ever try, and the few that do reap outsized rewards.</p>
<h2>start small, and build up (if you’re scared)</h2>
<p>Take your joke, and dial it up a notch. And again. And again. And then maybe half a notch down, so you don’t overdo it.</p>
<p><strong>Some starting points:</strong></p>
<ul><li><p>Subvert expectations. Take a popular social phenomenon and flip it on its head.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-2" id="footnote-anchor-2"><sup> [2] </sup></a></p></li><li><p>Identify a problem and craft a comically over-engineered solution. </p></li><li><p>Create a genuinely useful tool then add a bunch of silly, useless features. </p></li><li><p>Take a subject/solution from one domain and apply it to a very unrelated domain. </p></li><li><p>Combine two things that should be polar opposites.</p></li><li><p>Search for loopholes and exploit them. </p></li><li><p>Take something you hate doing and invent a way to make it fun.</p></li></ul>
<p><em><strong>Tech bros in San Francisco always complain about the gender ratio</strong> → everyone says we need more women → but maybe we just need less men → <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/death-duel">I should make men fight “to the death”</a> with giant inflatable “weapons” → we’d play songs like “Kung Fu Fighting,” “Eye of the Tiger,” and soundtracks from Super Smash Bros → the men could be shirtless and get oiled up before they fight</em></p>

<p><strong>Bounce ideas off of friends.</strong> “Yes, and” your thoughts. Enlisting a friend also makes creation less intimidating, and helps you focus on process over outcome. Because worst case scenario, if you’re the only two that care about your project, that means you had fun creating something with your friend! And that is always worthwhile.</p>
<p><strong>Start in your home.</strong> Fabricate some <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/advanced-concepts-in-interior-design">interior design</a>. Throw a <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/stupid-party-ideas">silly party</a>. Mail your friends <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/joyful-pranks">absurd items</a>. Your scheme doesn’t have to be a grand spectacle. Begin in a safe space to get comfortable with play.</p>
<h2>form factors</h2>
<p>My scheming trifecta is: flyers, websites, and parties.</p>
<h4>Flyers (they’re like shitposting but IRL)</h4>

<p>Generally, you should include a call to action (CTA): e.g. <strong>a QR code</strong> that goes to a survey about one’s <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/beancoming-the-worlds-preeminent">bean habits</a>, <strong>a number you</strong> <strong>text</strong> to join an enigmatic quest, or <strong>a website </strong>to mobilize the people to <a href="https://stopclimbing.org/">STOP CLIMBING</a>.</p>
<p>The benefit of a CTA is you can gleefully watch as people engage with your work. But there’s also a beauty in having absolutely no feedback loop, as it leaves your audience even more puzzled on your abstract and enigmatic intentions.</p>
<p>You will be surprised and delighted by the number of people intrigued by your flyers.</p>
<p>After I’ve amassed enough responses, I like to gift something back to participants. It could be a <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/marked-variation-in-mating-tendencies">pseudo-scientific report analyzing data</a> from the survey, a <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/beancoming-the-worlds-preeminent">one-way plane ticket to Chicago</a>, or a <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/the-advice-line-a-reverse-hotline">song made from their voicemails</a>. It closes the loop.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-4" id="footnote-anchor-4"><sup> [4] </sup></a></p>
<p>I design my flyers in Figma, but if you’re a curmudgeon like me who resists learning new tech until you’re convinced of its value, you can literally make a flyer in google slides.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-5" id="footnote-anchor-5"><sup> [5] </sup></a> Just adjust the slide to be letter-sized and you can drag shit in there without having to learn some newfangled tech.</p>
<p>You could also just make flyers from pen and paper. That’s what summoned hundreds of <a href="https://rawandferal.substack.com/p/stupid-party-ideas">Alexes to the Alextravaganza</a>. No excuses! There are zero barriers to entry!</p>

<p>I’ve met many friends through my parties. I mean, I’m literally filtering for people who are down to clown, which is my favorite quality in a person.</p>
<p>The greatest blockers here are 1) where to host it and 2) the fear of no one showing up.</p>
<p>For the former - if you’re hesitant to have people in your home (especially if you’re advertising the party on the street), I’d recommend scoping out local parks and beaches. Some have more strict park rangers than others, but as long as you’re being reasonable (i.e. no crazy equipment, no excessive drinking, etc), you should be fine. If you live in San Francisco, I highly recommend <a href="https://www.thesfnook.com/">The SF Nook</a>, which was created by a group of friends as an affordable community/arts space.</p>
<p>For the latter - to mitigate the fear of no one showing up, plan it with a friend or several. Then, worst case scenario, you’re just hanging out with your friends on a beautiful day in the park.</p>
<p>There was a viral Substack essay circling a few months ago, about how <a href="https://danioffline.substack.com/p/everyone-wants-to-be-a-dj-no-one">everyone wants to be a DJ, but no one wants to dance</a>. I.e. everyone wants to be the creative, the star of the show, but few want to be the audience purely enjoying others’ work. I believe the arbitrage opportunity here is in event hosting, that’s an area in which everyone wants to be a participant, but few want to be the host.</p>
<h2>doubts to dispel</h2>
<p>Stepping outside of the prescribed path, I sometimes feel like a grand piano’s gonna fall out of the window and bonk me on the head like in a cartoon. But that doesn’t happen, actually.</p>
<p>It’s surprisingly very easy to do any of this. The hardest part is just fighting the inertia and starting.</p>
<p>Creativity is a muscle. If you’re worried that your idea is too stupid, who cares? Do it, so you’ll learn from it and have less stupid ideas in the future. That’s part of what’s freeing about schemes, they’re not meant to be taken seriously. By nature, they’re not a reflection of your intelligence or capability. So there’s no such thing as failure. I’ve learned to say: no matter what happens, it’ll be funny.</p>
<h2>it’s not that deep bro, but it also is that deep, bro.</h2>
<p>Resistance to the status quo is also a muscle. I’m not saying to be contrarian just for the sake of it, but it’s important to habitually question norms and take agency. Mischief is intrinsically tied to agency: it’s questioning why things are the way they are, even if it’s just to make fun of them. It’s toeing the line of what’s permissible, even if it’s just for a joke.</p>
<p>In doing so, you’ll learn to bend implicit rules you didn’t even realize you were following. There’s many things in life we have a vague sense that we’re “not allowed to do,” but we don’t pause to think critically about <em>why</em> that is the case, or if that’s even <em>true.</em><a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-7" id="footnote-anchor-7"><sup> [7] </sup></a></p>
<p>As children, we always asked why. Why does this exist? How does it work? Why should I listen to you? Somewhere, somehow, most people stopped asking questions. I mean, it’s not practical to be curious about every single thing. But it’s a cold and dreary world in which one has no sense of curiosity, of wonder. And it’s a complicit world in which we all just blindly follow rules, especially rules that don’t even <em>exist</em>.</p>
<p>And in a world so focused on the ends justifying the means, where every endeavor is expected to be monetized or awarded, it’s freeing to do something that exists for nothing but itself. It’s a helpful tool for unconditioning yourself from perfectionist tendencies, as there is no end goal, it’s about the process and living in the moment.</p>
<p>Of course I get some snide remarks from people online, who comment, “You must have a lot of time on your hands. I wish I had the time to do that.” And my retort is: if you have an active Netflix account or over two hours of daily screen time, I don’t want to hear it.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-8" id="footnote-anchor-8"><sup> [8] </sup></a></p>
<h2>a note on “pranks”</h2>
<p>I don’t really categorize my work as “pranks,” as that has a negative connotation of being at someone’s expense, and I firmly believe in doing no harm.<a data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" href="#footnote-9" id="footnote-anchor-9"><sup> [9] </sup></a> I scheme within the realm of chaotic good to chaotic neutral. Humor purely based on degrading someone else is not only morally reprehensible, it’s also just lazy and uninspired.</p>
<p>If the target of your scheme wouldn’t find it funny, don’t do it. (Unless perhaps they’re some notoriously depraved individual.) Let’s all just agree to be cool guys. Use humor for good.</p>
<h2>go forth and be mischievous</h2>
<p>If you read this far, then you spent ~10 minutes with me. So I challenge you to spend another 10 minutes today scheming a fun little way to break out of the monotony of everyday life. Start small. Write a silly note to a friend. Craft a quick lil poster. Tape something weird to your wall. Create something that exists for no other reason than to spark delight.</p>
<p>Today, your mission is to make yourself giggle. And then report back, because in the universe of mischief, you have <em>at minimum</em> an audience of me.</p>

<p>One of the few books that had a drastic impact on my worldview is The Four Agreements. Its premise is that most of your misery can be attributed to ingesting implicit rules of how you should behave, punishing yourself when not meeting those rules, and resenting others who seem free of those rules. When people say rude and bitter things, they’re simply projecting their own miserable inner world onto you. And the more demeaning thoughts you have of others, the more you’ll assume others have demeaning thoughts of you. When you can realize all this, it’s easier to train yourself out of getting hurt by the negativity of others, as well as the habits of inflicting punishment upon yourself.</p>
