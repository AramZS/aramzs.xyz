<p>Minimalist retractable door handles became a signature feature of electric vehicles (EVs), but the controversial designs are now at the centre of China's biggest car recall, affecting more than 4 million vehicles.</p>
<p>The recall, which includes 2.98 million Chinese-made Teslas, follows safety concerns that the hidden door handles are difficult to locate and open in emergency situations.</p>
<p>Other brands affected by the recall include Chinese carmakers XPeng, Xiaomi and Geely.</p>
<p>Made popular by multi-billionaire Elon Musk's Tesla, hidden door handles are designed to tuck seamlessly within the car's door panel and only extend outwards approaches their vehicle.</p>
<p>The design came under scrutiny after two fatal crashes in China involving Xiaomi EVs, in which power failures were suspected to have prevented doors from being opened.</p>
<p>Tesla said in a statement on Friday that it was recalling some of its Model 3, Y, S and X vehicles due to door handles that are "difficult to identify and operate because their colour is similar to the interior trim".</p>
<p>This could hinder occupants or rescuers from quickly opening the doors in an emergency, such as during a "severe collision causing the vehicle's low-voltage system to fail."</p>
<p>Tesla said it will place warning labels on the recalled vehicles and issue a software update to automatically lower a vehicle's windows after a collision.</p>
<p>It is not clear if the affected firms plan to make a similar recall for the rest of the world.</p>
<p>The BBC has contacted Tesla, XPeng, Xiaomi and Geely for comment.</p>
<p>In February, Chinese authorities <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cp37g5nxe3lo">announced a ban on hidden door handles</a>.</p>
<p>Under new rules due to take effect on 1 January 2027, cars will only be allowed to be sold in China if they have a mechanical release on both the inside and outside of their doors.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c203q2ywn88o">Tesla's door handles were also involved in a probe by US safety regulators</a> after reports that they suddenly stopped working, leaving children trapped in the cars.</p>
<p>In July, the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration suggested creating a new formal safety standard for all carmakers.</p>
<p>The BBC has contacted the agency for comment.</p>
