<p>The Food and Drug Administration approved updated Covid vaccines on Thursday, clearing the way for manufacturers to ship doses to pharmacies and doctors’ offices.</p>
<p>Moderna and Pfizer, which make the most commonly used Covid vaccines, are expected to begin shipping soon; Pfizer said it would do so “immediately,” and Moderna has done so within days of approval in past years. Sanofi, which distributes a non-mRNA option called Nuvaxovid, said doses would be available in September, with the timing varying by location.</p>
<p>However, criteria set by the F.D.A. and a legal dispute stemming from past Trump administration actions could complicate access for some people.</p>
<p>Here is what we know. This article will be updated as more information becomes available.</p>
<h2>Who is eligible for the vaccines?</h2>
<p>As was the case last year, the F.D.A. approved the shots for all people 65 and older, as well as for younger people who have a condition that increases their risk of severe illness from Covid. The Moderna shot is approved for ages 6 months and older; the Pfizer shot for ages 5 years and older; and the Sanofi shot for ages 12 years and older.</p>
<p>About 30 percent to 60 percent of Americans have a qualifying condition, F.D.A. officials <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/05/23/health/covid-vaccine-restrictions-eligibility.html">estimated last year</a>. Aside from age, a <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/underlying-conditions.html">long list of factors</a> can increase the risk of severe illness. They include asthma, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity and pregnancy, as well as cancer, immune deficiency, some mental illnesses and various heart and lung diseases.</p>
<p>People who are not formally eligible may still be able to get vaccinated. Doctors can administer vaccines off label, meaning outside F.D.A. approval criteria.</p>
