<p>In my <a href="https://www.patreon.com/merrittk/posts/log-off-or-how-165930507">piece on Brianna Wu</a>, I talked a little about how the structure of the modern internet encouraged her to become who she is today. Certainly people bring their own personalities and drives to systems like social media, but those systems also reward specific kinds of behavior, shaping people in ways that are subtle and insidious. I believe that similar processes have shaped Taylor Lorenz, and that given the state of our information environment, it was always the case that she and others like her would spin out into paranoic antipathy towards the left.</p>
<h1>The Atomization of Media</h1>
<p>Most people realize that the profession of journalism — actually hitting the ground and talking to people and covering the news, not just cranking out opinion columns or newsletters — has been utterly crushed over the past decade and change. There are a number of explanations for this: the decline of print media, the disastrous "pivot to video" of the 2010s encouraged by falsified Facebook view counts, the financialization of the economy and consequent selling-off of legacy publications to vulture capitalists, and the deliberate destruction of trusted sources by the organized right wing.</p>
<p>As stable jobs in media have vanished, startups like Patreon and Substack have stepped in to provide writers with a means of making an income. And while platforms like Substack, Patreon, YouTube, TikTok and Twitch have proclaimed themselves harbingers of the "democratization" of media, a more descriptive term might be "atomization."</p>
<p>In the past, people received news and related information from outlets and organizations. Newspapers and television programs reported on current events and conveyed them to their audiences. These channels had their own institutional issues, biases, and blind spots. But crucially, they were institutions. An individual working in a traditional news environment was working alongside their peers to produce something that none of them could accomplish individually.</p>
<h1>The Rise of the Journalist-Influencer</h1>
<p>I have never been a journalist. I've written plenty, been the editor for a couple of cultural outlets, and published some books, but I've never really done any actual reporting. Still, I have been a staff member at a media organization, and the experience of working with a group of people is vastly different from being out on your own.</p>
<p>In an organization, there are guardrails — fact checkers, legal departments, higher-ups, collaborators. In some cases, these guardrails can be a problem. For instance, at the games media site Fanbyte, we were pressured by ownership to avoid coverage of any topic which might paint China in a bad light. (Our refusal to submit to this dictate was one reason why the site was eventually shut down by Tencent.)...</p>

<p><a href="https://www.patreon.com/merrittk/posts/deadly-secrets-160745611">The Deadly Secrets of Online Street-Fighting</a></p>

<p>Many individuals believe themselves to be capable of holding their own in an internet fight. Yet time and time again, we see these "internet tough guys" make mistakes that condemn them to days, weeks, or even years of ridicule.</p>
<p>What is the average person meant to do? One possibility is to simply hope that one never lands in such a situation. Well, hope in one hand and shitpost in the other and see which fills up faster.</p>
<p>The following advice will help you navigate the terrifying world of online conflict. It is based on over a decade of real experience, but assumes no prior training. Proper use of these techniques will not allow you to win 100% of the time — no system of self-defense can claim that. But it will help you survive situations that are all too real in today's "fiberoptic jungle."</p>
<h1>1. Avoid Combat Wherever Possible</h1>
<p>Avoiding internet fights is preferable to deescalation is preferable to escape is preferable to combat. It is impossible to live an interesting and fulfilling live without attracting some enemies, opps, and/or haters. However, one should avoid being drawn into confrontation with them.</p>
<p>Has someone insulted your honor, your appearance, your craft? It is best to ignore such provocations.</p>
<p>To paraphase the ancient masters, "Hahahahahahahaha How The Fuck Is Cyber Bullying Real Hahahaha, Just Walk Away From The Screen Like Close Your Eyes Haha."</p>
<h1>2. Do Not Seek Out One's Haters</h1>
<p>Namesearching is the act of the coward. We must all strive daily to rid ourselves of this noxious habit.</p>
<p>Repeat after me: "it is none of my business what people think or say about me." If you find yourself namesearching, then at the very least do not engage with your detractors. This never makes you look cool or smart — quite the opposite. He who digs around in the muck comes out looking like a hog.</p>
<h1>3. Be Patient in All Things</h1>
<p>The arc of history may not bend towards justice, but the arc of being a total dipshit bends towards people realizing what an insufferable bitch you are. If someone is being rude or ill-mannered to you, this probably reflects a broader pattern of behavior.</p>
<p>Resist the immediate impulse to refute claims, decry slander, and counterattack. Do not rage. Do not apologize. Do not explain yourself.</p>
<p>A rookie boxer is drawn into an emotional state by a jab to the face — this leads her to make further mistakes. A true warrior is able to shrug off a hail of posts and consider their next action from a position of calculated strategic thinking.</p>
<h1>4. Let Your Enemy Think You Are Chill When You Are Mad</h1>
<p>Ideally you don't want to allow online provocations to affect your emotional state at all. But few of us have reached this level of proficiency. The next best thing is to conceal your anger. If you absolutely must respond to an attack, do so from a position of detached irony.</p>
<p>Point out how mad your opponent seems to be, while you remain totally unflappable. Deploy apparent non sequiturs and indecipherable image macros as a confusion tactic. Simply reply "lol."</p>
<h1>5. Go Totally Nuts and Flip Out</h1>
<p>If all else fails, post constantly about how you're under attack. Become obsessed with your own victimhood and claim weakness when you are, in fact, strong. Argue that your assailants are robbing you of security and financial well-being and use this to beg for donations. Learn terms like "DARVO," "emotional labor," and "punching down" and make use of them as frequently as possible regardless of the context. Write long and wildly out-of-touch with reality essays and/or threads on social media proclaiming your innocence. Never miss an opportunity to remind people that you are the precious Omelas child being abused by strangers. Accuse your foes of silencing you and point out all of the ways in which they are more socially privileged than you. Threaten lawsuits. File lawsuits. Salt the earth. Cut off your nose to spite your face. Cut off your face to spite your body. Claim that you're shutting down your account. Shut down your account. Come back two hours later to post anime porno. Repeat these steps in the order of your choosing until everyone sees how great you are and finally admits that you were right all along.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.patreon.com/merrittk/posts/millennial-164275916">Millennial Cringe</a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.patreon.com/merrittk/posts/millennial-164275916">July 19</a></p>
<p>A large part — maybe even the majority — of my generation has nothing to say and no culture of our own. And that part includes many artists, writers, directors, and creators of all kinds. Millennial culture has degenerated into endless remakes, demakes, overhauls, and reinterpretations of childhood trash.</p>
<p>What if the Power Rangers had trauma? What if modern video games was the PlayStation? What if an old TV show was scary? What if Chuck-E-Cheese was scary? What if websites were scary? What if a Nintendo game was scary? What if Batman was a ninja? What if Toxic Crusaders was a beat-em-up? What if the Samurai Pizza Cats came back? What if Harry Potter was a TV show? What if we just rewatched Friends and The Office over and over again? What if we got really into cartoons for children with Disney sitcom-level humor but actually the lore is really dark when you think about it? What if you were a kid on the internet in the 90s again talking to strangers and no social media, no phone?</p>
<p>Whenever I bring this up, the explanation people give is that millennials have been fucked over so much by global crises, financial and otherwise, that we've retreated into being eternal children, collecting toys and continuing to obsess over films and television shows intended for the kids we don't have. I can accept that to a degree, even if I find it depressing. But as more of us have clawed our way into positions of cultural capital, it's become clear the problem isn't just with consumption. Even as producers, we're stuck reliving the same scenarios over and over again. Blame corporate risk aversion, blame the economic base, blame the spectre of the boomer, it doesn't matter. Video game developers once spoke of being inspired by childhood experiences exploring caves or growing gardens. Today, they talk about being inspired by their childhood video games.</p>
<p>Nostalgia is human. But nostalgia as the dominant mode of relation to life is narcotizing. We look back because we can't see anything better ahead. To some extent that's understandable. It's also deeply pathetic. We were supposed to be better than the hoseposters, the people who couldn't ever shut the fuck up about being latchkey kids. To accept our helplessness, to make ourselves willing victims because the world is a Heckin' Mess and we'll never afford homes so we might as well indulge in spending all our money on children's toys and corporate merchandise is cowardly.</p>
<p>(By the way, do you even really want a house or do you just like bitching about how you'll never afford one? Do you really want to be immobilized by property, to pay a bank instead of a landlord every month, to have to shell out thousands every time something goes wrong just because you've been taught that it's the goddamn American dream? How fully have you settled into generational victimhood without even realizing it?)</p>
<p>Aren't there things that shaped your childhood besides the cartoons you watched and the toys you played with? Don't you remember waking up to the sound of a distant lawnmower on a summer Saturday morning? The vinegar smell of dying Easter Eggs with your siblings? Imagining a long scythe cutting down the telephone poles as you passed them in the backseat of your family's car on a drive that seemed like it took years?</p>
<p>We are not victims of history. We are participants in it. Dredging up and remixing the corporate-created landscape of our past will not heal us or change our circumstances. We can do better, and we owe it to ourselves to try.</p>
