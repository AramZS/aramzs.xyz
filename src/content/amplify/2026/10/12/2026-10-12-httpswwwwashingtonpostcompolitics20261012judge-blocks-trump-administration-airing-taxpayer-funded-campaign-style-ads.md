<html><body><figure><img alt="" src="https://cloudfront-us-east-1.images.arcpublishing.com/wapo/OMIGK2JWJSBWPDJQFH77Z7PI2Y.jpg"/><figcaption>President Donald Trump speaks with reporters Saturday at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland. (Alex Brandon/AP)</figcaption></figure><p>A federal judge issued a temporary order Sunday blocking the Trump administration from airing taxpayer-funded campaign-style<b> </b>television commercials. But the administration blew past a Sunday evening deadline and ran two of the ads into the early hours of Monday, advertising data from AdImpact shows.</p><p>U.S. District Judge Jennifer L. Rochon in Manhattan wrote <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638.41.0.pdf" rel="" target="_self" title="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638.41.0.pdf">in an order</a> Sunday that the administration must “cancel or cause to be cancelled” any ads from airing or dissemination, including those that were scheduled to air Sunday evening.</p><p>Her decision came after the Department of Homeland Security said in a <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638.37.0.pdf" rel="" target="_self" title="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638/gov.uscourts.nysd.674638.37.0.pdf">court filing</a> Saturday that the ads would “not continue” past Sunday and that it had no plans to purchase additional ad spaces past that date. President Donald Trump said earlier that campaign funds would be used to pay for the ads going forward.</p><p>The two-week order prohibits the administration from “using, disbursing, or transferring funds” appropriated to DHS to “develop, create, disseminate, place, distribute, publish, or air” the commercials. </p><p>However, two of the ads aired numerous times after the deadline passed at 6:45 p.m. Eastern time Sunday, according to data provided byAdImpact. The two ads ran during airings of various shows, including “America’s Funniest Home Videos,” “ABC World News Tonight” and CBS News’s “60 Minutes.”</p><p>“DHS did not receive notice from the court in time to cancel the ad buys,” the White House wrote in a Monday statement. “The PSAs will be paid for by outside groups moving forward.”</p><p>The ads, which began airing in late September, included one that resembled a commercial for Trump’s 2024 presidential campaign and another of him and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth advocating the Iran war. </p><p>The ads were paid for with $20 million the Office of Management and Budget added to a DHS budget line for “commemorative events,” <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/01/trump-ads-use-money-border-security-slain-officers-memorials/" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/01/trump-ads-use-money-border-security-slain-officers-memorials/">The Washington Post previously reported</a>. Congress had approved that spending for “Line-of-Duty death memorials, Department or agency anniversaries, and commendation ceremonies,” according to a House Budget Committee report.</p><p>The lawsuit challenging the commercials was brought by a coalition that included the advocacy group Common Cause. It argued that the ads violate federal law prohibiting agencies from using taxpayer money for unauthorized<b> </b>“publicity and propaganda.” </p><p>Rochon wrote that the plaintiffs are “likely to succeed on the merits” of their challenge and “will suffer irreparable harm in the absence of an injunction.”</p><p>Trump has not publicly commented on the order, but after backlash from <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/05/trump-reverses-course-says-he-wont-use-taxpayer-money-campaign-style-ads/?itid=lk_inline_manual_9" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/05/trump-reverses-course-says-he-wont-use-taxpayer-money-campaign-style-ads/?itid=lk_inline_manual_9">Democrats and some Republicans</a>, he said on Oct. 5 that his super PAC would cover future ad costs.</p><p>In a statement issued by Democracy Forward, a nonprofit legal organization, the coalition said it was “pleased the court acted swiftly,” calling the ads “blatantly unlawful political propaganda.”</p><p>OMB and DHS did not respond to requests for comment on the ads that ran after the judge’s deadline.</p><p>Last week, the Democratic National Committee <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/07/democratic-national-committee-sues-stop-taxpayer-funded-trump-ads/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/07/democratic-national-committee-sues-stop-taxpayer-funded-trump-ads/">filed a separate lawsuit</a> over the ads, arguing that they violate campaign spending rules and make it harder for the group’s candidates to compete.</p></body></html>
