<p><a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crq5nzxv9q4yo"><strong><u>European supermarkets hit by mass-hack targeting refrigeration units</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>BBC News ($): </strong>We start this week in France, where unknown hackers caused thousands of refrigerator units in hundreds of supermarkets and grocery stores across the country and wider Europe to not only defrost but actively <em>heat up </em>and spoil the food inside. The Danish maker of refrigerators, Danfoss, said the models were older and no longer received security patches. The mass hacks resulted in huge food waste and caused <a href="https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/hack-of-danfoss-refrigeration-controllers-in-europe-causes-tens-of-millions/832832/"><u>massive disruption</u></a> to food supplies. As hacks go, targeting the food supply is about as real-world as it gets — and there are still thousands of exposed fridges exposed to the internet. According to <a href="https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/10/cyberattack-on-connected-fridges-in-france.html"><u>Bruce Schneier</u></a>: "Any IoT device that can’t be updated is vulnerable. And, probably, any IoT device that can be updated is also vulnerable." He already <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Schneier%27s_law"><u>has a law</u></a> named after him… so, is this Schneier's paradox?<br/>

<p><a href="https://www.grocerydive.com/news/ahold-delhaize-usa-sites-still-down/832900/"><strong><u>Ahold Delhaize goes offline after detecting cyberattack, causing widespread outages</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>Grocery Dive: </strong>Food-related hacks also hit stateside this week, as grocery stores across the U.S., including Stop &amp; Shop, Hannaford, and Food Lion, continue to experience website and app outages. <p><a href="https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/13/openai-engineer-arrested-alleged-bugging-executive-conference-room/"><strong><u>OpenAI charged after allegedly planting covert recording device in conference room</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>San Francisco Standard: </strong>Gobsmacking details from OpenAI about an brazen insider threat, after a Saudi national on the product team allegedly planted a recording device in one of the company's executive conference rooms in its San Francisco office. OpenAI allegedly caught him in the act with a hidden camera. The suspicion is that the employee was conducting espionage — for whom it isn't clear, such as another company or <a href="https://x.com/ScottMStedman/status/2110108735855333403"><u>a government</u></a>. The FBI arrested and charged the employee, and OpenAI fired him. While the company called the recording device "sophisticated" in nature, it looks more like an off-the-shelf internet-connected microphone. <br/>

<p><a href="https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/"><strong><u>Governments are using online ads to infect people with zero-click spyware</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>Citizen Lab: </strong>A <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/nightmare-scenario-zero-click-spyware-is-infecting-phones-via-targeted-ads/"><u>"nightmare scenario"</u></a> just came to pass: Government spyware can now infect phones and computers by simply displaying an online advert on someone's phone or computer. Citizen Lab found an Israeli ostensible adtech firm called Discover-Tech that they say pretty much only exists to give spyware makers a way of targeting and hacking specific people's devices with "zero-click" spyware — that's when it invisibly plants on your device without having to click or tap on anything. Citizen Lab found this company was used to launch spyware attacks on dozens of devices belonging to media organization <a href="https://x.com/AlJazeera/status/2110763313810354492"><u>Al Jazeera</u></a> with the notorious Candiru zero-click spyware. Using an ad-blocker helps, but as threats go, this is a hair-raiser. More from <a href="https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming"><u>Confiant</u></a>.<br/>

<p><a href="https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/who-is-planting-backdoors-in-ledger-crypto-wallets/"><strong><u>Someone is planting hidden implants in Trezor and Ledger hardware crypto wallets</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>Kim Zetter's Zero Day: </strong>Security researchers are sounding the alarm that some <a href="https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/trezor-wallets-also-compromised-with-implants/"><u>Trezor</u></a> and <a href="https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/who-is-planting-backdoors-in-ledger-crypto-wallets/"><u>Ledger</u></a> customer hardware wallets, which are used to store seed phrases for securing crypto on the blockchain, may have been compromised by hackers who added an implant to their devices. The implant appears to allow the theft and transmission of those seed phrases over the cell network. The attacks appear to be "deliberately targeting authorized sellers" of the wallets. Around $100 million in crypto has been stolen so far as a result in recent weeks, likely in rapid succession as word of hacks spread quickly among the crypto community. This was spectacular reporting by Zetter.<br/>

<strong>CSO Online: </strong>Always a good read from <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/metacurity.com/post/3mxtq7xcuxd25"><u>@metacurity</u></a>, who dives into some bullsh*t-busting on the recent spate of AI models breaking out of their containment and hacking into companies.
