<p>The biggest star of New York Comic Con this year was, without question, Matt Dinniman, the author of the <em>Dungeon Crawler Carl </em>book series. As I walked the halls of the Javits Center on Thursday, it was clear that of all the emerging fandoms jockeying for attention, this was the year <em>Dungeon Crawler Carl</em> officially reached escape velocity. But there’s a wrinkle here.</p>
<p><em>Dungeon Crawler Carl</em> is not just a popular book series that was big on the internet before normies heard about it. The series was — and in some ways is still — written in conjunction with Dinniman’s Patreon followers. There’s an argument to be made that it is one of Patreon’s biggest success stories ever. And yet, it’s not really that big of a hit <em>on </em>Patreon. And, even stranger, Dinniman has continued to use Patreon to manage his fanbase even as he rockets up the bestseller lists.</p>
<p>First, let’s establish a baseline for Patreon popularity because it doesn’t line up the way you might imagine. I track Patreon’s biggest accounts every month and <em>Dungeon Crawler Carl</em> is big, but nowhere near the top of the charts. There wasn’t even a major bump last summer, after Dinniman and his fans <a href="https://variety.com/2026/film/news/dungeon-crawler-carl-ryan-reynolds-jeanpool-comic-con-1236822244/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">stormed</a> San Diego Comic-Con.</p>
<p>The accounts sitting at the top of Patreon’s leaderboard every month are eclectic, to say the least. Not counting private pages, two of the three most popular creators on the platform make mods for the racing game <em>Assetto Corsa</em>, which is roughly the 100th most played game on Steam. Besides them, six of the 50 largest creators make porn games. In both cases, Patreon allows them to get continuous support since they make games you’re not allowed to sell through regular retailers.</p>
<p>The <em>Dungeon Crawler Carl </em>Patreon is currently at number 20 on the platform, with over 31,000 paid members. The series started on the indie publishing site Royal Road in January 2020, and its Patreon launched soon after. Like many of the platform’s successful users, Dinniman was a veteran creator who was popular in a niche online community. He had been writing LitRPG fiction online since 2013, and was such a dedicated Redditor before that he created r/IronMaiden in 2011. And also like a lot of other successful Patreon creators, paying fans have been able to directly influence the creator’s output.</p>
<p>In addition to getting early access to the stories before they’re collected as books, Dinniman’s patrons can <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/DungeonCrawlerCarl/comments/1u7jdjx/is_there_a_list_of_all_things_that_were_decided/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">vote</a> to decide elements to be added to the series, like characters’ full names or future transformations. The fans’ effect on the series is part of the narrative, since the books follow a deadly intergalactic game show where the main characters are contestants. They regularly receive “fan boxes” of helpful items and weapons which have been picked by patron votes. But the story the patrons help write has recently found a much larger audience.</p>
<p>After two years, the series <a href="https://deadline.com/2022/10/dungeon-crawler-carl-author-matt-dinniman-wme-1235155412/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">sold over 400,000 copies</a>, and was the number one audiobook on Amazon. In 2024, Dinniman got a <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/DungeonCrawlerCarl/comments/1fj3jtg/ace_books_picks_up_books_46_in_dungeon_crawler/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">traditional publishing deal</a> from Ace Books and a <a href="https://deadline.com/2024/08/dungeon-crawler-carl-seth-macfarlane-universal-chris-yost-series-1236045866/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">TV adaptation</a> announced for Peacock. The <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/12/books/review/dungeon-crawler-carl-matt-dinniman.html?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">fandom</a> kept growing larger with each new book, and yesterday I got to be immersed in it.</p>

<p>I attended a panel sponsored by Audible, with Dinniman and his audiobook narrator Jeff Hays in attendance.Theroom was filled to the brim with 800-plus fans, many of them dressed as Carl or his talking cat Princess Donut, and they made it feel like a touchdown at the Super Bowl when Hays read an unreleased dispatch from the upcoming ninth book. It was all charged and lavish enough to make you forget that, underneath the glitz of a superstar reception, Dinniman is still on Patreon and that many of the fans in the room are paying him every month to be a part of the fandom.</p>
<p>Patreon rewards creators who are popular in a niche and incentivizes them to stay in that niche. The creators want the fans to keep paying month after month, so they do what the fans want, which means anyone who isn’t paying feels like they aren’t really in the inner circle of fans. <em>Dungeon Crawler Carl </em>was well-written enough, and available on Amazon like a normal book since the beginning, which broke this paradigm completely. Now it’s the biggest thing at Comic Con, not just among the fans but the professionals.</p>
<p>Dinniman’s books were stacked higher than any other at multiple booksellers. Audible is one of many companies with a hand in the series, from Vault Comics to Webtoon, with appearances at the convention placing <em>Carl </em>front and center, a prize example of their property. All the deals Dinniman has struck with these corporations are non-exclusive, so he can keep giving his patrons what they paid for. A savvy bit of contracting, but it still raises the question of who really owns what.</p>

<p>It’s also worth pointing out that Dinniman isn’t the only Patreon creator to grow beyond the platform like this. As big as his NYCC panel was, it was dwarfed by the season premiere of <em>Helluva Boss</em>, the YouTube animated show made with Patreon money. Except that at Comic Con it was presented by Amazon, whose <a href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/hazbin-hotel-spinoff-helluva-boss-prime-video-1236201132/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">distribution deal</a> for the show came after the creator made <a href="https://variety.com/2023/tv/news/prime-video-orders-hazbin-hotel-animated-series-a24-1235738430/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">another deal</a> with A24. The highest tier of patrons for <em>Helluva Boss </em>can still get regular interviews with the animators, but exactly how long can that kind of relationship with fans meaningfully exist when the <a href="https://www.animationmagazine.net/2025/08/helluva-boss-how-vivienne-medranos-devilishly-fun-comedy-found-a-new-home-on-prime-video/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">real money</a> starts coming in?</p>
<p>When Dinniman started the series, he was only answerable to his patrons and the online storefronts where he sold the finished books. Now, he’s at the helm of a multimedia juggernaut, and has to juggle the demands of print books, audiobooks, TV, merch, game spinoffs, and even a <a href="https://www.webtoons.com/en/fantasy/dungeon-crawler-carl/list?title_no=8177&amp;utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">manga version</a>. There isn’t much room for patrons in there anymore, and maybe there shouldn’t need to be, because it means all those corporations still have license to treat him like just another creator.</p>
<p><em>Dungeon Crawler Carl </em>is an insane success story for everyone involved, but that means every publisher and producer will be looking for the next one. The hundreds of creators following in Dinniman’s footsteps will now be expected, like him, to carry their original fan communities with them. Not all of them will have the same success — creatively or professionally — that he did, and they’ll have to pick between their Patreons and their paymasters. Because if even Dinniman, the corporate darling, still has to ask for subscriptions and regular support, then it’s impossible for anyone to escape.</p>

<p>Discord has kind of the opposite problem to X: It’s already the platform of a new generation, but no one important or official wants to use it. And whenever someone tries, we’re all reminded why.</p>

<p>On Monday, an official Sonic The Hedgehog Discord server <a href="https://www.eurogamer.net/sonic-the-hedgehog-official-discord-oh-no?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">opened</a>. Within its first 24 hours, it had over 70,000 users. To give you an idea of how big Sonic is online, that doubles the users for the <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/media/2022/nov/15/uk-treasury-joins-chat-app-discord-torrent-abuse?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">UK Treasury</a> Discord they tried in 2022 when Elon took over Twitter. All those users are reportedly overseen by just five human moderators.</p>
<p>The server reportedly needed at least one lockdown in its first day, and has already banned thousands of users because of moderation policies that Sega’s Jason Webber <a href="https://x.com/RubyEclipse/status/2107513140015722898?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">called</a> “probably too strict”. It’s not strict enough to stop the chaos of the Sonic fans, though. Especially since they seem to have help on the inside: The server’s bot moderator has the username “JohnSega1960.”</p>
<p>There are more pragmatic reasons than emoji spam to want to stay off Discord. Earlier this week, one of the platform’s security partners, Double Counter, <a href="https://www.gamesindustry.biz/discord-protection-bot-double-counter-hit-by-breach-exposing-around-1-million-email-addresses?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">announced</a> they had been hacked, exposing personal data for millions of Discord users.</p>
<p>For those keeping score, Discord just <a href="https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/discord-age-verification-rolls-out-today-with-changes-spurred-by-user-backlash/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">relaunched</a> its age verification programs, after its first try earlier this year needed to be <a href="https://discord.com/blog/getting-global-age-assurance-right-what-we-got-wrong-and-whats-changing?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">suspended</a> over security vulnerabilities. The new hack reportedly didn’t sweep up any of the new age verification data, like Google account info or credit card numbers, but you’re starting to see an idea of why Sonic is such an outlier. At least TikTok is safer, right?</p>
<p>New reporting says the lawsuits are focusing on TikTok experimenting with safety features by making them do nothing, with one experimental group having the “Algo Refresh” button disabled and <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/senators-demand-answers-from-tiktok-over-experiment-that-disabled-safeguards/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">another group</a> losing the platform’s safeguards for harmful content.</p>
<p>When I wrote about <a href="https://www.garbageday.email/p/miles-morales-probably-couldn-t-happen-now">Petra Parker</a>, the fan-created trend of drawing Spider-Man as a trans woman, I talked about how online fandoms are now subject to the same whims of the algorithm as any other online space. We now have another example, that’s both very similar to Petra Parker and completely opposite.</p>

<p>The trend is drawing <em>Harry Potter</em>’s Draco Malfoy as an anime girl who, in anime tradition, is only a hateful bully to hide her attraction to Harry. It started with <a href="https://x.com/redteneri/status/2102398387194626330?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">this image</a>, posted by X user Redteneri on September 22nd. It took until October for the trend to peak and lead to <a href="https://cybernews.com/entertainment/harry-potter-malfoid-backlash-anime/?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">drama</a>, because it’s called “<a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/malfoid-female-draco-malfoy?utm_campaign=dungeon-crawler-carl-hits-the-big-time&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_source=www.garbageday.email">Malfoid</a>”, from the incel slang for a woman “foid”. Which gives you an idea where it’s most popular, and why even on X it needed some time to capture attention.</p>
<p>Just like Petra Parker, this trend exploded thanks to the huge numbers of fan artists who are still on X. Since they’re posting on the platform for visibility, they’re incentivized to draw whatever the algorithm tells them to. Even when it’s something as contradictory as an incel fantasy female version of a male character created by an anti-trans billionaire activist.</p>
