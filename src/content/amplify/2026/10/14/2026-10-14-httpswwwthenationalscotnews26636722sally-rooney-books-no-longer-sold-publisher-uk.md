<p>BESTSELLING Irish author Sally Rooney’s books will no longer be supplied in the UK by her publisher after UK anti-terror legislation left it unable to pass on earnings from sales of her work.</p>
<p>Publisher Faber &amp; Faber said it had agreed with Rooney that it could no longer supply her books in the UK “in any format” because of <a href="https://www.thenational.scot/news/25398320.number-10-warns-sally-rooney-risks-terror-offence-palestine-action-comments/?ref=ed_direct">the legal situation arising from her support for Palestine Action</a>.</p>
<p>The group was proscribed as a terrorist organisation by the <a href="https://www.thenational.scot/topics/uk-government/?ref=au">UK Government</a> in July 2025.</p>
<p>Rooney <a href="https://www.thenational.scot/news/25764357.sally-rooney-top-writers-voice-support-palestine-action-prisoners/?ref=ed_direct">subsequently publicly stated that she intended to continue supporting Palestine Action</a>, which is not proscribed in the Republic of Ireland.</p>
<p>The award-winning author, whose novel Normal People has sold more than one million copies in the UK alone, said the situation meant fulfilling contracts to publish her work in Britain could now constitute a criminal offence.</p>
<p>She said: “In July 2025, the direct-action protest group <a href="https://www.thenational.scot/topics/palestine/?ref=au">Palestine</a> Action was proscribed as a terrorist organisation in the UK.</p>
<p>“The following month, I wrote an opinion piece expressing my intention to go on supporting Palestine Action, which is not a proscribed or otherwise prohibited group here in the Republic of Ireland.</p>
<p>“As a result, the fulfilment of contracts to publish my work in the UK could now constitute a serious criminal offence.</p>
<p>“After a process of consultation and deliberation, and with my full agreement, my publisher Faber has therefore determined that it can no longer supply my work within the UK.</p>
<p>“I have made every effort to be honest with the public about my beliefs and intentions, and I am wholly prepared to accept the consequences. I have no further comment at this time.”</p>
<p>Faber said the current UK legislation meant it was unable to pass on Rooney’s UK earnings.</p>
<p>The publisher said: “To sell a writer’s work and not be able to pass on earnings is an unconscionable situation for a publisher.</p>
<p>“As such, and in full agreement with Sally, we have taken the decision that we can no longer supply her works in the UK in any format.</p>
<p>“We remain the proud publisher of her remarkable body of work, which we will continue to sell in other territories. We have no further comment at this time.”</p>
<p>As well as Normal People, her novels include Conversations with Friends and Beautiful World, Where Are You, both of which have sold more than 500,000 copies.</p>
<p>Green Party leader Zack Polanski said: “A couple of weeks ago, Labour said they'd got it wrong on <a href="https://www.thenational.scot/topics/Gaza/?ref=au">Gaza</a>. Now they have banned books by Sally Rooney, one of the most celebrated and influential authors of recent times.</p>
<p>"Labour's authoritarian and ludicrous law has turned the country into a laughing stock around the world and the proscription on Palestine Action must be overturned now.”</p>
<p>Palestine Action co-founder and claimant in the ongoing legal challenge to the proscription, Huda Ammori, said in response to Rooney's announcement: "Sally Rooney being unable to publish her work in the UK marks a new low in the escalating assault on free speech caused by the proscription of Palestine Action.</p>
<p>"A world-renowned author fearing arrest and being forced to remove her books from our bookstores, exposes the chilling consequences of this authoritarian law, which has been used to make over 3700 arrests for simply holding seven word signs.</p>
<p>"The ban on Palestine Action was only introduced to serve the interests of the Israel lobby and the Israeli arms industry at the expense of the fundamental freedoms of British citizens. Andy Burnham's government has the power to overturn this authoritarian ban, but they are choosing instead to defend it in the Supreme Court. This is a political choice, and one with profound consequences for freedom of expression in this country.</p>
<p>"Sally Rooney took a brave and principled stand from the moment plans to ban Palestine Action were announced. She has refused to compromise her convictions, even at considerable personal and professional cost.</p>
<p>"Her work should be celebrated and widely available — not driven off the shelves of British bookshops because she refuses to be complicit in the suppression of solidarity with the Palestinian people. No author should have to choose between their principles and their freedom to publish. Her treatment should be deeply alarming to everyone who cares about freedom of speech."</p>
