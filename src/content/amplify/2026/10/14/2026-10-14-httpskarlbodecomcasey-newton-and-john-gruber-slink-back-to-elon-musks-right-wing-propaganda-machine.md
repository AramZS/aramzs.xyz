<p>Quick reminder: Elon Musk is an unremarkable and virulent racist who <a href="https://karlbode.com/the-press-is-still-propping-up-elon-musks-supergenius-engineer-mythology/">cosplays as a supergenius engineer</a>. <p>There's bottomless evidence <a href="https://karlbode.com/the-press-is-still-propping-up-elon-musks-supergenius-engineer-mythology/">he doesn't know how anything works</a>, yet has found unsurprising financial success in a country being devoured alive by <a href="https://karlbode.com/ai-artifice-and-authenticity/">artifice</a>.</p>
<p>In 2022, butthurt from growing public and journalistic criticism of his bottomless violations of <a href="https://sites.uab.edu/humanrights/2025/03/30/human-rights-concerns-at-teslas-texas-gigafactory/?ref=karlbode.com">labor rights</a>, <a href="https://karlbode.com/musk-tries-to-bribe-memphis-so-theyll-overlook-his-ai-data-centers-are-killing-black-toddlers/">environmental law</a>, <a href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/black-tesla-employees-detail-racist-182341820.html?ref=karlbode.com">civil rights</a>, and <a href="https://futurism.com/tesla-nhtsa-autopilot-report?ref=karlbode.com">public safety protections</a>, Musk bought Twitter and immediately turned the platform into a safe space for assholes, bigots, cryptocurrency scammers, right wing zealots, and trolls.</p>
<p>Musk quickly set to work tweaking the social media website's algorithms <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/feb/15/elon-musk-changes-twitter-algorithm-super-bowl-slump-report?ref=karlbode.com">specifically to ensure his ego was adequately coddled</a> and to force-feed users a steady diet of right wing agitprop (one study found that just two-months' worth of new Twitter use <a href="https://karlbode.com/data-shows-elon-musks-x-quickly-rots-your-brain/">was enough to drive a person ideologically rightward</a>).</p>
<p>Musk fired 6,000 employees, <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1ejq154nqzo?ref=karlbode.com">refusing to give many of them severance</a>. And despite his virulently-racist anti-immigration rhetoric (despite <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2024/10/26/elon-musk-immigration-status/?ref=karlbode.com">being an illegal immigrant himself</a>), he overloaded and worked his remaining H-1B visa employees (who couldn't quit without risk of deportation) into the ground.</p>
<p>Recognizing the quickly-devolving nature of the diseased website and its pudding-brained owner, many journalists around that time wisely <a href="https://www.theverge.com/2021/4/16/22387231/why-i-left-twitter?ref=karlbode.com">decided to leave Twitter entirely</a>, abandoning a platform that had, until then, become a centralized hub for lightning-fast journalistic conversation and debate.</p>
<p>Musk, meanwhile, proceeded to <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/13/elon-musk-political-donor-midterm-elections?ref=karlbode.com">purchase an autocracy</a>, used his new platform to <a href="https://counterhate.com/research/rated-not-helpful-x-community-notes/?ref=karlbode.com">seed 2024 election disinformation</a>, and subsequently <a href="https://newlinesmag.com/reportage/the-zero-people-who-died-after-usaid-was-dismantled/?ref=karlbode.com">began murdering people en masse</a> via DOGE's dismantling of global relief programs the Musk cult was neither empathic nor ethical enough to understand.</p>

<p>It's been a few years, and despite Elon Musk getting <em>continually and indisputably worse in every conceivable way</em>, there's a growing contingent of journalists who've decided that they're no longer really <em>feeling the whole ethics thing</em> and have announced they're returning to the platform. Cooked brains be damned.</p>
<p>Case in point: prominent tech journalist Casey Newton, the heir apparent to <a href="https://thebaffler.com/latest/the-miseducation-of-kara-swisher-ongweso?ref=karlbode.com">Kara Swisher Silicon Valley access journalism</a>, quit Twitter three years ago. He also was among a flood of prominent journalists that laudably left Substack after revelations that <a href="https://karlbode.com/why-is-naomi-klein-funding-substacks-hate-machine/">management had courted white supremacists to goose clicks</a>.</p>
<p>But Newton <a href="https://www.platformer.news/does-intelligence-need-a-hard-cap/?ref=karlbode.com">announced last week</a> on his Platformer newsletter that he was returning to X. Why? He claims that efforts to build better social media alternatives have failed, and he simply <strong>has</strong> to use the platform because that's where all of the biggest AI enthusiasts still post:</p>
<blockquote>in the coming days, I plan to resume posting on X. I am doing so not because I think it’s any better — it isn’t — but because I have exhausted all of the alternatives. I continue to believe that anyone who can avoid posting on X should. But my calculus about the t.radeoffs [sic] involved for my own work has shifted: as the stakes of the AI conversation have risen, boycotting X over <strong>my disagreements with management</strong> has grown both more costly and less effective.</blockquote>
<blockquote>Should the AI crowd ever move elsewhere, I will happily follow them. But I’ve become convinced that it’s important I engage with them, even if doing so makes me feel like I’m coworking inside a Port-a-Potty.</blockquote>
<p>And by "disagreements with management" he means Elon Musk is a <em>nazi</em>.</p>
<p>Newton claims the only way he can do compelling reporting on AI is to submerge himself in sewage. That's not only false, it masks what I think are the real reasons: having ethics imperiled his financial pursuit of raw engagement at scale; and X is full of a lot of AI enthusiasts more receptive to his particular brand of myopia.</p>
<p>It's worth noting that Newton has routinely been under fire on platforms like Bluesky for potential conflicts of interest (he's engaged to an Anthropic employee), <a href="https://www.wheresyoured.at/how-to-argue-with-an-ai-booster/?ref=karlbode.com">rank boosterism</a>, a tendency to lazily <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/karlbode.com/post/3mwbhvju7z22s?ref=karlbode.com">anthropomorphize AI software</a>, and the <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/casey-newton-strikes-out">snotty antagonization</a> of those who have valid criticisms of big tech AI.</p>
<p>BlueSky certainly can be extremely hostile to AI, but that's less a <em>specific failure of the platform and </em>more a natural result of the fact that BlueSky is a niche social media platform <em>filled with refugees from other degraded online platforms</em>, like X and Instagram, ruined by <em>consolidative oligarchic chucklefuckery</em>.</p>

That sort of stuff plays much better on X, Threads, cable TV news, Facebook, LinkedIn and pretty much the <em>entirety of establishment corporate traditional and modern media</em>.</p>

<p>This same dynamic plays out with politically-obtuse AI enthusiasts who can't process that the intense hostility to big tech AI is – entirely fairly or not – now inextricably linked to a virulent disdain for the tech industry's <a href="https://karlbode.com/apple-ceo-tim-cooks-legacy-is-dull-products-and-spineless-capitulation/">enthusiastic</a> coddling of deadly authoritarianism (see how last season's commencement speakers left their establishment bubbles and were immediately shocked by being <a href="https://karlbode.com/anger-at-ai-is-inextricably-fused-with-justified-loathing-of-the-extraction-class-deal-with-it/">booed for lazy AI boosterism</a>).</p>
<p>The most bullish AI boosters don't want to talk about politics. They don't want to think about authoritarian corruption. They want to play with their toys. They're quite often predominantly <em>interested in money</em>. When they wander into spaces where the tech industry's abysmal ethics are prominently disputed, they get squeamish and <em>conclude the problem is somehow everybody else</em>.</p>
<p>That's not to say that AI opponents don't engage in their own myopia, but if you do an honest assessment of the last few decades of tech industry behaviors, it's <em>not exactly a mystery why such hostility is bubbling over, or why the opposition to rapid data center expansion is white hot and broadly bipartisan</em>.</p>
<p>You'd think journalists, whose careers are built on understanding bias, would be aware of this particular dynamic and the risks of submerging yourself in X AI group think under the belief you're getting a well-rounded view of reality. Especially given data on how X/Twitter usage quickly <a href="https://karlbode.com/data-shows-elon-musks-x-quickly-rots-your-brain/">cooks your brain</a>.</p>
<p>But access journalism and punditry remains particularly obtuse to audience capture and the brain-rotting impact of taking-up residence in Elon Musk's recreational-ketamine-fueled fever dreams. In part because it's<em> financially disadvantageous for mainstream American writers to behave ethically</em>. But also because they falsely believe that <em>propaganda can't work on them</em>.</p>
<p>As a result, journalists who can't be honest about their unrelenting quest for engagement – and don't really want to address broader public backlash to their boosterism – construct elaborate scaffolding to make themselves feel good about their decision to dunktank themselves in billionaire-sanctioned toxic waste.</p>
<p>I concede it's a challenge for <em>everyday consumers</em> to ethically shop with their wallet in a country full of giant predatory corporations and a broken public interest press. But I do think journalists should be held to a higher standard when it comes to helping normalize and fund <a href="https://karlbode.com/why-is-naomi-klein-funding-substacks-hate-machine/">fashy-friendly</a> platforms like <a href="https://karlbode.com/why-is-naomi-klein-funding-substacks-hate-machine/">Substack</a> or X.</p>
<p>Especially if you're aware of how important it is to have healthy online public interest spaces unpolluted by the whims of the richest and soggiest manbabies. Platforms that not only give you transparent control over your feed and data, but don't actively shovel right wing propaganda down your gullet or <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/01/22/technology/grok-x-ai-elon-musk-deepfakes.html?ref=karlbode.com"><em>monetize the abuse of children</em></a><em> and the harassment of marginalized human beings</em>.</p>
<p>Folks like Newton and Gruber have financial resources I'll never dream of. They're incredibly well positioned to leverage their contacts and love of technical innovation to <em>help build better platforms for journalists</em>. Instead, we got a lot of weak-kneed white supremacy apologia and a face full of sorry-ass gaslighting.</p>
<p>Criticism of continued journalistic use of Twitter gets dismissed as "virtue signaling," but I really don't think it's a huge ask for folks employed in the factual information business to lead by example and maintain <em>some fleeting semblance of integrity and ethical self awareness</em> in the authoritarian era.</p>
