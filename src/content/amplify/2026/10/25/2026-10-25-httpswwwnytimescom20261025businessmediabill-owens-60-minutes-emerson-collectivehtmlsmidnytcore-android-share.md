<p>Bill Owens, the former executive producer of “60 Minutes,” left the program last year after clashing with the corporate owners of CBS News. In <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/22/business/media/cbs-60-minutes-trump-bill-owens.html">an emotional meeting</a> announcing his resignation, Mr. Owens said additional layers of oversight had encroached on the independence of the show, a staple of the newsmagazine for decades.</p>
<p>On Thursday, Mr. Owens said he had found a place he could make journalism free from what he viewed as corporate interference: <a href="https://newpress.com/">Newpress</a>, a start-up that publishes video journalism from independent creators on YouTube and other digital platforms.</p>
<p>“Legacy media right now is worried about the politics in the country,” Mr. Owens said. “They’re worried about lobbies and sponsorships and advertisers. They’re looking over their shoulder.”</p>
<p>Mr. Owens said he was not interested in that. “I want people to have the truth,” he said.</p>
<p>Mr. Owens, who will become the start-up’s executive producer on March 1 when his noncompete agreement with CBS ends, is joining the company shortly after it raised $12 million from a group of funders led by Emerson Collective, the company founded by the investor and philanthropist Laurene Powell Jobs and the owner of media companies like The Atlantic. The money will be used to produce new shows and explore expansions into newsletters and podcasting, said Iz Harris, chief executive of Newpress.</p>
<p>“The way that we’re going to get there is by doubling down on what we do best,” Ms. Harris said. “Right now, that’s creating shows that are in a creator’s voice and vision that help people understand the broad context of how the world works.”</p>
<p>Over the past decade, the traditional news industry has been disrupted by news creators — journalists who create email newsletters, YouTube videos or podcasts on their own. Newpress takes a hybrid approach, giving news creators some of the research, fact-checking, production and business support offered to journalists in traditional newsrooms. Founded in 2024 by Ms. Harris and her husband, the journalist Johnny Harris, the company recruits journalists who make compelling visual stories and shares the profits with them.</p>
