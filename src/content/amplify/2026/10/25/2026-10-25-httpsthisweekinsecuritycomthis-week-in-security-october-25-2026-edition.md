<p><a href="https://thephp.foundation/blog/2026/10/21/todays-php-security-release-and-how-were-strengthening-php-security/"><strong><u>Global websites pulled down as PHP bugs threaten to set the internet on fire</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>The PHP Foundation:</strong> Well… you know it's going to be a rough one when <em>PHP </em>makes mainstream news and the family group chats. Where do we start? On Wednesday, the internet almost burned down thanks to two bugs, dubbed PHP2Shell, which allows unauthenticated remote code execution (such as planting malware) on around 70% of the world's internet-facing servers. <em>Not great! </em>Researchers at <a href="https://calif.io/php2shell/"><u>Calif</u></a> found the bugs, then OpenAI and Wiz validated them, and alerted The PHP Foundation, which began coordinated disclosure to get patches to many of the web's biggest players ahead of Wednesday… when the embargo lifted and the rest of the world <a href="https://thephp.foundation/blog/2026/10/21/todays-php-security-release-and-how-were-strengthening-php-security/"><u>found out</u></a>. Governments <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/finland-takes-down-websites-as-precaution-due-to-vulnerabilities"><u>shut down</u></a> websites, as did big corps to block attacks. <a href="https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/amazon-threat-intelligence-identifies-php2shell-exploitation-prior-to-public-disclosure-in-madpot/"><u>Amazon</u></a> said there was <em>some </em>detected exploitation a week before disclosure and <a href="https://x.com/eastdakota/status/2113079352682570051"><u>Cloudflare</u></a> says exploitation attempts are now spiking that patches are out. <em>Thankfully</em>, there was no initial wave of mass exploitation in part because the bugs are a pain to actually exploit — <em>but</em> <a href="https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/cve-2026-109618-cve-2026-109619/"><u>Palo Alto Networks</u></a> and <a href="https://x.com/dguido/status/2113825891021144306"><u>Trail of Bits</u></a> said Friday they have or have seen working exploits, so it's a matter of time before big hacks happen against unpatched systems. The reality is that PHP is just one of the many, <em>many </em>legacy technologies on the web today from yesteryear waiting to blow up now that AI is <a href="https://www.404media.co/php2shell-black-october-ai-is-changing-everything-about-hacking-no-one-knows-what-happens-next/"><u>undoubtedly</u></a> making vulnerability research and discovery easier. Never a better time for big tech to <a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/vulnerability-apocalypse-and-why-we-should-fund-open-source"><u>fund open-source projects</u></a>, eh!<br/>

<p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/disgraced-emudhra-drops-out-of-tls-business-following-certificate-debacle/"><strong><u>eMudhra shuts down certificate issuing unit after hack exposes weak private keys</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>Ars Technica: </strong>More<em> majorly</em> bad cyber news after eMudhra, an Indian-based intermediate certificate authority trusted to issue HTTPS certificates, was hacked and its private keys leaked. This hack now allows hackers to impersonate pretty much <em>any</em> HTTPS-enabled website as if they were the rightful owners. The major browser makers almost <a href="https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mozilla-google-apple-block-compromised-emudhra-ca-certificates/"><u>immediately blocked</u></a> eMudhra's compromised certificates, preventing attackers from launching attacks on the visible web, but that still leaves developer tools, libraries, and other software that <a href="https://abyssdomain.expert/@filippo/117478838791731289"><u><em>haven't </em>yet revoked trust</u></a> in eMudhra still able to be spoofed. Worse of all, one of the private keys was <a href="https://scotthelme.co.uk/eight-bits-of-trust-emudhras-ca-keys-cracked-in-milliseconds/"><u>simply "4"</u></a> — nothing more, just "4" — meaning these certificates can be easily brute-forced. You can check your exposure on <a href="https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/"><u>this web-based certificate trust checker</u></a>. The hack prompted eMudhra to <a href="https://emudhra.com/en/press-release/emudhra-to-exit-global-ssl-tls-certificate-business"><u>exit the HTTPS certificate-issuing</u></a> business altogether… which, let's be honest, was probably for the best. <br/>

<p><a href="https://www.csoonline.com/article/4239028/china-linked-hackers-used-agentic-ai-in-attack-on-2250-microsoft-365-tenants.html"><strong><u>ConnectWise breach allowed Chinese hackers to spy on thousands of organizations</u></strong></a><br/>
<strong>CSO Online: </strong>IT and remote access provider ConnectWise had its cloud backup application breached, allowing hackers to steal credentials used for connecting to thousands of organizations that rely on ConnectWise to back up their corporate data hosted on Microsoft 365. ConnectWise <a href="https://www.connectwise.com/company/trust/advisories"><u>shut down</u></a> its backup system to contain the incident but is now working on restoring it. Microsoft, which has visibility into the incident (by nature of having its customers breached), <a href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/21/storm-0940-uses-artex-agentic-ai-connectwise-supply-chain-compromise/"><u>said</u></a> at least 2,200 tenants were compromised at a rate of about 3,000 attempts per minute — strongly suggesting that AI was being used to rapidly target affected organizations. The tech giant also blamed a China-aligned actor for the hacks, strongly suggesting an espionage motive. <br/>
