<p>These are just a handful of the data center projects that have been completed since the end of 2022, when the release of ChatGPT supercharged the race to build bigger and better artificial intelligence models.</p>
<p>According to <a href="https://cleanview.co/">Cleanview</a>, an energy data company, 180 new data center buildings were completed last year, a 170 percent jump from 2021. Those new projects were spread across 22 states, including almost 50 new buildings in Virginia alone. (Cleanview considers a data center to be any building dedicated to computing or holding servers.)</p>
<p>It’s a surge of construction that can be best seen from the sky, using aerial imagery.</p>

<p>So far in 2026, another 88 data center buildings have been completed, with many more on the way.</p>
<p>The annual investment in A.I. construction could reach over 3 percent of U.S. gross domestic product from 2025 to 2032 — a bigger annual share than what the country spent on building its railroads, electric grid and highways, according to estimates from a <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/4c_Van-Nieuwerburgh.pdf">working paper</a> by Stijn van Nieuwerburgh, a Columbia Business School economist.</p>
<p>A big reason for all these new data centers: Researchers have found that increasing computational power for training these systems makes them <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/07/29/technology/ai-chips-data-center-boom.html">more capable</a>, even without changing the underlying training data or methodology.</p>
<p class="rw-outer-content"><span>Some content could not be imported from the original document.</span> <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/10/05/upshot/data-center-scale.html">View content ↗ </a></p>
<p>In the race for more of that “compute,” tech giants, energy companies and data center developers are holding nothing back.</p>
<p>Facilities previously used to mine cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin are transitioning to <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c07ler8x9kpo">A.I</a>. Meta is resorting to putting data centers in large tents to help <a href="https://newsletter.cleanview.co/p/metas-data-center-strategy-and-the">decrease construction times</a>.</p>
<p>Cleanview’s chief executive, Michael Thomas, refers to this as the “Mad Max” phase of the A.I. boom: “Everyone is just gobbling up any power they can, any generation equipment, any transformers, and building as many data centers as they can.”</p>
<p>It’s not just the rate of construction that’s increasing — the data centers themselves are getting bigger, too.</p>
<p class="rw-outer-content"><span>Some content could not be imported from the original document.</span> <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/10/05/upshot/data-center-scale.html">View content ↗ </a></p>
<p>The Stratos Project, developed by the celebrity entrepreneur Kevin O’Leary, is primed to be one of the world’s largest data centers, set on nearly 20,000 acres about 60 miles north of Salt Lake City. But like other data center projects, <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/14/us/elections/kevin-oleary-utah-data-center.html">it has faced recent headwinds</a>: The acreage has <a href="https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2026/06/04/oleary-agrees-to-reduce-stratos-data-center-size/">already been halved</a> after public and political outcry, and some Utah residents are still seeking a way to halt it.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/14/us/elections/kevin-oleary-utah-data-center.html">Mr. O’Leary has said</a> the project would provide thousands of jobs and billions of dollars of investment to the state.</p>
<p>Most of the Stratos land won’t be devoted to the actual computing halls. As is true with many large-scale projects, the land is also meant to create a buffer between data center operations and surrounding communities, amid complaints about noise, resource use and environmental effects. Most of the Stratos land will be used for “open space, allowing for wildlife corridors, continued grazing and significant distance from the Great Salt Lake,” according to the <a href="https://governor.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/FAQ-on-Stratos-Project.pdf">Utah governor’s office</a>.</p>
<p>Large plots of data center land also need to be devoted to something else: energy.</p>
<p>Training new models takes immense amounts of electricity for the computing resources that fill most data center halls. After years of climbing only gradually, the electrical capacity of the latest data centers has soared.</p>
<p class="rw-outer-content"><span>Some content could not be imported from the original document.</span> <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/10/05/upshot/data-center-scale.html">View content ↗ </a></p>
<p>According to Epoch A.I., the total global A.I. computing power driven by data centers <a href="https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-chip-production">has been doubling</a> every seven months since 2022.</p>
<p>Many newer large data centers are “behind the meter,” meaning they generate power completely off the grid. Some new power plants devoted to data centers are expected to become the <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/31/climate/pennsylvanias-ai-gold-rush-meets-second-thoughts.html">largest in the country</a>.</p>
<p>Data centers focused on A.I. also generate much more heat than traditional data centers because of how densely packed the computing resources are, said Ben Cottier, senior researcher at Epoch A.I., who leads its A.I. Data Centers project.</p>
<p>“Pretty much all of the energy that goes in, has to go out,” he said. That means data centers require large cooling systems that use air or <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/01/27/technology/microsoft-water-ai-data-centers.html">water</a>.</p>

<p>As the pace of data center construction has grown, so has public opposition. Residents cite noise and <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/08/climate/amazon-data-center-texas-pollution.html">emissions</a>, with particular concerns about local air quality and rising carbon levels that contribute to global warming. Others worry about water usage and a rise in <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/05/climate/data-centers-pollution-trump-ai-energy.html">energy</a> needs that could affect electricity prices, although <a href="https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/data-centers-lowered-electric-bills-in-some-places-for-now/">evidence</a> is <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/ai-data-centers-and-water/">mixed</a> on these effects on a larger scale.</p>
<p>Aaron McLear, an OpenAI spokesman, said the company was dedicated to “paying our own way on energy so our operations don’t increase residents’ electricity prices, minimizing water use, and investing in local jobs and community priorities.” He also noted various tax incentives and grants for residents of Abilene, the home of Stargate.</p>
<p>In a statement, Francis Brennan, a spokesman for Meta, said the company was “committed to Richland Parish,” where its new Hyperion campus is being developed, and was covering the cost of new energy and water infrastructure.</p>
<p>For the communities that welcome data centers, the financial benefits can be substantial. In Virginia’s Loudoun County, home to what’s known as “data center alley,” a tax on data center equipment is expected to generate over <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/19/technology/data-centers-backlash-loudoun-virginia.html">$1 billion in local tax revenue</a>.</p>
<p>But in a New York Times/Siena University poll in September, <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/09/15/polls/times-siena-poll-likely-voter-crosstabs.html">more than 60 percent</a> of respondents opposed data center development to support A.I. in the United States.</p>
<p>This resistance has set off a <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/23/us/politics/data-centers-midterm-elections.html">messaging scramble</a> among Republican and Democratic politicians ahead of the midterm elections. In contrast, President Trump has voiced <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/31/us/politics/trump-data-centers.html">unalloyed support</a> for construction.</p>
<p>(The Times has <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/12/27/business/media/new-york-times-open-ai-microsoft-lawsuit.html">sued OpenAI and Microsoft</a>, claiming copyright infringement of news content related to A.I. systems. The two companies have denied those claims.)</p>
<p>The business of construction has also come under stress. Investors are expressing some doubt in <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/21/business/ai-data-center-ipos.html">data center construction companies</a>. A shortage of equipment has caused <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-01/us-ai-data-center-expansion-relies-on-chinese-electrical-equipment-imports">construction delays</a>. And several states are considering moratoriums on new construction. In <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/14/nyregion/new-york-data-center-moratorium-hochul.html">New York</a>, Gov. Kathy Hochul has already signed a yearlong permitting pause.</p>
<p>But Mr. Thomas, of Cleanview, said he had seen no evidence of a construction slowdown, and the data indicates the opposite may be occurring.</p>
