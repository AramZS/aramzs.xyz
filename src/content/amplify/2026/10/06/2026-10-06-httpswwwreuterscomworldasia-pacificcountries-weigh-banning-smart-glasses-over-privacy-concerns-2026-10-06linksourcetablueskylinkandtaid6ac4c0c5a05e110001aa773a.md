<p>Oct 6 (Reuters) - Governments, courts and companies are considering restrictions and examining existing rules on smart glasses amid worries that the technology, brought to mainstream by ​Meta's <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/META.O">(META.O)</a> Ray-Bans, could be used to photograph, film or audio-record confidential ‌materials or people without their knowledge.</p>
<p>Here is a summary of what countries and regions are doing to protect people's privacy, in alphabetical order.</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">AUSTRALIA</h2>
<p>The federal government said in September ​it was <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/australia-considers-banning-use-smart-glasses-government-buildings-2026-09-17/">considering barring</a> the use of camera-equipped smart glasses in government ​workplaces due to privacy and security concerns.</p>
<p>Public Service Minister Katy Gallagher ⁠asked the country's Public Service Commission for advice on whether their use ​should be prohibited and what exemptions might be required.</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">BRITAIN</h2>
<p>Courts in England and Wales ​have prohibited the use of Meta smart glasses because of restrictions on taking images and videos in courts.</p>
<p>British cinema operators are also <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/uk-cinemas-restricting-meta-ai-other-smart-glasses-over-piracy-concerns-2026-08-20/">introducing policies</a> to prohibit or restrict camera-enabled smart ​glasses, the UK Cinema Association said in August.</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">EUROPEAN UNION</h2>
<p>Smart glasses have attracted ​scrutiny from European privacy regulators and some lawmakers, and the European Data Protection Board has ‌commissioned ⁠a report into their "social acceptability".</p>
<p>Member of the European Parliament Veronika Cifrová Ostrihoňová also urged the European Commission, which has not proposed legislation specifically targeting smart glasses, to consider possible action.</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">NORWAY</h2>
<p>The government said it planned <a data-testid="Link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/norway-propose-temporary-ban-ai-glasses-some-public-places-2026-10-05/">to propose a temporary ​ban</a> on the use ​of AI glasses ⁠in places where the public regularly gather, such as parks, beaches, museums, shopping centres and public events.</p>
<p>Norway's capital Oslo ​banned smart glasses in schools in August.</p>
<h2 data-testid="Heading">UNITED STATES</h2>
<p>New York's ​Unified Court ⁠System banned smart glasses from all its court facilities from July 20. The rule covers prescription and non-prescription eyewear or headwear containing cameras, microphones, computers or ⁠other ​technology capable of recording video or audio. Visitors ​carrying them must hand them over to court personnel for safekeeping before entering.</p>
