<html><body><div>Donald Trump’s attacks on the First Amendment</div><figure><img alt="a collage of Trump and news organizations" src="https://cdn.theatlantic.com/thumbor/SWxykATbbs06iTBxs86a797taMU=/0x0:2000x1125/960x540/media/img/mt/2026/09/TrumpCNN/original.png"/><figcaption> (Illustration by Paul Spella / The Atlantic*)</figcaption></figure><p>On the first day of his second term in the White House, President Trump <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/restoring-freedom-of-speech-and-ending-federal-censorship/">signed an executive order</a> on free speech stating that his administration would ensure that “no Federal Government officer, employee, or agent engages in or facilitates any conduct that would unconstitutionally abridge the free speech of any American citizen.” He has gone on to violate that order many times.</p><p>Trump <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/18/business/media/trump-cnn-ms-now-politico-white-house-ban.html">announced today</a> on his social-media network, Truth Social, that he was banning CNN, MS NOW, and <em>Politico</em> from the White House in retaliation for their reporting, and complained that “outlets shouldn’t be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they’re covering the President of the United States, the Trump Administration, or the United States of America.” In other words, if media outlets publish stories that Trump does not like, he will punish them.</p><p>This is far from the first time Trump has tried to pressure the media into giving him favorable coverage or punish those who run stories he does not like. As <em>The New York Times</em> <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/12/us/politics/trump-free-speech.html">has documented</a>, Trump’s Federal Communications Commission has repeatedly tried to intimidate networks over airing material that Trump finds offensive, including <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/09/this-wont-stop-with-jimmy-kimmel/684251/">criticism of Trump</a> and <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/fcc-chairman-brendan-carr-accuses-163016321.html">interviews</a> with Democratic politicians, by threatening to <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://rollcall.com/2026/09/16/push-to-restrict-jawboning-advanced-by-senate-commerce-panel/">revoke their broadcast license</a>. Three journalists fired from <em>Stars and Stripes</em>, an independent media outlet that is federally funded, are suing the Pentagon, accusing it of silencing them because of their factual coverage of <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/27/us/politics/stars-and-stripes-fired-journalists-lawsuit-pentagon.html">deplorable conditions for U.S. service members</a>. The Pentagon also sought to force media organizations to sign a self-censorship pledge in <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/why-news-organizations-are-rejecting-the-pentagons-new-press-rules">exchange for press credentials</a>. His Federal Trade Commission is investigating the <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/05/22/technology/ftc-investigates-media-matters.html">liberal media watchdog Media Matters</a>, whose work mostly consists of just publishing the things conservatives say, and NewsGuard, <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.newsguardtech.com/ratings/rating-process-criteria/">a nonpartisan outlet</a> that provides “scores for news and information websites based on nine journalistic criteria.”Trump sought to ban the Associated Press for refusing to identify the <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.npr.org/2025/04/08/nx-s1-5342369/ap-white-house-court-ruling-oval-office-gulf-of-mexico-america">Gulf of Mexico as the “Gulf of America,”</a> as Trump had ordered. This is not even close to an exhaustive list.</p><p>During an exchange with the White House today, a reporter described China as “one of the most repressive countries for journalists in the world.” Trump <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3mvsypf4bsz23">interrupted to respond</a>, “Well, I don’t know about that. I think we’re repressive also.” When he’s right, he’s right. (He went on to suggest that the “fake news” was the one doing the repressing in America.)</p><p>Trump’s campaign of censorship is not limited to journalists, however, and the First Amendment does not belong only to the media. Trump’s Justice Department has sought to pressure American <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.aaup.org/issues-higher-education/political-attacks-higher-education">colleges and universities</a> over what they are allowed to teach, whom they are allowed to hire, and whom they are allowed to admit. It has <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/05/trump-defund-schools-research-republicans/682742/">used federal funding</a> to censor technological, medical, and scientific research. The administration has singled out left-wing organizations <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trumps-war-left-inside-plan-investigate-liberal-groups-2025-10-09/">for scrutiny</a> and used federal resources to investigate them for opposing the president. It has sought to deport immigrants because of their <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/03/mahmoud-khalil-ice-detention/682001/">pro-Palestinian views</a>.</p><p>In a <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/09/30/judge-young-ruling-trump-deportation-free-speech-00588114">2025 decision</a>, William Young, a federal judge and President Ronald Reagan appointee, called those deportations a violation of the First Amendment, writing that Trump’s “palpable misunderstanding that the government simply cannot seek retribution for speech he disdains poses a great threat to Americans’ freedom of speech.”</p><p>Courts have tried to correct that misunderstanding: <a data-event-element="inline link" href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-vowed-bring-free-speech-back-judges-75-cases-ruled-that-he-has-stifled-it-2026-08-04/">Reuters</a> has counted at least 75 federal rulings that found the Trump administration had “infringed on First-Amendment rights.” But Trump keeps on doing it.</p><p>The president doesn’t see any disconnect between his big talk on free speech and his attempt to censor or punish the media and his political opposition—and neither, apparently, do many in the Republican Party. They simply hold their own definition of <em>free speech</em>: Conservatives can say what they want. Anything else Americans want to say, they say at their own risk.</p><hr/><p><em><small>*Source Images: Samuel Corum / Bloomberg / Getty; Kevin Dietsch / Getty; Al Drago / Bloomberg / Getty; Avishek Das / SOPA Images / LightRocket / Getty; Win McNamee / Getty.</small></em></p></body></html>
