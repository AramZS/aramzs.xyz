<p>At Madison Square Garden in New York City on November 9, 2017, at the G-Shock 35th Anniversary Celebration, G-Shock creator <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/casio-mr-g-watch/">Kikuo Ibe</a> showed off three <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://www.g-central.com/kikuo-ibes-speech-at-g-shock-35th-anniversary-in-nyc/"}' data-offer-url="https://www.g-central.com/kikuo-ibes-speech-at-g-shock-35th-anniversary-in-nyc/" href="https://www.g-central.com/kikuo-ibes-speech-at-g-shock-35th-anniversary-in-nyc/">sapphire-crystal prototype</a> watches: one clear, one ruby, and one sapphire blue. Because machining sapphire was so difficult at the time, each watch took over a month to produce.</p>
<p>These were built with, for the time, high-end Casio features such as <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/solar/"}' data-offer-url="https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/solar/" href="https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/solar/">Tough Solar</a> power and <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/radio/"}' data-offer-url="https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/radio/" href="https://gshock.casio.com/uk/technology/radio/">Multiband 6</a> radio-controlled timekeeping—but as they didn’t meet the G-Shock shock-resistance standards, the trio was never intended for sale. They were primarily a symbol of innovation for the Japanese brand, which started in 1983 specializing in super-tough performance <a href="https://www.wired.com/tag/watches/">timepieces</a>.</p>
<p>Now, nine years later, in what Casio is calling the culmination of an “eight-year project” (so presumably the ball started rolling soon after the excited reception Ibe’s crystal concepts got on stage at <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/madison-square-garden-celebrity-database-surveillance/">MSG</a>), G-Shock is launching a three-watch collection using blue sapphire as an expression of what happens when G-Shock toughness meets Japanese engineering. The flagship model, and by far the most expensive, is the $80,000 MRG-D5000, the first-ever retail G-Shock with an outer case and bezel hewn entirely from blue sapphire crystal.</p>
<p>The collection is called the “Blue Rose Project” because the <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_rose"}' data-offer-url="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_rose" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_rose">blue rose</a> is viewed as a symbol of making the impossible possible since it doesn’t exist in nature. It sees the super-expensive MRG-D5000 released alongside the $9,500 MRG-B5000SA-2, and the GMW-B5000SB-1A for $850.</p>
<p>Only 20 of the $80,000 MRG-D5000 full-sapphire G-Shocks will be made, putting these rugged “luxury” timepieces somewhat implausibly in the same price bracket as a <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/patek-philippes-aquanaut-travel-time-is-packed-with-innovation/">Patek Philippe</a> or <a href="https://www.wired.com/review/review-porsche-macan-electric-2024/">Porsche Macan</a>. This is quite the flex from a company that also sells its watches for <a href="https://www.wired.com/gallery/best-casio-watches/">less than $90</a>.</p>
<p>The 2017 concept watches required metal bracing to hold the crystal sections together, which is partly why they couldn’t pass G-Shock’s rigorous toughness tests. The new MRG-D5000, however, is a fully functional G-Shock, taking construction lessons from the MR-G engineering team who found that dividing the outer case into multiple interacting components could disperse impact more effectively than a conventional single-piece construction.</p>
<p>G-Shock fans will have already seen this technique employed in the <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://gshock.casio.com/intl/products/mr-g/mrg-b5000/"}' data-offer-url="https://gshock.casio.com/intl/products/mr-g/mrg-b5000/" href="https://gshock.casio.com/intl/products/mr-g/mrg-b5000/">titanium MRG-B5000</a> from 2022, but this “Multi-Guard Structure” has now been reengineered specifically for sapphire crystal, with Casio claiming the construction takes inspiration from traditional Japanese timber-frame techniques, using interlocking pieces rather than placing stress around screw holes or other details that could result in cracking.</p>
<p>While the MRG-D5000’s bezel and outer case are entirely machined from blue sapphire crystal ingots grown over several months, sadly—unlike the 2017 concepts—the strap isn’t matching sapphire but instead a rubber strap that repeats the brick pattern seen on the watch face in raised detail. To round off the luxe makeover, the MRG-D5000’s MR-G mark, “Shock Resist,” and other lettering on the face are in 24-carat gold.</p>
<p>The G-Shock development team responsible for the new collection tells WIRED the fundamental challenge was building a “near-unbreakable” watch using an inherently brittle material. Making watch <a data-event-click='{"element":"ExternalLink","outgoingURL":"https://oracleoftime.com/best-sapphire-cased-watches/"}' data-offer-url="https://oracleoftime.com/best-sapphire-cased-watches/" href="https://oracleoftime.com/best-sapphire-cased-watches/">cases from sapphire is not new</a> in the high-end watch world, and many distinguished makers, including <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/how-richard-mille-takes-quartz-watches-to-a-surprising-level/">Richard Mille</a> and <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/hublot-big-bang-unico-nespresso-origin-watch/">Hublot,</a> have released pieces made of the crystal.</p>
<p>To make the sapphire case work for a G-Shock, however, Casio had to develop a brand-new, dedicated shock-resistant structure, with the Multi-Guard construction using virtually invisible seams, alongside a completely screwless assembly method to hit the required shock performance. To do this, the outer case assembly required the development of completely new, dedicated jigs and fixtures, a process apparently so demanding that only one person at Yamagata Casio (the primary “mother factory” for Casio) can currently perform it.</p>
<p>All three new watches, along with the proper G-Shock resistance to knocks and drops, are water-resistant to 200 meters, solar-powered, and feature radio-controlled timekeeping, world time, stopwatch, countdown timer, five daily alarms, and a full-auto LED backlight.</p>
<p>If the $80K G-Shock is too steep an investment, the MRG-B5000SA-2 with two two-carat facet-cut blue sapphires and a grade-5 titanium exterior is limited to 500 pieces. The cheapest GMW-B5000SB-1A2 marries a full-metal black exterior with four round brilliant-cut blue sapphires.</p>
