<p>"I barely want to do this."</p>
<p>It was a message plastered on election signs across Kelowna that made headlines internationally.</p>
<p>Now, despite his (satirical) misgivings, 33-year-old Brady Rogers is going to have to "do this" — "this" being serve on Kelowna city council.</p>
<p>"It's been strange to have people recognize me — it's very new to me, but overwhelmingly positive," Rogers told CBC's <em>Radio West</em> on Monday.</p>
<p>Rogers earned 12,635 votes in Saturday's municipal election, making him the fourth-most popular candidate.</p>
<p>That followed a campaign built largely on self-deprecating humour, with other campaign slogans including, "How hard could it be," "Just trying to pay my rent," and "Let's see what happens."</p>

<p>Rogers, a small business owner and an engineer by trade, said until recently he would have considered the idea of running for council "laughable."</p>
<p>But after giving it thought when someone pitched him the idea, he concluded he "unfortunately" had many of the right skills.</p>
<p>From there, he said, if he was going to run it would have to come from a place of authenticity and honesty — hence the campaign slogan, "Fine, I'll do it."</p>

<p>While Rogers' campaign harnessed comedy, his platform was built on serious policy ideas targeting housing, transit, taxation and homelessness.</p>
<p>"I think for the most part people saw the tongue-in-cheek humour, and once they dug into my platform they recognized I do actually have some fairly sophisticated ideas," he said.</p>
<p>"I think that those [younger] voices who are actually experiencing the economy as it is now are important voices to have on council," he added.</p>
<h2>'Serious fun' </h2>
<p>David Black, an associate professor at the School of Communication and Culture at Royal Roads University, said Rogers hit on a winning mix of satire and serious policy.</p>
<p>He said the fun sign and social media campaign was well integrated with an in-depth website that laid out real ideas.</p>
<p>"It's serious fun. It's sincere satire," he said.</p>
<p>"It's not the Rhinoceros Party ... it's not just satire for its own sake or just to mock government. It is satire with policy attached."</p>
<p>In a crowded field of 37 council candidates, Black said Rogers was able to stand out by stepping away from the traditional approach to politics built on gravity and conflict.</p>
<p>The lighter approach, he said, resulted in a message and style aligned with today's digital culture, while tapping into public distrust of career politicians "that seem to be born from the lab."</p>
<p>Black added that Rogers leaned into his personality to sell his message.</p>
<p>"We like people to come to power — even a council seat in Kelowna, not king of the world — reluctantly," the professor said.</p>

<p>Rogers, for his part, says he feels a "sense of duty" to bring a fresh perspective to council where he hopes to do real work to improve the city of around 167,000.</p>
<p>That will start next week with a crash course in the actual day-to-day work of a city councillor.</p>
<p>"They'll presumably tell me how to be a councillor," he said. "But until then, it's just me taking it day by day."</p>
