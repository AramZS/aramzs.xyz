<html><body><figure><img alt="" src="https://cloudfront-us-east-1.images.arcpublishing.com/wapo/KWSFTME6NTW7IFFIOQRI4RNP3U.jpg"/><figcaption>Emergency responders work at the collision scene in Urda, Spain, on Wednesday. (Juanma Jiménez/Europa Press/AP)</figcaption></figure><p>Two trains collided on a high-speed railway in central Spain, killing more than 60 people and injuring dozens more,<b> </b>authorities said Wednesday. </p><p>Emergency services in Castilla-La Mancha said <a href="https://x.com/112clm/status/2115430861902369170" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://x.com/112clm/status/2115430861902369170">on social media</a> that the death toll had risen to 64, while at least 58 others were injured.</p><p>The collision between the trains — one operated by Spain’s public rail operator, Renfe, and the other by private rail company Iryo — took place early Wednesday near Mora, Orgaz and Urda,<b> </b>the country’s Railway Infrastructure Administrator (Adif) said. All trains on the route had been stopped, it added.<b> </b></p><p>Esther Padilla, spokeswoman for the Castilla-La Mancha regional government, told public broadcaster <a href="https://www.rtve.es/play/videos/la-hora-de-la-1/gobierno-castilla-mancha-numero-fallecidos-heridos-va-ir-aumentando/17286826/" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://www.rtve.es/play/videos/la-hora-de-la-1/gobierno-castilla-mancha-numero-fallecidos-heridos-va-ir-aumentando/17286826/">RTVE</a> that one of the trains appeared to have stopped on the line between Madrid and Seville before the collision knocked some carriages off the track.</p><p>Citing Adif, she said the Iryo train was carrying 146 passengers and a baby, while 397 passengers were on board the other. </p><p>The incident is the second major train accident in Spain this year: In January, <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/01/19/spain-high-speed-train-derail-crash/" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/01/19/spain-high-speed-train-derail-crash/">more than 40 people were killed</a> in a collision involving high-speed trains from the same two companies.</p><p>Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez <a href="https://x.com/sanchezcastejon/status/2115373443998765147" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://x.com/sanchezcastejon/status/2115373443998765147">wrote on X</a> that he was “shocked” by Wednesday’s accident. “My affection, solidarity and support to all the victims and their families,” he added.</p><p>Sánchez will travel to the area “to support the work of the emergency teams and to convey his support to those affected,” the government said.</p><p>A passenger on the train that was<b> </b>traveling from Madrid to Seville <a href="https://www.elmundo.es/espana/2026/10/28/6ae1c511fdddffeb558b4572.html?utm_medium=Social&amp;utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1793181124" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank" title="https://www.elmundo.es/espana/2026/10/28/6ae1c511fdddffeb558b4572.html?utm_medium=Social&amp;utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1793181124">told El Mundo</a> that the scene was “apocalyptic.” </p><p>“I find it very hard to believe that there are any living people. It’s as if someone planted a bomb,” Sebastián Herrero told the newspaper.</p><article class="rw-embedded-tweet" data-rw-tweet-id="2115394745878917198">
