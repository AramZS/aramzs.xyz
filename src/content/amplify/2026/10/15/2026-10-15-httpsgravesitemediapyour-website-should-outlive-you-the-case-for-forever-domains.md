<p>HellFireside Chat Episode 4: Digital Death</p>
<p>Your website can vanish after one failed credit card payment. In the latest episode of HellFireside Chat, Mike De Socio and Ernie Smith explain how to plan your digital afterlife.</p>
<p>For much of the 1980s and 1990s, Jon Postel, a computer scientist at the University of Southern California, managed the internet’s top-level domains — .com, .net, and .org — along with more than 200 two-letter country codes like .uk and .jp, largely by himself. At first, it was a fitting — albeit hefty — undertaking: Postel had, after all, helped create the internet’s direct predecessor, ARPANET.</p>
<p>But as the dot-com boom dawned in the late 1990s and websites proliferated, this arrangement quickly became untenable, and <a href="https://commdocs.house.gov/committees/science/hsy268140.000/hsy268140_0.HTM">Postel pushed</a> for a “fully self-regulated system” supported by the U.S. government. With his help, the nonprofit <a href="https://www.icann.org/">Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers</a> (ICANN) was incorporated in September 1998. The next month, Postel, who was expected to be ICANN’s chief technology officer, died of complications from heart surgery. ICANN moved forward without him.</p>
<p>These days, many longtime bloggers, creators, and individual website owners feel the tension embodied in <a href="https://icannwiki.org/Jon_Postel">Postel</a>’s sudden death and cut-short legacy: You could be gone in an instant, and without a plan, your website — which perhaps houses your creative output, career portfolio, or other vestige of your life and life’s work — could disappear from the public square.</p>
<p>Ironically, the system Postel helped build — a system that today manages <a href="https://www.dnib.com/articles/the-domain-name-industry-brief-q2-2026">an estimated 401.6 million</a> domains — makes this kind of succession management difficult. That’s because a domain is purchased through a subscription, with a seller like GoDaddy or Namecheap allowing the buyer to register it in their name for up to 10 years at a time.</p>
