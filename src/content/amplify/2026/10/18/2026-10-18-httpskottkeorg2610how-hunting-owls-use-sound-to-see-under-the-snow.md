<h2> <a href="https://kottke.org/26/10/how-hunting-owls-use-sound-to-see-under-the-snow"> How Hunting Owls Use Sound to “See” Under the Snow</a></h2>
<p>The great grey owl basically has a sound cone for a face, which helps it <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ykcs1U0lNtE">hunt down</a> voles burrowing through snow in the winter.</p>
<blockquote><p>The owl’s large facial ruff, formed of dense feathers, helps amplify sound and funnel it towards his ears. Through half a meter of snow, the sound of scampering feet is inaudible to us. But not to him. With the help of a special acoustic camera equipped with 60 highly sensitive microphones, we can see what the owl is hearing.</p></blockquote>
<p>The footage in this video is amazing — and so is the owl’s sense of hearing. A YouTube commenter says “Cat software in Bird hardware. Bad news for the Rodents.” You can <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kXucblbKmFA">watch a short video</a> on the science behind owl hunting techniques and <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYjVVBFgl3c">watch the whole episode</a> this is from about how animals use sound (but only in Canada, it seems…VPN works fine tho).</p>
<p>The only problem with this video is that now I want two hours of just closeup footage of the owl’s face and it flying directly towards the camera. Those eyes! And its mid-flight loaf-of-white-bread-shaped body! I would also like the owl to be my friend.</p>
<p>BTW, this clip is from a show called <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nature_of_Things">The Nature of Things</a>, which is a CBC nature series that’s been going since 1960. A good thing to have on your radar if you’re looking for <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@natureofthings/videos">well-produced science &amp; nature programs</a>.</p>
