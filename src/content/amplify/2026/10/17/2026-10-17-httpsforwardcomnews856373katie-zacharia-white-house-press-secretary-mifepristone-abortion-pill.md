<p>Katie Zacharia’s five-day stint as White House Press Secretary ended almost as soon as it began. But before she was fired on Friday, she managed to draw a rebuke from the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum.</p>
<p>The museum’s reprimand came in response to a claim Zacharia <a href="https://x.com/KatieZacharia/status/1992831533129118199">posted to X</a> in November 2025, asserting that the abortion pill is “connected to the manufacturer of the deadly gas Zyklon B, used in the World War II concentration camps.”</p>
<p>The supposed connection? A company descended from the conglomerate involved in producing Zyklon B later owned the company that developed the abortion pill, Zacharia intimated in her post.</p>
<p>The official Auschwitz Memorial X account <a href="https://x.com/auschwitzmuseum/status/2109225405613838432?s=46">rebuked</a> Zacharia on Monday, calling the argument “a misleading chain of guilt by association.”</p>
<p>Mifepristone, commonly known as the abortion pill, was first synthesized in 1980 — more than three decades after World War II’s end — by Georges Teutsch, a chemist at the French pharmaceutical company Roussel-Uclaf, based on an idea developed by Jewish endocrinologist <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/01/17/health/abortion-pill-inventor.html">Étienne-Émile Baulieu</a>.</p>
<p>The tenuous connection to the poison gas used in concentration camps goes something like this: Zyklon B was distributed by a company called Degesch, which was partly owned by the German conglomerate I.G. Farben. After World War II, I.G. Farben was broken up into successor companies, including Hoechst AG, which in 1997 acquired a majority stake in the French company that developed mifepristone.</p>
<p>“A corporate relationship between companies separated by decades is presented as if it proved continuity of products, purposes, or chemical composition,” the Auschwitz Memorial wrote on X. “That is not historical analysis, but the selective combination of unrelated facts to produce a predetermined conclusion.”</p>
<p>Zacharia offered another version of the claim in a June 2025 <a href="https://youtu.be/66fI20ZdKnA?t=2449">podcast episode</a> of <i>We the People with Gates Garcia</i>, saying that “the abortion pill has the same chemicals that they used in the exterminant (sic) camps in Nazi Germany.”</p>
<p>Suffice it to say, mifepristone, a steroidal progesterone blocker, is not the same as cyanide. Zacharia, the Auschwitz Memorial wrote, “confuses corporate history with chemical composition.”</p>
<p>After the overturning of <em>Roe v Wade</em> in 2022, anti-abortion activists have increasingly set their sights on banning the abortion pill nationwide. Prescribing, dispensing, or mailing mifepristone is <a href="https://usafacts.org/articles/which-states-ban-the-abortion-pill/">currently banned</a> in 13 states. In May, the Supreme Court <a href="https://www.npr.org/2026/05/14/nx-s1-5821591/mifepristone-supreme-court-louisiana-telehealth">blocked</a> a lower court ruling that would have effectively ended mail delivery and telemedicine prescribing of mifepristone nationwide, including in states where abortion is legal.</p>
<p>Zacharia has framed her opposition to abortion as deeply personal. Zacharia said that, when she met her biological mother at 18, she learned she was “approximately 20, 30 minutes away from being aborted.” A Christian counselor, Zacharia said, convinced her mother to place her for adoption instead.</p>
<p>On the podcast, Zacharia also spoke about her work on the board of the <a href="https://thewhiterose.life/about/">White Rose Resistance</a>, an anti-abortion advocacy group named for the Nazi resistance movement of the same name.</p>
<p>Groups including the <a href="https://holocaustremembrance.com/statements/ihra-holocaust-alabama-abortion-bill?">International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance</a> and the <a href="https://www.jta.org/2019/12/04/ideas/leave-the-holocaust-out-of-your-self-promotion-political-agenda-and-profit-seeking">Anti-Defamation League</a> have condemned comparisons between abortion and the Holocaust, arguing that they trivialize the Holocaust and exploit its victims’ memory for political ends.</p>
<p>Zacharia has also made Nazi comparisons in other contexts: According to <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/12/politics/kfile-katie-zacharia-deleted-tweets">CNN</a>, in September 2022, she reposted a Truth Social post comparing then-President Joe Biden and anti-fascist protesters to Nazis. It included the hashtags #Antifa, #PedoHitler and #WrongSideOfHistory.</p>
<p>The White House Press Office did not respond to the <i>Forward</i>’s request for comment Friday morning, before Zacharia’s firing had been announced.</p>
