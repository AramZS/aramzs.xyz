<h3><b>Poly Fighter – A Knockout For Solo Fighting Fans</b></h3>
<p>I have fond memories of 90s fighting games. I spent plenty of time enjoying series like <i>Mortal Kombat</i>, and I still have a soft spot for the days when you could pick up a fighter, enjoy its story mode and spend an afternoon beating up increasingly ridiculous opponents.</p>
<p>Over time, though, fighting games have become increasingly associated with competitive play, thanks to events like EVO. There’s nothing wrong with that, but it’s sometimes left me feeling that the genre has moved away from the casual fighting player. Enter <i>Poly Fighter</i>, a single-player roguelike fighting game that feels like a welcome return to those old arcade days. After spending some time with it, I’m already rather impressed.</p>

<h3><b>Fight, Win, Steal, Repeat</b></h3>
<p>The premise has you fight your way through a branching tournament, taking on opponents and working towards becoming the champion. What makes <i>Poly Fighter</i> stand out is how it handles progression. Every time you defeat an opponent, you can take one of their moves and add it to your own moveset. This means your fighter gradually develops throughout each run, allowing you to experiment with different combinations and create a fighting style that suits you.</p>
<p>Along the way, you can choose different paths, including opportunities to improve your health or upgrade your moves. There are also minigames, like smashing barrels similar to ones seen in the <i>Street Fighter</i> series. You’ll encounter different types of fights, too, including tougher elite opponents, rival battles and mirror matches where you face a version of yourself using your current moveset. It’s a neat idea, especially when you consider that you’ve been building that moveset yourself. Rather than simply making incremental stat improvements, you’re constantly changing how your fighter plays. It gives each run its own identity and encourages experimentation.</p>

<h3><b>Easy To Pick Up, Fun To Fight</b></h3>
<p>The combat has been one of my biggest highlights so far. It feels snappy and responsive, with a familiar, <i>Street Fighter</i>-inspired approach to blocking, punches, kicks and special moves. Importantly, <i>Poly Fighter</i> offers two control options. You can use traditional motion inputs if you enjoy the more technical side of fighting games, or opt for simplified controls that let you perform special moves with straightforward directional and button inputs. I like that flexibility. Sometimes you want to practise your inputs and get to grips with the mechanics; other times, you just want to jump in and start with no fuss. Both approaches have their place, and this game seems happy to let you choose. There are also different difficulty options, including a more approachable setting and tougher challenges for those who want to test themselves. You can train between matches, practise moves and experiment with your current build, which feels very much in keeping with the arcade fighting games I remember.</p>

<h3><b>Low Poly Feel</b></h3>
<p>Visually, <i>Poly Fighter</i> uses a low-poly art style that works surprisingly well. It gives the game a distinctive look while retaining the bold poses and readable animations you want from a fighting game. It brought to mind some of the more stylised modern entries in the genre, and the different fighters help keep encounters visually varied. I’m not sure the cast has quite the same instantly recognisable personalities as some of the genre’s biggest names, but that’s a difficult bar to clear. There’s still a good variety of unusual characters to discover, and I particularly like seeing previously encountered fighters hanging around in the backgrounds of different stages.</p>

<h3><b>Final Thoughts</b></h3>
<p>My time with <a href="https://store.steampowered.com/app/3977340/Poly_Fighter/"><i>Poly Fighter</i></a> has been a very positive first impression. It brings back the appeal of single-player fighting games while adding a clever roguelike progression system that encourages you to experiment with your moveset. My main question at this stage concerns balance, particularly during some of the tougher boss encounters. These fights can feel very meaty with excessively large health bars, so hopefully there’s room for further tuning as development continues. That aside, I’m genuinely excited to see where <i>Poly Fighter</i> goes next. It feels accessible without losing the satisfying feel of a proper fighting game, and its focus on solo play is something I’ve been wanting more of. This is still an early impression rather than a final verdict, but so far, <i>Poly Fighter</i> has reminded me why I fell in love with fighting games in the first place. I’m looking forward to getting back in the ring and seeing what other ridiculous combinations I can build. A free demo is available on Steam.</p>
