<p>Sunday may have been the best day for global democracy in several years. In Brazil and Serbia, pro-democracy coalitions pulled off surprise victories against formidable authoritarian forces.</p>
<p>In Serbia, a scrappy cross-ideological opposition movement made up almost exclusively of first-time candidates — including doctors, scientists, academics, and athletes — beat the odds and scored an absolute parliamentary majority against the longtime, autocratic governing party. The opposition, running under the banner of “Student List-Students Win” (or the “Student List”), won with over 50 percent of the votes cast and the highest recorded voter turnout in decades.</p>
<p>And in Brazil, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a surprise victory against the far-right candidate Flávio Bolsonaro. Flávio Bolsonaro sought to continue the political legacy of his father, Jair Bolsonaro, a <a href="https://www.ifyoucankeepit.org/p/were-not-the-only-country-going-through">Trump-like</a> figure who purged the civil service, weaponized institutions, and attempted a coup after he lost the 2022 election.</p>

<p>Bolsonaro appeared likely to win the presidency after he <a href="https://www.reuters.com/live/brazil-election-live-lula-bolsonaro-face-off-presidential-runoff-2026-10-25/">led the first round</a> of presidential elections earlier this month. Prediction markets held Lula’s odds Sunday at a <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/lula-brazil-election-prediction-markets.html">paltry 21 percent</a>. But instead, Lula won by a <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/25/world/americas/brazil-election-president-lula-bolsonaro.html">considerable margin</a> — over 4 million votes — pulling off the <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/live/2026/oct/25/brazil-election-runoff-live-updates-flavio-bolsonaro-lula">biggest electoral comeback</a> in modern Brazilian history.</p>
