<p>When Marie Gottschalk was a graduate teaching assistant, she swore that once she was in charge of her own classroom she would never give in-person exams. She wanted her students to practice careful writing and deep thinking, to develop nuanced arguments, to search books and articles for quotes that reflected the ideas they wanted to discuss. None of those things, she believed, lend themselves to a timed test.</p>
