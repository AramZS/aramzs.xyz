title: Abortion Bans Linked to 'Excess' Spike in Women's Homicides
---
<p>Before Jennifer Rothwell went missing in 2019, she Googled, “What to do if your husband is upset you are pregnant.” Three years later, just one month after <em>Dobbs</em>, a Missouri court <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/beau-rothwell-sentenced-life-in-prison-killing-pregnant-wife-jennifer-rothwell/">sentenced</a> Jennifer’s “upset” husband to life in prison for murdering her.</p>
<p><strong>The <a href="https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/homicide-leading-cause-of-death-for-pregnant-women-in-u-s/">leading cause of death</a> among pregnant people is homicide</strong>, often by an intimate partner. So it stands to reason that forcing women into pregnancy puts them at increased risk of violence. That common sense connection just became a bit clearer: a new study shows that since the end of <em>Roe v. Wade</em>, women’s risk for being murdered in states with abortion bans has increased.</p>
<p>The research, published in <em><a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2855391?widget=personalizedcontent&amp;previousarticle=0">JAMA</a></em>, found that the homicide risk for women and girls of reproductive age rose by 7% in 2023 and 15% in 2024 in banned states, compared with states without bans. According to the study by Liana Woskie at Tufts University and Maeve Wallace at the University of Arizona, that increase equates to <strong>135 deaths—48 excess deaths in 2023 and 87 in 2024.</strong></p>

<p>The study, which wields several different statistical models to measure changes in state homicide rates, found the increase in risk is particularly pronounced among women and girls aged 15 to 29, a group that may be especially vulnerable to the consequences of abortion restrictions.</p>
<p>The researchers say their study can’t prove that bans directly caused those deaths. But as <em>Medical Daily </em><a href="https://www.medicaldaily.com/abortion-ban-states-homicide-women-study-limits-480105">notes</a>, their findings are, if “not a settled conclusion,” then an important “warning signal worth investigating.”</p>
<p>“Abortion bans often contain exceptions when a pregnant patient’s life is at risk. But when we think about threats to life, we tend to focus narrowly on immediate medical emergencies,” Woskie told <em>Abortion, Every Day</em>.</p>
<blockquote><p>“Homicide is also a threat to life, and one that may be responsive to both healthcare interventions and public policy. Our findings suggest that <strong>violence deserves greater attention when we consider the health consequences of abortion restrictions</strong>.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Amid the Covid pandemic, Woskie and Wallace note the national homicide rate among women—broadly, not specifically related to abortion—increased sharply, then decreased immediately after shelter-in-place policies ended. Then, it increased in states with abortion bans (but not other states) after <em>Dobbs </em>in 2022.</p>
<p>This finding is in line with what we’ve long known. <strong>Abortion bans are innately violent, and also render pregnant people exponentially more vulnerable to domestic violence.</strong></p>
<p>Nearly a decade after Jennifer Rothwell’s death, pregnant women like her are at greater risk of fatal, intimate partner violence than ever thanks to abortion bans. (In banned states like Texas, Mississippi, and Arkansas, women <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/here-are-the-states-where-you-cant-finalize-a-divorce-if-youre-pregnant">can’t even finalize a divorce</a> if they’re pregnant.)</p>
<p>The landmark Turnaway Study, which observed the effects of being denied abortion on women’s lives over 10 years, <a href="https://www.ansirh.org/research/ongoing/turnaway-study">found</a> a third of women reported domestic violence as one of their reasons for seeking abortion. After being denied care, they were more likely to be entrapped in long-term domestic violence.</p>
<p>So it follows that violence against women would escalate when abortion is banned.</p>

<p><em><strong>One</strong></em><strong> death would be too many. </strong>These projected homicides from Woskie and Wallace’s research are in addition to the half-dozen women <em>that we know of</em> who have died from abortion bans in medical settings, where they were unable to receive life-saving emergency care. And while the leading cause of death for pregnant women is homicide, often by an intimate partner, these deaths are not counted in government maternal mortality tracking.</p>
<p>The study notes that for IPV victims,</p>
<blockquote><p>“abortion access may reduce barriers to leaving unsafe situations or avoiding prolonged contact with an abusive partner,<strong> all of which may contribute to heightened risk of homicide</strong>.”</p></blockquote>
<p>In 2024, the same researchers published a <a href="https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/epdf/10.1377/hlthaff.2023.01098">study</a> of the impacts of abortion restrictions on IPV homicides from 2014 to 2020. Their research focused on TRAP laws, involving the targeted regulation of abortion providers to shut down clinics. They found that enforcement of one TRAP law on one clinic equated to a 3.4% increase in the rate of domestic violence homicides in the whole state. In this six-year time period, an estimated 24 women and girls aged 10 to 44 were the victims of an IPV-related homicide associated with TRAP laws.</p>
<p>Through both studies, we can see the harrowing impact of <em>Dobbs</em>: a notable increase in projected homicides, from 24 women killed in a six-year period in connection with abortion restrictions, to <em>135 women</em> killed in a two-year period, in connection with bans.</p>
<p>Woskie and Wallace’s new research builds on a growing catalog of data showing bans’ impact on domestic violence, and how these laws function as a tool for abusers. Last year, one research paper found<strong> bans <a href="https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33916/w33916.pdf">resulted</a> in at least 9,000 additional IPV incidents</strong> in the immediate aftermath of <em>Dobbs</em>, while a research paper from 2024 <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2814274">estimated</a> that tens of thousands of rape-induced pregnancies occurred in banned states within an 18-month period.</p>
<p>The Texas Council on Family Violence <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/abortion-bans-are-making-it-impossible-for-advocates-to-help-abuse-victims">reports</a> that from 2017 to 2023, at least 72 Texas women were killed by an intimate partner while they were pregnant or postpartum. The Texas reproductive rights organization Avow <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/rape-induced-pregnancy-roe-dobbs-abortion-ban-1851209068">told</a> me in 2024 that since <em>Dobbs</em>, “we have heard the countless stories out of Texas of abusers using abortion bans to further exert control over survivors,” and stressed that abortion is often “part of a survivor’s safety plan.”</p>
<p>In 2023, the National Domestic Violence Hotline reported that calls involving reproductive coercion <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/domestic-violence-hotline-reports-99-increase-in-calls-1850641660">doubled</a> in the first year after <em>Dobbs</em>. The hotline <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/post-dobbs-abortion-bans-have-given-abusers-a-new-power">published</a> additional data in which victims and survivors recounted partners threatening to sue or report them to police if they had abortions. Others described access to a reproductive care clinic as life-saving—only for these clinics to shutter en masse since <em>Dobbs</em>. One 2025 <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41197224/">study</a> associated greater increases in travel distance to abortion providers with seven to 10% increases in reported IPV crimes.</p>
<p>It can’t be stated enough: <strong>women affected by abortion bans are more than statistics</strong>. In reporting these horrific projections, we can’t allow the women and girls who have been killed or subjected to unthinkable gender-based violence under abortion bans to ever be just numbers.</p>
<p>They are <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/abortion-bans-are-making-it-impossible-for-advocates-to-help-abuse-victims">women like Erica DuBois</a>, who endured years of domestic violence after her abuser impregnated her against her will and while beating her, insisted that “if the baby survived, then it was God’s will.” Or <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/a-22-year-old-texas-man-fatally-shot-his-partner-for-tr-1850432906">women like Gabriela Gonzalez</a> of Texas, who was killed by her partner in 2023 after she returned from traveling to Colorado to get an abortion without his permission.</p>
<p>Abortion bans are taking women and girls’ lives across a continuum of violence: through the denial of emergency medical care, resulting in death or permanent impairment; <a href="https://jessica.substack.com/p/katie-zacharia-trump-abortion-death-penalty?open=false#%C2%A7using-texas-bounty-hunter-law-to-target-students-for-life">robbing us of our livelihoods</a> and futures; and, of course, rendering us exponentially more vulnerable to domestic violence, sometimes fatally.</p>
<p>In their study, Woskie and Wallace acknowledge how policymakers are increasingly justifying attacks on abortion—specifically, medication abortion by mail—by fearmongering about “coerced abortions.” (This is a strategy we’ve <a href="https://jessica.substack.com/p/abortion-coercion-meaning-explained">reported on at length</a> at <em>AED</em>.)</p>
<p>But to the contrary, the researchers emphasize that “<strong>more restricted abortion access is associated with greater violence-related harm</strong>, including its most extreme manifestations.”</p>
<blockquote><p>“Claims that restrictions protect women should be weighed against other real and measurable harms, including risk of lethal violence.”</p></blockquote>
<p>At the same time that abortion bans are leaving women more vulnerable to murder, we are forced to navigate policy and criminal legal systems that regard <em>women</em> as murderers for having abortions or losing pregnancies. Often, I think about what If/When/How’s legal and policy director, Sara Ainsworth, <a href="https://www.jezebel.com/abortion-bans-are-making-it-impossible-for-advocates-to-help-abuse-victims">told</a> me last year about the agony of telling women, “‘You live in a state where you’re more likely to be criminalized than the person who’s abusing you.’”</p>
<p>“It’s devastating,” she said.</p>
