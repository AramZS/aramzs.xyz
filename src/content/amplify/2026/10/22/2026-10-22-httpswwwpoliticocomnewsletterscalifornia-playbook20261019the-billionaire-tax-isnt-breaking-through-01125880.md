<p><strong>RUNNING BEHIND —</strong> A proposed tax on billionaires remains short of majority support as Californians begin casting ballots, <a href="https://www.politico.com/f/?id=000001a1-471e-dc9a-a9a3-5f7e57680000">according to a new Citrin-POLITICO poll.</a></p>
<p>Likely voters are essentially where they were a month ago, the survey found, with 46% in favor of Proposition 40 and 43% opposed — despite a recent <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/03/bernie-sanders-billionaire-tax-san-francisco-rally-01105995">push by backers of the measure, including Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.)</a>, to rally support for the one-time, 5% tax on billionaires’ assets.</p>
<p><strong>Sitting below a majority this late in a campaign is a red flag</strong>, according to ballot initiative veterans, and the static support reflects how difficult it has been for proponents to convert anti-billionaire sentiment into likely votes, said <strong>Jon Cohen</strong>, founder and CEO of <a href="https://www.truedot.ai/">TrueDot</a>, whose firm fielded the poll.</p>
<p>“Californians do think billionaires should pay more,” Cohen said, but “it doesn’t translate so easily into them voting for this tax.”</p>
<p>Time is running short for supporters, led by health care union SEIU-UHW, as they contend with middling poll numbers and a staggering financial deficit against billionaires who have spent hundreds of millions of dollars to block the tax.</p>
<p><strong>That lopsided advantage has allowed opponents to monopolize the airwaves</strong>, where they have warned the tax could be expanded. The tax’s proponents insist that is false, and pushing an additional tax through the Legislature would face daunting political and legal hurdles.</p>
<p>But the argument resonates with registered voters: Two-thirds doubted it would be a one-time tax, and the opponents’ most effective message was the claim that lawmakers could later expand it to cover people who aren’t billionaires, according to the poll.</p>
<p><strong>Still, the survey suggested there are diminishing returns to the opposition onslaught.</strong> A strikingly high share of registered voters (84%) said they’d been exposed to advertisements or messages about the tax, but a 45% plurality said the communications had not swayed them, and those whose views had shifted were just as likely to be more opposed as more supportive. <strong><em>(</em></strong> <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/19/california-billionaire-tax-poll-01125462"><strong><em>Read Jeremy’s full story here.</em></strong></a> <strong><em>)</em></strong></p>

<p><strong>GRANITE STATE GAVIN</strong> — <strong>Gavin Newsom</strong> swept through New Hampshire, stumping for Democrats up and down the ballot this past weekend. But, as <strong>Tyler Katzenberger</strong> reports, his California connections may prove to be a stumbling block in the important early primary state, if he decides to run for president.</p>
<p>In interviews with more than a dozen Democratic strategists, party activists and public officials in New Hampshire, many said they saw Newsom’s California roots as a liability, even as they praised his combativeness, his looks and his strength as a fundraiser.</p>
<p>Since his last visit to the state in March, his support in the 2028 presidential primary fell from 15% to just 7% in <a href="https://www.anselm.edu/about/anselmian-hub/news/new-saint-anselm-college-poll-finds-republicans-facing-midterm-headwinds-democratic-lead-widens">the most recent Saint Anselm College survey</a>, trailing <strong>Pete Buttigieg</strong>, New York Rep. <strong>Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez</strong>, Georgia Sen. <strong>Jon Ossoff</strong> and Arizona Sen. <strong>Mark Kelly.</strong></p>
<p>Newsom, speaking to POLITICO in Manchester, suggested he could sell the state’s record if he runs.</p>
<p>“If I run,” he said, “I run. And I get out there and I start to provide a vision that I think can address some of the challenges of this country. I can promote some of the work we’ve done in California. I could talk about lessons learned, these struggles, these challenges that we face, the mistakes we’ve made, where we were slow.”</p>
<p><a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/17/new-hampshire-dems-question-what-newsom-has-to-offer-01125415"><strong><em>Read more of Tyler’s dispatch from New Hampshire here.</em></strong></a></p>
<p><strong>VIBE CHECK:</strong> Rep. <strong>Robert Garcia</strong>, who is poised to chair the House Oversight Committee should Democrats flip the House, reiterated his pledge over the weekend to launch a series of investigations into “the most corrupt family to ever profit from the American people.” But what was striking was <em>where</em> he said it — and the reception he received.</p>
<p>Even in Huntington Beach, in a packed high school gymnasium in one of the centers of conservative politics in California in recent years, his promises on Saturday to probe the Trump family elicited cheers and roars.</p>
<p>Supporters filled every seat in the bleachers. Signs across the gym depicted an Orange County blue wave with images of an orange and breaking swell. High up in the bleachers, a group of people waved exaggeratedly large headshots of Trump, <strong>Stephen Miller</strong>, <strong>Eric Trump</strong>, <strong>Pete Hegseth</strong> and <strong>Jared Kushner</strong>.</p>
<p>“Those are the guys I’m going to go after pretty soon,” Garcia said with a smile at the podium. “All those guys.” <em>—</em> <strong><em>Will McCarthy</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>HIGH ALERT —</strong> Two California members of Congress said their offices received envelopes containing suspicious substances over the weekend. Rep. <strong>Derek Tran</strong> (D) said his district office in Cypress — and the entire building — was evacuated on Friday after receiving mail with an unidentified substance. On Sunday, Sen. <strong>Adam Schiff</strong>(D) said the U.S. Capitol Police and the Sergeant at Arms confirmed that at least one envelope mailed to his California offices was intercepted. The senator said the incident fit “a disturbing pattern of lawmakers being targeted across party and geography.”</p>
<p>“This attempt to intimidate elected officials and our staff will not succeed,” Schiff <a href="https://x.com/senadamschiff/status/2111925205085929619?s=46">posted on X</a>. “We instead condemn these threats as antithetical to the values of our nation, recommit to our work and thank the law enforcement and postal officials who are helping keep our teams safe.”</p>
<p><strong>NO THANKS</strong>— Mayor <strong>Karen Bass</strong> urged an independent committee backing her mayoral campaign on Sunday to return a <a href="https://ethics.lacity.gov/pdf/disclosure/CA497/CA497_147837.pdf#view=Fit?58490BFB-2FAA-4EAA-91AF0F398F4694B7">$50,000 donation</a> from Chevron.</p>
<p>“I’ve spent my career fighting the oil industry, from helping South LA communities organize against neighborhood oil wells to banning oil drilling in LA as mayor,” Bass said in a statement. “So I was surprised to learn that an oil company donated to an outside committee that I am legally barred from coordinating with or soliciting funds for. They should take the money back and use it to lower the astronomical prices Angelenos pay at the pump.”</p>
<p><strong>Meanwhile</strong>… the <a href="https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2026-10-18/nithya-raman-says-la-needs-more-transparency-from-olympic-organizers">Los Angeles Times looked at</a> mayoral challenger <strong>Nithya Raman</strong>’s skepticism about the Los Angeles-hosted Olympics in 2028. Raman opposed the Olympics outright when she first ran for City Council in 2028, and she has consistently voted against the city’s agreements with the hosting committee, LA28. Raman now says she wants the Games to succeed but wants to make sure the city is protected financially. Bass, a strong backer of the Olympics, said Raman’s position threatens to “squander” the upside that comes with hosting the Games.</p>

Read <a href="https://www.politico.com/newsletters/california-currents/2026/10/16/deadly-extreme-heat-is-coming-this-lawmaker-wants-us-to-be-prepared-01124964">California Currents’</a> latest Q&amp;A with state Sen. <strong>Henry Stern</strong>, who’s pushed to establish an indoor heat standard for homes, and to learn more about how state officials have made big steps in preparing for devastating heat waves and where more work is needed to protect vulnerable residents.</p>
<p><strong>TAX REBELLION —</strong> A Proposition 13-style uprising against rising property taxes is sweeping the country, with the most pitched battles occurring in red states, <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2026/10/18/property-taxes-housing-affordability-issue-01105514"><strong>Liam Dillon</strong> reports from Hartville, Ohio</a>. Fueled by the rising cost of homeownership — soaring home prices, mortgage rates and insurance bills — they are revolting against property taxes at a scale not seen in a half century. The 1970s property tax uprising that one journalist called a “Revolt of the Haves” reshaped American politics. The depth and breadth of today’s proposals threaten to do so once again.</p>
<p>In the last two years alone, plans to substantially reduce or altogether repeal property taxes have emerged in more than two dozen states. Georgia, Indiana and Wyoming already sliced residents’ tax bills by billions of dollars. Republican Govs. <strong>Ron DeSantis</strong> and <strong>Greg Abbott</strong> have Florida and Texas, respectively, competing to see who can lower property taxes the most. Gubernatorial candidates elsewhere are putting tax reform at the center of their campaigns in the hopes of staving off insurgencies that risk overrunning their tenures. Public figures as disparate as <strong>Elon Musk</strong>, the world’s richest man, and <strong>Francesca Hong</strong>, the socialist who narrowly lost the Wisconsin Democratic gubernatorial primary, have joined the cause.</p>
<p><strong>THE NEW DSA —</strong> <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/10/26/how-the-dsa-learned-to-love-elections">The New Yorker looks at how</a> the Democratic Socialists of America are embracing getting involved in elections, highlighting Nithya Raman’s campaign as an example of their evolving political strategy.</p>
<p>Democratic socialists in Los Angeles voted to endorse Raman, after internal debate given the council member’s friction with the group. <strong>Keshav Kundassery</strong>, a M.D.-Ph.D student at UCLA who introduced the resolution to endorse her, said to achieve DSA’s goals, they can’t be picky about ideology.</p>
<p>“If we want to become the kind of organization that can actually not just elect Nithya, or not just elect Zohran [Mamdani], but become a mass socialist party that is regularly electing people into office while also playing an outsized role in the cultural and political transformation of the United States, we are going to have to shed some of those self-defeating habits and be ready to embrace the messy reality of what it actually means to get involved in politics,” he said.</p>
