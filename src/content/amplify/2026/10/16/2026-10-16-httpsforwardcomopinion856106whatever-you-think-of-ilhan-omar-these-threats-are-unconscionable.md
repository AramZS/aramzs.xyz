<p>The Trump administration is <a href="https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/ilhan-omar-investigation-aims-to-build-case-for-stripping-her-citizenship-6adf1edc">reportedly planning</a> to build a case to strip Congresswoman Ilhan Omar (D-MN) of her citizenship and eventually deport her.</p>
<p>It is my hope that American Jews — even those who do not like Omar and can recite <a href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/rep-ilhan-omar-defends-2012-tweet-accusing-israel-of-hypnotizing-the-world/">every</a> questionable <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ilhan-omar-twitter-anti-semitism-aipac-its-all-about-the-benjamins/">comment</a> <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-57436573">she has made</a> over the past 15 years to illustrate why — will understand this as offensive and outrageous not only because they are Americans, but because they are Jews.</p>
<p>The Trump administration may claim that it is building a case against Omar due to paperwork (reports suggest that they are interested in whether her father listed her age as 17 on her citizenship application when she was really 18). It is similarly <a href="https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/ilhan-omar-investigation-aims-to-build-case-for-stripping-her-citizenship-6adf1edc">filing</a> dozens of lawsuits attempting to denaturalize citizens by trying to prove they lied in the process of gaining citizenship. However, that this is happening now, ahead of the midterm elections against a sitting member of Congress, a high-profile progressive whom the president has said should “<a href="https://www.politico.com/story/2019/07/14/trump-congress-go-back-where-they-came-from-1415692">go back</a>” to her country of origin, suggests that it is, to put it mildly, politically motivated.</p>

<p>Evidently, the Trump administration sees citizenship not as a right or a responsibility but as a reward, one that can be taken away if that is seen as useful to the president and his adherents. That use could be silencing political opposition or something as simple (and callous) as an opportunity to <a href="https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/news-releases/justice-department-moves-to-strip-citizenship-from-40-criminals-in-largest-single-period-filing">brag</a> about the sheer number of attempted denaturalizations.</p>
<p>Jews, too, have found themselves on the wrong end of political denaturalization. I do not mean to compare the Trump administration to the Nazis, but the stubborn fact is that Jews were indeed <a href="https://www.lbi.org/1938projekt/detail/denaturalized/">stripped</a> of their citizenship as part of a political project in Germany and <a href="https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/723353">elsewhere in Europe</a> in the 1930s and 40s. And in the early 20th century in the United States, it was Jews who politicians <a href="https://www.haaretz.com/us-news/2024-10-29/ty-article-magazine/.premium/profoundly-disturbing-u-s-jews-sound-alarm-on-ugly-immigration-discourse/00000192-d2db-df2b-a5db-d6ff82320000">claimed</a> were incapable of assimilating, of ever <i>really</i> being a part of the United States. These arguments culminated in legislation that kept out Jews who <a href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/1924-law-slammed-door-immigrants-and-politicians-who-pushed-it-back-open-180974910/">could</a> have found refuge here. The threat of denaturalization and the experience of perpetual otherness is a grievous part of modern Jewish history; we should be offended by its presence in our political present.</p>
<p>Jewish tradition as well as history encourages us to stand up to authority when that authority is wrong. Mordecai refuses to bow to Haman; Nathan yells at King David for sleeping with Bathsheba and having her husband killed; Moses famously urges Pharoah to change his oppressive ways. Even God is on the receiving end of pushback when Abraham pleads and negotiates to spare Sodom.</p>
<p>I do not think one needs to be Jewish to be outraged by the attempt to denaturalize Ilhan Omar. I think it is also bone-chilling as an American who believes we, including our elected officials, have the right to openly oppose our president, and I am worried as a person who thinks it would be good to continue to live in a democratic republic. And I am sure that there are Jews who will say that Omar deserves to lose her citizenship because she has said things about Israel they do not like.</p>
<p>But the Jewish lens through which I see this is that it is right to challenge power when it is abused, and it is wrong for the powerful to make a show of trying to cast their opponents out of the country.</p>
<p>The point is not whether Trump can make good on the threat to strip Omar of her citizenship and deport her, but that it is obscene to use denaturalization as punishment for being a high-profile political opponent in the first place. If he is trying to make it more dangerous to speak out while being from the “wrong” country, then the rest of us need to say that this is a fundamental misunderstanding of what it means to be an American citizen.</p>
<p>We should do this because we are Americans and we should do this because we are Jews. We should do this because of the ways in which our American and Jewish identities bleed into one another. And, we should do this because we may not know exactly whether it is as Americans or as Jews that we know we have to protect the right to speak truth to power, but we know we need to do it all the same.</p>
