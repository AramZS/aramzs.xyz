<p>French-Belgian manufacturer of expensive cast-iron cookware <a href="https://www.lecreuset.co.uk/en_GB/">Le Creuset</a> have <a href="https://www.lecreuset.co.uk/en_GB/c/collections/le-creuset-x-star-trek">released a </a><em><a href="https://www.lecreuset.co.uk/en_GB/c/collections/le-creuset-x-star-trek">Star Trek</a></em><a href="https://www.lecreuset.co.uk/en_GB/c/collections/le-creuset-x-star-trek"> tie-in pot</a>. For a mere $500 you madame, and you, sir, can purchase a heavy Le Creuset casserole that is exactly like their other, considerably less-expensive iron pots except that its lid comes with a (liable to slice painfully into tender handflesh, surely) Federation, and/or Klingon, lid-handle. There are, as you can see, a number of other Trekkish items on sale, including a shuttlecraft rectangular casserole, a Vulcan ‘live long and prosper’ hand-shaped spoon rest, a Spock-ear-handle mug and Gorn and Balok ‘mini cocottes’ for smaller cooking. Why Le Creuset have decided now is the time to issue tie-in Trekkerie is unclear to me, although they’ve indulged the embarrassing Muskishness of having one of their casseroles elevated into low-orbit on a large helium balloon as a publicity stunt:</p>

<p>There’s a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFg-EzXrhro">3-minute video about this stunt on YouTube</a>, if you’re interested. From the comments to this I select one that seems to me to cut to the heart of the matter, from <strong>@betterburnout</strong>: ‘I’m a Star Trek fan, I cook… But why, WHYYY’. The lack of a terminal question mark only adds to the force of this, I feel.</p>
<p>I myself own a Le Creuset, bought in yer-actual France, and I use it often: a lazy-person’s guide to cooking involves the three step: (a) bung a bunch of vegetables and meat in a cast-iron casserole sluiced in two pints of stock (b) bung whole in oven and (c) leave. But the specific market crossover Le Creuset are aiming this product puzzles me. Are Trekkers notable foodies? Are foodies prepared to pay extra (my Le Creuset cost me two hundred quid; this Trek is about twice that) for a bit of Trekkie branding? I can see that the circular lid of a Le Creuset pot has certain geometric analogies with the circular saucer-section of a Federation starship, but then—doesn’t that open ‘merchandisable possibilities’ to <em>anything and everything circular whatsoever</em>? And, however much Anson Mount Pike flourishes the frying pan, ‘cooking up’ meals in an attempt to gorblimey some sense of him as an actual ‘character’, with distinctive traits and personality—the fact remains: the universe of Star Trek has superseded the onerous folderol of ‘cookery’ with the miracle of replicator technology. Replicators can generate your perfect meal, or drink, instantly. Why would a cookware company want to allign themselves with such a world?</p>
