<h2>Social Sharing</h2>
<p>The new <em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em> brings old classics back to the franchise: stealing cars, flying planes, evading cops. But this iteration also has some changes.</p>
<p>Instead of hiring sex workers, the early part of the game has you doing chores for your in-game girlfriend, like buying her flowers and fixing the leak in the roof. And as a result, a fringe group of players and streamers say the latest version of <em>Grand Theft Auto</em> is too woke.</p>
<p>"The game is presenting this option of treating women respectfully," said Joshua Foust, an assistant professor in public relations at Syracuse University, who studies the intersection of gaming and gender.</p>
<p>"And the response, at least from these accounts that seem to be mad about it, is that even the request to treat a woman respectfully in a game, they find offensive and somehow diminishing of their own sense of manhood."</p>
<p>That's apparently not what Braden Eric Peters, better known as the looksmaxxing influencer Clavicular, signed up for.</p>
<p>"GTA 6 is insanely cucked, half of the missions are jestermaxing and simping for girls," Peters, <a href="https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/clavicular-rape-charge-not-guilty-9.7391554"><u>who is currently on trial for rape</u></a>, posted on X.</p>

<p>Despite this pushback, the open-world action adventure game isn't struggling to sell. The game ‌recorded $3.79 billion US<a href="https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/take-two-set-shatter-sales-records-gta-vi-launches-massive-fanfare-2026-11-19/"> in retail sales in the first 24 ⁠hours ⁠of ‌its launch</a>, according to game developer Rockstar Games, making it the world's biggest video game release ever.</p>
<p>"If you look at something like<em> Grand Theft Auto</em>, that is a prime example of how the online feedback from certain groups of people in no way mirrors what's actually going on here," said Alyssa Mercante, a culture critic and gaming journalist.</p>
<h2><strong>Buying flowers and putting up drywall</strong></h2>
<p>The game lets you play as both Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, though you spend a majority of the early missions controlling Jason.</p>
<p>During that time — amid smuggling drugs and stealing cars — players have the option to perform what is essentially a "honey-do" list, including putting up drywall, painting the bedroom and fixing the stairs leading up to your house.</p>
<p>You can even go buy Lucia flowers (she is just getting out of a prison, after all), though all of these tasks are optional.</p>

<p>In another part of the early game, you have to unclog a woman's sink for her, which did not impress streamer Zack Hoyt, known online as Asmongold.</p>
<p>"Are you kidding me?" he said while streaming. "Why can't she do this? Why do I have to do this for her?"</p>
<p>Foust says that underneath what he calls this "toxic criticism" is a belief by some that these games should only be made for them and shouldn't serve anyone else. He says this is especially true among streamers who play these games professionally and expect everything in these games to be specifically tailored to them.</p>
<p>"That's not me diagnosing anything, just like, usually, if you're unable to accept that some things are not for you, that's emotionally immature," said Foust.</p>
<h2><strong>Monetized rage bait?</strong></h2>
<p>Mercante, who is currently working on a book about rage baiting, says all of that pushback may not be totally genuine.</p>
<p>These streamers — such as Hoyt, who has millions of subscribers on his video channels — make their money off people watching their tirades. And clips posted on platforms such as X are often boosted by the algorithm, she says.</p>
<p>"Money is a hell of a drug," said Mercante.</p>
<p>She says many of these streamers who make money of this behaviour either end up believing the things they spout on their streams or adopting it as their online persona.</p>

<p>She says many of the people who follow streamers like Hoyt may watch the clips, and may even share them, but often are still buying the game.</p>
<p>And she says <em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em> isn't an albatross — most major video game releases over the past few years have come under fire by this same group for things it considers woke.</p>
<p>When <em>Assassin's Creed Shadows </em>came out in 2025, some people complained about it featuring a Black lead character. Also last year, the <em>Ghost of Tsushima</em> sequel — <em>Ghost of Yōtei</em> — received some backlash for featuring a female character as its protagonist.</p>
<p>"Of course this was going to happen with GTA. Someone is going to try and find something to trot out as an example of, you know, the 'wokeification' of video games or about how games are being taken away from straight white men," said Mercante.</p>

<p>Overall, Mercante says studios aren't too concerned with this type of reaction. While an online movement may impact small independent studios, she says large publishers like Take-Two Interactive, which publishes <em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em>, aren't going to see this pushback affect their bottom line.</p>
<p>So, what do you do if you see a particularly outrageous review? Mercante says to take a look at who is posting it, and who stands to gain from it. If someone has a verification checkmark on X, they are paying to be able to monetize their posts.</p>
<p>"Recognize that, in many cases, this type of content is meant to manipulate and is meant to misinform and ultimately is meant to put money in the pocket of the person posting it," she said.</p>
<p>"We live in a place where people don't trust the media anymore, but I promise you a content creator who is monetizing their — for lack of a better term — shit posts, is not more trustworthy."</p>
