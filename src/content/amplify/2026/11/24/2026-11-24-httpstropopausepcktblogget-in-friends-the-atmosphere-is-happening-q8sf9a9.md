<h2>The Atmosyear is nearly over</h2>
<p>Paul Frazee coined "Atmosyear" back in early 2026 at Atmosphereconf. Looking back, though, I'd say 2026 was really the year of Atmosphere building blocks. Between permissioned data, groups, and many apps gaining momentum, there has been a lot of groundwork made on key pieces for building atmospheric apps. But now, y'all, the Atmosphere is Atmos-here.</p>
<p>There are a lot of things landing in the Atmosphere right now and I think we're about to hit an inflection point. That feels fitting, because in my opinion atproto is the only exciting, not-AI-related thing happening on the internet these days, and we are due for another major end-of-year change to how we think about building software.</p>
<p>A quick disclaimer: I work at Bluesky on the exploration team (not on the Bluesky app, but like adjacent to it) so I am pretty invested in this. But I was also bought in on atproto before working here. I would love to see it become mainstream enough that it could be the backbone of a better, open, and more equitable internet. I'm not doing this because I think atproto might one day make me rich, like a lot of startups. That's not why I'm here. I'm here because I want to see the internet work for people. I want to see people going online to find more real human connection. And I'd love to see the open internet be used to allow humans to create all the incredible, wacky, and weird things that they tend to do, and get the recognition and funding they deserve for doing it.</p>
<h2>What's landing</h2>
<p>A lot is landing in the next few weeks and months. Together it adds up to a big LFG moment (Let's Fucking Go, not Looking For Group, for my fellow WoW nerds). So let's talk about them!</p>
<h3>Spaces and private data</h3>
<p>Spaces moves from alpha to public beta on December 1 (<a href="https://atproto.com/blog/getting-ready-for-the-spaces-public-beta">AT Protocol blog)</a>. That means private data on protocol, and it's just as easy to build on as the rest of atproto. I can vouch for the building side, because I've done a lot on the alpha, including putting my own AI agents on it. I'm not the world's most technical engineer, so if I can do it, you can too. I have very high hopes for seeing Spaces show up in other apps.</p>
<p>Private data was the biggest missing piece between giant public-square apps and an Atmosphere that works as a protocol for any kind of app. Major props to the protocol and Atmosphere teams for this one. It's been so cool to cheer them on and watch Spaces come together into something as composable and developer-friendly as public atproto.</p>
<h3>Atmosphere Groups</h3>
<p>I was a kind of involved in this process and it's something I've <a href="https://brittany-ellich.offprint.app/a/3mmxmmnku3n23-representing-groups-in-atproto">wanted for a long time</a>. The <a href="https://tangled.org/opensocial.group/proposal">Atmosphere Groups proposal</a> gives people real ownership of their online communities, which I haven't seen anywhere else on the internet.</p>
<p>One of the hardest parts of running an online community is how stuck you are inside one app. You start a Discord server that works a certain way, outgrow it over time, and add a Google Drive, a Facebook page, or whatever else, fracturing the community along the way. With Atmosphere Groups, you set up a community once and carry its roles and permissions to any atproto app that supports it. The shared data belongs to the community instead of a platform.</p>
<p>Groups is still a proposal, so right now is the time to read it, try it out, and weigh in.</p>

<h3>Governance is growing up</h3>
<p>The pieces holding the Atmosphere together are maturing, and that matters as much as any new feature. Without this, Bluesky and atproto could become yet another lock-in platform. It's hard to argue for fighting against tech oligarchies while building another one, and decentralizing the Atmosphere puts real barriers in the way of that ever happening.</p>
<p><strong>Atproto is at the IETF.</strong> An <a href="https://atproto.com/blog/kicking-off-the-atp-working-group">Authenticated Transfer Protocol (ATP) working group was created there this spring</a>. That gives the protocol third-party governance and reduces how much control Bluesky has over it. That's a good thing! If Bluesky ever decided "hey, let's enshittify this protocol we started for financial benefit," it would be MUCH harder to make changes without independent oversight.</p>
<p><strong>The </strong><a href="https://blog.plcred.org/3mwlphq42d227"><strong>PLC has its own organization now</strong></a><strong>.</strong> In normal human terms: the PLC directory is essentially the DNS service for did:plc identities, and it's one of the biggest pieces of infrastructure behind the Atmosphere. It's moving out from under Bluesky into its own independent organization, which is great for long-term sustainability and decentralization.</p>
<p><strong>The Atmosphere Community Cooperative kicked off</strong>. The AT Protocol Community Fund is becoming a cooperative governed by the organizers, developers, artists, and others building in the ecosystem. That puts governance in the community's hands instead of a single business's, which is good for a healthy, balanced ecosystem.</p>
<p>Introducing the Atmosphere Community Cooperative: a new organization that will be governed by and for the organizers, developers, artists, and many others who are building tooling, services, and community on the AT Protocol ecosystem.</p>
<p><a href="https://atmosphere.coop/updates/introducing-the-atmosphere-community-cooperative">As we look toward the third AtmosphereConf in 2027, we’re happy to announce that the AT Protocol Community Fund transforming into a cooperative dedicated to supporting the health, growth, and stabilit...</a></p>
<p>Any one of these would add some juice to the case that atproto is a worthwhile long-term bet. All of them together are a big sign that the people leading the protocol inside Bluesky are serious about governance.</p>
<h2>The Bluesky side of this</h2>
<p>I started writing about what it's been like to work at Bluesky for the last six months, after coming from the Atmosphere community, and it turned into its own post. I'll share that one soon. For now, I'll say this: I was nervous about what I'd find on the inside, and I've never worked with a more dedicated and creative group of people.</p>
<p>None of this happened only because of folks who work at Bluesky, though. The Atmosphere has taken tireless work from many people, including lots of volunteers, to build something much larger than any one of us could build alone.</p>
<h2>What I mean by "get in"</h2>
<p>With Atmosphere apps getting a lot more visibility and new kinds of apps becoming possible, I predict a big first-mover advantage in the Atmosphere over the coming months. Apps that already exist are in the best spot to get it, but this is also an excellent time to start building your first atproto app. If we want to make the Atmosphere more mainstream, here's what I mean by get in:</p>
<ul><li>Get your app ready for more people, and fix the small issues that make it feel not quite "done."</li><li>Swap your private data workarounds for Spaces where it makes sense. Most of us have been using an internal app database in place of private data, and that changes when Spaces hits public beta on December 1.</li><li>Use Groups, so your users' communities can come with them.</li><li>Participate in the Coop. Community governance only works if the community shows up.</li><li>Go to Atmosphereconf if you can. The <a href="https://atmosphereconf.org/speak">first phase of the CFP is still open until November 30th</a>!</li></ul>
