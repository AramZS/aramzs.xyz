<p>One of the very first characters you encounter in <a href="https://www.vulture.com/article/how-surprise-dangelo-song-kamikaze-landed-in-gta-vi.html"><em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em></a>, well before you’ve logged dozens of hours in its digital Florida, is a woman named Mandy. At this early stage, you’re still playing as Jason Duval, the game’s male lead portrayed by Dylan Rourke, and you’ve been dispatched to her house to collect rent on behalf of your boss. Once there, Mandy draws you inside under the pretense of needing help with her sink, which, it turns out, is actually broken. But the plumbing ain’t the focus here. Mandy, of course, is trying to seduce him.</p>
<p>Jason is having none of it. Some of that has to do with his heart belonging to Lucia, the game’s other protagonist. Some of it, though, is that Mandy is a little weird. She’s introduced as a fairly classic Rockstar caricature: a cartoonish portrait of an aggressive, flirtatious woman who takes personal offense at Jason’s lack of interest. But there’s an extra edge to Mandy: She’s also unhinged, maybe even dangerous. There’s the matter of a suspicious truck parked outside her house and whatever it is Jason fishes out of her sink, both of which leave open the suggestion she might be some kind of serial killer.</p>
<p>With just this one interaction, Mandy has made an outsize impression on the game’s audience. She’s become something of a fan favorite, thanks to her outlandish personality, her skimpy-underwear seduction routine, and, well, her curvaceous nature — Mandy’s visual appearance is apparently based on Marissa DuBois, a Miami-based model and influencer. (One <em>could</em> also theoretical read her as a self-empowerment icon, as evidenced by her home being filled with framed pictures of herself.) And as you can imagine, some corners of the internet have taken things a little out of control, producing copious Mandy thirst content and even spawning a small but vocal corner of the peanut gallery who’d prefer Jason to run off with her instead of Lucia.</p>
<p>The voice behind Mandy, meanwhile, had no idea any of this was coming. Lexie Shoaibi got the job through her commercial agent, responding to what looked like an ordinary audition for some video game. Since then, Mandy has become an unlikely calling card for Shoaibi, an actor and model with theater and television credits (including a small role on <em>The Good Fight</em>) who also makes <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/@lexieshoaibi?lang=en">fashion and comedy content online</a>. Naturally, the newfound attention comes with downsides, too; reactionary trolls have been combing through her old posts and seizing on her appearance on Sunday <a href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/kUIL6TxYVrc">on Hasan Piker’s livestream</a> as fodder to complain about <em>GTA</em>’s “wokeness.” Vulture spoke with Shoaibi about the unusually secretive process of making the game, building a character from scattered bits of script and motion-capture performance, and the strange experience of watching a character she wasn’t even sure would make the cut become quite the memetic force.</p>
<p><strong>Take me back to how you got involved with <em>GTA VI. </em></strong><br/>
I got an audition notice through my commercial agent in New York, and it looked just like any other commercial audition. The entire time, through filming, through the release on November 19, not one person confirmed that this was for <em>Grand Theft Auto</em>. I think it was called something like “Project Bubblegum.” They sent me some sides to read, and I did it like any other commercial audition.</p>
<p>I don’t remember the brief being particularly detailed. It was something like “sexy ex-girlfriend, be seductive.” And what they sent wasn’t at all the script of what my character ended up doing, so that was a little different.</p>
<p><strong>How long ago was this?</strong>I think it was at the top of 2024, maybe around 2023. It feels so distant now.</p>
<p><strong>What was your relationship to the franchise?</strong><br/>
I’m not a gamer, but I’d seen the memes: “<a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/we-got-x-before-gta-6">We have blank before <em>GTA 6</em></a>.” I knew there was so much anticipation, but not being in this space, it was floating in my head with other video-game terms I knew. <em>Call of Duty,</em> having seen a boyfriend play it, and <em>Mario Kart</em>. These were just words from a world I wasn’t part of.</p>
<p><strong>How strict was the NDA? </strong><br/>
Incredibly. I signed one for my first self-tape audition, and there was more paperwork throughout the process. I think what I’ve said so far is fine, but there’s a lot I can’t say. No pictures, no recording, nothing. When you’re on set as an actor, you want to post “Here I am on set” on your Instagram Story. None of that.</p>
<p><strong>Tell me about building Mandy’s character and the whole studio process.</strong>I had an idea of who she was supposed to be when I first received the material. Then there was a lot of working with the director within the moment on the role she plays in Jason’s life and the player’s life. I can’t say I came in with a rigid, set-in-stone version of Mandy. It was very fluid. There was also little context to what’s happening. There’s no “read the whole script,” just chunks of text.</p>
<p>The motion-capture process is so different than what I’m used to. I performed opposite the actor who played Jason, and all the things you learn in acting school about how to drop into your character, how to build your character, how to use your imagined circumstances — it’s a really different beast. You’re in a room that doesn’t look like the room you’re supposed to be envisioning, with a big camera in front of you and a light in your eye. I remember the director said, “Okay, action,” and I completely flubbed my first line. I was so embarrassed, and everyone gently, supportively giggled. The director said, “I don’t think I’ve ever seen an actor not mess up their first line in motion capture. Everyone trips at the starting line a little bit,” which was reassuring.</p>
<p>It was a very inclusive, family-type cast, which I didn’t expect. Sometimes you’re on projects where everybody loves each other and there’s a wrap party. Given the nature of this one, I thought it would be much more isolated: You go to the little pod and you sit there. But everyone was friends with each other, and it was so much warmer than I expected. I’m like, “Wow, this feels like a little Off Broadway theater production. What the heck?” Really sweet.</p>
<p><strong>When did you first see the completed Mandy?</strong><br/>
Not until the 18th, when there was some kind of online activation and my friend sent me a video of a big streamer reacting to Mandy. That was the first time I thought, <em>Oh, they didn’t cut my character.</em> Until then, it was entirely possible.</p>
<p><strong>How did it feel watching that?</strong>Very exciting. But the most exciting part was “Oh, they kept it.” It had been so long since I recorded it, and I wanted there to be something to tell. Then, with the official release on the 19th, there was this big flood of energy and attention. I feel like I’m living in a dream. It’s crazy.</p>
<p><strong>What did you think of the scene and Mandy as a character?</strong><br/>
She’s gorgeous. She’s very sexy. I had no concept of what the finalized animation would be, but she’s hot as hell. She’s doing what she came to do, which is try to seduce this man, and she’s pulling out all the stops, and it’s really fun to see. There’s a scene where she lies on the bed and strokes the top of her laptop, and I remember doing that. There are obviously some differences in our appearance, so it’s funny to try to put all the puzzle pieces together in my head. It’s really cool.</p>
<p><strong>I’m sure you’ve noticed that Mandy has </strong><a href="https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gta-6-players-captivated-by-mandy-apparently-include-a-former-nintendo-president/"><strong>become a fan-favorite character online</strong></a><strong>, and some people are also being weird about it, as tends to happen with video games and the internet. I’m curious how you’ve experienced that response.</strong>I think because I have some experience with online attention as a content creator, I’ve developed a pretty effective lens for seeing more good than bad. There are weirdos out there, and they’re going to try to bring you down, so it’s important to stay around the people and the places that aren’t doing that. It’s easy enough to do, which I’m very grateful for. There’s enough support in my real-life community and online that I can say, “I could just hang out over here. I don’t actually need to go to the scary place.” I’m living in the positive, for sure.</p>
<p><strong>To what extent have you been following the attention and conversation around Mandy online?</strong>A fair bit. Well, the algorithm’s doing a lot of work for me. Friends are sending me stuff. When you hear your voice or see your headshot, you’re going to linger on that post, and then the algorithm does what it does. My friends and family have been so supportive: “Can’t believe I saw this. Look at this article I saw.” So yeah, I’m inside of it for sure.</p>
<p><strong>Has your agent or management prepared you for this kind of attention? Especially as a woman online, that’s a very specific kind of dynamic.</strong>It totally is, and with the response to this character specifically, that’s all amplified. My team has been really great. This is a big moment and we’re meeting it. But I don’t think anyone knew this was coming. I didn’t get an email on the 17th or 18th saying, “Hey, look out, a big meteor is about to hit Earth.” It’s hitting all of us at the same time. No one knew a thing. I had no idea I was this early in the game, and I had no idea they were keeping the part at all.</p>
<p><strong>Has the attention translated into new opportunities yet?</strong>It’s in the works. Some things were immediate, like Hasan Piker hitting me up to do his stream. Others are in the works, on the brand-deal and commercial side. But there’s definitely momentum that I wasn’t feeling before.</p>
<p><strong>How did the Hasan Piker appearance come about?</strong>It’s really funny. He and I have a mutual friend.</p>
<p><strong>L.A.’s small, I guess.</strong><br/>
Exactly. When the Mandy stuff blew up, somehow this friend of mine said to Hasan, “I know this girl, she’s blowing up.” I don’t know what their full conversation looked like, but I think Hasan said, “Oh, very cool. Would she want to come onto the pod?” It all happened in personal text messages, not through our teams. It was literally texts and Instagram DMs from personal accounts, and then there we were.</p>
<p>The stream was super-informal. It felt like friends. I don’t know if that was partly the friend’s connection, or if he’s just a very down-to-earth guy running a very down-to-earth operation, but it was like, “Hey, hang out, pet the dog, come on, say what’s up.” He handed me the controller at one point. Coming from very serious, structured sets, <em>GTA </em>being the most so, it was really just, “Hey, buddy, get in here.” It was awesome.</p>
<p><strong>Were you a fan of Hasan’s before?</strong>I was already following him. Somewhat distantly a fan, in a general sense. I wasn’t tuning into the streams every day. But I was thrilled to see his name in my DMs, absolutely.</p>
<p><strong>Piker’s a huge figure but also a lightning rod. Did you notice that you going on the stream triggered another wave of people being weird about you online?</strong><br/>
Maybe I’m too chill about this, but a lot of navigating is just scrolling along. That sounds so easy, and of course you’re completely right: People feel a type of way about Hasan Piker, and now some people feel that way about me.</p>
<p><strong>That’s a healthy response. When I’ve seen people go through this in the past, the response is, like, “Because I’m facing greater scrutiny, I have to think differently about my public presence.” But you seem supercomfortable with all this.</strong><br/>
Here’s the thing: A lot of my content before the fashion stuff was hot takes genuinely from inside my brain, so I’m used to people not liking what I have to say. I’m also used to people really liking and responding to it. And I have videos up there that pretty obviously express or imply my political leanings. These people were going to follow me, scroll a couple of times, and see what was up. I wasn’t hiding anything before meeting Hasan. So no, I’m not scared of it.</p>
<p><strong>Looking forward, what are you hoping to parlay this into? </strong>My passion is acting, so I’d love for this to turn into acting opportunities, voice-over and otherwise. But I’ve always approached my career very flexibly: I’m an actress, and at some point modeling moved into the front seat, then content creator moved into the front seat. I’m first and foremostly hoping for more acting gigs, but if Hasan invited me back on the stream, I’d love to do that more. And I love making little outfit videos on my TikTok and sharing that side of myself. Ideally, all the levels rise.</p>
<p><strong>One last thing. You’ve seen the scene. There’s a moment in which Dylan Rourke’s character, Jason, is working on something at the sink and pulls something out. I’ve rewatched the scene a few times and I still can’t tell what it is. Is it a finger?</strong>When we were recording, it wasn’t specifically clarified or intended for me to base my acting around. On the motion-capture set, it was just a little colorful block. I think we played with “finger,” but it might also at one point have been “a toe” — not that that’s so different. But I wasn’t led to believe it was an important clue to who she is and what she’s up to. It was just some weirdness in the sink. That’s what I got.</p>
<p><strong>So we still can’t definitively say what that is.</strong>Honestly, I wish I could.</p>
