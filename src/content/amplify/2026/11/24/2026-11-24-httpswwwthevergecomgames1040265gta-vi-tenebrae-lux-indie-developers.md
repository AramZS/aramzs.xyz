<p>One of the most intriguing games of the year is called <em>Tenebrae Lux</em>. It’s a moody, black-and-white title with obtuse puzzles and a main character that constantly narrates to himself with dramatic language. Its credits attribute pretty much everything to a developer named Johannes Zuch. It’s generated a lot of discussion for seemingly mocking games like Jonathan Blow’s <em>The Witness</em> or The Chinese Room’s <em>Dear Esther</em>. It’s also a game that only exists inside of <em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em>.</p>
<p>Developer Rockstar Games has stuffed a lot of satirical media inside of <em>GTA VI</em>, including TV shows you can watch, books you can read, and radio shows you can listen to. It’s all in service of making the world feel like a funhouse-mirror version of our current reality — <em>Ted Lasso</em> star Jason Sudeikis plays a right-wing radio host, for example. There are more games you can play inside <em>GTA VI</em> in addition to <em>Tenebrae Lux</em>, including a zombie-themed roguelike. But <em>Tenebrae Lux</em>, which you have to see as part of <em>GTA VI</em>’s main story, is the one that has struck a chord, including with indie developers like <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/mrsambarlow.bsky.social/post/3n2qvf5uihs2p">Sam Barlow</a> and <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/radiatoryang.bsky.social/post/3n2n7at7kbk2t">Robert Yang</a>.</p>

<p>“I have to admit it’s kind of funny for the biggest blockbuster game of all time to go after arthouse indie,” Yang tells <em>The Verge</em>, noting that it’s “like a fighter jet getting obsessed with an ant, petty in a funny way.” (Given that <em>GTA VI</em> earned $3.79 billion in its first day, the gulf between Rockstar and indie game studios that are <a href="https://www.theverge.com/games/994187/polyarc-known-for-its-vr-series-starring-a-mouse-appears-to-be-shutting-down">struggling to get by</a> is vast.)</p>
<p>But Yang suggested that Rockstar developers are probably well versed in the types of games they’re lampooning in <em>GTA VI</em>. “I don’t think gamers understand how much indie experimental stuff any decent AAA dev plays,” Yang said, pointing out that Rockstar’s environmental artists and level designers “all probably love <em>Dear Esther</em>,” the influential first-person game that helped popularize the “walking sim” concept when it launched in 2012.</p>
<p>“I was mostly taken with what it meant that the millions of people playing the game were having to sit down and play through an extended gag about indie games,” Barlow tells <em>The Verge</em>. “That made me feel like we were part of larger culture.” He added that he hopes some of these players might “seek out what these arty indie games might be.”</p>
<p>Rockstar is self-aware enough to use its video games to poke fun at the medium itself. As part of a long conversation between protagonist Jason Duval and his friend Cal Hampton before they start playing <em>Tenebrae Lux</em>, Cal hypes the game by saying that none of it is “just sitting around waiting for people to finish talking.” Initially, you only play <em>Tenebrae Lux</em> for a few minutes, which is enough time to get stumped on the initial puzzles, before the game cuts back to Jason being bored. “Got anything else, a little less, uh… y’know?” he asks. They play the zombie game, titled <em>Street Trash 3 Dezombifier</em>, right after. (You can go back and play the entirety of <em>Tenebrae Lux</em> later if you want.)</p>
<p>Barlow said that the cut “felt very true to the character and story. I gather there <em>is</em> more to the game, but it felt natural to me to hit a wall with the pretentious puzzle design and then move on!”</p>
<p>Yang also pointed out that from a design perspective, <em>GTA VI</em> has more in common with <em>Tenebrae Lux</em> than <em>Street Trash 3 Dezombifier</em>. <em>GTA VI</em> is “basically a walking sim where you walk around Florida and listen to people talk,” Yang said. “What’s the difference? Well, just like a billion dollars I guess.”</p>
