<p>Los Angeles County health officials are ordering the operators of the troubled Chiquita Canyon Landfill in Castaic to provide hotel accommodations and other emergency assistance to nearby residents after a violent eruption sent a towering geyser of decomposing garbage and methane gas into the air Monday afternoon.</p>
<p>In a <a href="https://lacounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/DPH-CCL-Directive-11-16-26-Landfill-Gas-Relief.pdf">directive issued late Nov. 16</a>, the Los Angeles County Department of Public Health said the release caused noxious odors affecting residents in Val Verde, Hasley Hills, Industry, Cambridge and Hillcrest.</p>
<p>The department ordered Waste Connections and Chiquita Canyon LLC to immediately provide temporary hotel stays, filtered-air refuge centers, transportation assistance and air purifiers or other materials to improve conditions inside affected homes.</p>
<p>The directive also requires the landfill operators to keep residents informed about available assistance and how to access it.</p>
<p>The orders came hours after an apparent explosion at the landfill sent what initially appeared to be a massive cloud of black smoke into the sky.</p>
<p>But according to fire officials, the plume was largely decomposing trash, debris and gas released under pressure.</p>
<p>“They were drilling this hole about 150 feet deep and 3 feet wide down into this mound of trash,” <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reels/Df1CacZBn1s/">Los Angeles County Fire Capt. Aaron Katon said</a>.</p>

<p><a href="https://ktla.com/news/local-news/chiquita-landfill-explosion-prompts-evacuation-advisory-injures-4/">Firefighters responded shortly after 4 p.m.</a> to the landfill along Highway 126 after reports of an explosion.</p>
<p>Investigators said workers drilling into the landfill apparently encountered a pressurized pocket of methane, causing material to erupt from the opening.</p>
<p>“There was no fire at any point,” Katon said, explaining that the eruption involved pressurized gas escaping from beneath the surface.</p>
<p>Footage captured a powerful geyser blasting debris high above the landfill.</p>
<p>Six workers were decontaminated by hazardous materials crews, and four were transported to hospitals as a precaution.</p>
<p>The emergency prompted evacuation warnings for several nearby communities, with residents urged to remain indoors, close windows and doors, and prepare to leave if instructed.</p>
<p>By Monday night, all evacuation advisories had been lifted, according to county fire officials.</p>

<p>Katon said extensive air quality monitoring had found no danger to surrounding neighborhoods.</p>
<p>“All of our tools, and we have the best tools, they all say there is no danger,” he said.</p>
<p>Still, the county’s public health directive acknowledged the impact of odors from the release and ordered immediate relief for affected residents.</p>
<p>In a <a href="https://chiquitacanyon.com/statement-by-chiquita-canyon-11-16-2026/">statement</a>, landfill operators said the incident occurred while crews were drilling a new well to address the site’s ongoing underground heat-producing chemical reaction.</p>
<p>The company said gases and particulate matter were released, but no liquids, and that work was immediately stopped.</p>
<p>The landfill has been the subject of years of environmental complaints involving foul odors, contaminated liquid and an underground chemical reaction.</p>
<p>It stopped accepting waste in January 2025.</p>
<p>Last month, California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued the landfill’s operators over alleged hazardous waste violations. The company has disputed many of the allegations.</p>
<p>The cause of Monday’s violent eruption remains under investigation.</p>
