<p>Work on <a href="https://gizmodo.com/carrie-has-power-but-doesnt-quite-stick-the-landing-2000819035"><em>Carrie</em></a> season two is full steam ahead, according to Mike Flanagan, who said <a href="https://gizmodo.com/mike-flanagan-gives-an-update-on-carrie-season-2-2000844939">he’s hoping to go into production</a> on it next year. That <em>Carrie</em> is even getting a second season is down to its ending, which saw Carrie White, played by Summer H. Howell, escape the carnage at her prom and flee town to points unknown.</p>
<p>Deep-cut <em>Carrie</em> fans will recall the one previous adaptation that let her survive—the <a href="https://gizmodo.com/mike-flanagan-gives-an-update-on-carrie-season-2-2000844939">2002 made-for-TV movie</a>—never got the sequel or series it seemed to be hoping for. But with Prime Video’s support, Flanagan’s version of the “what happened next?” story is moving full steam ahead.</p>
<p>Presumably it will explore Carrie learning to control and amplify her powers. Also, it will surely dig into the intentions and activities of that mysterious group of women led by Adelaide, played by Kate Siegel. Might it even tie into <a href="https://gizmodo.com/how-mike-flanagans-carrie-could-tie-into-the-dark-tower-2000811975">that other Stephen King story</a> Flanagan’s been hoping to adapt for awhile now?</p>
<p>Before we get there, Flanagan has backtracked a little bit to explain the changes he made to King’s <em>Carrie</em> story. In an interview with the <a href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/mike-flanagan-carrie-interview-1236761678/">Hollywood Reporter</a>, he said the show’s particular take on Carrie informed the choice to keep her alive at the end.</p>
<p>“It happened organically in breaking that first season, in contemplating this very different version of who Carrie White was. She’s a very different character than she’s ever been,” Flanagan said. “That character—starting her from a place of such kind of wide-eyed innocence and this core of her that’s so earnest and eager and kind and curious about the world. It broke my heart to imagine a version of the story that she didn’t survive.”</p>
<p>Even when he initially envisioned <em>Carrie</em> as a one-season show—much like his Netflix horror series had been, including <em>Midnight Mass</em> and <em>The Haunting of Hill House</em>—he was still planning to let her live.</p>
<p>“It felt right to me that she survived the story and [went] out into the world. I thought she’d earned it. Whether we’d ever follow her into that world was the open question, but we’d see her go,” he said.</p>
<p>Flanagan’s emotional connection to the character also played into <a href="https://gizmodo.com/5-burning-questions-we-have-about-the-ending-of-carrie-2000821940"><em>Carrie</em>‘s most controversial change</a>: the introduction of Emaline (Thalia Dudek). She astral-projects her way into Carrie’s life, encourages her emerging telekinesis, and then “takes the wheel” at the prom, reducing Carrie to a bystander even as her own powers are being used for mass destruction.</p>
<p>“Killing her at the end, or even just reducing her to a killer at the end, really worked against the purpose of the story for us, and the purpose of the story that Steve was excited about too,” Flanagan explained. “The revenge tragedy version of it, we thought, had already been told beautifully and exists in amber forever.”</p>
<p>In the original novel and most adaptations, Carrie—who’s been pushed to the brink by bullying and abuse—brutally kills her classmates and teachers. Then she goes home and kills her mother. Then she dies. In Flanagan’s take, Emaline is the main perpetrator of the prom disaster, and Adelaide takes care of Margaret. Carrie does inflict pain and accidentally causes at least one death (RIP, Tommy Ross), but at its core it’s “a different story,” the show’s creator explains.</p>
<p>“As we’re talking about cruelty and school violence and reconciling that tug of war in each of us between empathy and kindness and cruelty, what is the message we’re leaving people with about this? The message of kill your tormentors and then be dead [just] doesn’t feel like the story we’re telling,” Flanagan told THR.</p>
<p>“It feels like a horrible message to put into the world, today especially. All of us, Stephen King included, were very committed to a different road for this. It had a different heart to it by the end of it.”</p>
