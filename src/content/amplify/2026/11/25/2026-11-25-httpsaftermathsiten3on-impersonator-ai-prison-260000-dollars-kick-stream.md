<p>What goes around comes around, I suppose. Earlier this year, Kick streamer N3on, a <a href="https://www.mmafighting.com/2024/7/3/24191953/influencer-plead-for-forgiveness-after-disrespecting-nate-diaz-jorge-masvidal-n3on?ref=aftermath.site"><u>weasly little twig person</u></a> who spends all his time hanging out with UFC fighters for some reason, <a href="https://x.com/N3onOnYT/status/2097010767904084030?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2097010767904084030%7Ctwgr%5Ef96ff16748045bc298656295a61c172c2f5d21b1%7Ctwcon%5Es1_&amp;ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.dexerto.com%2Fkick%2Fn3on-defends-ai-version-of-himself-that-keeps-streaming-while-he-sleeps-3406809%2F&amp;ref=aftermath.site"><u>caught flak for experimenting with an AI version of himself</u></a> to keep his stream going indefinitely, even while he slept. Now a man from—where else—Florida has been sentenced to 47 months in prison for using AI to impersonate N3on.</p>
<p>This week, the US Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of Florida <a href="https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdfl/pr/port-st-lucie-man-sentenced-47-months-prison-using-ai-impersonate-popular-streamer?ref=aftermath.site"><u>announced</u></a> that David Anthony Sanchez, 36, of Port St. Lucie will be serving time as the result of a scheme to defraud N3on fans of approximately $260,000 in cryptocurrency.</p>
<p>Sanchez used AI to trick organizers of a memecoin project into believing that N3on was fully on board with their whole (also inadvisable) thing. Here’s how it worked, according to the Attorney’s Office:</p>
<blockquote>To carry out the scheme, Sanchez created online accounts designed to appear as though they belonged to N3on and communicated with project organizers while posing as both the streamer and his representative. Sanchez also executed agreements using N3on's identity and sent an AI-generated voice recording that mimicked the streamer’s voice to falsely confirm his participation in the project. </blockquote>
<blockquote>Deceived by Sanchez’s impersonation and fraudulent representations, the victims transferred approximately $260,000 in cryptocurrency to accounts he controlled.</blockquote>
<blockquote>Sanchez then converted a substantial portion of the cryptocurrency into U.S. dollars through financial transactions designed to conceal the nature, source, and ownership of the fraud proceeds. He used the money for his personal benefit.</blockquote>
<p>Sanchez has been ordered to pay restitution to the victims.</p>
<p>AI impersonation is far from uncommon, with scams that draw on the likenesses of massively popular figures like Jimmy “MrBeast” Donaldson <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4By-hTVtPc&amp;ref=aftermath.site"><u>as numerous as stars in the night sky</u></a>. Some stars, <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/nba/lebron-james-sues-makers-ai-sports-betting-app-saying-used-likeness-ni-rcna609973?ref=aftermath.site"><u>like LeBron James</u></a>, have sued over illicit, AI-generated usage of their image. But until—and perhaps even if—the AI bubble bursts, this is just the world we live in now. Rank cynicism all the way down.</p>
<p>At least you can <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/@realstableronaldo/video/7710452397708971277?ref=aftermath.site"><u>smack the phone out of the hand of a N3on lookalike in <em>GTA 6</em></u></a>. Might help you feel a little better.</p>
