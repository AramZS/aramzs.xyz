<p>Long before the Fallout series as it is known today, with epic battles between soldiers in power armor and vicious deathclaws presented in a wacky science fiction style, the now non-existent Black Isle Studios alone were responsible for the franchise and the direction it was headed in. <i>Fallout</i> and <i>Fallout 2</i> were the only mainline games in the series, and they were much different than Bethesda Game Studios’ take on the post-apocalyptic genre. They presented a severely unforgiving world, where the infamous fallout shelters known as vaults were seen as a safe haven from the outside world, social experiments and all – and said outside world was overflowing with misery and suffering at virtually every turn. Prior to the eventual and sudden closure of the company in 2003 as a result of financial difficulties within both Black Isle Studios itself and its parent company, Interplay Entertainment, there had been plans for a Fallout 3 following on from the first two installments in the series, internally codenamed Fallout: Van Buren. And by plans, I mean there was an entire design document and, by all accounts, a playable early build of the game.</p>
<p>Elements of Van Buren were later reused for <i>Fallout: New Vegas</i>, such as the antagonistic faction of slavers and warmongers known as Caesar’s Legion, the war between the New California Republic and the Brotherhood of Steel, and the primitive tribal population of post-apocalyptic America’s west. However, much of the original story and worldbuilding of Van Buren has been lost to time, and for years it was unclear if they would ever get their time to shine. Until July 2026, that is, when a surprise announcement made it clear that Obsidian Entertainment would be given another chance to make a new Fallout spinoff.</p>
<p>With Obsidian’s history of making their own installments of already established franchises, and with the company’s founding members having originally worked at Black Isle Studios, it seems like a no-brainer to give them the opportunity to craft more of their own stories within the world of Fallout. And with the popularity of the Fallout TV series for Amazon Prime Video, the interest in the franchise has never been stronger. The exact nature of the agreement between Bethesda and Obsidian may be unclear, but it seems more than likely that the same approach that was taken when <i>Fallout: New Vegas</i> was being developed will be taken again, with Obsidian more or less having free creative reign to do as they see fit, assuming they do not somehow infringe upon Bethesda’s own plans for future Fallout titles. I think it would be a tragic missed opportunity if Obsidian did not take the chance to incorporate more of Van Buren into their next Fallout game.</p>

<p>The rough outline of Van Buren would have seen the player assume control of a prisoner, whose race could be human, ghoul, or super mutant, depending on the player’s choice. This prisoner would then go on to discover that they had been infected with a virus whose effects would always have one of two outcomes; sterility or outright death. Van Buren would have seen this virus originate from the New Plague, a disease that does already exist in the Fallout universe as a sort of precursor to the Forced Evolutionary Virus. In the current Fallout universe as it stands, the New Plague ravaged the United States before the Great War of 2077, and attempts to develop a cure would eventually lead to the creation of the weaponized FEV responsible for the creation of super mutants and a number of wasteland horrors throughout the series. Van Buren instead focused on a version of the New Plague developed by a mad scientist of the NCR called Victor Presper, an antagonistic figure who manipulates the player throughout the story with the hope of enacting his grand plan. This plan includes gathering all infected individuals, as well as mutants and other wasteland residents considered “impure” by Presper, together so that he can carry out a nuclear holocaust and “cleanse” the wasteland.</p>
<p>The New Plague has links to the state of Colorado, and the cities of Boulder and Denver are also choice locations for the dispersal of the virus by Presper during Van Buren. But Presper and the New Plague aren’t the only interesting aspects of Van Buren’s story. Also in the design document for the cancelled game is a woman named Hecate, who seemingly found herself as the only survivor of a tribe slaughtered by Caesar’s Legion before going on to found her own tribe known as the Daughters of Hecate. This tribe acted as something of a counterpoint to the misogynistic, male-dominated Legion, a matriarchal faction of mostly women who ruled a number of other tribes in their territory, tribes that also paid tribute to the Daughters of Hecate for protection and out of an almost religious reverence to Hecate herself.</p>
<p>In Obsidian’s next Fallout game, the Daughters of Hecate seem like quite the fitting stand-in for the formidable villainous factions we have seen before, such as the Enclave and Caesar’s Legion. Colorado also seems to be a significant location tied to the future of Fallout, as the TV series has all but confirmed its third season will take place there. While it may end up being a location marked as off-limits by Bethesda so that they can utilise it themselves in future, it would also line up very nicely if Obsidian’s next Fallout was set in Colorado, with a revived Daughters of Hecate and a fresh depiction of Victor Presper’s crusade against the impurity of the wasteland. There are plenty of as-of-yet unused parts of Van Buren for Obsidian to choose from, and resurrecting Van Buren in this way would make for one of the most interesting Fallout games to date.</p>
