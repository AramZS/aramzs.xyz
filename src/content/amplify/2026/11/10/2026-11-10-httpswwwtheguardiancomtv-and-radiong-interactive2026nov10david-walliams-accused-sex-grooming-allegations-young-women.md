<h2 data-gu-name="standfirst">Comedian and former Britain’s Got Talent judge alleged to have groomed fans he met when they were as young as 15 and harassed women in the workplace over a number of years. He denies the allegations</h2>
<p>Alex had just returned from school and was watching The Simpsons in her bedroom when the phone rang. She distinctly recalls the first thing he said. “Hi, it’s <a data-component="auto-linked-tag" data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/david-walliams">David Walliams</a>. You know, off the telly!” It was January 2004. Alex was 15 and studying for her GCSEs. Walliams was 32 and a rising star with the comedy sketch show Little Britain.</p>
<p>A few days earlier, Alex had been to a fan event where Walliams had signed autographs as his character Lou. Wearing a frizzy wig and a black leather jacket, he looked up at Alex as her mother took a photograph. As she approached Walliams, Alex remembers him saying: “You’re a very attractive lady.”</p>
<p>With ambitions of becoming a comedy writer, Alex says she gave him some printouts of scripts she had written. She included a cover letter with her contact details, but she did not expect him to get in touch.</p>
<p>When he did, she was so excited that immediately after the call she made a record of the conversation in youthful scrawl across four sides of A4 paper. “David Walliams phoned me!!” she wrote. “He said he thought my scripts are VERY FUNNY!” She noted how Walliams had been surprised when she told him she was still at school, asking her: “Right, so you’re, what, 15, 16?”</p>
<p>The notes describe how they talked about her exams, as well as the enticing possibility of him getting tickets for her to watch a live performance of Little Britain. At the end of the call, he told her to stay in touch. He gave her his email address after she reassured him she would not pass it on to anyone. “He said that he trusts me,” she wrote.</p>
<p>Over the next two years, Alex says, she and Walliams spoke on the phone on a couple of occasions and exchanged multiple emails. She was amazed that a rising star of the comedy world was suggesting he could help her. By the time she was 17, she says, they were having sex.</p>

<p>Alex is one of multiple women who allege Walliams abused his power and position as a celebrity to prey on them for sexual purposes when they were teenagers or young women, an investigation by the Guardian and <a data-component="auto-linked-tag" data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/media/itv-news">ITV News</a> has found. Some, like Alex, say their interactions with Walliams were traumatising and have requested that pseudonyms be used to protect their anonymity.</p>
<p>Most say they were over 18 when they had interactions with Walliams. But a woman whom the Guardian is calling Jade alleges that, like Alex, she met him when she was 15. She says that weeks after he first made contact, he invited her to his house and initiated sexual contact.</p>
<p>The allegations extended to his conduct in the workplace, with several sources describing inappropriate behaviour towards women at both the prime-time show Britain’s Got Talent (BGT) and his publisher HarperCollins.</p>
<p>In September, HarperCollins announced it would no longer publish Walliams’ extensive back catalogue, <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2025/dec/19/david-walliams-dropped-by-publisher-over-alleged-inappropriate-behaviour">having dropped its bestselling author last year</a>. Few reasons were given for the seismic move. The Guardian and ITV News can now reveal that the publisher severed ties with the star author after concerns were raised about his behaviour over several years, including an allegation that he had inappropriate sexual relationship with a junior female employee and pursued another.</p>
<p>Walliams denies any wrongdoing. Earlier this year his lawyers took a case to the high court to try to stop the allegations being published. The case was dismissed, so now the women’s stories can be revealed.</p>
<p>In a letter addressed to journalists working on this investigation, Walliams wrote: “Looking back, I realise that at times I behaved thoughtlessly towards other people I had casual sexual relationships with.</p>
<p>“I realise I did not always take people’s feelings properly into account. That is something I regret. And whilst a power imbalance is inevitable when one party is famous, abusing that power is something else entirely and it is something that I never did.”</p>
<p>He added: “I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law, but I am devastated that some of the women I met 10 to 20 years ago have now expressed hurt or anger towards me.”</p>
<h2>Journey to success</h2>
<p>A multimillionaire and OBE recipient, Walliams, 55, is one of the most famous comedians and writers in the UK. He built a career on his cheeky, flamboyant persona, which has permeated his children’s books, comedy work and the decade he spent as a much-loved judge on BGT.</p>
<p>Back in 2004, Walliams was at the beginning of his journey to success. His big break had been Little Britain, the provocative sketch show he created for BBC Radio 4 with his comedy partner, Matt Lucas. The show, which later moved to television, featured now infamous characters including the perma-tracksuited Vicky Pollard and the interdependent duo Lou and Andy. In December 2004, <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2004/dec/23/media.bbc">it won the top accolade</a> at the British Comedy Awards.</p>
<p>At the awards ceremony, Walliams was captured on film dressed in a black dinner jacket, seemingly utterly relaxed and enjoying himself. He threw his head back in laughter as the compere, the TV chatshow host Jonathan Ross, opened the show with a joke pointedly referencing him: “The comedy awards is 15 years old – 15 years old! And like most 15-year-olds, it’s drunk, it’s unpredictable and it’s probably going home with David Walliams.”</p>
<p>Ross, responding through lawyers, said he did not write the joke himself.</p>

<h2>‘Come to my hotel’</h2>
<p>Standing under the bright television lights, Sarah smiled as she posed next to the BGT judges. He had called her, Sarah and another friend up to the BGT judges’ table for a photograph.</p>

<p>Walliams continued to message Sarah and soon after the filming he invited her to stay at his house as she was intending to come to London, where she was considering going to study. Excited to spend more time with a celebrity and curious to see how someone so rich lived, she accepted the invitation. “I said to him I’ll only come on the condition my friend also comes.” She says Walliams agreed.</p>
<p>Later that month, Walliams sent Sarah an email. He was 44. “Are you looking forward to next weekend…?” he wrote, signing off with a kiss. Sarah recalls thinking it would be a “fun little weekend. That was literally all I thought it was gonna be. I was so innocent.”</p>
<p>Sarah says Walliams booked the two 18-year-olds a spa day at the Bulgari hotel in Knightsbridge, central London. When the women checked the spa’s website, they found the prices eye-wateringly expensive.</p>
<p>Sarah remembers being equally impressed when they arrived at Supernova Heights. Walliams gave them the tour: the huge kitchen island, the grand bathtub in the bedroom and the awards in his office.</p>
<p>He made them pasta for lunch, she recalls. When Sarah got out her phone to check if there was food in her teeth, she says Walliams told her: “No photos.”</p>
<p>That evening, they ordered pizza, picked out some DVDs and the young women drank wine while they all stretched out on Walliams’ L-shaped sofa. Both women got progressively more drunk, Sarah says.</p>
<p>When she got up to go to the bathroom, Sarah remembers that Walliams followed her. She says he then put his hands around her waist and moved in to kiss her. “I had a boyfriend at the time, so I was just like: ‘Absolutely not.’” When she looked at him, she remembers thinking: “You’re the same age as my mum.”</p>
<p>At another point in the evening, she recalls Walliams stroking her lower leg while they were sprawled on the sofa.</p>
<p>The two women kept drinking. Sarah became so drunk, she says, that she struggled to walk. She alleges that Walliams took her by the arm and helped her up the stairs. The women had previously laid out their things in his two spare bedrooms. But as they passed the rooms, it became clear to Sarah that Walliams was leading her up more stairs, towards the top floor, his bedroom.</p>
<p>Sarah’s friend intervened, she says, and led her down the stairs to the spare room. Sarah says she remembers how, despite having drunk so much, she became suddenly on her guard. “When you’re in an environment like that and if something happens, you kind of automatically remember what’s going on.”</p>
<p>She believes Walliams had been taking her upstairs because he wanted something sexual to happen, despite her being inebriated and despite her having told him she was not interested. If it were not for her friend pulling her away, she believes, she would have ended up in Walliams’ bedroom.</p>
<p>Instead, the women slept in the spare rooms. Aged 18 and in a city they did not know, Sarah says she could not think of another option. “I could have run out the door and I wouldn’t have known where I was. I wouldn’t have known who to call. I wouldn’t have known what to do.”</p>
<p>In the morning, the two women started to discuss what had happened, but it felt odd to talk about it while they were in Walliams’ house. Before they left, Sarah and her friend wrote a note thanking Walliams for letting them stay and went to the Bulgari hotel spa, where she recalls an employee telling them that other female friends of Walliams had been sent there in the past.</p>
<p>Sarah recalls thinking at the time: “What a weird old man, like, trying his luck.”</p>
<p>Looking back now, at 29, she feels differently. “I think now I’m older I can realise how uncomfortable I actually was.”</p>
<p>She says: “If you have celebrity status, why can you not get women closer to your age range? … Is it, like, for manipulation? What do we have in common with a 44-year-old man?”</p>
<p>Walliams’ lawyers say he “refutes the suggestion that he got [Sarah] drunk to take advantage of her”. Although he recalls they drank wine together, they say, he does not recall anyone being drunk. They add that he did not force himself on to Sarah and that there was no sexual contact with her. Both women were friendly the next day and did not raise any issue with Walliams, they say.</p>
<p>The lawyers add that he denies targeting teenage women and using his celebrity status to manipulate them to have sex with him.</p>
<h2>Threatening letter</h2>
<p>Eight months after the incident in the house, Sarah was walking through a supermarket when she got a call from her friend with a startling development.</p>
<p>The friend had remained in touch with Walliams and been to watch more BGT shows. While there, she had met two other young women whom she saw go backstage. Later, she had contacted them to tell them about what had happened in his house. And then she had received a threatening letter from Walliams’ lawyers.</p>

<p>The letter was written by Jenny Afia, a renowned lawyer specialising in reputation management for the law firm Schillings. Afia is best known now for representing the Duchess of Sussex. <p>The legal letter demanded a written undertaking within 24 hours that the woman would not talk about her experience with Walliams. She had no representation and Sarah says she was shaken by the threat.</p>
<p>“If that was me, I’d have freaked out,” Sarah says. Looking back, she wonders if such action had a chilling effect on other women speaking out. “I think all this should have come out a long time ago.”</p>

<p>He continued to entertain the public on BGT and his children’s books dominated the market. Many were developed into TV and <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2019/jan/28/the-boy-in-the-dress-by-david-walliams-to-become-stage-musical">stage shows</a>. He sold Supernova Heights and moved to a grander property.</p>
<p>He continued to message young women on social media. The Guardian has spoken to seven other women who say Walliams contacted them on social media platforms around this time. One was an 18-year-old college student at the time. Six were models who were between 19 and 22 when he made contact. None had a sexual encounter with him, but all the women feel the approach was odd, given Walliams was so famous.</p>
<p>In the course of the investigation, the Guardian and ITV News contacted more than 100 people. A few spoke positively of their interactions with Walliams, saying he was always professional and pleasant, but many expressed concerns about his behaviour towards women.</p>
<p>By 2020, Walliams’ contributions to culture were starting to be reassessed. In the wake of the Black Lives Matter movement, his work with Little Britain was attracting criticism. The sketch show was <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2020/jun/09/little-britain-removed-from-bbc-iplayer-netflix-and-britbox-blackface">removed from various streaming platforms</a> over concerns about Walliams’ and Lucas’s use of blackface, something for which both men apologised.</p>
<p>More criticism came when a clip from Little Britain’s Australian tour in 2007 resurfaced. It captured Walliams, in character as the washed-up TV presenter Des Kaye, pulling a 16-year-old boy on to the stage and proceeding to kiss him on the lips and pull down his trousers and underwear. Walliams had been aware that the act, also performed at other shows, was controversial even at the time. In a book documenting the UK tour of the same show, Walliams was quoted as saying: “If we did the show in America, I’d probably get lawsuits from everyone I molested, but in Bournemouth I can somehow get away with it.”</p>
<p>His children’s books came under fire, too. In July 2020, the activist and food writer Jack Monroe posted a viral thread on Twitter criticising them as “sneering classist fatshaming nonsense”.</p>
<p>The day after that post came a series of tweets from Sarah’s friend who had met Walliams backstage at BGT and stayed in his house. In the posts, which went viral, she wrote: “Why did I ever think it was ‘normal’ for a 44yr male to invite four girls aged 16/17 into his dressing room without any adults – just his PA, who would bring refreshments once in a while. And the fact that everyone at that TV production company would see it and say NOTHING.”</p>
<h2>Dropped by publisher</h2>
<p>Inside the offices of his then publisher, HarperCollins, the tweets from Monroe and Sarah’s friend were a cause for concern among staff. Several people remember managers reassuring them that the flurry of criticism on Twitter would pass.</p>
<p>Signed to HarperCollins in 2008, Walliams was one of the publisher’s biggest success stories. For years, his titles were the bestselling books in the publisher’s catalogue. He has sold more than 26m copies in the UK alone, worth £156m.</p>
<p>Several employees suggested this success protected him for years.</p>

The young woman was later sent to Walliams’ house alone and they began what Walliams’ lawyers describe a “consensual sexual” relationship. At one point, Walliams sent her flowers to the office.</p>
<p>The young woman later said that although consensual, looking back she felt the relationship was an “abuse of power”, according to multiple sources.</p>
<p>Around the office, women started to warn each other not to go to Walliams’ house alone.</p>
<p>Katie, a former employee, says she went to HR after a young colleague in her mid-20s showed her messages that Walliams had been sending her, in which he was asking her out.</p>
<p>He had met that young woman at a promotional event at a school for World Book Day, then had obtained her email address and invited her to dinner. The woman ignored the email. Months later, he tried again.</p>
<p>“It’s just not appropriate for an author to be messaging a member of staff like that. She definitely wasn’t pleased about it,” Katie says.</p>
<p>Lawyers for Walliams say he messaged the young woman at a time when he was “heartbroken and lonely after his divorce and was hoping to meet someone to spend time with”.</p>

<p>More concerns bounced around the HarperCollins’ office when, in 2022, the Guardian reported on <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2022/nov/10/david-walliams-recorded-derogatory-remarks-britains-got-talent-bgt-contestants">derogatory and sexually explicit remarks</a> that Walliams had made about contestants on BGT. The story caused a storm. Walliams apologised and said they were private conversations. Nevertheless, it was the first time the media had reported on his inappropriate conduct and it led to him being dropped as a judge after 10 years on the show.</p>
<p>Around the same time, the children’s section of HarperCollins brought in a new head, Cally Poplak. After hearing concerns about Walliams’ alleged inappropriate behaviour from staff members, Poplak initiated an inquiry in late 2023. At least five female members of staff discussed their experiences and managers were told about the sexual relationship Walliams had with the junior employee.</p>
<p>The investigation wrapped up in 2024 with no consequences for Walliams. For one woman who spoke to the inquiry, it was the end of her career at the company. She was reportedly given a five-figure payout and signed a separation agreement with a strict non-disclosure clause.</p>
<p>For nearly two years after the investigation concluded, the company continued to publish Walliams’ books, only <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2025/dec/19/david-walliams-dropped-by-publisher-over-alleged-inappropriate-behaviour">dropping him in December last year</a> after a change of management. He said at the time that the issues raised in the inquiry were never brought to his attention.</p>
<p>Then, in September, it was announced that it would no longer publish any of his works.</p>
<p>Lawyers for Walliams say he denies the allegations of sexual harassment and inappropriate behaviour in the workplace.</p>
<p>At the time, a HarperCollins spokesperson said the company had decided not to publish any new titles by Walliams and that they took “employee wellbeing extremely seriously”. When approached for this investigation, they said they could add nothing further.</p>
<p>When news broke that Walliams had been dropped by his publisher, many of the women he had had encounters with held their breath. Some thought this would be the catalyst for detailed allegations to come out. But there was no moment of reckoning.</p>
<p>For some of the women who have encountered Walliams over the years, the silence left them angry.</p>
<p>Jade, who said she had met up with Walliams when she was 15, is now a mother. She has banned her younger children from bringing his books home from school. “And I look at my daughter, who’s not far off [being 15] now, and to think of a 36-year-old man being any way like that with her makes me feel sick,” she says.</p>
<p>“It angers me that he is still on the TV, as famous as he is. He used that fame to get women.”</p>
