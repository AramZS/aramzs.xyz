<h3><br/>THE HEADLINER:<br/>The incremental evolution of indie rock in 50 songs (1980-2026)</h3>
<p>As you may have heard, this week Pitchfork published a list of the <a href="https://pitchfork.com/story/the-100-best-indie-rock-albums-of-all-time/">100 Best Indie Rock Albums of All Time</a>, and naturally, <a href="https://stevenhyden.substack.com/p/ask-a-music-critic-can-you-parse">lots</a> <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/indieheads/comments/1xrtxax/pitchfork_the_100_best_indie_rock_albums_of_all/">of</a> <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DfkHugoARu-/">people</a> <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DfhpP_DjEgw/?img_index=1">have</a> <a href="https://substack.com/search/pitchfork%20indie%20rock%20list">thoughts</a>. That’s par for the course with any list Pitchfork puts together, but this one has sparked debates on three fronts. There’s the usual “why isn’t [Album X] on there?” complaints, and the usual “that album doesn’t even belong in this genre” complaints, but in this case there’s also the overarching existential question of what that genre actually is—and even <a href="https://pitchfork.com/story/what-is-indie-rock-we-asked-33-artists-labels-and-critics/">with 33 experts weighing in</a>, we’re no closer to a definitive answer.</p>
<p>All the Pitchfork discourse reminded me of a playlist exercise I did for a start-up website called <a href="http://the-dowsers.com/">The Dowsers</a> back in 2018. At the time, indie rock—like R&amp;B—had become a genre whose modern iteration bore almost zero aesthetic relation to its original incarnation. In fact, indie rock holds the rare distinction of being perhaps the only genre that gradually mutated into the <em>complete ideological inverse</em> of everything it once stood for, while still retaining its name. As an alternately tuneful and experimental offshoot of hardcore, indie rock began as a fuzz-blasted assault on both the sleek veneer and materialist values of glossy ’80s mainstream pop, but by the dawn of the 2020s, it had become difficult to tell the difference between what was considered modern indie rock and the smooth sounds emanating from your parents’ favourite Lite-FM station.</p>
<p>In effect, the term “indie rock” had been rendered meaningless on both a musical and philosophical level, given that once-rigid aesthetic divisions had dissolved in the post-digital era and artists on all levels of the industry totem pole became a slave to streaming stats. So with the playlist linked below, I tried to answer this question: How did the popular conception of indie rock shift from the freak-scene fury of Black Flag and Butthole Surfers to the soft-focus pop of Bon Iver and Tame Impala?</p>
<p>Let me be clear: this playlist is not a rundown of my personal indie-rock favourites (a lot of my all-timers don’t make appearances here, though many do). Rather, this is an objective chronological survey of the songs that both established what indie-rock is for their respective generations and redefined what it could be. It’s an attempt to give something meaningless a little bit of meaning again.</p>
<p>Each track featured below is a link in a chain, one that built upon the innovations of its immediate predecessors and subtly pushed the ball forward into new directions, to demonstrate that, say, the frantic agit-punk of the Minutemen and the placid dad-rock of The War on Drugs are indeed part of the same continuum. Ideally, if you listen to this playlist front-to-back, you won’t sense a dramatic vibe shift on a track-by-track basis, but over long stretches, you’ll have travelled very far from your point of origin.</p>
<p>Alas, in trying to chart a linear course through four decades of knotty stylistic evolutions, I had to bypass a number of significant subgenres (sorry, math rock, post-rock, disco-punk, electroclash, folktronica, etc). And with few exceptions, the playlist skews heavily to North American artists, because I’ve always seen UK indie as a separate beast, with different influences and legacies (though the worlds occasionally did overlap).</p>
<p>That said, since I first drafted this playlist in 2018, indie rock has taken a noticeable turn back to something more rootsy and ragged, and I have extended the tracklist below to reflect this. So whether your idea of indie rock is Mission of Burma or Phoebe Bridgers, let this playlist anchor the current moment to an era when a band could really be your life, and not just an algorithm-generated background soundtrack to one.</p>
