<p>A Manhattan judge has upheld a city board’s decision to enact a two-year rent freeze for roughly 1 million New York City apartments after landlords sued to reverse the first-of-its-kind directive.</p>
<p>In an <a href="https://iapps.courts.state.ny.us/nyscef/ViewDocument?docIndex=DleFpuB2/BIfs_PLUS_DyMNjitw==">order</a> Wednesday, Judge Brendan Lantry rejected the bid to block <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/nyc-rent-guidelines-board-approves-2-year-rent-freeze-fulfilling-mamdani-campaign-pledge">the rent freeze</a> for stabilized apartments approved by the city’s Rent Guidelines Board in June. The pause in rent increases fulfills of Mayor Zohran Mamdani's campaign pledges. Lantry wrote that although the freeze “may have detrimental impacts on the New York City residential real estate industry,” the court did not have the authority to overturn the decision.</p>
<p>A group of landlords represented by Randy Mastro — a former first deputy mayor under previous Mayor Eric Adams — argued that the board had conducted a “sham” process that ignored owners’ rising expenses in order to deliver on one of Mamdani’s signature campaign pledges. Mastro asked a judge to order the board to hold a new vote.</p>
<p>Mamdani and city lawyers denied any interference in the process and countered that the board arrived at its decision independently.</p>
<p>City Hall and Mastro did not immediately respond to requests for comment and Mastro did not say whether he planned to appeal.</p>
<p>Legal Aid Society attorney Ellen Davidson, whose organization intervened in the case on behalf of tenants, said she was pleased with the ruling but not surprised.</p>
<p>“The law is clear that courts do not have the power to override the decisions of the Rent Guidelines Board,” she said.</p>
<p>The closely watched decision reflected the unusual attention paid to the board over the past year, after Mamdani made a rent freeze a key plank of his victorious campaign. The board’s nine members are each appointed by the mayor for multiyear terms, but the panel is considered an independent body that analyzes data on landlord and tenant expenses and listens to public testimony before holding its annual vote.</p>
<p>Mamdani named six of the board’s members, but stopped publicly calling for the rent freeze after taking office in January.</p>
<p>In an effort to find evidence that City Hall swayed the board’s decision, Mastro requested communications between top city officials, including Mamdani, and the board members and staff. Lantry<a href="https://gothamist.com/news/judge-orders-nyc-officials-to-turn-over-phone-messages-keeps-rent-freeze-in-place"> granted the request</a> and ordered officials to turn over emails and texts.</p>
<p>Mastro released roughly a dozen of those exchanges. They <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/lawyer-challenging-nyc-rent-freeze-releases-mamdani-officials-emails-and-text-messages">largely showed</a> City Hall officials informing new members of their appointments, coordinating meeting dates and planning a presentation on a new “true cost of living” report not previously considered by the board.</p>
<p>Mastro said the materials were “shocking” in a letter to the judge. Top city lawyer Steven Banks said there was nothing “improper” about the communications.</p>
<p>At an unrelated news conference Oct. 1, Mamdani sought to turn the tables on Mastro and cited <a href="https://gothamist.com/news/attorney-trying-to-overturn-nyc-rent-freeze-tried-influencing-previous-vote-ex-board-chair-says">reporting from Gothamist</a> that revealed how Mastro himself has attempted to influence the board’s vote during his time as top deputy to Adams in 2025.</p>
<p>Former Rent Guidelines Board Chair Doug Apple told Gothamist that Mastro had spoken with him by phone last year and “advocated for a low vote” when the board was considering an increase up to 4.75%.</p>
<p>Mastro confirmed that he had spoken with Apple, but only to say how he and Adams “disagreed” with the board’s eventual decision to raise rent by 3% that year.</p>
