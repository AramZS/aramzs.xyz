<div><div class="BaseText-fEwdHD SplitScreenContentHeaderDek-juwmcJ hhRWTn bFhTNF">With massive upsets in deep red states and stunning wins in Texas, Democrats have redrawn the political map—and decisively taken the House and Senate.</div><figure><img alt="Donald Trump." class="ResponsiveImageContainer-dkeESL cQPiWi responsive-image__image" src="https://media.wired.com/photos/6ae8e5568f654d90749164d0/master/w_2560%2Cc_limit/GettyImages-2303174995.jpg"/><figcaption>Photograph: Finn Gomez/Getty Images</figcaption></figure><div class="body__inner-container"><p><span class="body lead-in-text">Democrats stormed through</span> key <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/the-internets-conspiracy-theorists-are-desperate-for-voter-fraud/">battleground races</a> across the US in their battle to capture the House and Senate on Tuesday night, marking a decisive repudiation of <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/this-is-what-the-gops-final-midterms-push-looks-like/">President Donald Trump's</a> second term.</p><p class="paywall">The scale of victory for <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/a-right-wing-think-tank-has-a-plan-to-prevent-democrats-from-being-seated-in-congress/">Democrats</a>—in some races, the party flipped seats that Trump won by double digits just two years ago—marked a historic night that exceeded the party's highest expectations.</p><div></div><div></div><p class="paywall">Democrats pulled off arguably the greatest upset in <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/elon-musks-ties-to-texas-republicans-go-much-deeper-than-the-midterms/">Texas</a>, where James Talarico defeated scandal-plagued Republican state attorney general Ken Paxton and Gina Hinojosa defeated Greg Abbott for the governorship, despite the <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/elon-musk-is-almost-single-handedly-funding-the-republicans-midterms-push/">millions and millions of dollars spent by billionaire Elon Musk</a> on behalf of the Republicans. No Democrat had won a statewide seat election since 1994.</p><div class="Container-ioqmpI jzsvml"></div><p class="paywall">Democrats also seized other critical Senate races on election night, including in Kansas, South Carolina, Iowa, and North Carolina, while retaining control of seats in Georgia and New Hampshire, according to the <a class="text link" href="https://apnews.com/live/midterm-election-2026">Associated Press</a>.</p><p class="paywall">At the White House, stunned Trump officials tell WIRED that the high gas prices and increased cost of living brought on by the Iran war were the principal reasons for widespread GOP losses.</p><p class="paywall">“If any good comes out of tonight for the Republican party, it’s that no Republican president will ever listen to imbeciles like Mark Levin and Marc Thiessen again,” one source close to the White House said of two commentators who had urged Trump to prolong the Iran war.</p><p class="paywall">Exit polling showing massive defections from other parts of the 2024 Trump coalition show there was more to the losses than that, with Latino voters turning to Democrats as the administration pursued brutal and unpopular immigration policies.</p><p class="paywall">Some officials, who were granted anonymity to speak candidly, privately laid the blame at the feet of the president for taking the nation into the Middle East conflict, which he wrongly assumed would be over in a matter of weeks and has continued with no clear end in sight.</p><p class="paywall">“President Trump was not on the ballot, and it’s proven that Trump voters show up when he is on the ballot,” a White House official said in a statement, even as Trump had implored his base on the trail to pretend that he was on the ballot.</p><p class="paywall">“Also, candidate quality matters, and President Trump did a better job promoting many of the candidates on the ballot than the candidates did themselves,” the official added.</p></div><div class="body__inner-container"><p class="paywall">Many of the losing candidates were hand-picked by Trump, who forced out incumbents like Texas senator John Cornyn and blessed candidates like Darline Graham, the sister of late senator Lindsey Graham, who ran a dismal campaign in South Carolina.</p><p class="paywall">Still, other Trump officials have cast about for other scapegoats, blaming a failed redistricting effort as well as candidate quality.</p><p class="paywall">Republicans embarked on a strategy earlier this year to redraw numerous congressional maps, as part of a gambit that <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/its-the-presidents-fault-the-white-house-blame-game-starts-early-ahead-of-the-midterms/">White House aides</a> thought could yield 10 to 15 House seat pickups if Trump voters turned out. The downside, political scientists pointed out, was that it made seats harder to defend, as while there were more Republican districts, each was less Republican.</p><p class="paywall">On election night, the strategy failed to yield results. Democrats won at least three House seats in Florida that Republicans had redrawn, as well as the seat in North Carolina.</p><p class="paywall">The closest thing to a bright spot for Republicans came in the Senate race in Florida, which became abruptly competitive in the final days of the midterm campaign. In the end, Ashley Moody barely fended off progressive candidate Democrat Angie Nixon.</p><p class="paywall">As the likely control of the House and Senate became clear, top House Democrats separately ended the night relishing the prospect of mounting an aggressive oversight campaign against the Trump administration.</p><p class="paywall">Robert Garcia, the incoming chair of the House oversight committee, issued a <a class="external-link text link" href="https://bsky.app/profile/oversightdemocrats.bsky.social/post/3mzhfqwxn6c2g">two-word statement</a>: “Lawyer up.”</p></div></div>
