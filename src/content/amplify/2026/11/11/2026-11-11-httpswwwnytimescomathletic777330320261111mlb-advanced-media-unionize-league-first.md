<p>MLB Advanced Media employees have unionized, in what may be a first for a major U.S. sports league. (Hunter Martin / Getty Images)</p>
<p>In what appears to be a first for a major sports league in the U.S., a group of workers inside the commissioner’s office at Major League Baseball has unionized.</p>
<p>A collection of 127 editorial employees at Major League Baseball Advanced Media, the arm of baseball’s central office that operates the league’s official website and social media channels, has organized under the Writers Guild of America East, per a joint announcement from MLB and the guild on Wednesday.</p>
<p>The employees in the new bargaining unit include producers who post and edit content for news stories and social channels in multiple languages. But one prominent group is not included.</p>
<p>Team beat writers as well as national writers for MLB.com — the league’s public-facing, regularly bylined writers and columnists who author news stories — are not a part of the new union. WGAE and MLB declined to comment as to why.</p>
<p>An election to certify the union has already been held and MLB has agreed to recognize the union.</p>
<p>“The Guild is excited to welcome more workers from the sports industry into the labor movement,” Tom Fontana, President of the Writers Guild of America East, said in a statement. “The official MLB website and app produces quality content for baseball fans around the world, and this bargaining unit now has earned a seat at the table with Major League Baseball to negotiate a fair first union contract.”</p>
<p>MLB also issued a statement acknowledging the new bargaining relationship.</p>
<p>“Immediately upon being informed by the WGAE of their efforts to organize our content staff, MLB worked cooperatively and in good faith to identify the appropriate bargaining unit and to respect the rights of the affected employees to decide whether to unionize,” the league said. “We look forward to working with the WGAE as their new representative.”</p>
<p>The editorial employees’ unionization comes at a time when MLB’s future is uncertain because of a different labor matter.</p>
<p>Baseball’s owners <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7731128/2026/10/29/mlb-lockout-guide-league-union-labor-questions-answered/">plan to lock out the players</a> in December in a fight over economics. The expected stoppage has the potential to cancel games in 2027, leaving employees across the baseball industry anxious about the possibility of furloughs or layoffs.</p>
<p>WGAE said the organizing process took about a year. While sports leagues are used to dealing with their respective players’ unions, central league offices — their corporate headquarters — are not typically unionized workplaces.</p>
<p>In baseball, umpires are unionized league employees, but they work on the field and are not considered a part of the commissioner’s office. The unionized players, meanwhile, are team employees.</p>
<p>Per WGAE, the groups inside MLB that are represented in the new union include editorial producers, the social media teams in English and Spanish, MLB Español, Pipeline (which is dedicated to prospects) and “NewsDesk.” Japanese-language content producers are included as well.</p>
