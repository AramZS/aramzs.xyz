<p>If there’s a reliable prediction to make each year it’s that the International Energy Agency will underestimate the annual output of solar photovoltaic panels.</p>
<p>That remarkably consistent undershoot is a focus of an upcoming book by Netherlands-based energy researcher, <a href="https://www.tue.nl/en/research/researchers/auke-hoekstra">Auke Hoekstra</a>, “Power to the People”.</p>
<p>In social media posts, including on <a data-id="https://bsky.app/profile/aukehoekstra.bsky.social/post/3mzfwqemnpc2l" data-type="link" href="https://bsky.app/profile/aukehoekstra.bsky.social/post/3mzfwqemnpc2l">Bluesky</a>, Hoekstra said he had started noting the discrepancy almost two decades ago.</p>
<p>Back in 2008, for instance, the IEA was forecasting annual output by 2030 of solar PV that is now churning out of – mostly Chinese – factories every couple of days, if that.</p>
<p>The actual output, shown in the dark green line, is almost perpendicular, while the annual IEA updates in their <a data-id="https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2025" data-type="link" href="https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2025">World Energy Outlook</a> reports are generally forecast fairly flat production growth in coming years.</p>

<p>As Hoeskstra notes, he has used the IEA’s “stated policies” scenario because its “current policies” scenario is even more pessimistic.</p>
<p>“It’s clear reality keeps surprising the IEA,” he said, in a note accompanying the chart.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2025">IEA’s latest WEO</a>, released last November, preceded the soaring price of oil in the wake of the US-Israel war on Iran that broke out in February.</p>
<p>Even so, the agency was also expecting a shift in solar panel demand to sunnier parts of the world:</p>
<p>“Mapping the new geography of demand onto the distribution of global energy resources reveals that, by 2035, 80% of energy consumption growth occurs in regions with high quality solar irradiation,” it stated.</p>
<p>“This is a sharp contrast with the past decade when medium- to low-solar regions drove half of the growth,” it said. “This helps explain the swift uptake of solar technologies in our scenarios, as well as the rise in demand for cooling.”</p>
<p>Put down your glasses, in other words, there are solar surprises yet to come.</p>
