<p>In the not-so-golden year before the pandemic, no comer had figured out the antidote to Justin Bieber’s arena-scaled pop techno, Ed Sheeran’s big beat scruffy acoustic ditties, and Ariana Grande’s ebullient variety show plundering of sounds. Then a pair of siblings perhaps influenced by Lorde and Kate Bush and goodness knows what else released what sounded like demos in search of Dev Hynes for a round of Pilates. I had<a href="https://thesinglesjukebox.com/billie-eilish-you-should-see-me-in-a-crown/"> no idea how to respond</a> to <em>When We Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go</em> when the first tracks leaked in 2018; one line in my TSJ blurb looked prescient: “Watch out, assholes, for if you give her a little power she’ll destroy you all.” Not true == <a href="https://thesinglesjukebox.com/billie-eilish-all-the-good-girls-go-to-hell/">I did better</a> 11 (!) years ago — but it did predict how almost seven years later no one has even tried to approximate the texture of Billie Eilish’s rocky wormhole. When she and Finneas O’Connell wanted <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d5gf9dXbPi0">a big ol’ Diane Warren-type ballad</a>, Grande owed them nothing.</p>
<p>Finneas and Eilish taught pop audiences to listen differently, to pay attention to the whorls of distortion, the hacked tree limbs of melody, and the thunk of modest percussion over which Eilish offered what would be anyone else’s scratch vocals. Count me among those who learned how to listen. By recording the quietest goth album in history, Eilish already performed a service; she and Finneas wrote a bunch of tunes as catchy as Robert Smith’s but observational and meditative like his didn’t pretend to be.</p>
<p>I liked <em>Happier Than Ever</em> better than the debut because as noted she had taught me how to listen. By the time of last year’s <em>Hit Me Hard and Soft</em> she and O’Connell got <a href="https://humanizingthevacuum.wordpress.com/2024/05/20/liberated-billie-eilish-and-chief-keef/">friskier with lyrics and beats</a>; being the less famous half of the partnership allows Finneas the anonymity to break loose, as <a href="https://archive.ph/J8kEK">Carl Wilson noted</a>.</p>
