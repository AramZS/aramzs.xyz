title: 'Classified NSA network is still down, leaving some staff working from home'
---
<p>An internal <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/classified-nsa-network-went-no-indication-was-attack-sources-say-rcna609859">classified computer network used by the National Security Agency remained down </a>on Thursday, resulting in some employees being ordered to work from home for a third day in a row, according to two people with knowledge of the matter.</p>
<p>The network first went down on Tuesday due to a technical problem that <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/classified-nsa-network-went-no-indication-was-attack-sources-say-rcna609859">occurred when maintenance on the network was underway.</a> Both sources told NBC News the issue has created an inconvenience, but it has not affected the NSA’s work collecting electronic intelligence around the world and overseeing cyber security.</p>
<p>There is no sign that the outage was caused by a cyber attack, the sources said.</p>
<p>Even though the NSA’s network issue came amid an unusual series of mass outages that have wreaked havoc <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/flights-air-traffic-delays-groundstop-faa-duffy-rcna609545">on air travel</a>, banks and emails over<a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/transportation-secretary-apologize-nationwide-ground-stop-airports-rcna609397"> the past few days, </a>U.S. officials have said say there is no indication that the technical problems were caused by a coordinated cyberattack.</p>
<p>Non-essential staff at the NSA are being told to check in with their supervisors and find out if they need to come into the agency’s headquarters at Fort Meade, Maryland. Otherwise, they are advised to work from home, according to a telephone information line for the workforce and the two sources familiar with the matter.</p>
<p>While the NSA does not release the precise number of agency employees, former officials have said the agency employs tens of thousands of people. In 2024,<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20240930185337/https:/militarycompatibility.maryland.gov/Documents/NSA-Slicksheet_FINAL.pdf"> a Maryland state government document </a>put the number of NSA employees at 39,000. The agency has since made some workforce reductions as part of broader cutbacks across the federal government.</p>
<p>The outage also affected a work site in Georgia that handles the NSA’s electronic intelligence work in the Middle East, the sources said.</p>

<p>With powerful eavesdropping and code-breaking tools as well as a well-educated workforce, the NSA <a href="https://urldefense.com/v3/__https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/2026/01/20/open-hearing-nomination-of-lieutenant-general-joshua-m-rudd-to-be-director-of-the-national-security-agency/__;!!PIZeeW5wscynRQ!tF7efsb5bekeCP56FMq8K-l4MqdXYJ3xZBAnCkq7b5jAaPIKfu0HAtUvqsD5Of46xzlR5fFkog8gA67ttQPyQCYOShdwvneVHiNnTL9N$">prides itself as a center of digital expertise</a>.</p>
<p>While it is not uncommon for government networks to suffer from brief disruptions or outages, such a high-profile technical problem threatens to tarnish that reputation the agency has built, according to former officials and experts.</p>
