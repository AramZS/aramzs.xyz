title: The market is down
---
<p>Talk to anyone who has to work for a living and this is the common complaint you will hear atleast once in a conversation <em>“the market is down”</em>/”<em>the market is bad”</em> followed by fears of AI, job loss, increased prices and unaffordable homes. This conversation can be had without cultural and geographic barriers and across all age groups, more so with the young. Yet, Anthropic debuted with the best ever IPO worth over a trillion dollars yesterday. Technology that was meant to connect people and make our lives easier has slowly inched to the point of making copyrights and human labor irrelevant, focusing on an opaque chatbot do the work as one sits back and copy-pastes the output.</p>
<p>The entire nature of human survival has been reduced to precarity with the hopes that it remains this way or even becomes worse, because that's what a trillion dollar valuation for an AI company signals. A world where art isn't created for fun, where reading is relegated to information extraction and everything is a dashboard for pleasure or resource maximization. It's a time of great technological upheaval, yes, but makes one wonder how life was just 30 years ago when the chains of Wi-Fi and always on communications were still not delivered to the masses.</p>
<p>The market is down, but only for you and me. For the super-super rich, it's an upmarket time, a time of cheap slaves and plentiful labor. A world where our voice doesn't matter as much as long as they can hunker in their bunkers and slow drip us the promise of a capitalist salvation. And all of this alone, because who can plan for a family now? Children are the ultimate status symbol in this new world economy. With families successfully pulverized and the social contract broken, we sit alone trying to consume as little as possible for the incoming doom cycle to subside. The market is down and so are we.</p>
