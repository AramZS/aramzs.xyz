<p>The news outlet revealed paying Trump adviser Jason Miller, former Trump counselor Kellyanne Conway and former Harris aide Adrienne Elrod, after The Post reported on Miller’s income.</p>
<p>Semafor on Friday updated 11 articles and newsletters with disclosures that it paid three political consultants — including longtime Trump adviser Jason Miller — for help securing guests for its events.</p>
<p>The articles mentioned Miller or two other political figures employed by Semafor: Kellyanne Conway, former senior counselor to President Donald Trump, and Adrienne Elrod, a Democratic consultant and former senior adviser and senior spokesperson for Kamala Harris’s presidential campaign.</p>
<p>The updates came one day after The Washington Post reported that Miller had reported <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/19/trump-adviser-jason-millers-firms-took-15-million-court-records-show/">$15 million</a> in income in bank records made public in a family court case.</p>
<p>In April, the records show, Semafor paid one of Miller’s firms, Scipio Strategic, $100,000 for help booking guests for its World Economy 2026 summit in D.C. The event featured Cabinet-level officials including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.</p>
<p>Semafor previously confirmed that it paid Miller for a brief engagement around the event — a relationship that ended after it concluded — but it has not said what guests Miller specifically helped land.</p>
<p>While Miller assisted only with the World Economy event, Conway and Elrod, according to the disclosures, helped with that event and others. Semafor still works with Conway and Elrod.</p>
<p>Semafor, like many other news organizations including The Post, bills its events as live journalism. Though some news organizations hire outside groups to aid with their events, it is standard practice for outlets to print disclosures in articles when covering someone with a financial relationship to the company.</p>
<p>In a statement, Meaghan Koppel, Semafor’s global head of communications, wrote: “Like many other media companies, Semafor works with bookers and consultants across the political spectrum to secure speakers for our events. We don’t pay speakers. Both our speakers and guests come for tough, fair, and independent interviews.”</p>
<p>She continued: “Semafor did not quote Jason Miller or mention him in our Washington coverage during our engagement. We did make two passing references of him in business coverage. We updated those stories with disclosures, and made similar disclosures about our work with Democrat Adrienne Elrod and Republican Kellyanne Conway.”</p>
<p>In a note sent to Semafor staffers that was obtained by The Post, co-founder and editor in chief Ben Smith said that consultants who help with booking “have no influence on our journalism, online or on our stages.”</p>
<p>“News organizations don’t always disclose this sort of contract relationship, but we will going forward when we work with consultants who can themselves be the subjects of coverage,” he said.</p>
<p>Smith also said that the company’s leaders could have better communicated internally. “Some people in the newsroom and on the events team knew they were working with us, but most didn’t,” he said.</p>
<p>Trump tapped Miller to be his first White House communications director but in December 2016, amid reports of Miller’s extramarital affair, he withdrew from the position. Miller has remained in Trump’s orbit and advised him on his presidential campaigns in 2020 and 2024.</p>
<p>The Post’s reporting on Thursday showed that while Miller’s total income in 2023 was $879,000, it ballooned to $15 million in the 12 months that ended in September 2026.</p>
<p>The payment from Semafor was a small percentage of that total, which he earned in payments from varied industries and foreign governments.</p>
<p>Miller, Conway and Elrod did not immediately respond to requests for comment.</p>
