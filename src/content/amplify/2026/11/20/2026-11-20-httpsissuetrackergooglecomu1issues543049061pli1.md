<p>Vivaldi Technologies is a European company based in Norway. Vivaldi is a business user of the designated Google Android core platform service under the Digital Markets Act (DMA) and provides browser services to end users throughout the European Union.</p>
<p>Google makes WebAPK functionality available to Chrome. That functionality enables OS level installation and integration of Progressive Web Apps (PWA) through the designated Google Android operating system. Vivaldi cannot achieve equivalent functionality through currently available mechanisms. Vivaldi therefore wishes to make an Article 6(7) request to access the WebAPK Minting API, subject only to strictly necessary and proportionate security safeguards, so that PWAs installed through Vivaldi integrate with Android no less effectively than equivalent PWAs installed through Chrome do now, while also receiving any relevant future improvements made available to Chrome.</p>
<p>Multiple browser vendors with browsers on Android are interested in the functionality. See: <a href="https://issues.chromium.org/issues/40195497">https://issues.chromium.org/issues/40195497</a></p>
<p>WebAPK minting is provided through Google Play Services. The European Commission expressly included Google Play Services within the designated Google Android core platform service insofar as it acts as middleware for Android’s basic functions or enables applications to run effectively. WebAPK minting performs precisely such a function by enabling PWAs to be installed as Android packages and integrated with Android’s launcher, application settings, link capture, sharing and related OS facilities.</p>
<p>"Finally, as regards Alphabet’s middleware, the Commission notes that, based on the information available to it, it cannot be excluded that some of the operating system features of Google Android are included in or require Alphabet’s middleware in order to function, such as certain elements of Google Play Services. With regard to Google Play Services, for example, the Commission notes that virtually all of the devices that implement and are enabled by Google Android in the European Economic Area (“EEA”) also make use of Google Play Services and Alphabet itself describes Google Play Services as “a core system software that enables key functionality on every certified Android device”. Therefore, Alphabet’s middleware, insofar as it contributes to controlling the basic functions of Google Android tablets and smartphones and to enabling software applications to run on them, thereby ensuring an effective functioning of Google Android, should be considered part of the operating system CPS Google Android. References to ‘Google Android’ should therefore in the remainder of this Decision be understood to also cover Alphabet’s middleware as defined above."</p>
<p>See: <a href="https://ec.europa.eu/competition/digital_markets_act/cases/202344/DMA_100002_145.pdf">https://ec.europa.eu/competition/digital_markets_act/cases/202344/DMA_100002_145.pdf</a></p>
<p>In documentation last updated in 2017, Google stated that it was working to make the seamless installation process available to all Android browsers and that further details would follow.</p>
<p>See: <a href="https://web.dev/articles/webapks">https://web.dev/articles/webapks</a></p>
<p>However, currently only Chrome has access to this feature, excluding Samsung Internet which uses a separate Samsung controlled implementation limited to Samsung devices. We have not requested such functionality from Samsung, as there is no legal mechanism requiring Samsung to provide broader access because Samsung is not designated as a gatekeeper under the DMA and, in any event, its implementation would not provide interoperability across the wider Android device ecosystem.</p>
<p>"The aim of the obligations is to allow competing third parties to interconnect through interfaces or similar solutions to the respective features as effectively as the gatekeeper’s own services or hardware."</p>
<p>See Recital 57: <a href="https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?toc=OJ%3AL%3A2022%3A265%3ATOC&amp;uri=uriserv%3AOJ.L_.2022.265.01.0001.01.ENG">https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?toc=OJ%3AL%3A2022%3A265%3ATOC&amp;uri=uriserv%3AOJ.L_.2022.265.01.0001.01.ENG</a></p>
<p>The shortcut-based installation mechanism currently available to Vivaldi is not sufficient and does not meet the “as effectively” requirement of Recital 57 due to lower levels of integration. This includes having an icon in the app launcher, appearing in settings and apps, badging, app shortcuts, sharing and directly linking to the PWA in certain contexts.</p>
<p>"While WebAPKs provide the best experience for Android users, they can't always be created. When they can't, browsers fall back to creating a website shortcut. Because Firefox, Microsoft Edge, Opera, Brave, and Samsung Internet (on non-Samsung devices) don't have minting servers they trust, they'll create shortcuts. Google Chrome will too if the minting service is unavailable or your PWA doesn't meet installation requirements."</p>
<p>See: <a href="https://web.dev/learn/pwa/installation">https://web.dev/learn/pwa/installation</a></p>
<p>Our desired outcome is for any PWA installed through Vivaldi to obtain equivalent Android integration available to the same PWA installed through Chrome currently, including any future improvements. This could be achieved by allowing Vivaldi to call the WebAPK Minting API, in the same manner as Chrome, and modifying the generated WebAPK wrapper so that it launches and delegates relevant browser controlled functions to the browser through which the PWA was installed.</p>
<p>Google should establish objective, transparent and non-discriminatory eligibility criteria under which Vivaldi and other qualifying browsers can access the WebAPK minting functionality, limited to safeguards that are strictly necessary and proportionate to protect Android’s security and integrity. Any such safeguards must be specifically justified and applied consistently. For example, only providing the entitlement to browsers that promptly patch security issues within a prescribed timeframe may be a reasonable security measure. Vivaldi requests prompt approval under those criteria once the necessary changes to support other browsers have been made to the WebAPK minting API.</p>
<p>“Google Play services:<br/>
 Receives automatic updates—independent of OS, OEM, or app updates—so your users receive improvements and bug fixes more quickly.”</p>
<p>See: <a href="https://developers.google.com/android/guides/overview">https://developers.google.com/android/guides/overview</a></p>
<p>Vivaldi seeks interoperability across the existing base of Android devices that support Google Play services. Because Google Play Services can be updated independently of the underlying Android operating system, delivery through Google Play Services appears to be the only presently identified means of providing effective interoperability across all supported Android devices. Any alternative proposal by Google must provide at least equivalent device coverage and must not depend on users receiving a future Android OS upgrade, since many devices in active use never receive the latest Android version. One of the purposes of Google Play Services is precisely to address this fragmentation by enabling Google to deploy new functionality across the Android ecosystem independently of full operating system updates.</p>
<p>Server side validation, package generation and signing may remain under Google’s control. Vivaldi is not aware of any security or system integrity risk that could not be adequately mitigated such that it would justify denying access to the WebAPK minting functionality. If Google contends that such risks exist, it should identify and substantiate them in detail, explain the mitigations it has considered, and demonstrate why any resulting restriction is strictly necessary and proportionate.</p>
<p>Google should provide Vivaldi access to the WebAPK minting functionality without undue delay. As the underlying functionality already exists and is available to Chrome, Google should promptly identify any technical changes strictly necessary to support third-party browsers and provide a concrete implementation timetable. Any delay must be objectively justified and must not undermine Vivaldi’s ability to interoperate with the relevant Android features as effectively as Chrome.</p>
<p>Vivaldi is, of course, ready to engage constructively with Google on this request.</p>
