<div><div>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>President Donald Trump’s nominee to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency offered little information about how he would steer the beleaguered agency during his confirmation hearing on Thursday.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><a href="https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/trump-cisa-director-nominee-michael-losapio/874256/"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>Michael LoSapio</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span>, the chief information security officer at the data analytics firm Palantir, provided only high-level answers to members of the Senate Homeland Security Committee who asked him how he would restore CISA’s ability to combat cyber threats after two years of Trump administration cuts.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“I believe CISA is one of the only agencies in the government that plays a defensive cyber role in our nation,” LoSapio told Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., who will chair the homeland-security panel in the next session of Congress.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>CISA’s “sole purpose is to provide resources [and] information to the defenders of state, local, and private entities for the purpose of cyber defense,” LoSapio continued. “If confirmed, I intend to bring together the community and the national labs to help collaborate and coordinate and to give defenders the tools they need to defend their networks.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>LoSapio also described CISA as “the cybersecurity version of the 911 system” because of its role in responding to significant cyber incidents.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>But LoSapio, whose firm has </span></span></span></span></span></span><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/05/30/technology/trump-palantir-data-americans.html"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>extensive and controversial connections</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span> to the Trump administration, refused to weigh in on the administration’s downsizing of CISA, which has included </span></span></span></span></span></span><a href="https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/cisa-layoffs-reassignments-dhs-white-house-government-shutdown/802723/"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>mass layoffs</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span> and </span></span></span></span></span></span><a href="https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/cisa-cybersecurity-assessments-ending/829371/"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>significant changes</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span> to the agency’s </span></span></span></span></span></span><a href="https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/cisa-cybersecurity-division-reorganization/812155/"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>programs and services</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span>.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“I can’t comment on the current state of CISA, because I’m not inside today,” he told Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>When Slotkin pressed LoSapio on whether he would support the Trump administration’s proposed $385 million cut to CISA’s budget, LoSapio again dodged the question.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“I'm going to get involved and understand where the gaps are,” he said, “and I will come back to this committee with a gap analysis.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>Slotkin said LoSapio was too afraid of angering Trump to be an effective CISA director.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“You should be able to say clearly, there’s increasing threat, therefore we need increasing resources to deal with it,” she said, “and the fact that you can’t say that means that you’re more worried about the political ramifications than you are about the national security of the country.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“I don’t necessarily believe more money means more security,” LoSapio responded. “I believe spending money on the right solutions in the right ways actually decreases risk.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>At other times, LoSapio appeared unprepared to discuss the details of CISA’s work. When Sen. James Lankford, R-Okla., asked him what his top priorities would be as CISA’s director, he gave a vague answer that did not mention any of CISA’s specific responsibilities.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“It’s really important to understand the landscape of the systems that you have out in the wild,” LoSapio said, “the things that you operate day to day, the systems that you rely on, the systems that are ancillary to the work you do, and do an extensive evaluation on their vulnerabilities, whether or not they’ve been patched or not, and how they have been architected and their defensive capabilities — both things we need to add and the things that exist today, as to how that maps to the the threats we’re facing.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<h3><span><span><span><strong><span><span>Politically controversial answers</span></span></strong></span></span></span></h3>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>LoSapio’s nomination </span></span></span></span></span></span><a href="https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/some-cyber-industry-have-qualms-about-trumps-latest-cisa-nominee/417275/?oref=ng-homepage-river"><span><span><span><span><span><span><span><span>has generated skepticism</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></a><span><span><span><span><span><span> within the cybersecurity community because of his low profile and limited cybersecurity leadership background. His comments at his confirmation hearing could raise new doubts about whether he is the right person to lead CISA, which has been without a Senate-confirmed director since the beginning of the Trump administration.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>His answers to several questions from Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., highlighted LoSapio’s loyalty to Trump.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>First, he refused to acknowledge that President Joe Biden won the 2020 election, prompting incredulity from Blumenthal, who pointed out that LoSapio was nominated to lead the agency overseeing U.S. election security. Next, LoSapio denied that the Jan. 6, 2021, insurrection at the U.S. Capitol constituted an attack.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“I don’t believe it was an attack,” he said. “I wasn’t involved in politics. ... I know what I saw on the news.”</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>Hours later, that comment prompted several senators to say they would block LoSapio’s confirmation, </span></span></span></span></span></span><span><span><span><span><span><span>potentially dooming his prospects in the final weeks before Democrats take control of Congress.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>Sen. Thom Tillis, R-N.C., who is retiring, and Sen. Ron Wyden, D-Ore., both plan to place holds on LoSapio’s nomination, their spokespeople told Cybersecurity Dive.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
<p><span><span><span><span><span><span>“You can’t have an insurrectionist Palantir executive in charge of our nation’s cybersecurity and election security,” Wyden said in a statement.</span></span></span></span></span></span></p>
