<h3>This is the first year in my career I wasn't approached by an editor to write about TDOR and it's a sign of the media's disinterest in trans lives</h3>
<p>The media stopped caring about dead Black trans women about four years ago, in 2022. Before then there was robust interest in reporting on the deaths and lives of the trans women we've lost to transphobic violence from lefty news editors. One year I fielded a dozen requests to write about the day for large, national outlets.</p>
<p>This year, for the first time, not one single editor mentioned it to me when talking about potential November coverage ideas.</p>
<p>It's distressing to watch the opinion shapers and pundits of the English speaking west just stop caring in unison about the dead bodies of your disadvantaged sisters. And you might be thinking, Katelyn why don't you just cover it? My answer is, y'all don't read it! One of my least read pieces on this newsletter was <a href="https://www.burnsnotice.com/black-trans-folks-have-been-here-before/">last October's interview with Chastity Bowick</a>, who was then the newly appointed executive director of the Marsha P. Johnson Institute.</p>
<p>We had an in depth discussion about how everyone has lost interest in supporting Black trans lives and then nobody read it and nobody shared it online. As an indie journalist, I have to get eyeballs on my work to survive and you the reader sent a loud and clear message of disinterest.</p>
<p>It is endlessly frustrated and I suspect the same dynamic is at play when editors at larger outlets weigh up their options for coverage of trans people. Why write about a dead Black trans woman when a white trans girl somewhere might be playing a sport and coverage would result in millions of views?</p>
<p>The coverage interest in TDOR is directly inverse to how much traditional media thinks of trans issues as a purely political issue. When a person's rights turn political, the media at large tends to step back and prioritize an abstract, ideological framing, and the dead body of a murdered trans girl only complicates the narrative.</p>
<p>In order to debate a human being's existence, you must de facto dehumanize that person. Their death becomes a statistic to be debated rather than a real human life that bigotry helped snuff out.</p>
<p>Not long ago, I got in an argument on X the nothing app with a conservative writer who claimed very confidently that because the number of reported trans murder victims was falling, that "trans activists" (shorthand for trans people at large) were too hysterical about the issue. When I pointed out that the numbers only represent reported deaths and that there are myriad ways trans women's murders slip through the statistical cracks, he simply refused to believe me.</p>
<p>A few years ago, someone ran a "data analysis" that used the reported number of trans deaths and divided it by the generally accepted estimation of the total trans population and declared that trans women were statistically the safest demographic in the country. It was amateur hour analysis with more holes than a block of Swiss cheese. But it didn't matter, as the argument eventually made its way to the editorial pages of the mainstream and coverage of these deaths fell off a cliff.</p>
<p>I don't know how to live in a society that works so hard to minimize murder because my demographic has become "political." I have lost so many trans friends, mostly to suicide, and every trans person I know has lost at least one trans person too early in life.</p>
<p>To be trans is to be intimately familiar with mourning a friend. I've found myself trying to explain this to the cis loved ones in my life, the sheer number of hours I've spent talking with a friend who is clearly hinting that they want to end it all. The interviews with friends and families of dead trans women. The erasure from government officials and the media alike. My cis friends simply cannot comprehend the scale of loss and erasure the trans people in their lives have experienced.</p>
<p>I no longer attend TDOR events because i find the simple name reading ceremony inadequate to express the emotion of the commemoration of these people's lives.</p>
<p>On this holy day in our community, I don't say "Happy Trans Day of Remembrance," I say "God Damn Trans Day of Remembrance." We shouldn't have this day because people should stop killing us. And we as white trans people (who are the vast majority of readers of trans people reading this) must reprioritize the safety of Black trans women in our political calculations.</p>
<p>They need us, we need them. We are in this together.</p>
