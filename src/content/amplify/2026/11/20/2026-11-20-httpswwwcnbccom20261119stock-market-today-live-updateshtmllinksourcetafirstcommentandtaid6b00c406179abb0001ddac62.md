<p>Stocks were slammed on Friday, closing out a brutal week for markets as the much-hyped <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ANT/">Anthropic</a> IPO caused investors to take profits on the artificial intelligence-led rally rather than revive it. The <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/.IXIC/">Nasdaq Composite</a> turned negative for the year, and the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/.SPX/">S&amp;P 500</a> entered correction territory alongside the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/.DJI/">Dow Jones Industrial Average</a>.</p>
<p>The tech-heavy Nasdaq shed 5.28% to close at 22,617.73. The index was down 2.7% year to date. The broad market S&amp;P 500 dropped 3.7% to end at 6,910.39, while the blue-chip Dow fell 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33. Both the S&amp;P 500 and Dow moved 10% below their closing records.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/.NDX/">Nasdaq-100</a> plummeted 5.6% and entered a bear market on an intraday basis, down 20% from its recent high.</p>

<p>Anthropic lost 8.5%, giving back a large chunk of its <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html">17% jump in its Nasdaq debut</a> Thursday and pressuring the broader AI trade. Chipmakers <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AMD/">Advanced Micro Devices</a> and <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/NVDA/">Nvidia</a> slid more than 13% and around 14%, respectively. Nvidia saw selling pressure increase after Reuters reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is exploring possible accounting issues at the company. It also recorded its <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/nvidias-7-week-losing-streak-is-longest-since-2008-financial-crisis.html">longest weekly losing streak</a> since the 2008 financial crisis. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/MU/">Micron Technology</a> dropped almost 14%.</p>
<p>Those declines in tech built on the losses seen in Thursday’s horrid session, where the major averages each losing more than 2%, and the Dow closing below the key 50,000 level for the first time since June.</p>
<p>“This is not just a valuation-driven correction. It’s becoming broader,” said Massimo Santicchia, head of U.S. equities at Procyon. “Right now, it’s definitely risk-off.”</p>
<p>The losses are alarming investors who were hopeful that <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html">Nvidia’s earnings</a> and <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html">Anthropic’s IPO</a> would revive enthusiasm for the AI trade. Now, the market looks to be on the cusp of a true capitulation after getting roiled in recent weeks with a series of negative headlines. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/microsoft-google-ai-capex-estimates.html">Signs of moderation</a> in the capital expenditures splurge, Democratic wins in the 2026 midterms and Jensen Huang’s ouster at Nvidia all <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-ai-financing.html">raised questions</a> about the future of the AI buildout.</p>
<p>On top of that, the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html">potential for more interest rate hikes</a> from the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/federal-reserve/">Federal Reserve</a> is spooking traders as well.</p>
<p>Investors hid out in defensive areas of the market, with retailers such as <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/WMT/">Walmart</a> and <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/COST/">Costco</a> as well as others like <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/JNJ/">Johnson &amp; Johnson</a> and <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/PG/">Procter &amp; Gamble</a> seeing gains. Walmart gained more than 1%, while Costco, Johnson &amp; Johnson and Procter &amp; Gamble each rose around 1%.</p>
<p>“The stock market is going through an AI repricing, reflecting an environment of slowing data center buildouts, reduced funding sources for AI-related projects and a slower trajectory of technology-related earnings growth,” said Richard Saperstein, chief investment officer at Treasury Partners.</p>
<p>For the week, the Dow fell 8%, notching its worst weekly performance since March 2020. The Nasdaq finished down about 10%, also posting its largest one-week pullback in more than six years. The S&amp;P 500 decreased 8.6%, seeing its worst week since April 2025 in the wake of President Donald Trump’s <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/02/trump-tariffs-live-updates.html">sweeping tariffs announcement</a>.</p>
<h2>Stocks tumble Friday</h2>
<p>The three leading U.S. indexes experienced a massive sell-off on Friday.</p>
<p>The S&amp;P 500 dropped 3.7% to close at 6,910.39, while the Nasdaq Composite plummeted 5.28% to 22,617.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33.</p>
<p><em>— Sean Conlon</em></p>
<h2>‘Risk is to the downside’ when below the 200-day moving average, BTIG says</h2>
<p>All three major averages were trading below their 200-day moving averages on Friday, and BTIG believes more turmoil could be ahead.</p>
<p>“Generally speaking once something is below a 200 DMA, the risk is to the downside,” said Jonathan Krinsky, the firm’s chief market technician.</p>
<p>Thursday marked the sixth consecutive day of declines for the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/.SPX/">S&amp;P 500</a>, and Krinsky found that over the last 30 years, there are only three prior instances of six-day losing streaks that close below the 200-day moving average but not by more than 3%.</p>
<p>“The ‘slightly’ good news is that all three saw SPX higher five days later. The not so good news is that all of them saw meaningful downside after that small pop,” he said.</p>
<p>When assessing Thursday’s performance, Wolfe Research found that the broad market index’s “violent” reversal after its attempt to move back to its 200-day moving average at the open was succeeded by the “worst breadth reading” since last December, seeing decliners outnumber advancers by 12:1.</p>
<p>“Broken trends and deteriorating momentum remain significant headwinds,” wrote technical analyst Rob Ginsberg in a Thursday note. “The bulls need to mount a powerful defense here to stabilize the market and begin reversing the broader deterioration.”</p>
<p><em>— Sean Conlon</em></p>
<p>The national average for regular gasoline fell to $3.69 a gallon on Friday, down from $3.72 on Thursday, according to <a href="https://gasprices.aaa.com/">AAA</a>. Prices are still about 59 cents higher than a year ago.</p>
<p>The drop comes ahead of Thanksgiving next week, with<a href="https://newsroom.aaa.com/2026/11/aaa-thanksgiving-holiday-travel-forecast/"> 73 million people</a> expected to take road trips.</p>
<p>“It’s possible the national average will drop another 15 cents by next Thursday and avoid setting a new record,” <a href="https://gasprices.aaa.com/gas-prices-keep-dropping-as-thanksgiving-approaches/">according to AAA’s post.</a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/@LCO.1/">Brent ​crude</a> futures, the international benchmark, were up 0.64% to $92.57 a barrel on Friday, while U.S. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/@CL.1/">West Texas Intermediate</a> futures for December rose 0.93% to settle at $83.98 a barrel.</p>

<h2>BTIG’s Krinsky says technicals show the sell-off isn’t over yet </h2>
<p>BTIG’s chief market technician Jonathan Krinsky in an appearance on CNBC’s “<a href="https://www.cnbc.com/the-exchange/">The Exchange</a>” Friday pointed to technicals that make him think the sell-off isn’t over just yet.</p>
<p>He specifically noted <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AAPL/">Apple</a>, the largest stock by market cap, which is only down by 3% from its 2026 highs earlier this month. “When the S&amp;P has been down at least 8% or more, when it’s made its final bottom, the median drawdown for Apple has been 23%,” he said. “It would be historically unprecedented to see a final bottom on the S&amp;P with Apple where it is right now.”</p>
<p>He added that 50% of the S&amp;P 500′s stocks are above their 50-day moving average, a level that is higher than a number closer to below 40% — or even 20% — when durable bottoms hit.</p>
<p>Krinsky did note, though, that relief for semiconductors may be coming, as the group is retreating to levels that it last traded at in early April when the <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/ai-chip-rally-in-q2-adds-2-trillion-in-value-to-micron-intel-amd-.html">group started to surge higher</a>. The <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/SOXX/">iShares Semiconductor ETF</a> (SOXX) in late trading Friday was at levels it last closed below on April 9.</p>

<h2>‘Lot of upside’ in AI stocks despite growing market skepticism, Anthropic investor says</h2>
<p>The market is skeptical about “all layers of the AI layer cake” but that can be a “great setup for investors,” Brett Winton, chief futurist at ARK Investment Management who has Anthropic in all his portfolios, told CNBC’s “Squawk Box Europe” on Friday.</p>
<p>A key worry is that the huge R&amp;D spend of Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX will generate sufficient returns, he said.</p>
<p>“The market really thinks that <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ANTHR.FG/">Anthropic</a>’s revenue per gigawatt of compute has essentially peaked and is only going to go down from here. And the history and the returns they’ve got to their training suggest exactly the opposite, where they already have amazing gross margins,” he said.</p>
<p>“What they’re getting is the ability to serve more intelligence per GPU [graphics processing unit] that they use, and as they deliver on that, they’re able to monetize per gigawatt of GPU more successfully... That’s why you’ve seen Anthropic’s growth margins expand as quickly as they have, even as their top line has been explosively growing. So we think that’s going to continue.”</p>
<p>On concerns about the infrastructure buildout around AI, Winton said people increasingly doubted whether companies will be willing to pay for the compute they’re delivering to market.</p>
<p>“We take exactly the opposite view. So the overall market for knowledge workers we think is a $40 trillion market for AI software selling into the knowledge workforce, and so there’s plenty of room to run if collectively Anthropic and OpenAI are only annualizing revenue on the order of $150 billion per year right now.”</p>
<p>“So we’re really in the earliest, earliest stages of what will be a really dynamic game, and we think there’s a lot of upside from here.”</p>
