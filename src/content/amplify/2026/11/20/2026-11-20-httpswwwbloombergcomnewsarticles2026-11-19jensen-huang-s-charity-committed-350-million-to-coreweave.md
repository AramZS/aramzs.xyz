<body><figure><img alt="Jensen Huang" src="https://assets.bwbx.io/images/users/iqjWHBFdfxIU/igi_60M5709g/v0/-1x-1.webp"/><figcaption>Jensen Huang / Photographer: Erik Flyg/Bloomberg</figcaption></figure><div><p>Former <a href="">Nvidia Corp.</a> Chief Executive Officer<a href=""> Jensen Huang</a>’s charitable foundation plans to spend $350 million renting computer time from <a href="">CoreWeave Inc.</a> </p><p>The Jen-Hsun &amp; Lori Huang Foundation disclosed the value of the pledge in its most recent tax filing. A separate June <a href="bbg://screens/DOCV%20CF%200001045810-26-000036">filing</a> by Nvidia indicated that the money will go to CoreWeave and that $108.3 million had already been spent.</p><p>The transaction highlights how even tech billionaires’ personal charities are entangled in the AI industry’s circular financing. Nvidia and other computer hardware providers have invested heavily in their own customers, fueling concerns the arrangements are creating artificial demand for their products. Nvidia is a major shareholder of CoreWeave, which Huang publicly defended after its stock price slumped on fears that its spending on new hardware is unsustainable because of its large debt levels.</p><p>“The Huang Foundation is not a user of the compute; it goes to their grant recipients to do research,” a CoreWeave spokesperson said. A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment.</p><p>Huang <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jensen-huang-face-of-the-ai-boom-ousted-by-nvidia-board-as-ceo">was forced out</a> of Nvidia last week after admitting that he cheated on his wife, Lori, who is co-president of the foundation alongside him. He has publicly apologized to her and his family. He’s also appealed to the company’s board to consider reinstating him. The board <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/huang-is-willing-to-return-as-nvidia-ceo-if-board-allows-it">rejected</a> that overture earlier this week.</p><p>Nvidia’s stock performance over the past several years has turned the Huangs’ foundation into one of the largest private charities, with assets of $12.7 billion as of Dec. 31, 2025, up from $9.2 billion a year earlier. Close to 90% of the charity’s assets at year-end were comprised of Nvidia stock, which has slumped about 30% since revelations first surfaced of Huang’s affairs, including with a suspected Chinese spy. </p><p>The foundation more than doubled its holdings in mutual funds last year, to almost $1.3 billion, in a small but significant shift from Nvidia shares, which in prior years made up more than 95% of the foundation’s assets.</p><p>Internal Revenue Service rules mandate that foundations distribute at least 5% of their investment assets annually to philanthropic causes, meaning the Huang foundation has had to swiftly ramp up its giving in recent years. The charity donated about $373 million in total last year, about triple what it gave in 2024, which in turn was double the amount it gave in 2023. </p><p>Read More: <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-26/nvidia-s-rally-means-ceo-s-foundation-must-double-giving-again">Nvidia’s Rally Means CEO’s Foundation Must Double Giving, Again</a></p><p>The program supported by the CoreWeave computing, dubbed the Computing Initiative for Open Science, is “intended to unlock one of the most significant barriers to modern scientific discovery: access to large-scale GPU-computing power,” according to the tax filing. </p><p>The $350 million donation is by far the biggest gift the Huang foundation made last year. Its other donations include $19 million to the California College of the Arts, $1.2 million to the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and $1.05 million to College Track, which supports first-generation college students in earning degrees. It also gave smaller amounts to the Maui Food Bank, a private school called Creekside School and a Bronx-based program, Per Scholas, that provides free IT courses. </p><p>Read More: <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-13/vanderbilt-buys-troubled-arts-college-nvidia-ceo-tried-to-save">Vanderbilt Buys Troubled Arts College Nvidia CEO Tried to Save</a></p><p>In 2024, the bulk of the foundation’s giving went to a donor-advised fund at Schwab Charitable that wasn’t listed as a recipient in 2025. </p></div><div></div></body>
