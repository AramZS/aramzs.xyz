<p>BY <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/user/joe-berkowitz">Joe Berkowitz</a></p>
<p>Bluesky is a niche Twitter-clone frequently accused of being an echo chamber for progressives. After the blue tsunami of this week’s midterms, however, its inhabitants don’t seem to be echoing themselves so much as the vast majority of American voters.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91635400/midterm-elections-2026-list-of-historic-firsts-diverse-winners">sheer scale of Democrats’ victory</a> is staggering. They won control of both chambers of the U.S. Congress, flipping Senate seats in seven states Trump carried in 2024—including Republican stronghold Texas, which now also has a Democratic governor and attorney general. All in all, this year’s midterms represent the biggest electoral shift in state-level politics to hit the U.S. in generations. They also represent a Texas-sized validation of the worldview Bluesky has become notorious for over the past two years.</p>

<p>Although it officially <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91271080/bluesky-most-innovative-companies-2025">launched in the spring of 2023</a>, Bluesky might as well have been born on election eve in 2024. That was the moment millions of disillusioned Twitter users decamped from a website owned by the <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91634243/elon-musk-spent-nearly-500-million-on-the-midterms-it-bought-him-almost-nothing">largest single contributor</a> to Trump’s reelection. Bluesky quickly became a haven for Twitter expats and <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91255918/in-the-social-media-wars-bluesky-is-destroying-truth-social">grew at a frenetic pace</a>. It went from 9 million users in September 2024 to <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2024/nov/12/us-election-bluesky-users-flee-x-twitter-trump-musk">15 million by mid-November</a>, eventually reaching <a href="https://bsky.jazco.dev/">its current</a> 47 million.</p>
<p>Because many of Bluesky’s users arrived out of principled opposition to X, formerly Twitter, the two sites have arguably existed in opposition to each other. Both portray their counterpart as out of touch, with Bluesky alleging <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2">Elon Musk’s algorithm tweaks</a> have skewed X’s political vortex too far to the right, and X suggesting Bluesky is a bubble of liberals who can tolerate anything except a difference in opinion.</p>
<p>The latter idea seemed to largely prevail.</p>
<p>In 2025, <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2025/06/08/blue-sky-twitter-liberals"><em>Washington Post</em> columnists</a>, tech world figures like <a href="https://fortune.com/2025/06/12/bluesky-backfiring-mark-cuban-lack-of-diversity-of-thought-x-users">Mark Cuban</a>, and <a href="https://www.ms.now/opinion/bluesky-elon-musk-twitter-replacement">myriad</a> other <a href="https://www.foxnews.com/media/politico-writer-has-bluesky-account-briefly-suspended-receives-graphic-insults-post-about-musk">pundits</a> dismissed Bluesky as smug, self-righteous, and irrelevant. It was the fantasy sandbox where progressives went to play among themselves, protecting reality dwellers from having to hear their drivel. (<a href="https://www.joshbarro.com/p/bluesky-isnt-a-bubble-its-a-containment">“Like a containment dome over a nuclear reactor,”</a> as the writer Josh Barro put it.)</p>
<p>So, how well did Bluesky’s users stay in touch with reality between elections, compared with X’s <a href="https://searchengineland.com/guide/twitter-users">exponentially larger</a> population? Consider the post-midterms perspective.</p>
<p>Rather than accept the narrative that 2024 represented <a href="https://www.axios.com/2024/11/11/harris-biden-election-democratic-party-liberals">a rejection</a> of <a href="https://www.wral.com/archive/21716527">“wokeness”</a> and <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/14/opinion/identity-groups-politics.html">a political realignment</a> toward Trump, the manifestly trans-friendly Bluesky suggested the election was more a referendum on <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91282941/how-trump-take-egg-captured-the-mood-of-an-inflation-weary-nation">postpandemic inflation</a> and <a href="https://newrepublic.com/article/189429/anti-incumbent-bias-america-worse-global-wave">global anti-incumbent bias</a>. As an emboldened Trump then proceeded to antagonize <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91480913/videos-of-deadly-minneapolis-shooting-contradict-trump-administrations-claims-experts-say">U.S. citizens</a> and <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91300967/the-trump-administration-has-started-detaining-some-european-travelers-and-things-could-get-dire-for-u-s-tourism">the rest of the world</a>, while <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/30/us/politics/trump-financial-disclosure-crypto-windfall.html">making billions for himself in crypto</a>, Bluesky meticulously chronicled the state of play. It became the premier destination for seeing <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/cwebbonline.com/post/3miadanqn2s2n">radicalized</a> older white <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/bubbaprog.xyz/post/3m4sqrnulns2m">ladies</a>, abysmally attended <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/bigbash.bsky.social/post/3mdqs6b7sfk2c">pro-ICE rallies</a>, and complaints about the lack of mainstream news coverage around massive <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/robertscotthorton.bsky.social/post/3myac2d2jsc2k">No Kings protests.</a> Any random day would find endless posts with <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/proptermalone.bsky.social/post/3mptjnoy4qs25">evidence</a> that, per the motto of the political philosopher and Bluesky power user Olúfẹ́mi O. Táíwò, <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/olufemiotaiwo.bsky.social/post/3mzhiyoycds2l">“We are going to win.”</a></p>

<p>Above all, Bluesky proved to be less a bubble than an expression of the multitude of ideological factions that had united under the umbrella of non-MAGAness . . . to argue with one another.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, X’s millions of liberal and progressive folks were often drowned out by an algorithm that <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2">seemed to favor right-wing voices</a> and their preferred narratives. A bubble chart from <a href="https://x.com/i/trending/2040941070482301188">Nate Silver, the political analyst, visually depicted the accounts with highest engagement rates</a> back in April. Its many MAGA-red circles made it look like the blood-filled elevator from <em>The Shining</em>. As <a href="https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/news/2026/detox-for-social-media-redesigning-and-diversifying-feeds.html">a May 2026 study from Northwestern University</a> suggests, X’s algorithm rewards posts that provoke strong reactions, so the loudest and most emotionally satisfying political claims become disproportionately visible. That’s how we got <a href="https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/10/elon-musk-is-the-midterms-biggest-disinformation-risk/">viral fever dreams</a> about James Talarico, the then-senatorial candidate, <a href="https://apnews.com/article/fact-check-talarico-mental-health-lindner-634591c8818aeab31c52526032d0387c">checking into a mental hospital</a>, something that never in fact happened.</p>
<p>It should come as little surprise that many <a href="https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/larry-kudlow-my-prediction-voters-not-polls-decide-election.amp">high-profile MAGA voices</a> on X spent the days leading up to the election <a href="https://x.com/dansenor/status/2117719543552589969">incorrectly predicting</a> how it would turn out.</p>

<p>Who looks <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/paleofuture.bsky.social/post/3mziqsimmfs2g">out of touch</a> and stuck in an echo chamber now?</p>
<p>While X may have been more plugged into the political discourse online for the past two years, Bluesky now appears better attuned to what’s been happening on the ground. Its users were right about pretty much everything.</p>
<p>They were right that <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/rohnkat.bsky.social/post/3mz2vqersmk2d">anti-trans ads</a> would have <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/katelynburns.com/post/3myp5j5vqi22f">little or no impact</a> on the <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/oriana.bsky.social/post/3mwnqysjmtk2m">2026 election</a>, and that their impact on the previous election had been <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/cadaverformosus.bsky.social/post/3mvdklm4ldc2d">vastly overstated</a>.</p>
<p>They were right that <a href="https://slate.com/technology/2026/07/graham-platner-x-democrats-spencer-pratt.html">Graham Platner wasn’t the only Democrat who could beat Susan Collins</a> in Maine, and that <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/29/meet-hasan-piker-twitch-streamer-dividing-democrats-midterms">Abdul El-Sayed’s association</a> with a controversial Twitch streamer relatively few voters had even heard of was <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/oldmanweldon.bsky.social/post/3mv4ur4isvc25">not as big a deal</a> as X users <a href="https://www.foxnews.com/politics/el-sayed-torched-online-brushing-questions-hasan-piker-campaign-ties-what-pal.amp">seemed to think</a>.</p>
<p>And they were right that fleeing X’s algorithm helped them view the world with clear eyes.</p>
<p>If Bluesky is a bubble, then it is currently a champagne bubble awash in celebration.</p>
<p>The festivities will likely last only a matter of hours, though, until infighting explodes around just <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/hamiltonnolan.bsky.social/post/3mzilu3eifc2d">how the Democrats should spend their newfound political capital</a>.</p>
<figure><img alt="This Texas startup is turning oil well waste into EV batteries" data-nimg="1" src="https://images.fastcompany.com/image/upload/f_webp,c_fit,w_1920,q_auto/wp-cms-2/2026/10/p-1-91628438-this-texas-startup-is-turning-oil-well-waste-into-ev-batteries.jpg"/><p>[Source images: Adobe Stock]</p></figure>
<p>BY <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/user/patricksisson">Patrick Sisson</a></p>
<p>Across the Permian Basin, the vast, mineral-rich swath of land in West Texas that supplies much of the nation’s crude oil, derricks pump out massive amounts of oil, roughly 6 million barrels a day.</p>
<p>But that amount pales in comparison with the amount of dirty, brackish water that’s also extracted during oil drilling. Drilling in the Permian Basin also unearths roughly 26 million barrels of water a day, about the same daily water use as all of New York City. This water is often black-gray from oil residue, and has a funky odor from underground bacteria.</p>
<p>Traditionally considered a by-product of energy production, this water, concentrated seawater from prehistoric oceans, is mineral rich. The Houston-based startup Element3 found that this so-called wastewater is a rich source of lithium, and endeavored to filter it to create a domestic source of this high-demand mineral. (Lithium is the third element on the periodic table, hence the company name.) It’s a creative concept that, in a poetic twist, turns a waste product of petroleum drilling into the raw materials for <a data-internallinksmanager029f6b8e52c="3" href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/evs">EV</a> batteries and a clean energy future.</p>
<p>“It’s a wonderful circular economy, waste-to-value story,” said the company CEO and founder, Hood Whitson.</p>
<p>Founded in 2021, Element3 is commercializing U.S. Department of Energy research from the Oak Ridge National Laboratory on ways to filter this oil by-product, and is working across Texas to figure out ways to scale up production. The firm has raised a little over $20 million and has 15 employees, per PitchBook. For years, DOE researchers have been looking into what Whitson called “unconventional brine mining,” finding ways to extract lithium from California’s inland Salton Sea, for example, or pull rare earth metals out of industrial waste sites.</p>
<p>Aged for hundreds of millions of years, the Permian Basin water is five times as salty as today’s oceans, boasting lithium as well as magnesium and strontium. Whitson discovered the properties of this water, and realized that energy companies had built up a massive infrastructure to move it; in effect, paying for the infrastructure for extraction and returning filtered water to the larger system. It seemed like an ideal way to meet the rising demand for lithium, and build a domestic source for the metal, which is <a href="https://www.element3.io/lithium-market">mostly processed in China.</a> The U.S. imported <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/energy/articles/china-dominates-world-lithium-supply-182419477.html">more than $16 billon worth</a> <a href="https://prosperousamerica.org/americas-ai-boom-has-a-trade-policy-blind-spot/">of lithium-ion batteries</a> from China in 2024.</p>
<p>“This is one of our nation’s largest untapped natural resources,” said Whitson. “It’s really a grand opportunity.”</p>
