<p>Salon.com, the digital news site known for its political and cultural commentary, said Thursday it is closing up after 31 years.</p>
<p>The left-leaning outlet <a href="https://www.salon.com/2026/11/05/goodbye-from-salon/">shared in an open letter </a>titled “Goodbye from Salon” that it could no longer operate in today’s volatile media climate.</p>
<p>“It’s difficult to find the right words for the end of something that has meant so much to so many people,” the letter said. “Salon was never just a website. It was an idea about what journalism could be, born at a moment when the internet was still a largely unexplored frontier.”</p>

<p>Launched on Nov. 20, 1995 by a small group of journalists led by David Talbot, Salon was a place with a strong voice that helped pioneer a new kind of online journalism.</p>
<p>“Over three decades, Salon has brought together politics, culture, criticism and ideas in a way that was distinctly our own,” the letter said.</p>
<p>“Our journalism examined power and politics, questioned assumptions, surfaced stories that deserved attention and made room for perspectives that were too often overlooked.”</p>
<p>Journalists like Jake Tapper, Glenn Greenwald, Rebecca Traister and Alex Pareene wrote for the outlet over the years. The publication saw a string of top editors including Talbot, Joan Walsh and outgoing editor Joseph Neese.</p>

<p>The outlet’s closure followed years of downsizing in Salon’s newsroom. It also came amid widespread cuts in the media industry driven by tech-related changes and a volatile advertising market, among other factors.</p>
