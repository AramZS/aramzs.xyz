title: New York Times Company in Advanced Talks to Acquire Letterboxd
---
<p>The roughly $330 million deal for the cinephile community site would be part of an effort to add new products and services to the company’s portfolio.</p><figure><img alt="A cellphone showing a sign-in page of Letterboxd." src="https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/10/29/business/29BIZ-LETTERBOXD/29BIZ-LETTERBOXD-articleLarge.jpg"/><figcaption>Letterboxd attracts movie buffs who use the site to review and recommend films, curate personal filmographies and complete viewing challenges. Sebastian Gollnow/picture alliance, via Getty Images</figcaption></figure><p>The New York Times Company has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire <a href="https://letterboxd.com/">Letterboxd</a>, an online community for cinephiles, according to two people familiar with the discussions who spoke on the condition of anonymity because the talks are confidential.</p><p>The price under consideration is roughly $330 million, the people said. That would be more than 20 times its projected earnings this year of roughly $15 million. A deal has not yet been reached, and talks could still fall apart.</p><p>The Times Company declined to comment. Matthew Buchanan, a co-founder of Letterboxd, did not respond to a request for comment.</p><p>The Times Company and Letterboxd have discussed keeping Mr. Buchanan and his co-founder, Karl von Randow, in charge at the company, reporting to an executive at the company, one of the people said.</p><p>The parent company of Letterboxd, which was launched in 2011, has been working with the investment bank LionTree on a sale. Prospective suitors, <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/24/business/media/letterboxd-sale-sony-a24-nyt.html">including the A24 and Sony film studios</a>, were intrigued by the site’s highly engaged user base of 30 million movie buffs, who use Letterboxd to review and recommend films, curate their personal filmographies and complete challenges like “<a href="https://letterboxd.com/kynky/list/horrorx52-2026/">Horror x 52</a>” (in which users track how many types of horror films they have watched over a year).</p><p>Though A24 promised the founders of the company independence and to leave its business model unchanged, the studio was edged out as the price tag rose, according to a person familiar with the matter.</p><p>An acquisition of Letterboxd would be part of a yearslong effort by the Times Company to develop a bundle of products and services beyond its flagship news offering. The company has spent hundreds of millions of dollars to acquire businesses, including The Athletic, a sports news outlet; Wirecutter, a product recommendation site; and Wordle, a popular addition to its bundle of games.</p><p>Two people familiar with the company’s interest in the deal said the Times Company would integrate its advertising technology with Letterboxd’s systems. One of the people said Letterboxd would most likely continue operating as a stand-alone site for the foreseeable future.</p><p>Letterboxd and the Times Company have similar business models, pursuing both subscription and advertising revenue. Though Letterboxd does not disclose the number of paying users, acquiring a popular subscription business could help the Times Company — which <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/04/business/media/new-york-times-earnings.html">has 13.8 million subscribers</a> — reach its goal of 15 million subscribers by the end of 2027.</p>
