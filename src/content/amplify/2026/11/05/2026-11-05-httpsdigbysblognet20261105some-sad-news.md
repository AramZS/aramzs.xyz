<p>I have been writing a column for Salon three times a week (more in the early days) for almost 13 years. Yesterday’s was my last one. I just got this news:</p>
<blockquote> <p>Today, after 31 years, we are saddened to announce that Salon is ceasing publication.</p> <p>It’s difficult to find the right words for the end of something that has meant so much to so many people. Salon was never just a website. It was an idea about what journalism could be, born at a moment when the internet was still a largely unexplored frontier.</p> <p>When Salon launched in November 1995, the web was a very different place. A small group of journalists set out to create something original: a publication with a strong voice, a curiosity about the world and a belief that the internet could bring journalism and readers together in new ways. They helped pioneer a form of journalism that would come to define an entirely new era of publishing.</p> <p>Over three decades, Salon has brought together politics, culture, criticism and ideas in a way that was distinctly our own. Our journalism examined power and politics, questioned assumptions, surfaced stories that deserved attention and made room for perspectives that were too often overlooked. Our writers and editors helped shape conversations about the forces changing our lives. The work was never simply about keeping up with the news. It was about making sense of it.</p> <p>The history of Salon is also the history of the people who made it. We are profoundly grateful to the journalists, editors, photographers, producers, designers, business teams and countless other colleagues who brought this publication to life across its many chapters. To the founders who imagined it, the staff members who built it, the contributors who gave it their voices and the alumni who carried its spirit into the world: Thank you. Salon’s legacy belongs to all of you.</p> <p>We also want to thank our readers. You came to Salon for the journalism, the dialogue, the essays, the criticism, the unexpected discoveries and the sense that there was a place on the internet where ideas mattered. You read, shared, challenged, subscribed, wrote to us and kept coming back. Your engagement made Salon a publication and a community, and it gave our work meaning. We are so grateful for the time you spent with us.</p> <p>The business realities of independent digital journalism have changed profoundly over the years. After careful consideration, we have reached the difficult decision that our publishing model is no longer sustainable. We know this news will be deeply disappointing to many of you, and we share that sadness.</p> <p>Thirty-one years is a remarkable run, but what matters most is what happened during those years: the journalism, the ideas, the voices, the conversations and the community that grew around them. Salon helped demonstrate what digital journalism could be and could become. We hope that legacy will continue to matter long after today.</p> <p>Thank you for being part of our story. It has been an extraordinary privilege.</p> </blockquote>
<p>I have no idea of the details.</p>
<p>The loss of income is unfortunate but I’m lucky that my meager stipend there was always supplemented by my loyal readers here at Hullabaloo. I doubt that most of the writers there have the same and I feel for them.</p>
<p>I have read Salon since the early days of the internet and it was one of the first places to unabashedly provide the liberal perspective in a sea of conservatism in the early days. Under the leadership of the great Joan Walsh, it became a leading voice of the left during the Bush years as the progressive internet grew into an influential part of our politics.</p>
<p>It went through some bumpy times with changes of ownership, including the departure of Walsh, but in my years there since 2014, it has been a true pleasure to be a part of the team, even in times when the money was scarce. Under editors Andrew O’Hehir, Erin Keane, Sophia Tesfaye and Jason Howard, over the past decade I learned the art of writing a column, seeing what others may not see, and the pleasure of having a big platform to share my thoughts at a time of great historical change.</p>
<p>I sincerely hope that all the good people there will land at great places where their fine work will be appreciated. They are all a pleasure to work with.</p>
<p>Journalism has been in crisis for decades, and it finally came for Salon.com. It was a good run, and everyone should be proud of the work they did over this tumultuous time in history. It was a privilege I’ll treasure forever.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, I’m not going anywhere. This is my creation, my home, my happy place. Tom, Dennis and I will continue to do this as long as we can hold out and you still have an interest.</p>
<p>Stay tuned. The next couple of years are going to be lit.</p>
