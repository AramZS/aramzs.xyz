<p>A little over a week ago, on the night of the Brazilian election runoff, I spent several hours going slowly from despair, to cautious hope, to a sort of weary joy. I live, these days, in a downtown area of São Paulo. I generally hate crowds, but on that night I did walk the length of Avenida Paulista, the city's <em>de facto</em> public square, where people were gathering—at first just to celebrate, and later on to hear president Lula's victory speech, which he delivered in front of the iconic Modernist hulk of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Museum_of_Art">São Paulo Museum of Art</a>.</p>
<p>Ever since I have been periodically reminded of everything that was under threat in the event of another Bolsonaro government, of the losses we couldn't afford that now look to be averted, and of the effort needed to get this outcome.</p>
<p>And I'm also periodically reminded of who was on the other side of this effort; an unpleasant alliance of fascists and criminals who anticipated breaking the nation down for parts. And fighting right alongside them: Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg. Meta, in particular, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/meta-employees-said-lulas-accounts-were-hindered-during-brazil-election-folha-2026-10-16/">directly intervened</a> in suppressing pro-Lula messaging on Instagram while artificially boosting pro-Bolsonaro messaging.</p>
<p>Big tech behaves as a political actor, and they support fascists. Google and Amazon supply cloud infrastructure that Israel's government and military uses in the prosecution of their ongoing genocide against the Palestinian people, as does Microsoft. Tech CEOs have made repeated shows of support and even subservience to the Trump administration.</p>
<p>These companies are embedded in all of our lives, but at the same time they have to be understood as hostile and categorically evil entities. At present, they are all heavily involved in the AI push, which is its own novel form of mass harm that abuts, but isn't exactly the same as, the other forms of harm that these corporations are involved in.</p>
<p>I really do believe at this point that AI is going to be the most harmful new technology of the 21st century. If it is not reined in, it is our time's version of leaded gasoline: ubiquitous and ubiquitously poisonous, a massive corporate assault on the common welfare of human beings and the planet.</p>
<p>Some of those harms are inherent to the technology; I do not believe that it is plausibly safe for everyone to be in conversation with an LLM chatbot regularly, and I've found people who are have their judgment become severely questionable very rapidly. I don't think it is a positive development for mathematicians to be pulling the lever on a <a href="https://mathandai.org/">counterexample slot machine</a> that precludes actual development of new techniques or the personal development of new mathematicians. I don't think the rapid, thoughtless generation of large volumes of source code that nobody has ever read is likely to be positive for software.</p>
<p>The tech, in itself, will always be more adept at generating spam than useful output, will always be more useful to spammers and grifters than to well-meaning actors. It is a semiotic pollutant that attacks the foundations of social and epistemological trust. These systems should not exist. They should not be deployed, they should not be used, by anyone, for any purpose.</p>
<p>But beyond the inherent harmful nature of the tech, the social system that promotes and builds up these systems is <em>itself</em> engaged in mass harm, independently of what LLM systems themselves do. Hyperscalers are standing up new gas turbine capacity that, if they have their way, will set back the US' emissions targets by a decade. The data center build-up is an environmental, labor, and economic disaster. It is sucking the air out of everything else—literally the US would be in recession right now if not for data center construction.</p>
<p>The credit crunch is hitting other venture capital intensive industries like video games; the main impact of AI on the games industry are that no one can afford RAM and there's no funding to make video games. Microsoft's pivot to AI alone is responsible for thousands of jobs lost and the destruction of several important and storied game studios.</p>
<p>All of those harms and all of those forces are, fundamentally, connected. Big tech is a force for disinformation, for the promotion of fascism, for the ruination of our cultural environment and also our literal environment. <em>They have chosen a side,</em> and it's not the side of humanity.</p>
<p>What last month clarified for me is that we are not engaged in the normal to and fro of politics, we are in a hybrid conflict against a loosely-defined but very much unified set of forces: tech corporations, fossil fuels, and international fascism arrayed together. They can be fought, and they can be beaten, but it falls on all of us to understand that <em>this is a conflict and there are sides.</em></p>
<p>Whether you think of it in these terms or not, whether you like it or not, by virtue of being a human being you are in conflict with this adversary, because this adversary has put itself in conflict with humanity. The most basic and universal values are under assault, as is our ability to even articulate those values, as is our shared conception of reality, as is the biosphere itself.</p>
<p>It is fundamental to not understand the current moment as just the adoption shock of a new technology. The technology itself is harmful; but the tech corporations pushing it want to ensure it is an engine of dependency, surveillance, and monopoly.</p>
<p>The people who promote this technology, the people who deploy this technology, are themselves taking a side. It is not a neutral act or a matter of personal convenience. It's signing up to fight, and to fight on the side of those who very nearly knocked of Brazilian democracy a week and a half ago; on the side of new gas turbines; on the side of everything being choked with slop; on the side of total semiotic dissolution.</p>
<p>The actual prompt for this post is that I learned that a friend—or rather, someone I used to respect and consider a friend—has recently taken a job with the AI division of a major tech corporation.</p>
<p>I feel like a lot of us are currently watching people we know fail a moral test, and it can drive one insane to feel like what seems like a clear moral line is a "personal choice" to others. To see people delude themselves with self-serving narratives like "I can steer the way this technology develops from the inside" or "I need to confront what it is head on."</p>
<p>But I wanted to write this specifically to say to other people in this position: No, you're not insane. We are living through a horrific break in human history, and we are going to watch people fail to recognize the battle lines being drawn or simply fail a basic decency check. We are watching a mass betrayal of humanistic values unfold before our eyes. It is natural to be shocked and disgusted.</p>
<p>What it feels like is a civil war; a conflict where we find ourselves opposing people we used to take seriously or even cared about. In video games this has become very stark; big-name indie developers becoming various shades of AI chud while half the people I know in the industry are unemployed and the other half are miserable because of their AI-pilled bosses.</p>
<p>I don't know what the outcome of all this will be, other than I know it will be destructive. The world is simply worse for these systems existing, no matter how many insane bad-faith conversations about "utility" or "capability" you get roped into. It is exhausting and it is miserable to watch.</p>
<p>But it is also true that last month brought the realization that those corporations can be fought, and they can be beaten.</p>
