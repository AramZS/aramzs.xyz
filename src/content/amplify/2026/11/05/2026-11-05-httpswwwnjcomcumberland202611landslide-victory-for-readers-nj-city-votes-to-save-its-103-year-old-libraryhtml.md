<p>Along with their federal, county and local representatives, Bridgeton voters <a href="https://www.nj.com/cumberland/2026/11/nj-election-day-2026-cumberland-county-live-results.html">went to the polls</a> Tuesday to decide the fate of their city library near the heart of downtown.</p>
<p>After a unanimous city council decision this summer to leave <a href="https://www.nj.com/cumberland/2026/07/nj-city-wants-to-shut-down-its-103-year-old-library-locals-are-mad-and-ready-to-fight.html">dissolving</a> the century-old Bridgeton Free Public Library to a binding referendum, voters overwhelmingly chose to keep it.</p>
<p>“It just brought tears of joy to our eyes,” the library director, Michelle Azpiri, told NJ.com on Wednesday. She was up late last night watching the results come in.</p>

<p>The municipal question asked voters, “Shall the free public library…in the City of Bridgeton be dissolved, effective January 1, 2027?”</p>
<p>With 100% of precincts reporting, 1,927 (80.5%) of voters chose “No,” while 468 (19.5%) chose “Yes,” unofficial results showed.</p>
<p>The Election Day decision came after a sudden city council ordinance, which said that the city’s library was “not fiscally prudent” being 1.6 miles away from the Cumberland County Library. It further argued that dissolving the library would save taxpayers money.</p>
<p>NJ.com previously reported that the library tax raised just over $290,000 this year, less than 1% of the city’s $35 million budget. In 2025, that amount per taxpayer was $38.85, according to Bridgeton’s tax rate breakdown based on the close-to-average property assessment of $73,300.</p>
<p>While the Bridgeton Public Library and the Cumberland County Library are close to each other, walking between each requires more than a half hour and crossing multiple busy intersections.</p>
<p>Public transportation serves the Cumberland County Library, but Azpiri argued that would be difficult for patrons who don’t have the money to spend on it in a city where the median household income is just under $57,000 a year, about half of what it is statewide.</p>
<p>“Now you’re paying to go to the library, and then you have to pay again to come back from the library,” she previously told NJ.com.</p>

<p>After NJ.com’s original report, the city council held a sometimes-contentious meeting with public comments on the ordinance in July. Bridgeton residents and those from neighboring towns expressed concern about the ordinance and the possibility that the library could be dissolved.</p>
<p>“I am who I am because of the library. And I’m very emotional because library has a special spot in my heart,” said Maria Perez, a Bridgeton resident who said she grew up in a low-income family and using the library.</p>
<p>After the public hearing, council members made their case for moving with the ordinance as they unanimously approved it — including arguments around changing technology, city budget and future priorities.</p>
<p>“We have to look at the way we are serving our community. We have to be good stewards of the finite money we have,” Councilmember Marian King said at the meeting.</p>
<p>Council President Edward Bethea said the council is “not anti-library,” but “anti-deficit.”</p>
<p>“There’s some things that we simply cannot afford,” he said at the meeting.</p>
<p>Bethea, in a comment posted to Facebook and sent to NJ.com, said the council sought to find a way to manage budget challenges “while recognizing the importance of access to library services.”</p>
<p>“The people have spoken, and we must respect their decision as we continue the work of finding sustainable solutions,” he wrote. “We must be thoughtful stewards of public resources, transparent about the challenges we face, and accountable for the decisions we make.”</p>
<p>Azpiri, among others, campaigned for months to save the library. A <a href="https://www.change.org/p/save-the-bridgeton-free-public-library">Change.org</a> petition reached over 1,000 signatures.</p>
<p>“People kept coming in, talking to us everywhere we went,” Azpiri said. “We heard always positivity about how they were going to vote ‘No.’”</p>

<p>“Everyone is so excited that the library was saved by the community,” Azpiri said.</p>
