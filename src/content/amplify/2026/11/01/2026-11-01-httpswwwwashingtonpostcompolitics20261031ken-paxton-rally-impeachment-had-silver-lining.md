<p>FORT WORTH, Texas — Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton’s 2023 impeachment on bribery and corruption charges has been a major liability during his U.S. Senate race this year. But one lawmaker extolled a supposed bright side.</p>
<p>Speaking at a rally for Paxton on Saturday, state Rep. Andy Hopper cast Paxton’s impeachment as a partisan witch hunt that helped reveal and root out Republican lawmakers who were insufficiently committed to the movement.</p>
<p>After he was acquitted by the state Senate of all charges, Paxton went to political war with the House Republicans who voted to impeach him. He endorsed primary challengers and stumped against incumbent state House Republicans. Nine incumbents lost their primaries that year, and eight more were forced into runoffs. Six of those incumbents lost their runoffs.</p>
<p>“Our state legislature tried to impeach the greatest attorney general, just like they impeached Donald Trump,” Hopper said, who was elected to office amid the purge. “And because of the hard work of Ken Paxton, who honestly was the most significant just force in my race and in so many other races, like Shelley Luther, like Mike Olcott, like Brent Money, like Katrina Pierson, to make sure that conservatives were elected in the body that impeached this man, we have now a — just a force of conservatives.”</p>
<p>Paxton supporters at this rally were not bothered by the impeachment, saying they were satisfied with the state Senate’s acquittal.</p>
<p>“I’ve been in Texas all my life, so I know a lot about things he’s done. You know, some of it’s good. Some of it, I really don’t have much to say about because he was proven innocent,” said Steve Curtis, an attendee from Runaway Bay. “He’s just a better choice than our other choice.”</p>

<p>Days before consequential midterm elections, the head of the Social Security Administration blasted out an email to the public praising President Donald Trump.</p>
<p>“Under President Donald J. Trump, the Social Security Administration (SSA) is transforming into a model of excellence, one that meets and exceeds customer expectations for fast, accurate, and secure service,” Commissioner Frank J. Bisignano wrote to people with online Social Security accounts.</p>
<p>The emails were sent on Friday and Saturday, according to copies of the email reviewed by The Washington Post and posted on social media accounts. Online, some recipients <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/fednews/comments/1xjsj5l/official_ssa_email_reads_like_propaganda/?rdt=45532">complained</a> about the email, saying it read like propaganda.</p>
<p>The Social Security Administration did not immediately respond to questions Saturday about why it had sent the email and whether it was timed to coincide with Tuesday’s elections.</p>
<p>The agency did not say how many emails it had sent. More than 100 million people <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/press/releases/2026-02-09.html">have online accounts</a> with the Social Security Administration.</p>

<p>The Democratic chairwoman of a rural Iowa county said Friday that she would not vote for her party’s Senate candidate, state Rep. Josh Turek, over his advertising on transgender issues.</p>
<p><a href="https://x.com/AdImpact_Pol/status/2114091965935407128?a=yNAu1PrYUDb8f7Id5P0&amp;s=20">In a recent ad</a> that reiterated his earlier positions, Turek says that while “no Iowan should face discrimination,” he does not support “males competing in female sports” and is “against taxpayers paying for sex changes.”</p>
<p>Kathy Winter, chair of the Osceola County Democratic Party, said in a statement that Turek’s stance runs afoul of the <a href="https://www.bleedingheartland.com/2026/10/15/the-iowa-democratic-partys-2026-platform-democracy-in-action/">Iowa Democratic Party’s platform</a>, which opposes restrictions on transgender health care.</p>
<p>“At a time of unprecedented hate and bigotry being directed at transgender Iowans, I cannot in good [conscience] vote for a man who is promising to make their lives worse,” Winter said in a statement.</p>
<p>One Iowa Action, an LGBTQ+ advocacy group, also <a href="https://oneiowaaction.org/one-iowa-action-statement-on-anti-trans-political-ads/">condemned the ad</a>.</p>
<p>Iowa Democratic Party Chairwoman Rita Hart and other Democrats  defended Turek in statements provided by his campaign.</p>
<p>“While I disagree with his ad, I know I can talk to him and make my concerns heard,” said Adam Peters, a Democratic state House candidate who has worked for an LGBTQ+ nonprofit. “You can too. I can’t say the same for Ashley Hinson.”</p>
<p>Other Democrats running in Republican-leaning states this year have <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/30/democrats-stances-trans-issues-test-how-much-lgbtq-groups-will-tolerate/">faced criticism</a> from their own party and LGBTQ+ activists for airing similar ads. Some activists say such ads go too far and use conservative rhetoric insulting to trans people.</p>
<p>Turek is <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/30/locked-close-senate-race-iowa-republican-pledges-be-thorn-trumps-side/">locked in a close race</a> with Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson for an open Senate seat in a race that could determine control of the chamber.</p>
<p>Turek’s campaign started running the ad last week after Republicans spent millions of dollars attacking him on transgender issues, including an ad from Hinson and the National Republican Senatorial Committee that <a href="https://platform.adimpact.com/viewer/c2170af8-1a27-4053-b1e4-8a6fa5f06d48">accused him</a> of supporting “taxpayer-funded sex-change surgeries.” Turek <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/28/politics/video/turek-transgender-ad-upset-left-digvid">told CNN</a> that he thought it was important to “set the record straight.”</p>

<p>The social media platform X temporarily suspended an official account associated with Sen. Dan S. Sullivan (R-Alaska), claiming that the profile violated the site’s rules against inauthentic accounts.</p>
<p>Sullivan’s campaign confirmed the profile was suspended and that the account received a notice saying it was reported and suspended for violating the company’s impersonation rules. The account, @DanSullivan_AK, was back online as of Saturday afternoon. X did not immediately respond to a request for comment.</p>
<p>The suspension was <a href="https://x.com/alaskalandmine/status/2116421103635829222?a=C3HbXsFmTwEtYcDowQE&amp;s=20">reported </a>earlier by the Alaska Landmine.</p>
<p>Sullivan is in the final stretch of a highly competitive race to retain his seat in the Senate, with his main rival being Democrat Mary Peltola. On general election ballots, the incumbent senator’s name is set to appear alongside Peltola, Republican candidate Gerald Heikes and a similarly named Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher who is running as an independent.</p>
<p>The New York Times <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/28/us/politics/alaska-dan-sullivan-conspiracy-peltola.html">reported</a> this week that Peltola’s campaign was involved in recruiting Dan J. Sullivan in an effort to dilute the number of votes intended for the incumbent senator. Dan J. Sullivan has denied being recruited by the Democrats, and Peltola’s campaign has said it had no involvement with the campaign of either Dan Sullivan.</p>
<p>Republicans have said they are launching investigations into whether Democrats and Peltola’s campaign colluded to recruit spoiler candidates in the race.</p>

<p>One of Tom Corbit’s top concerns in this year’s elections is the proliferation of data centers. He knows the issue well.</p>
<p>Corbit, 70, retired from the BMW plant near Spartanburg, South Carolina, as a data center manager.</p>
<p>The centers he managed for the automaker, Corbit said, were no bigger than the ice cream shop where he attended a town hall this week with Democratic Senate nominee Annie Andrews, who is running against Sen. Darline Graham (R) in a very tight race.</p>
<p>“They were not the humongous monsters they’re putting in today,” Corbit said.</p>
<p>Residents of Spartanburg County have been <a href="https://www.postandcourier.com/spartanburg/news/spartanburg-northmark-data-center-power-increase-plan/article_918f2606-0e22-40ff-b07e-67a8b0d7dd99.html">riled by plans </a>for a $2.8 billion data center that began construction before the public realized what the massive project was.</p>
<p>“I always saw them as good things,” Corbit said of his 30-year career working on data centers. “But the way they’re coming in is wrong. The people don’t have a voice in it, just the companies making money.”</p>
<p>Andrews has called for a two-year moratorium on data center construction in South Carolina, pitching the pause as a chance for public policy to catch up to the fast-growing, resource-heavy industry.</p>
<p>Graham, by contrast, has said data center expansion is necessary to compete with China and last week became the first beneficiary of a new political action committee formed by data center interests. The New York Times <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/26/us/politics/darline-graham-data-centers-south-carolina.html?eafs_enabled=false">reported</a> that the Building the Future PAC has contributed nearly $1.5 million in advertising on behalf of Graham.</p>
<p>That’s enough for Corbit. “Darline is all about it, jumping on board,” he said, ensuring that he’ll vote for Andrews.</p>
