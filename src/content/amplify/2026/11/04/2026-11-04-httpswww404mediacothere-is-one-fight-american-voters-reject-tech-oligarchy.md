<p>Last month, OpenAI CEO <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/04/sam-altman-decoded-interview-ai-01106217?ref=404media.co"><u>Sam Altman told Politico</u></a> that “we believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology,” referring to ChatGPT users dying, people losing their jobs to AI, and the other negative externalities we have seen from AI.</p>
<p>Altman wasn’t running for office Tuesday. But this worldview—that the masses must shut up and suffer while oligarchs centralize power, self-deal, spend wildly to capture the political process, and automate away human endeavors so they can amass yet more wealth—was wholly repudiated in Tuesday’s election as people in Red States, Blue States, and Purple States rejected the cruelty, corruption, and <a href="https://www.404media.co/with-iran-war-kalshi-and-polymarket-bet-that-the-depravity-economy-has-no-bottom/">depravity economy</a> we’ve seen over the last few years. They are tired of kleptocracy.</p>
<p>The main difference between Donald Trump’s administration this term and his first term is that Silicon Valley’s oligarchs have lined up to kiss the ring; look no further than the <a href="https://apnews.com/article/trump-inauguration-tech-billionaires-zuckerberg-musk-wealth-0896bfc3f50d941d62cebc3074267ecd?ref=404media.co"><u>photos from his inauguration</u></a> and his gilded gala with China’s President <a href="https://www.npr.org/2026/09/25/g-s1-144973/trump-state-dinner-xi?ref=404media.co"><u>Xi Jinping earlier this fall</u></a>. Every major tech company CEO has spent the last two years flattering Trump, giving him gifts, and trying to capture the political process. They have been generously rewarded for their fealty and their money as the Trump administration has spent the last two years letting Big Tech do whatever it wants.</p>

<p>Elon Musk was given the keys to the federal government and used them to slash critical government programs, lay off tens of thousands of public servants, and replace collective human expertise with <a href="https://www.404media.co/i-watched-6-hours-of-doge-bro-testimony-heres-what-they-had-to-say-for-themselves/"><u>teenaged idiots</u></a> and <a href="https://www.404media.co/github-is-leaking-trumps-plans-to-accelerate-ai-across-government/"><u>AI algorithms</u></a>. His companies have continued to rake in billions in government contracts, his busted AI has been integrated into critical government systems, and the cuts led by his “Department of Government Efficiency” has, conservatively, <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-new-yorker-interview/the-human-cost-of-doges-war-on-usaid?ref=404media.co"><u>killed many thousands of people around the world</u></a>. Trump has <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/news/trump-pardoned-3-crypto-felons-133109095.html?ref=404media.co"><u>pardoned crypto criminals</u></a>, his family has made <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/trump-once-said-bitcoin-seems-183006830.html?ref=404media.co"><u>billions of dollars in cryptocurrency scams</u></a>, and has overseen the rise of a gambling and <a href="https://www.404media.co/with-iran-war-kalshi-and-polymarket-bet-that-the-depravity-economy-has-no-bottom/"><u>prediction market-based “economy” rife</u></a> with insider trading and misery (the idea of the administration doing anything to crack down on this is so implausible that to even suggest it could happen is laughable). He has installed manosphere podcasters, X trolls, and conspiracy theorists into top administration roles; they have performed even worse than their already low expectations. He has leveraged commercial surveillance companies to build a brutal, <a href="https://www.404media.co/elite-the-palantir-app-ice-uses-to-find-neighborhoods-to-raid/"><u>tech-powered mass deportation campaign</u></a>. His government’s communications strategy can be described in no other way than <a href="https://www.404media.co/the-ai-slop-presidency/"><u>inscrutable AI slop shitposting</u></a>. This is to say nothing of the cost of living crisis Trump has caused as he’s started an illegal war in Iran that has driven up gas and diesel prices and levied tariffs on America’s allies for reasons that defy explanation. This has driven up the price of everything even as Trump’s administration slashes regulations on fossil fuel companies and big corporations.</p>

<p>And then there’s AI. The Trump administration has let Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Dario Amodei, Mark Zuckerberg, Sundar Pichai, and Satya Nadella do whatever they want, even as their products drive mass anxiety, the <a href="https://www.404media.co/ai-slop-is-a-brute-force-attack-on-the-algorithms-that-control-reality/"><u>destruction of the internet as we know it</u></a>, and have caused corporations to bet that the future is widespread mass unemployment. Trump has <a href="https://abcnews.com/Politics/data-reign-trump-stands-firmly-controversial-data-centers/story?id=136092127&amp;ref=404media.co"><u>cheered on the construction of data centers</u></a> even as his base of supporters have said they don’t want them in their communities. Trump has said American communities should <a href="https://abcnews.com/Politics/data-reign-trump-stands-firmly-controversial-data-centers/story?id=136092127&amp;ref=404media.co"><u>“let data reign”</u></a> and that if communities “want to be successful and rich” they should build more data centers.</p>
<p>As a reward for letting Silicon Valley run the country, Elon Musk spent $474 million trying to help Republicans maintain the status quo; super PACs tied to the cryptocurrency and artificial intelligence industries spent roughly $250 million to largely support Republicans and oppose Democrats, <a href="https://influence.citationneeded.news/?ref=404media.co"><u>according to Molly White’s Tech Influence Watch project</u></a>. A16z and people tied to it spent roughly $94.4 million <a href="https://influence.citationneeded.news/2026/companies/andreessen-horowitz?ref=404media.co"><u>trying to influence the election</u></a>, according to White. This is just a small sample of the ways that Silicon Valley attempted to put its thumbs on the scale of this election to prop up a deeply unpopular administration.</p>
<p>And, in nearly every conceivable way, voters aggressively rejected Trump and Silicon Valley’s vision for America. It turns out people do not want to accept “some bad things happening” to pad the valuations and pocketbooks of billionaires and trillionaires when the “bad things” include worsening wealth inequality, a housing and cost of living crisis, mass automation and unemployment, mass surveillance, cuts to government services, foreign wars, <a href="https://www.404media.co/data-centers-power-energy-grid-epa-clean-air-act/"><u>loud, polluting data centers</u></a> that employ a few construction workers while they’re being built and no one after that are being used to increase profits for out-of-state corporations, and a culture war that infringes on the civil rights of immigrants and trans people.</p>
<p>This was a rejection of Trump and the Republican Party, but also a rejection of big tech. Across the country, there were 40 ballot measures about AI data center projects; <a href="https://datacentersontheballot.com/?ref=404media.co">voters chose to restrict 39 of them</a>. In Ohio—a state that has become extremely Red in recent cycles—Democrat Sherrod Brown cruised to a five-point win <a href="https://www.huffpost.com/entry/sherrod-brown-defeats-jon-husted-ohio-senate-race_n_6ad79710e4b04f71533c6370?ref=404media.co"><u>running on a campaign to rein in data centers</u></a>. Vivek Ramaswamy, a Peter Thiel acolyte and billionaire venture capitalist was absolutely trounced by Amy Acton, a public health official who supports data center moratoriums and vaccines and presented herself to the state’s voters as being a normal person and not a deranged Twitter troll. In New York State, Governor Kathy Hochul won reelection after enacting the <a href="https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/first-statewide-moratorium-new-hyperscale-data-centers-launched-governor-kathy-hochul?ref=404media.co"><u>nation’s first statewide moratorium</u></a> on new hyperscale data centers in July; her opponent <a href="https://www.404media.co/bruce-blakeman-ai-campaign-video-mamdani-hochul/"><u>Bruce Blakeman</u></a> <a href="https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/politics/2026/07/15/hochul-blakeman-data-center-moratorium?ref=404media.co"><u>called the moratorium “crazy”</u></a> and vowed to end it if he won.</p>
<p>In Texas, Musk spent $212 million trying to elect the wildly unpopular Ken Paxton (who, as an aside, once <a href="https://www.404media.co/404-media-objects-to-texas-attorney-general-ken-paxtons-subpoena-to-access-our-reporting/"><u>threatened to subpoena</u></a> 404 Media’s reporting); in exchange for lighting this money on fire, Texas has, in James Talarico, its first Democratic Senator in nearly 40 years. Talarico <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/04/opinion/talarico-hinojosa-texas-democrats.html?ref=404media.co"><u>began his campaign by saying</u></a> “The people at the top — the Elon Musks, the Rupert Murdochs, the Mark Zuckerbergs of the world — they spend so much time and energy keeping us angry and divided because our unity is a threat to their wealth and their power.” Musk barely tweeted Tuesday; once it became clear his preferred candidates had lost and that people do not want AI data centers, <a href="https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2117862462976594112?ref=404media.co"><u>he finally suggested</u></a> “data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now,” presumably because it’s clear people don’t want them here on Earth. Jason Calacanis, the intolerable venture capitalist and host of the unlistenable All In podcast <a href="https://x.com/Jason/status/2117823943050424362?ref=404media.co"><u>tweeted a bunch of prediction market screenshots</u></a> of the Talarico race and said he “never expected Texas to turn blue. Ugh, I moved here to get away from the left lunacy!”</p>
<p>"Silicon Valley billionaires want us to believe that Al robots will destroy humanity," Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez <a href="https://x.com/charlesmonaco/status/2101056243863277861?ref=404media.co">said recently</a>. "But the biggest threat to humanity are the tech oligarchs themselves."</p>
<p>Silicon Valley spent wildly on the election and Republicans have spent much of the last decade trying to press structural advantages to gerrymander districts, suppress the vote, and sow doubt on our elections. The sheer anger voters feel was enough to overwhelm these efforts Tuesday, nearly without exception (the one main outlier: A billionaire wealth tax ballot measure in California looks likely to fail after billionaires <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/nation/2026/11/02/californias-mega-rich-vastly-outspend-foes-bid-sink-billionaire-tax/?ref=404media.co"><u>spent $379 million lobbying to kill it</u></a>, outspending the opposition 250-to-1, with many billionaires threatening to move their companies and themselves out of the state). Given this, it should not be taken for granted that elections can or will always go this way, and Democrats need to show that they are not scared of governing.</p>
<p>Up and down the ballot, in all states, voters were generally given the choice between largely normal people trying to fight for the working class and sycophantic cartoon supervillains, nepo picks, fumbling buffoons, and Twitter trolls seeking to enable the regime and Big Tech’s vision of the future, where AI CEOs become trillionaires and everyone else loses their job to a robot and breathes methane gas from the data centers in their backyards. It turns out that from Michigan to Mississippi (<a href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/mississippi-senate-race-too-close-093944439.html?ref=404media.co"><u>yes, Mississippi</u></a>), people are done accepting “bad things happening” for the benefits of the richest people in the world. Nominally, there were a lot of issues at play in this election, but there is, really, only one issue. There is a vision of the world focused on improving the wellbeing of average people and there is a vision of the world that focused on making a few dozen people in Silicon Valley and Washington, D.C. even richer and more powerful. Newly-elected Texas Democratic Gov (!?!?) Gina Hinojosa <a href="https://x.com/davidsirota/status/2117892310554104242?ref=404media.co"><u>recently made this clear</u></a> to the <em>New York Times</em>, which still doesn’t get it.</p>
<p>Hinosa crushed three-term incumbent governor Greg Abbott by running as an “economic populist,” a label the media has wrongly given to Trump and MAGA because they villainize immigrants and trans people but govern like oligarchs. “We are facing an existential crisis in this country because tech giants are having their way with us,” Hinojosa <a href="https://x.com/davidsirota/status/2117892310554104242?ref=404media.co"><u>recently told the <em>New York Times</em></u></a><em>, </em>who, like normal, had fully missed the point.</p>
<p>“There is one fight,” she added, referring to the people vs the oligarchs. Now, Democrats need to govern like it.</p>
