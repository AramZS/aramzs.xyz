title: US midterms delivered a blow to tech’s AI ambitions
---
<p>There’s no question the US midterms elections delivered a blow to Donald Trump. The Democrats took not just the House and Senate back from the Republicans, but won a wave of governor’s races across the country — including in states it would’ve previously been hard to imagine a Democrat winning.</p>
<p>But it wasn’t just Trump who lost. The tech industry and its ambitions to cover the country in hyperscale data centers to power generative AI were potentially even bigger losers than the orange man himself.</p>
<p>It didn’t matter that Elon Musk shelled out <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cqkgk64z7p58o?ref=disconnect.blog">over $400 million</a> to try to save Republicans as polling increasingly suggested a wipeout was coming, especially in his chosen home of Texas, or that crypto and AI companies <a href="https://influence.citationneeded.news/?ref=disconnect.blog">deployed millions more</a> on races across the country. An NBC News exit poll still found <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-elections/exit-polls?ref=disconnect.blog">56% of voters</a> said AI and data centers were “crucial or a major factor” in their vote for Congress. And it showed in the results.</p>
<p>The Democrats <a href="https://www.axios.com/2026/10/31/data-center-hot-spots-ads-2028?ref=disconnect.blog">spent big on attack ads </a>presenting their Republican opponents as backing data centers that were harming communities and being in the pocket of the tech industry. That had a real impact on races, as you can see in Sherrod Brown’s fight for a Senate seat in Ohio.</p>
<p>He slammed his opponent John Husted, <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/02/data-centers-midterms-husted-abbott-shapiro-election-01128252?ref=disconnect.blog">saying</a> he “gave up Ohio’s money — our tax money — to Meta, a company worth $2 trillion, to make sure they come to Ohio.” Husted, like so many Republicans who backed data centers, tried to do a last minute pivot, in his case with the Ratepayer Protection Act that he claimed who protect ratepayers from price increases brought on by data centers. It wasn’t enough though: Brown beat him handily. You see <a href="https://heatmap.news/politics/data-center-election-results?ref=disconnect.blog">similar stories</a> in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Virginia, and beyond.</p>
<p>Those dynamics were repeated across the country, and not just in Congress. Texas, Ohio, and Georgia are at the center of the current data center buildout in the United States — and they’ve all flipped. Gina Hinojosa will now become the first Democratic governor of Texas since 1990, having <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/ai-data-center-boom-is-set-for-upheaval-after-us-election-rout?ref=disconnect.blog">run on a moratorium</a> on data center construction after using her opponent Greg Abbott’s relationship with Musk against him. Similar dynamics played out in the races in Georgia and Ohio, while Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro held on after <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/11/josh-shapiro-data-center-record-political-future-environment/?ref=disconnect.blog">reversing his previous embrace</a> of the industry, pulling data centers out of the state’s fast track approval process, and calling some companies “greedy” and “predatory.”</p>
<p>Voters across ten states also <a href="https://ballotpedia.org/Data_center-related_ballot_measures,_2026?ref=disconnect.blog">voted on ballot measures</a> related to data centers yesterday, delivering an overwhelming mandate for their governments to crack down on the projects, particularly in Ohio. Whether it’s on the state level, in Congress, or across many communities, Americans have made it clear they are not on board with the AI vision being foisted on them by Silicon Valley and are ready to take power back from large companies trying to shape their surroundings for the interests of billionaires located far away from where the impacts will be felt.</p>
<p>There’s no question this is a major win. It puts the tech industry on the back foot, and will undoubtedly have them recalibrating. Data center <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-drop-100424346.html?ref=disconnect.blog">stocks are down</a> following the results, and a bruised Musk is whining on his social media platform, <a href="https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2117862462976594112?ref=disconnect.blog">posting</a> that data centers in space “are looking pretty appealing” now that his plans to expand in Texas are likely on ice. But now that the blow has been delivered, we should be ready for how the industry will respond.</p>
<p>There will undoubtedly be the more openly right-leaning tech CEOs complaining about a Democratic victory, but the more clever ones will be trying to figure out how they recalibrate to ensure projects are still getting approved. We’ve already seen Amazon announce it <a href="https://www.wired.com/story/amazon-says-it-is-going-to-stop-using-ndas-for-data-centers/?ref=disconnect.blog">won’t use non-disclosure agreements</a> anymore and the industry more widely embrace closed-loop cooling to try to address community concerns about water use (without <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/08/us-datacenters-wastewater-pollution?ref=disconnect.blog">disclosing</a> the chemical tradeoff), to provide a couple examples. They will surely start taking more efforts designed to assuage concerns, while still trying to move forward hyperscale data centers that are fundamentally unsustainable and beyond what many communities can bear.</p>
<p>Another path is spending. While tech spending still leaned Republican in this cycle, the AI industry is (sort of) bucking that trend. According to figures tracked by Molly White, AI-focused super PACs <a href="https://www.citationneeded.news/2026-election-guide/?ref=disconnect.blog">spent more supporting Democrats</a> than Republicans as groups like Anthropic push for AI regulation, but try to ensure it’s focused on supposed existential risks like AI ending humanity rather than the present-day harms we see from its products. That’s all to say, while this is a win, we need to remain on guard. Some of the wealthiest people and most powerful companies will not just accept a setback to their interests. Their response is already in the works.</p>
