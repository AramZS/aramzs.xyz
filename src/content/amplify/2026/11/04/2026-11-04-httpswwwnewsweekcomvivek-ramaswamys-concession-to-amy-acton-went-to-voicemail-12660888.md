title: Vivek Ramaswamy’s Concession to Amy Acton Went to Voicemail

<p><a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/vivek-ramaswamy">Vivek Ramaswamy</a> said his call to Amy Acton went to voicemail when he contacted her to concede the <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/social-tags-ohio">Ohio</a> governor's race, as Democrats scored historic midterm victories Tuesday.</p>
<p>"We left her a message," Ramaswamy said, as he admitted "It's a tough night for us here in Ohio."</p>
<p>Tensions were high between the pair after the Republican put out a campaign ad last month which featured Acton's estranged mother calling her a liar.</p>
<p>With 81 percent of votes counted, Acton had garnered 50.7 percent of the vote, while Ramaswamy had 46 percent, when the Republican former presidential contender conceded.</p>
<p>"Ohio has chosen unity and decency over division and hate, the healing begins today," Acton told supporters in her victory speech.</p>
<p>Acton's win saw Democrats take back control in Ohio for the first time in two decades. In one of the <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/who-will-win-the-midterms-what-polls-show-10-days-out-12610772">most closely watched midterm elections in years</a>, her party also took the U.S. <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/topic/senate">Senate</a> by flipping key seats in Texas, Kansas, North Carolina and South Carolina, allowing the Democrats to secure control <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/midterm-results-tracker-house-live-updates-election-2026-12659126">of both chambers of Congress</a></p>

<p>The 60-year-old is the first woman to be elected to serve as Ohio's governor, and will end 16 years of Republican control in the Buckeye state.</p>
<p>"I wish her well to lead this state to hopefully greater heights in the future," Ramaswamy said during his concession speech, adding that it was a "tough race" which was "bitter at times in both directions."</p>
<p><em>Newsweek </em>reached out to Acton's campaign for comment outside of regular working hours.</p>
<h2>Vivek Ramaswamy Concedes After Contentious Race and Despised Campaign Advert</h2>
<p>The race had become one of the most expensive and contentious in the country.</p>
<p>The two did not meet for a debate during the campaign.</p>
<p>The Ramaswamy campaign advert featured Acton’s mother Donna Arthur saying her daughter was lying about her childhood.</p>
<p>Acton’s mother lost custody of her after her husband, Acton’s stepfather, was indicted for allegedly raping Acton at the age of 12.</p>
<p>The backlash drew bipartisan condemnation, and Ramaswamy was urged to remove the advert and apologize. President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance had backed the ad, and the campaign was due to spend $4 million airing it, before it was discontinued.</p>

<p>The advert had drawn condemnation from outgoing Republican Governor Mike DeWine, who called it "deeply, deeply troubling." He also urged Ramaswamy to take down the ad, but did not rescind his endorsement.</p>
<p>At a campaign event in northwest Ohio after the advert was released, Acton referred to it as the "big elephant in the room."</p>
<p>"The reason I talk about my childhood when I do is just one, I started to realize it might give other people hope that you can, you know, get out of your circumstances," she said.</p>
<p>Speaking at a rally in Cleveland on October 24 with former President Barack Obama, Acton said: "My opponent has chosen to exploit, intentionally my childhood to save his struggling campaign, but it's not going to work."</p>
<p>Acton will now lead the state, which sits at the forefront of debates over the economy, AI data centers and the country’s political economy.</p>
<p>With 95 percent of the vote counted, Acton stretched her lead with 51.9 percent of the vote to Ramaswamy’s 44.8 percent, according to The Associated Press. Donald Kissick of the Libertarian Party won 3.3 percent of the vote.</p>
<p>"The time tomorrow is to hopefully have her leadership bring this state together, and I have hope and confidence that she will do what is required to bring Ohio together," Ramaswamy added in his concession speech.</p>
<p>"We are rooting for the success of this state; we’re rooting for your success, and that means all of us in this room should be rooting for the success of her administration, and we’re hopeful for Ohio to achieve great things regardless of what happened tonight in this election," he said.</p>
<p>Acton is the first Democrat to win Ohio’s governorship since Ted Strickland won a single four-year term in 2006.</p>
<p>She will also be the first woman to fully serve in the role, following the Former Lt. Gov Nancy Hollister who served as governor for 11 days in 1999, after the Republican Governor George Voinovich moved to the Senate.</p>
<p>The win marks a shift for Ohio, which has moved decisively to the right since Strickland’s 2006 win, having backed Trump in three straight presidential elections by a comfortable margin.</p>
<p>Now, Democrat Acton succeeds Republican DeWine, a man she served under as health director in the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic.</p>
<h2>What Amy Acton Said in Her Victory Speech</h2>
<p>"I’m so incredibly honored that I have earned the trust of Ohioans to be the next governor of Ohio," Acton said while delivering her victory speech, after walking out to the Florence and the Machine Song, <em>You’ve Got the Love</em>.</p>

<p>"It has meant the world to me and my family," she said. "This entire campaign has been a movement by and for the people; this win is for every Ohioan who has longed for public servants again, who solve problems instead of making them and do not mistake kindness for weakness."</p>
<p>"We must never give up on one another," Acton said.</p>
<p>Like her opponent, she acknowledged that it had been a "tough" race.</p>
<p>The billionaire Ohio native had an endorsement from Trump, top strategists from his and Vance’s political teams advising him and an aligned super PAC.</p>
<p>"I wanna make something very clear to the people of Ohio. I will be a governor for all Ohioans," Acton said in her speech. "I am in this fight for all of us."</p>
<p>She then promised bipartisan cooperation, and said, "To those who voted for me, and those who did not, I will work with anyone and everyone."</p>
<p>She added that she looks forward to continuing the work that DeWine has done in the state.</p>
<p>Acton also spoke about her life and journey.</p>
<p>"I know what it’s like when people look the other way when you’re struggling," she said. "As a child, I experienced abuse and neglect. And I could have never imagined going from homeless to being a doctor."</p>
<p>"Let alone that I would be standing here, the next Governor of Ohio," Acton said.</p>

<h2><strong>2026 Midterm Results: Democrats Pull Off Wave of Historic Victories</strong></h2>
<p>Across the country, Democrats have secured victories in key races leading to <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/midterm-results-tracker-senate-live-updates-election-2026-12637517">control of both the U.S. Senate </a>and the <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/midterm-results-tracker-house-live-updates-election-2026-12659126">House of Representatives</a>.</p>
<p>In Michigan, <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/michigan-senate-midterms-mike-el-sayed-mike-rogers-12652381">Abdul El-Sayed defeated Republican opponent Mike Rogers</a> to become the first Muslim member of the U.S. Senate, after one of the most expensive and bitter contests of the election cycle.</p>
