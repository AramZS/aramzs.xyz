<p>The Jensen Huang era of Nvidia nearly came to an end this month. Huang, who three decades ago co-founded what’s come to be considered one of the most important companies in AI, resigned from his role on November 12th over mounting scrutiny around his alleged affairs. Huang admitted he had been “unfaithful” to his wife and <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1035420/jensen-huang-nvidia-return-as-ceo-statement">that he</a> “created problems for the board.”</p>
<p>Huang’s resignation came after weeks of escalating problems, both personal and professional. First, reporting from <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html"><em>The New York Times</em> said</a> he had a “close relationship” with a woman passing intelligence to China. Subsequent reporting said that China’s intelligence agency <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/06/us/politics/chinese-spy-agency-nvidia-jensen-huang.html">had enlisted</a> that woman, Alice Huang, then an executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, to spy on Nvidia’s leader, and that the Singapore-based company was <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/09/business/economy/nvidia-megaspeed-investigation-huang.html">under investigation</a> for potentially smuggling AI chips to China. Stories followed that indicated Huang was having an affair with Alice as well as an Nvidia executive, vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell.</p>
<p>So when Huang resigned less than two weeks ago, the decision seemed final. Even Nvidia’s board appeared unwilling to budge. “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” board chair Harvey Jones <a href="https://www.theverge.com/report/1035465/nvidia-says-it-will-not-reinstate-jensen-huang-as-ceo">said during an earnings call last week</a>.</p>
<p>But in the days following Huang’s resignation, the founder’s allies — including President Donald Trump — mounted a pressure campaign that brought him back to the company he created and sent Nvidia’s share price rocketing upward, almost as though he had never left.</p>
<p>The campaign in support of Huang began almost immediately after his resignation. A coterie of tech leaders <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1032434/nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-reactions">began posting in support</a> of Nvidia’s founder: Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney <a href="https://x.com/TimSweeneyEpic/status/2121083415487205652?a=GkC-fyXfE8yEC7vLKcc&amp;s=20">questioned</a> the motives of journalists who broke the stories about his affairs. Tesla CEO Elon Musk likened Huang’s <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1037285/elon-musk-on-jensen-huangs-departure-its-like-kicking-michael-jordan-off-your-team-when-youre-on-a-winning-streak">departure</a> to “kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you’re on a winning streak.” <a href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/tech-ceos-saga-jensen-huangs-exit-nvidia-138230541">OpenAI CEO Sam Altman later said on stage</a>, “it’s hard to imagine the AI revolution without Jensen.”</p>
<blockquote><p>“If I didn’t get involved, you’d probably have a very sad Nvidia for a long time to come,” Trump said.</p></blockquote>
<p>Meanwhile, some of those tech leaders began questioning aspects of the reporting that led to Huang’s exit. <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1035027/tech-leaders-nvidia-jensen-huang-theories">Venture capitalist and former White House AI czar David Sacks</a> cast doubt that Alice Huang was a spy, citing a lack of confirmation from US officials. Trump exacerbated the uncertainty, <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/trump-support-jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-rcna609597">telling reporters</a>, “I don’t know that she is a spy,” despite reports saying the assessment had <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/08/us/politics/rubio-nvidia-ceo-chinese-spy.html">relied on</a> US intelligence sources.</p>
<p>Nvidia’s board, meanwhile, found itself in a tough position. The company’s <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/nvidias-7-week-losing-streak-is-longest-since-2008-financial-crisis.html">shares crashed</a> following Huang’s departure, and they didn’t pick up even after a rosy earnings report. The board was also scrambling to find a new CEO, <a href="https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&amp;xs=1&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.morningstar.com%2Fnews%2Fmarketwatch%2F2026110971%2Fthere-is-no-one-at-nvidia-who-would-be-ready-to-replace-jensen-huang">something industry analysts speculated</a> would <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/nvidia-drama-triggers-difficult-search-for-huang-s-successor">prove exceptionally difficult</a>. Reports indicated that Nvidia was considering <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/arm-ceo-extends-contract-through-2033-after-nvidia-approach">hiring Arm CEO Rene Haas</a> and Apple hardware <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1031766/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-replacement-rumors">chief Johny Srouji</a>, both of whom already have powerful roles in the chip industry.</p>
<p>Trump quickly warmed up to Huang’s return, too. The president — whose investment accounts <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/trump-nvidia-stock-trades-jensen-huang.html">traded up to $97 million worth of Nvidia stock</a> since returning to office — initially floated the idea of having <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1032774/president-donald-trump-tim-cook-nvidia-jensen-huang">former Apple CEO Tim Cook take Huang’s place</a>, before <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1034355/trump-jensen-huang-nvidia-return">saying that it</a> “certainly isn’t a bad idea” to bring Huang back.</p>
<p>This past weekend, Trump appears to have inserted himself into the situation directly. A <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/23/us/politics/trump-nvidia-huang.html">report from <em>The New York Times</em> says</a> that Trump asked Vice President JD Vance to work with Huang and Nvidia’s board to “resolve the situation,” while the Trump administration reassured the board that it “did not believe that Mr. Huang himself posed a national security risk.” <a href="https://www.semafor.com/article/11/23/2026/huang-reinstatement-came-at-trumps-behest"><em>Semafor</em> reports</a> that Trump even called into Nvidia’s board meeting to advocate for Huang’s reinstatement. Sunday evening, Trump <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117663497310955803">said</a> in a post on Truth Social that he hopes Nvidia’s board “rehires Jensen Huang.”</p>
<p>Hours later, Nvidia’s board did just that. The board announced on Monday that it <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039937/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-reinstated">reinstated Huang as its CEO</a>, president, and board member. In its statement, the board said that it received input “from senior levels of the United States government,” and determined that “Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”</p>
<p>Trump immediately took credit for Huang’s reinstatement, with White House spokesperson Steven Cheung saying the president “was totally involved” in the decision. “If I didn’t get involved, you’d probably have a very sad Nvidia for a long time to come,” Trump said during an interview with CNBC. He <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117666141823845971">added in a post on</a> Truth Social that the US Attorney General confirmed that it doesn’t have evidence that Huang “engaged in any unlawful conduct.”</p>
<p><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-nvidia-shares.html">Nvidia’s shares rose</a> on the news of Huang’s reinstatement, while <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1040069/jensen-huang-return-elon-musk-sam-altman-yay">tech leaders celebrated his return on social media</a>. But the saga is likely far from over. The board said it will finish its investigation into Huang, the Securities and Exchange Commission is <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039305/nvda-down-sec-investigation-accounting">looking into Nvidia’s</a> “accounting practices,” and Megaspeed is <a href="https://www.theverge.com/policy/1035410/doj-megaspeed-nvidia-investigation-update">being investigated</a> by the Justice Department. Even Trump has said that Nvidia should continue its internal investigation about export controls compliance.</p>
<p>Trump’s apparent involvement also leaves plenty of room for scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers, <a href="https://apnews.com/article/midterm-election-house-senate-democrats-republicans-trump-f126e12e05b579a327b98481c0ae2226">who will control</a> the House and Senate next year. Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) <a href="https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/senator-warren-statement-on-jensen-huang-reinstatement-as-nvidia-ceo">called</a> Nvidia’s decision “reckless” and urged the Trump administration to explain the “input” government officials provided to the company’s board</p>
<p>Huang’s stunning reinstatement still leaves many questions unanswered. Though the backing of Wall Street, the White House, and Silicon Valley may have brought Huang back as CEO, a new wave of scrutiny from federal regulators and lawmakers is just getting started.</p>
