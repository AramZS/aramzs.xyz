<p><strong>The directors of <em>You Can See Everything</em> are pushing back against new reporting about the film’s controversial subject.</strong></p>
<p>At the end of the documentary, which follows disgraced pharmaceutical entrepreneur Elizabeth Holmes’ final days before going to prison, it is revealed that her husband, Billy Evans, negotiated — and later relinquished — the rights to payment for the family’s role in the film.</p>
<p>Last week, The Hollywood Reporter <a href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/billy-evans-profits-you-can-see-everything-ycse-charity-1236759761/">published</a> a story saying the film had omitted key details that made Evans look slightly better — principally, that he “did retain rights to the payout on <em>You Can See Everything</em> after all.”</p>
<p>“The payout still went out; it was just redirected to a charity, in a move that puts Evans in a slightly warmer light,” the outlet reported.</p>
<p>But during a Q&amp;A Saturday night at the Darryl F. Zanuck Theater on the Fox studio lot, Lance Oppenheim and Nathan Fielder said that the THR story’s framing and wording was inaccurate, and that none of the documentary’s subjects were paid, according to one person in attendance.</p>
<p>An A24 spokesperson declined to comment. But a studio source said that after Evans negotiated a profit participation deal, he ultimately relinquished his right to it. He and A24 mutually agreed to donate the proceeds to charity; as a part of the deal, the source said, Evans gave up his right to participate in the film’s profits.</p>
<p>Oppenheim told the audience he was surprised by the story because The Hollywood Reporter is a “great publication.”</p>
<p>“It is?” Fielder replied.</p>
<p>In an email, THR’s editor-in-chief, Maer Roshan, said that the organization stood by its reporting, and that A24’s view “seems like a pretty semantic argument.” Further, he noted that THR’s Julian Sancton raised the story with Oppenheim during THR’s yet-to-be-released director’s roundtable yesterday featuring the <em>You Can See Everything </em>director, plus Alex Gibney, Ron Howard, and Ava Duvernay, among others.</p>
