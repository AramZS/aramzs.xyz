 TO THE PARTICIPANTS IN THE MEETING ORGANIZED<br/>

<p><i><span>Clementine Hall<br/>
 Monday, 23 November 2026</span></i></p>
<p>[<b><a href="https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/events/event.dir.html/content/vaticanevents/en/2026/11/23/uci.html">Multimedia</a></b>]</p>

<div><p>In the name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.<br/> Peace be with you.<br/> Good morning and welcome, all of you!</p>
<p><i>Your Eminence,<br/> Your Excellencies,<br/> Representatives of international cycling,<br/> Dear athletes,</i></p>
<p>I am pleased to welcome you this morning and to share in your enthusiasm for the sport you love and for the achievements you have attained.  Cycling is indeed one of the most popular and beloved sports in the world.  Moreover, the bicycle is part of the history and identity of many cultures. On the one hand, it is a practical means of getting around with ease and embracing a healthy lifestyle; on the other, it can also offer opportunities for meaningful spiritual experiences.</p>
<p>I greet Cardinal José Tolentino de Mendonça, Prefect of the <a href="https://www.dce.va/en.html">Dicastery for Culture and Education</a>, and Mr David Lappartient, President of the <i>Union Cycliste Internationale</i>. Together with them, I extend my greetings to all of you and thank you for this visit. Your presence brings to mind the cyclists taking part in the Giro d’Italia, whom I welcomed and blessed in the very first days of my pontificate.</p>
<p>Like me, many people retain vivid memories of images from your races.  Witnessing such sporting achievements, so deeply rooted in our culture and social fabric, goes beyond the competitive aspect of sport.  The victories and the defeats — the joys and the disappointments you have experienced — reflect the values and challenges that make up human existence.  Cycling can thus be understood as a metaphor for that “life in abundance” which is characteristic of every authentic sporting experience (cf. <i><a href="https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/letters/2026/documents/20260206-lettera-giochi-olimpici.html">Letter on the Value of Sport</a></i>, 6 February 2026).</p>
<p>Your passion may also reveal a spiritual dimension that does not separate itself from physical exertion but rather ennobles it.  Cycling offers opportunities to seek out places of silence and contemplation, where we can cultivate an attentive appreciation of creation, away from the noise and traffic of the city.  In a society that risks reducing our relationship with the environment to the purely functional realms of production and consumption, the humble way of traveling by bicycle can offer a valuable means of nurturing our interior life.</p>
<p>In this regard, Madeleine Delbrêl associated spirituality with the bicycle itself.  In our journey of faith, she observed that “a little like a bicycle that will only stay upright when it is on the move,” we can bear fruit only “if we keep moving, launching ourselves forward in an élan of Love” (Madeleine Delbrêl, <i>The Joy of Believing</i>, 89).</p>
<p>For example, you are well acquainted with the classic cycling routes and the prestigious mountain-top finishes. At the summit, one often finds a shrine or a votive chapel. In this way, your commitment to the sport embraces the faith of those who built these sacred places. It was for this very reason that my predecessor, <a href="https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/holy-father/pio-xii.html">Pope Pius XII</a>, proclaimed Our Lady of Ghisallo the patroness of all cyclists on 13 October 1949. Over the years, the votive offerings (<i>ex voto</i>) of champions and amateur cyclists have transformed that little church, situated on a beautiful hill, into a spiritual landmark for all who love the world of cycling.</p>
<p>Devotion to Our Lady of Ghisallo has brought together several pilgrimages of cyclists here to the Vatican, bearing witness to the fact that the bicycle can even become an instrument of evangelization. In this regard, it is significant that the bicycle belonging to Blessed Father Giovanni Fornasini, a martyr, is preserved in Bologna.  He used it to travel from village to village, personally encouraging the people and bringing them the sacraments.  It was precisely because of this apostolic zeal that he was killed in 1944, at the age of twenty-nine.</p>
<p>Mindful of this history, I encourage all of you — leaders, race organizers and experts in the world of cycling — to ensure that this sport continues to foster experiences of authentic humanity.</p>
<p>Lastly, let us call to mind those cyclists who have lost their lives in road accidents. Among them are professional athletes and amateur cyclists, women and men who chose the bicycle as their means of travelling to work or to their places of study. Let us remember them in our prayers.  Upon all of you and your families, I gladly impart my Apostolic Blessing.Thank you!</p>
