<p>The Federal Communications Commission (<a href="https://thedesk.net/tag/fcc/">FCC</a>) will vote next month to eliminate certain rules that limit the number of commercial AM and FM radio station licenses a company or person may directly own at any given time.</p>
<p>The vote, scheduled for the FCC’s December 17 Open Meeting, will replace fixed local ownership limits with a system allowing regulators to approve transactions exceeding existing caps on a case-by-case basis.</p>
<p>The vote is expected to pass along party lines, with FCC Chairman Brendan Carr supporting the measure and two other Republican commissioners to follow suit. The move comes about three months after <a href="https://thedesk.net/2026/10/fcc-formalizes-elimination-of-broadcast-ownership-cap/">Carr pushed through a vote that eliminates broadcast TV ownership limitations</a>, an act that is expected to invite legal challenges in the coming weeks.</p>

<p>Congress requires the FCC to consider the effects of its broadcast TV and radio ownership rules on a four-year basis, a deadline that the agency has consistently missed. The current review and subsequent vote scheduled for December is rooted in the agency’s 2022 review.</p>
<p>Under existing radio ownership rules, broadcasters in markets with at least 45 radio stations may own up to eight, including no more than five on either band. Smaller markets face tighter restrictions.</p>
<p>Current ownership limits would remain as baselines, with broadcasters required to demonstrate that additional consolidation would serve the public interest. But the vote will eliminate separate limits on AM and FM radio station ownership, allowing companies to determine the mix of stations they operate within a market.</p>
<p>“For too long, the FCC’s broadcast ownership rules have been stuck in the past,” Carr said in a statement on Monday, which was published in a blog post on the agency’s website.</p>
<p>Carr pointed to recent FCC waivers permitting station acquisitions beyond existing limits, including transactions involving Connoisseur Media and Draper Media.</p>

<p>The National Association of Broadcasters (NAB), the commercial TV and radio industry’s main lobbying group in Washington, welcomed the proposed changes.</p>
<p>“Updating these outdated restrictions is critical to ensuring local radio and television stations can better compete with streaming and tech giants in today’s media marketplace and continue investing in the local content their communities depend on,” NAB President and CEO Curtis LeGeyt said in a statement.</p>
<p>Other industry groups have opposed relaxing ownership restrictions: The National Association of Black Owned Broadcasters has warned that eliminating AM and FM subcaps could accelerate consolidation and reduce minority ownership.</p>
<p>One limitation that won’t change: The FCC is expected to maintain existing rules that prevent the owners of the major four broadcast TV network — ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC — from combining.</p>
