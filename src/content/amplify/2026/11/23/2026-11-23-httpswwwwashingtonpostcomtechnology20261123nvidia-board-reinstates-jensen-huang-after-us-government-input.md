<html><body><figure><img alt="" src="https://cloudfront-us-east-1.images.arcpublishing.com/wapo/ME4XBQJQD5GMDJQ7Q6ITNLY62U.JPG"/><figcaption>President Donald Trump with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, on Oct. 8. (Evan Vucci/Reuters)</figcaption></figure><p>SAN FRANCISCO — Computer chip giant Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as its chief executive, 11 days after Huang resigned after admitting to an affair with a woman believed to be linked to Chinese intelligence services.</p><p>The stunning turnaround comes after Nvidia’s board “received input from senior levels of the United States government,” the company said in a statement Monday. “Based on that input, the board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”</p><p>President Donald Trump consistently voiced his support for Huang, and some tech leaders had advocated for his return over the past week and a half. They argued that the CEO is a key reason for Nvidia’s success and without him the company could falter in its critical role providing funding and computer chips for other artificial intelligence firms.</p><p>Trump posted his support of the reinstatement minutes after Nvidia’s announcement.</p><p>“The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct,” the president said in a post on Truth Social. “On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure.”</p><p>Huang’s reinstatement <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/16/fight-between-bessent-lutnick-over-nvidia-chips-heats-up/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/16/fight-between-bessent-lutnick-over-nvidia-chips-heats-up/">emphasizes</a> the White House’s influence over decision-making at the United States’ top tech companies, even after Democrats won control of Congress in the midterm elections, forcing tech leaders to recalibrate their <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/silicon-valley-courted-trump-now-its-bracing-democratic-congress/" rel="" target="_self" title="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/silicon-valley-courted-trump-now-its-bracing-democratic-congress/">previously close associations</a> with the president.</p><p>Congressional Democrats have pledged to investigate Nvidia and its work selling chips in Asia. Nvidia had made billions of dollars selling computer chips to companies that went on to rent out access to Chinese AI firms, creating a loophole that has helped boost the Chinese AI industry’s ability to keep up with U.S. technology advances. </p><p>“The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, Nvidia executives, and the company’s board still owe Congress answers about the company’s compliance with U.S. export controls,” Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Massachusetts) said in a statement Monday. “The Trump Administration also needs to explain to Congress what ‘input’ was given.” </p><p>Huang had also drawn support from a cohort of tech leaders including Elon Musk. An online petition on the site <a href="http://standwithjensen.com" rel="">standwithjensen.com</a> drew more than 200,000 signatures, including what it said were more than 100,000 Nvidia shareholders and 10,000 current employees, roughly a quarter of the company’s workforce. </p><p>“The mistake has been corrected,” Musk said Monday on X.</p><p>Trump last week said he would not mind if Huang returned, adding that the executive had learned a “big lesson.” </p><p>Huang’s reinstatement sent Nvidia’s stock price skyrocketing more than 30 percent in early trading Monday, helping to pull up the entire S&amp;P 500, which rose more than 4 percent. Tech stocks fell last week as the Nvidia saga contributed to concerns that the AI industry had overextended itself with debt. </p><p>Michael Monaghan, who oversees the Founders 100 ETF, an investment fund, said in an interview Friday that he believed the tanking stock prices were disconnected from reality. Asked what he thought might turn around the sour investor mood that had battered the stock market, Monaghan pointed to two possibilities: the end of the Iran war and a reinstatement of Huang.</p><p>Despite the reinstatement, questions about Nvidia’s business in Asia, whether Huang knew about the allegations against the executive he had a relationship with, and whether the company improperly tried to influence U.S. export policies still surround the firm and its CEO. </p><p>In his Truth Social post Monday, Trump said he still wanted Nvidia to look into export control concerns.</p><p>“NVIDIA should continue its thorough internal investigation of the Company’s compliance with U.S. export controls in order to ensure that it is fulfilling its legal obligations and Patriotic Duty to protect U.S. National Security,” he said. “Congratulations to Jensen, the NVIDIA Board, to the People of the United States — and, indeed, the World! Thank you for your attention to this matter.”</p></body></html>
