href="https://unsplash.com">Unsplash</a></figcaption></figure></div><p><span>Washington’s Initiative 638, the anti-transgender sports ban that </span><a href="https://www2.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/finaltext_3276.pdf">requires girls’ “biological sex” to be verified</a><span> with genital exams and/or cheek swabs, appears to be blowing up in its supporters’ faces. The law takes effect December 3rd, and any girl who doesn’t have the correct form signed by then may be blocked from participating. Meanwhile, doctors’ offices are </span><a href="https://kxly920.com/2026/11/20/spokane-parents-and-doctors-scramble-to-meet-wiaa-gender-verification-deadline/">reporting mass confusion</a><span> and are being flooded with requests to sign the forms from parents who suddenly realize they may need to take their daughters into the exam room just to keep playing. All of this is happening with the winter sports season already underway, threatening to sideline girls across the state.</span></p><p><span>“We’ve been talking to parents this morning about the attestation form that needs to be signed to prove that girls are biological girls in the wake of I-638. The consensus: chaos. Doctors offices are scrambling to determine procedures and families aren’t sure what they need,” KXLY’s Melissa Luck </span><a href="https://x.com/MelissaKXLY4/status/2123852497261584757">posted on Twitter</a><span> Friday before continuing, “Keep in mind, the winter sports season started Monday and players need to have a certain number of practices to play in games.”</span></p><p>The form itself, released by the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association last week, is just one page. It requires the provider to check “male” or “female” based on their “professional medical assessment and our records,” and to certify that they have “reviewed the relevant medical information and/or findings necessary to make this determination.” Doctors will put their licenses on the line in making these determinations, and if they follow the law itself, they may need to perform a genital exam, draw blood for a testosterone test or run a genetic test such as a cheek swab. If so, many athletes who have never had such an exam would face one on short notice or be ruled ineligible, which could wreck girls’ sports in Washington.</p><div 1456w"/></picture></div></a><figcaption class="image-caption">WIAA Form</figcaption></figure></div><p>Dr. Jennifer Kalisvaart, a pediatrician based in Spokane, told KXLY that her office had received more than 20 of the forms since Thursday. “I didn’t realize what a quick turnaround they would need for the forms to be completed,” she said. “There was very little guidance on what it would actually entail on our behalf. A lot of questions unknown.”</p><p>Meanwhile, even parents who back the law are now finding themselves scrambling. One parent, Chelsea McKell, told KXLY that getting her daughters’ forms signed has already been a nightmare. “Moms are busy. We have a lot to do, and that form is one more thing on our to-do list,” she said, though she later reiterated that she still supported the law.</p><p><span>None of this should surprise the law’s supporters: opponents of the measure warned this would happen. Seven medical groups, led by the Washington State Medical Association, </span><a href="https://wsma.org/latest-news/washington-medical-organizations-support-pause-on-implementation-of-i-638/">said Nov. 17</a><span> that the initiative’s terms lack “clinical specificity” and put physicians in an “untenable position.” Genital exams, they noted, “are not part of today’s routine sports physicals and are not medically necessary.” “This is so clearly written by somebody who does not understand what I’m doing in a sports physical,” Dr. Glenna Martin, a family physician at a Seattle community health center, told the Seattle Times in October. Of the tests, she said, “At the end of the day, I wouldn’t do it because I think it is completely unethical.”</span></p><p><span>The initiative is currently being challenged in court. In a </span><a href="https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/first-lawsuit-filed-against-washington">lawsuit filed Nov. 9</a><span> by the ACLU of Washington, Legal Voice and Pacifica Law Group, the severe disruption over doctors’ visits is one of many cited reasons for a request to block the law, which they argue is discriminatory against both trans girls and cisgender girls and violates their rights to privacy. That request is set to be heard Dec. 3, the day the law takes effect, meaning that the disruption is likely to continue all the way up until the moment we find out whether the law remains in effect. Meanwhile, doctors, many of whom are taking time off for the holidays, are unlikely to be able to meet the strict requirements of the law in time for girls to continue participating in the sports they love.</span></p><p></p></div></div>
