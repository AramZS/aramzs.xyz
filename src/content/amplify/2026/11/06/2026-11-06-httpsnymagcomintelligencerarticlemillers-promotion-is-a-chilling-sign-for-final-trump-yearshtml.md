<p>Talk about a weave. <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/is-trump-trying-to-save-the-oval-office-from-himself.html">Donald Trump</a>’s reactions to his party’s midterm-election disaster have been all over the place. He signaled right away that he <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/politics/trump-midterm-election-results.html">accepted the terrible results</a> (a <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/midterms-2026-how-trump-will-claim-victory-on-election-night.html">first for this sore loser</a>) and even mumbled about <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/politics/trump-midterm-election-results.html">working with the Democrats</a> who will now control Congress in January. Yet he <a href="https://newrepublic.com/post/217544/donald-trump-blame-everyone-else-democratic-blowout">deflected blame</a> for the debacle everywhere other than to the man he sees in the gilded mirrors of the White House every day. Would he drift toward retirement quietly and somewhat passively, much like <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/trump-moves-obama-bush-portraits-revenge-redecorating.html">George W. Bush</a>, who was similarly rebuked by the electorate in a second midterm election? Or resort even more to wild theories of unlimited executive power to get his way without any significant involvement from Congress?</p>
<p>We didn’t have to wait long for the answer. Trump has now taken the step that best explains his take on the midterms and where he intends to lead his party and the country, <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117571000109141399">announcing</a> on Truth Social that Stephen Miller is his new White House chief of staff:</p>
<blockquote data-editable="text" data-uri="nymag.com/intelligencer/_components/blockquote/instances/cmw5dls56000x3b7ic91htxlx@published" data-word-count="76"><p>I am pleased to announce that Mr. Stephen Miller, the current Deputy Chief of Staff of the Trump Administration, and one of my most trusted advisors, will become the Chief of Staff starting Monday, November 9, 2026. Susie Wiles, who has done an outstanding job, will be moving over to the Trump Political Operation. I want to thank her for her service as Chief of Staff — She has done a great job! President DONALD J. TRUMP</p></blockquote>
<p>Blaming Susie Wiles for the midterms <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/the-13-biggest-losers-of-the-2026-midterms-ranked.html">isn’t totally surprising</a> since she was part and parcel of the 2024 Trump campaign team that moved into the White House and oversaw the midterm effort, to the limited extent that they were able to do anything other than following the boss’s orders. But putting Miller into her place is a pretty clear signal of defiance to Democrats, to Republicans who wanted a change of direction, and to the electorate.</p>
<p>For one thing, Miller has overseen policy for the White House with an extremely broad portfolio that extended beyond his signature obsession with immigration. As <em>The</em> <em>Atlantic</em> <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/2026/01/stephen-miller-trump-white-house/685516/">explained</a> earlier this year, he has placed his distinctive stamp of rigid ideological fidelity across the full range of controversial issues:</p>
<blockquote data-editable="text" data-uri="nymag.com/intelligencer/_components/blockquote/instances/cmw5e0p6x001b3b7i1ccyqtv0@published" data-word-count="95"><p>Steve Bannon, a former Trump adviser and a Miller ally, describes him as Trump’s “prime minister.” Miller has a role in nearly every area about which he cares deeply: immigration and border security, yes, but also national security, foreign policy, trade, military action, and policing. He may draft a flurry of executive orders one day, lead a meeting on lowering domestic beef prices the next, and travel to deliver a fiery speech of his own — think Trump at his angriest and most dystopian, without any of the president’s impish humor — the following week.</p></blockquote>
<p>Yes, dating back to the reputation he earned as a nativist crank while working for <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2020/10/doj-draft-shows-grievous-details-of-migrant-child-separation.html">Jeff Sessions</a> in the pre-Trump Senate, Miller is especially devoted to the task of mass deportation of undocumented immigrants and, beyond that, to eliminating to the maximum extent possible avenues for legal immigration, too. He has famously <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2025/06/09/stephen-millers-order-likely-sparked-immigration-arrests-and-protests/">lashed subordinates</a> in all the federal agencies involved in immigration policy to meet tight quotas for deportations, and he clearly has no problems with the <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/video/opinion/100000010650076/what-stephen-miller-is-signaling-to-ice-officers.html">enforcement excesses</a> that have made ICE widely regarded as an American gestapo. That Trump is promoting rather than firing Miller shows he doesn’t understand or doesn’t care about the loss of support among Latinos that more than any other individual factor fed the midterm debacle. Miller is deeply responsible for losing 2024 Trump voters who supported border control and deportation of violent criminals but never bargained for the terror ICE has inflicted on immigrant communities or the unapologetic racism that underlies Miller’s approach to immigration.</p>
<p>And that may be the scariest thing of all about his new base of power: He has two more years to act on his deep belief in the <a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/trump-xenophobia-reaches-its-apex-in-racist-campaign-post.html">Great Replacement Theory</a>. This is the idea that traitorous liberals are deliberately destroying the country through a conspiracy to drown native white Americans in a sea of shiftless immigrants who trade illegal votes for welfare benefits and the opportunity to commit crimes. Trump talks about this nightmare scenario all the time as part of his demagogic pitch to the MAGA base. But for Miller, it’s an article of faith.</p>
<p>Some Trump defenders will try to rationalize Miller’s elevation as insignificant, a semi-automatic move up the ladder for the Trump loyalist who was technically Wiles’s deputy. It’s true that he may bring more discipline and fewer leaks to the White House staff. But it will likely be the discipline of sheer fanaticism and intolerance for dissent.</p>
<p>Trump’s big decision in the wake of the midterms is whether to learn something from his party’s setback and seek to wind down his final term in office in a dignified manner or instead try to implement the worst and most divisive elements of his agenda come hell or high water. There’s little question Miller would take the administration down this second path, and his promotion will give him that chance.</p>
