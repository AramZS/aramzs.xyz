<p>On November 3 CDT filed an amicus brief in <i>United States v Spurlock</i>, calling on the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals to rule a tower dump warrant as an unconstitutional general warrant. CDT was joined in the brief by Defending Rights &amp; Dissent, the Electronic Privacy Information Center, and the Surveillance Technology Oversight Project.</p>
<p>Tower dumps are a surveillance technique that collects the location data for all cell phones that ping off a cell tower (or sets of multiple towers). CDT’s brief provides technical insights into how tower dump surveillance functions and the data collected. It also highlights the importance of Fourth Amendment protections for location data, especially following the Supreme Court’s ruling in <i>Chatrie</i> <a data-saferedirecturl="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://cdt.org/insights/the-supreme-court-just-protected-the-trail-your-phone-leaves-behind/&amp;source=gmail&amp;ust=1793825954517000&amp;usg=AOvVaw2_WWJ78H6sk-WvbUJFLMTX" href="https://cdt.org/insights/the-supreme-court-just-protected-the-trail-your-phone-leaves-behind/">earlier this year</a>.</p>
<p>As our brief notes, the tower dump here was needlessly broad, pulling in over 1000 individuals over a span of hours, and collecting Fourth Amendment-protected data without particularity, narrowing mechanisms, or probable cause of wrongdoing for any of the individuals’ whose private location information was seized. Given its overbroad nature, CDT hopes the court will rule this tower dump constituted precisely the type of general warrant the Founders sought to prohibit</p>
