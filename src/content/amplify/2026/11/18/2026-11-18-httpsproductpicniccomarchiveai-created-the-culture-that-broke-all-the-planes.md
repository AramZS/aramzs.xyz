<h2>It doesn't take a genius manager to demand "efficiency" and "velocity." But these demands end up increasing waste and decreasing productivity.</h2>
<p>This past Thursday, the world was rocked by a Microsoft outage so massive that it has <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Microsoft_outage?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">its own Wikipedia article</a>. Outlook was down, Teams was down, Office was down, Bing was down; the only thing that wasn’t down somehow was <a href="https://lnkd.in/p/etWahp7N?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">LinkedIn</a>.</p>
<p>The outage continued through Friday into Saturday, and would have been the thing everyone was talking about on the weekend<sup>1</sup> if not an even bigger outage hitting Friday evening: the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FAA_nationwide_ground_stop?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">FAA ground stop</a> of all US airspace flights. While the most recent assumption is that it was caused by <a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/airlines/the-single-router-in-southern-arkansas-that-ground-air-travel-to-a-halt-eedc326c?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">one faulty router</a>, the first culprit people were pointing at within minutes of the news (broken by, and I kid you not, musician and long-time air safety advocate <strong>Flavor Flav</strong> <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/flavorflav.bsky.social/post/3n27ufkokmk2p?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">on Bluesky</a>) was the FAA’s newest <a href="https://arstechnica.com/features/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">AI traffic control system</a>.</p>
<p>Two key points before we move on:</p>
<ul><li><p>The air traffic control tool (called SMART, which provoked no shortage of mockery) doesn’t seem to have been responsible for this particular breakdown</p></li><li><p>The type of AI it uses is not an LLM, but old-school machine learning (which is a distinction AI labs have done their best to blur, given that all the “AI cures cancer” headlines are about traditional techniques, while “AI deleted my codebase” headlines are about LLMs)</p></li></ul>
<p>So it is understandable that the internet’s first assumption was “LLMs broke all the planes.”</p>
<p>We now know that LLMs were not the <em>immediate </em>cause of this outage, much like they were not the immediate cause of many other recent outages. But that does not mean the tech (and the ethos driving its development, from which it is inseparable) can be <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/baldurbjarnason.com/post/3n2bb55i45g5z?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">absolved of responsibility</a>:</p>
<blockquote><p>Over the past couple of years, every time there has been a software system failure or disruption everybody involved has insisted "AI" and layoffs weren't the direct cause and that people should stop spreading fear about a new technology.<br/>…<br/>Trace it back enough and the penultimate cause will often indeed have been "AI" or layoffs. And the ultimate cause is always going to be bad management that prioritised stock price and bonuses over functioning companies.</p><p>— <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/baldurbjarnason.com/post/3n2bb55i45g5z?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">Baldur Bjarnason</a></p></blockquote>
<h1>Being wrong faster</h1>
<p>Long-time readers of the Picnic know that I don’t like to argue about the efficacy of language models. Partly because I’m not an ML engineer, and partly because talking about only the underlying technology, divorced from its implementation, is missing the point. When you stop arguing about whether or not it works, you can move on to the real question: works to <em>do what?</em></p>
<p>LLMs, like all machine learning, are a pattern-matching technology. What they do is, in simple terms, multiply anything you already have a lot of. If it’s trained on gigabytes of uninspired business emails, it can write acceptable emails. If it’s trained on an internet’s worth of buggy Javascript, it can generate replacement-level code. You can plug a chatbot into any business process and demand that, whatever employees were doing, they should now be doing that same thing faster.</p>
<p>Unfortunately, the thing organizations were trying to do was <a href="https://lnkd.in/p/erpEWq_T?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">building Feature Factories</a>, and juicing their stock prices with waves and waves of layoffs. And now they have put LLMs towards this exact purpose.</p>
<p>So it doesn’t really matter whether or not LLMs “work”, because the task they are being applied to is scaling up a social system <a href="https://thepit.social/@peter/117613573392623125?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">that is already broken</a>:</p>
<blockquote><p>When I say something was possibly "caused by AI", I don't mean LLMs hallucinating or agents going rogue or whatever. I mean that it was caused by bad practices implemented thanks to AI psychosis in management. This can include laying off knowledgeable staff, pushing to prod without a thorough review, "build build build" etc.</p><p>These are <strong>human</strong> problems.</p><p><a href="https://thepit.social/@peter/117613573392623125?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">Peter JK</a></p></blockquote>
<p>There is no better case-in-point than Proton’s CEO Andy Yen using “velocity” as the reason to advocate skipping pentesting for his product, causing droves of loyal users to <a href="https://cybernews.com/ai-news/proton-cancel-subscriptions/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">cancel their subscriptions</a>. The thing they turned to Proton for (secure email and small-tech ethos) was the diametric opposite of what “building faster with AI” could achieve.</p>
<p>Proton’s tragic tale shows that there’s more to the question “does AI work?” than how much code it lets you write, because creating customer outcomes involves far more than just writing code.</p>
<p>Conveniently, the work of enumerating that “more” was already done by the British GDS, with the Service Standard. Giuseppe Sollazzo takes us through <a href="https://puntofisso.net/blog/posts/ai-gds-service-standard/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">AI’s impact on all 14 steps</a> of that standard, and it does not fare well:</p>
<blockquote><p>The Service Standard was written to stop teams building the wrong thing well. While AI makes building cheaper, what it does speed up is writing code. But in complex digital services, coding is just part of the process, which is limited by users, organisations, governance and time.</p><p>Speeding up the build step without widening the real bottleneck doesn’t shorten the loop, it just moves the queue.</p><p>— Giuseppe Sollazzo, <a href="https://puntofisso.net/blog/posts/ai-gds-service-standard/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">Does AI speed up development? Using the GDS Service Standard as a testing ground</a></p></blockquote>
<h1 data-pm-slice="1 1 []">Efficiency doesn’t make us more productive. Resilience does.</h1>
<p>The cable that the FAA was upgrading wasn’t AI. The Microsoft router that went haywire wasn’t AI. But both of them were single points of failure in a system that was pared down to the leanest, least resilient version of itself. The kind of system that exists in every “mach 5 productivity” corporate culture obsessing over velocity instead of outcomes.</p>
<p>LLMs didn’t force companies to create AI mandates — but AI mandates were inevitable when this kind of culture encountered LLMs. Tokens burned became a proxy for productivity, at least until <a href="https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/enterprise-ai-budgeted-management-1806370?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">CFOs saw the bill</a> and immediately backpedaled. It turned out that even in this kind of culture, the balance sheet was still an option for making "the right people <a href="https://ashley.rolfmore.com/sometimes-you-just-gotta-make-some-noise/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">experience enough of reality</a>” to re-examine their choices. Notably, despite the extremely high profile failure of tokenmaxxing, the Proton CEO (as well as other business leaders) <em>still </em>want to experience it on their own balance sheets.</p>
<p>If those orgs were willing to move more slowly and deliberately, they could have realized that tokenmaxxing was stupid at a much lower cost to themselves. But the focus on velocity and simply doing <em>more things</em> meant that <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/spavel.bsky.social/post/3n2ah77rin22t?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">leaders were distracted</a> from thinking about what they were actually trying to accomplish.</p>
<p>Within the cult of velocity, only senior leaders have permission to hit the brakes — but senior leaders are <em>always </em>the last ones to discover problems, especially in this kind of environment. The more employees feel threatened (such as by AI mandates, or waves of layoffs, or similar rhetoric) the less likely they are to voice their doubts.</p>
<p>So the foot stays on the gas, until the system is shocked by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/scottphilipsmith_gotta-drive-the-car-off-the-cliff-to-find-share-7527815768126697474-AsP3/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">a signal it can’t ignore</a>:</p>
<blockquote><p>Gotta drive the car off the cliff to find out where the cliff is. In this case it’s bland overwrought vibe codes.</p><p>Value comes from slowing down, thinking, and trying things out deliberately. Or in writing, it comes from the way you speak. Both cases require our thinking brain to be coaxed out and given space to roam.</p><p><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/scottphilipsmith_gotta-drive-the-car-off-the-cliff-to-find-share-7527815768126697474-AsP3/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">Scott Smith</a></p></blockquote>
<p>Only this kind of deliberate slow-down can allow people close to the work to take a breather executing feature requests long enough to focus on the <a href="https://lalitm.com/post/find-problems-staff-engineer/?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">problems that lie underneath</a>. Refusing the efficiency of assembly line thinking and embracing the <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/shengokai.blacksky.app/post/3mydyujgxdp2b?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">inherent back-and-forth</a> that creativity requires is a prerequisite for any organization capable of actual innovation.</p>
<p>Once upon a time, this was Product’s job: prioritizing the work being done, making sure people were focused on what was important to the business. But PMs got tricked by AI lab hype men, and are now stuck vibing prototypes. Weirdly, nudging teams away from breaking their own business model has apparently <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/erikahall.bsky.social/post/3n27wyvl43c2g?utm_source=productpicnic&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=ai-created-the-culture-that-broke-all-the-planes">become Design’s job</a>.</p>
