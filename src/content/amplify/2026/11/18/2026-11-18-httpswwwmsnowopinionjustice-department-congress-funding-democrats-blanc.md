<p>It is a month and a half before Democrats take control of both houses of Congress, but party leaders are already talking about <a href="https://www.ms.now/news/lawyer-up-new-democratic-majority-trump-investigations">investigating Trump administration officials</a>, including those at the <a href="https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-blanche-democrats-oversight-investigations-justice-department">Department of Justice</a>. Even though Congress may use its investigatory powers to <em>impeach</em> high-ranking government officials, however, the Department of Justice has the exclusive authority to bring federal criminal charges against government personnel (or anyone else). Of course, that will never happen during this administration.</p>
<p>There is a better way to quickly deal with the horrific corruption and malfeasance currently plaguing one of the federal government’s most important agencies. The Constitution gives Congress authority over how the government collects and spends money. As Article 1 states, “all bills for raising revenue shall originate in the House of Representatives. … No Money shall be drawn from the Treasury, but in Consequence of Appropriations made by Law.” Democrats should aggressively use this power to force more moderate and less partisan leadership at the Justice Department.</p>
<figure><blockquote><p>There is no appreciation for the rule of law at the current Justice Department, and the country is much worse off because of it. </p></blockquote></figure>
<p>Before we get to that strategy, though, we must understand how infected the Department of Justice has become with President Donald Trump’s political priorities dominating everything its officials do and, equally important, do not do.</p>
<p>As an attorney in the Justice Department during George H.W. Bush’s administration, I participated in several important cases, including the Iran-Contra investigation and controversial free-speech and freedom-of-religion lawsuits. Several times during my tenure, I dissuaded political appointees at the department from taking certain actions or filing cases that were either too legally flimsy or simply immoral.</p>
<figure><img alt="" sizes="(max-width: 860px) 100vw, 860px" src="https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg" srcset="https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg 1920w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=300,169 300w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=768,432 768w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=900,506 900w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=1536,864 1536w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=1000,563 1000w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=860,484 860w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=760,428 760w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=560,315 560w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/stohr.jpg?resize=50,28 50w"/></figure>
<p>That kind of decision would never happen today. There is no appreciation for the rule of law <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/politics/trumps-justice-department-faces-a-new-era-of-scrutiny-under-a-democratic-led-congress">at the current Justice Department</a>, and the country is much worse off because of it. Attorney General Todd Blanche <a href="https://newrepublic.com/post/216921/todd-blanche-department-justice-supposed-run-president-whims">has made it clear</a> that he views the Justice Department’s central mission to be supporting the president’s political agenda, nothing more and nothing less. His predecessor, Pam Biondi, <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/how-the-trump-administration-erased-centuries-of-justice-department-experience">acted that way as well</a>.</p>
<p>Although the Justice Department has always done the president’s bidding to a great extent, in previous administrations there was also a concern for the rule of law, justice and the perspective that the attorney general represents the people of the United States of America as well as the president. The only decisions the department makes now are those that further Trump’s partisan agenda.</p>
<figure><blockquote><p>Given the election results and the widespread malfeasance at the DOJ, the American people would almost certainly side with the Democrats.</p></blockquote></figure>
<p>The DOJ’s exclusive partisan focus partly explains why <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/31/us/politics/trump-administration-exodus-of-lawyers.html">so many of its career lawyers and staff</a> have fled for less odious pastures. As a result, the department is understaffed and without the institutional memory so necessary for the agency to function. The pipeline of incoming talent has dried up as well. William Treanor, who just stepped down as dean of Georgetown Law School, <a href="https://www.americanbar.org/advocacy/governmental_legislative_work/publications/washingtonletter/november-25-wl/outside-the-gao-1125wl/">recently lamented</a> the drop in students interested in working for the DOJ: “What we are seeing is a total drop in who is applying … It’s very, very dramatic. It’s gone from a good amount of our graduating class to virtually no one applying for jobs at the Justice Department.”</p>
<p>Once Democrats control Congress, the most effective way to address the DOJ’s dysfunction is to threaten the White House using the power of the purse. Given the large advantage Democrats will have in the House, they can and should refuse to fund most of what the department does absent the firing of Blanche and other top officials, such as FBI Director Kash Patel.</p>
<figure><img alt="" sizes="(max-width: 860px) 100vw, 860px" src="https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg" srcset="https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg 1920w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=300,169 300w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=768,432 768w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=900,506 900w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=1536,864 1536w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=1000,563 1000w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=860,484 860w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=760,428 760w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=560,315 560w, https://www.ms.now/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/1794401523226_n_mj_maddow_doj_book_261111_1920x1080.jpg?resize=50,28 50w"/></figure>
<p>A law specifically reducing the department’s budget would require Senate approval and would be subject to a veto. But that does not mean the House cannot refuse to appropriate funds. Would that decision shut down the government? Maybe. But to the extent that this would hinder the department’s ability to accomplish its partisan agenda, it would be a positive development. And given the election results and the widespread malfeasance at the DOJ, the American people would almost certainly side with the Democrats.</p>
<p>The founders gave the “people’s house” the initial authority to decide how the federal government was funded, and the entire Congress the authority to spend or not spend as it saw fit. This appropriations power is a crucial element of the separation of powers and the founders’ desire for checks and balances. Supreme Court Justice Joseph Story, <a href="https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/articles/article-i/clauses/756">writing in the early 19th century</a>, said that the “power to control and direct the appropriations constitutes a most useful and salutary check upon profusion and extravagance, as well as upon corrupt influence and public speculation.”</p>
<p>Congress has given away much of that authority since then, but the Constitution allows legislators to take that power back. It should do so and use it to either hamstring the Department of Justice or force leadership changes in ways that will return the department to a functioning agency committed to <em>both</em> the president’s agenda and the rule of law.</p>
