<p>A man who relocated a collection of radical art, zines, and handbooks will spend the next three decades in prison.</p>

<p>Daniel Sanchez was driving his pickup truck through a college town in North Texas on a Sunday afternoon last summer when he noticed the glow of bright blue police lights behind him. As he pulled over, he realized it wasn’t one or two squad cars; it was half a dozen. Police officers rushed to his door, guns pointed at him. “I thought that I was going to die,” he told me. “Like, honestly, when I saw the amount of cops around me and the guns, I’m like, <em>Oh, whoa, what is this?</em>”</p>
<p>Sanchez was patted down, his arms cuffed behind him as an officer guided him into the back of a police vehicle. Born in Mexico, Sanchez came to the United States as a teenager and was undocumented for many years but later obtained a green card. As he sat in the backseat, “Always on Time” by Ja Rule played on the radio. An officer opened the door and repeatedly asked whether he had any explosives in his truck. No one would tell him why he was being arrested.</p>
<p>About 36 hours earlier, a police officer had been shot and injured outside the Prairieland Detention Center 60 miles away. What had started as a July 4 demonstration by a group of protesters shooting off fireworks had become the scene of a violent attack on the officer, who was airlifted from the scene but survived. Law enforcement flooded the area, arresting anyone involved, then widened their search. Ten people were already in custody—Sanchez’s wife was among them—but the shooter was still at large. Sanchez, though, hadn’t been at the ICE detention center that night. He told me he hadn’t even been aware of the plans or that his wife was going. The couple had gotten into a fight earlier that week, and he had been staying at his parents’ house. At the time of his arrest, he was still trying to piece together what had happened, what his wife had done, and how much trouble she was in.</p>
