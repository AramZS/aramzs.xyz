<p><i><span>GTA 6 </span></i><span>is a game that at times feels almost embarrassingly expensive and extravagant. I cleaned a boat with a pressure washer and watched the suds build up realistically in the concrete below my character’s flip-flops. I ran through a tram station and spotted over 100 different NPCs milling about, all unique and all moving. Hair gets wet. Sweat builds up on backs. Even for Rockstar, the master of tiny details, </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> is overflowing with examples of what happens when you let a studio work on one game for a decade. It’s a lot, but it all serves the purpose of creating a virtual world that players can truly get lost in. </span></p> <p><span>I’ve put over 150 hours into </span><i><span>GTA 6 </span></i><span>on PlayStation 5 Pro, and I expect I’ll spend even more time in this digital recreation of Florida and Miami, as there’s so much to do and see in Vice City. I doubt most players will see half of it. I bet I won’t ever see it all. </span></p> <p><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> is a massive third-person open-world game, like Rockstar’s previous offerings. It merges the slower pace and slice-of-life mechanics from <em>Red Dead Redemption 2</em> with the urban sprawl and crime-movie feel of <i>GTA IV </i>and </span><i><span>GTA V</span></i><i><span>.</span></i><span> It’s a more grounded journey than its predecessors, one which requires more planning and consideration if you want to survive and succeed as modern-day Bonnie and Clyde, robbing small stores as you work your way up to bigger scores, like banks. It’s often a tougher game than any past </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span>, but in a way that rewards skill and patience. Or bravery, stupidity, and luck. </span></p> <p><span>I have quibbles about some clunky controls and some story pacing problems in the game’s later chapters, but like crappy cupholders in a million-dollar supercar, they don’t really get in the way of what’s here. Instead, they’re minor issues to point at as I hit the gas and go 200 MPH with the radio blasting. </span></p>
<figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757845"><img alt="Gta 6 Couple" decoding="async" height="1080" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/GTA-6-couple.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games</figcaption></figure> <h2><span>We Were Desperate Then</span></h2> <p><span>The best part of </span><i><span>Grand Theft Auto 6 </span></i><span>isn’t what I expected. It’s not the crime, or the action, or the massive world to explore, though all of those parts are excellent and better than ever. The real standout of <em>GTA 6</em>, the part of the game that kept me hooked and which I won’t forget anytime soon, is its central duo, Jason and Lucia.</span></p> <p><span>Not only do they have very different motivations than past </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> protagonists, wanting to create a better life for themselves and escape their life of crime instead of vying to become crime lords or get revenge, but they are easily Rockstar’s most fully fleshed-out, believable, and relatable characters.</span></p>
<p><span>At the start of the game, Jason and Lucia are separated after a short relationship. Lucia tried to rob a payday loan place after breaking up with Jason and, in the process, got arrested. This moment has huge ramifications for both of their lives and ultimately pulls them together early on in <em>GTA 6</em>’s runtime.</span></p> <p><span>Jason, an Army vet, is more reserved than your average </span><i><span>GTA </span></i><span>protagonist. He’s a loyal man who’s conflicted about the things he does to get by and try to make things better for himself and Lucia. A lot of people tell Jason he’s a good guy, but he’s never sure if he really is. Meanwhile, Lucia, a petty thief living paycheck to paycheck, knows she’s flawed and doesn’t care nearly as much as Jason about that fact. She’s more focused on improving her life by any means and has a bigger goal that drives her to take bigger risks. </span></p> <p><span>Together they balance each other out, with Jason often acting as a voice of reason and Lucia pushing him to strive for more. Their dynamic is unlike anything I’ve seen in a Rockstar-developed game, and thanks to some stellar writing and performances, I found myself deeply caring about these two criminals just trying to make it. </span></p>
<p><span>Throughout the entire run of </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>, Jason and Lucia interact as you would expect two real people who care for each other would. They joke with each other, comment on events happening around them, share secrets, and help each other when they can. </span></p> <p><span>Spending time with them is never annoying. Neither Lucia nor Jason pesters the player when you’re hanging around with them in the open world. Sometimes they might point out that you missed a turn or a possible way to sneak into a gang-infested hideout. This never felt like some annoying video-gamey handholding, just a couple working together. </span></p> <p><span>While Jason and Lucia are both skilled at stealing cars, shooting guns, and running around causing chaos, they aren’t always treated as equals in the world. While playing as Lucia, I was hit on by random assholes and catcalled by men on the street. It wasn’t constant, but it happened enough that it was impossible to ignore. Some characters will also doubt Lucia’s abilities, but never question Jason’s skills as a murderous criminal. It adds an intriguing wrinkle to playing as either character. Lucia feels like she has to work harder to prove herself among her criminal peers. Jason often has her back in these scenarios, but lets her hold her own, and she does so with sharp words and strong punches. </span></p>
<p><span>Even outside of when they directly speak to each other to tell stories or give feedback, the other’s presence is always felt, sometimes in small gestures, like one holding the door for the other as they enter a building. And sometimes, depending on how you handle their relationship, through more intimate moments that either Jason or Lucia, regardless of whether the player is controlling them, can initiate when they’re feeling ready to fool around. </span></p> <figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757858"><img alt="" decoding="async" height="1080" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/Grand-Theft-Auto-VI_20261109223301.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games / Kotaku</figcaption></figure> <p><span>One time while I was playing as Jason, after spending all day and most of the night with Lucia, we came home, and I couldn’t even get through the door before both of them were on top of each other. If you were wondering: Jason and Lucia fuck a lot in this game, assuming you play them as a couple, which I did. </span></p> <p><span>It didn’t take long for me to fall in love with Jason and Lucia. And I spent the entire game rooting for them, even as their situation became more dangerous and desperate, largely due to factors outside of their control. </span></p>
<p><span>While Jason and Lucia’s relationship, be it platonic or romantic depending on your choices, never loses steam or feels like it stops evolving, <em>GTA 6</em>’s main overarching narrative of criminal conspiracy and mayhem does start to falter in the back half.</span></p> <p><span>In a later chapter of the story, the momentum that had carried everything forward up until that point fades. It feels like </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>’s story begins spinning its wheels at this point. By the end, the narrative picks back up, and the stakes grow even larger as Jason and Lucia rely on themselves to take on the world. </span><span>But even when the story started to lose steam, and I felt myself wondering how many more missions I’d have to do before something big happened, Jason and Lucia were there to keep me going. Lucia doing an impersonation of Jason’s deep voice always made me chuckle, as did any time Jason gave her crap about her bad driving after I crashed another car. </span></p> <p><span>Just know there are multiple endings, and after seeing two of them in my time with the game, I can confirm that how you play and what choices you make can have a huge impact on how things turn out. Happy endings are…not common in this line of work, but not impossible. </span></p> <p><span>Even if the rest of </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> didn’t work, Jason, Lucia, and their relationship would still carry it across the finish line. Thankfully, the rest of </span><i><span>Grand Theft Auto 6</span></i><span> is very good, too. </span></p>
<figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757846"><img alt="" decoding="async" height="1080" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/GTA-6-city.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games</figcaption></figure> <h2><span>She Tried to Make My World</span></h2> <p><span>The second-best part of </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>, behind our Bonnie and Clyde duo, is the world of Leonida, the state that Vice City is located in. It is the most detailed, gorgeous, and alive open world I’ve ever experienced in a video game. </span></p> <p><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> starts off in the southern part of the map, far away from the skyscrapers and glitz of Vice City. You can almost see it in the distance from the small coastal town where </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> begins. It’s the kind of scale I’ve not really felt before in an open-world game. And all that space…it ain’t empty. </span></p>
<p><span>It feels like every inch of </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> is filled with life. With movement. With…something. Even areas like the huge swamp, which at first glance might seem empty, reveal themselves to be filled with detail and specificity. That swamp, for instance, is teeming with creatures, big and small. The wind rustles the leaves of every tree and plant around. Planes fly overhead at different altitudes. I ran deeper in and stumbled upon a giant snake with a dead body in its mouth. Later, I found a crashed plane smuggling drugs. In the distance, I spotted dark clouds forming and bursts of lightning. Rain was coming. And later, I watched a lightning bolt crash into the woods and start a small fire. </span></p> <p><span>All this happened in the span of an hour in one relatively small section of the swamp, itself a small portion of the larger world. It’s at times overwhelming. Driving through the city, which I didn’t reach until nearly a dozen hours into the game, I felt guilty about all the life and detail I was speeding by at 100 MPH. </span></p>
<p><span>I was also much more focused on the road than in past </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> games. The streets in </span><i><span>GTA 6 </span></i><span>aren’t as wide as those in </span><i><span>GTA V</span></i><span>, and there are far more cars on the road. Crashing into these other drivers can quickly damage your vehicle, making it harder to steer or accelerate. But I didn’t find any of this annoying. Instead, it made it satisfying to weave through traffic to escape police. The act of just driving a car is so much more exciting than it’s ever been in the franchise. It felt like a real skill I’d developed after a few hours and one which I always enjoyed using, making long road trips across the map feel less like a chore and more like an opportunity to explore more of the game. </span></p> <p><span>All of these changes to driving and the roads in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> also forced me to sometimes slow down or stop, giving me more time to take in all the atmosphere and detail packed into the game. </span><span>And I do recommend slowing down whenever possible and just soaking in Vice City and Leonida. </span><i><span>GTA 6 </span></i><span>is a game that truly rewards exploration and poking around. Even 150 hours in, I’m still encountering new random events, side characters, and more.</span></p> <p><span>One day in Vice City, as I was walking down the street, I entered a small convenience store to buy some food and boost my health stat. But inside, I found a criminal behind the cash register and a dead body on the floor near them. I held the left trigger to lock onto the robber, a young woman, and questioned her about what was happening. She said to mind my own business and leave. I taunted her next. This was a mistake. The young woman robbing the small store was armed with a gun and pointed it at me. I quickly drew my own pistol, and we started shooting. A second later, she was dead. I grabbed the cash—I mean, I couldn’t just leave it there on the ground—and ran out the back where I found the robber’s car. In the trunk were drugs and ammo. Before I could look around more, I heard sirens. The cops were coming. I quickly hopped a fence and ran by some people who weren’t happy that I was running through their backyard. </span></p> <figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757847"><img alt="Gta 6 Campfire" decoding="async" height="1080" loading="lazy" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/GTA-6-campfire-1.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games / Kotaku</figcaption></figure> <p><span>Just another day in Leonida. I’ve truly never experienced an open world like this before, and every time I think I’ve seen it all or can’t be more impressed by it than I already am, I’m quickly proven wrong. </span></p>
<p><span>One night, I climbed the game’s tallest mountain, and when I reached the top, it was nighttime. Vice City and its sprawl were laid out in front of me, thousands of twinkling lights and bright dots floating in the darkness miles and miles away. Lucia took it in and exclaimed to Jason that it was gorgeous. He agreed. So did I. </span></p> <p><span>But it ain’t just a big pretty world with lots of moving parts. It’s also Rockstar’s attempt to simulate as much of life in Leonida as possible. You’ll find over 600 stores to shop at, gyms to work out in, gun ranges to visit, golf courses to play on, zoos to explore, gas stations to rob, bars to chill in, animals to hunt, mod shops to improve cars at, and even working buses and trains with actual schedules and passengers. </span></p> <p><span>You can, and I did, just live out a normal day in the life of Jason and Lucia, freely swapping between them to see what the other was up to at any time. </span></p> <p><span>During one session as Jason, I hit the gym early in the morning, then hopped in my off-road car and headed north to do some hunting. When I wanted to shake things up, I swapped over to Lucia, and a few seconds later I was playing as her. She was chilling on a beach, and a friend of hers—they each have their own social lives—texted, checking in on me. I replied and then got hit on by a random asshole. So I taunted him until he called me a bitch and left. A moment later I swapped back to Jason, and he was at a nearby gas station with his gun out. I took the hint and hunted a different kind of animal, and came away $450 richer. Other times I’d switch to find them watching TV, chatting up a random NPC, or driving around the city. </span></p>
<p><span>The incredibly realistic graphics and the focus on simulating not just the fact that your partner’s life continues when you aren’t near them, but that they exist in an entire living world, one where you can gain weight, get tired, and ride the bus, do at times clash with the parts of the game where Rockstar clearly didn’t want to annoy people with the sorts of inconveniences that can emerge out of realism. </span></p> <p><span>For example, every store in the game is open 24 hours a day. This means you can walk into a barber shop at 3 am and get a haircut. To the game’s credit, the shop was empty besides the employees at that time, something I found true in other stores and locations at odd hours. But these bits of video game–ness stood out all the more because the world was otherwise so lifelike.</span></p> <p><span>All this simulation, fancy visuals, and scale does have a cost. The game runs at 30FPS, even on PS5 Pro. And in some areas of the map, like on the packed streets of Vice City, I noticed framerate drops. I also spotted slowdown during large firefights with police. It never became unplayable, but it does make me curious to see how this game will look on a powerful PC. </span></p> <figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757848"><img alt="Gta 6 Car Racing Jason" decoding="async" height="1080" loading="lazy" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/gta-6-car-racing-jason-1.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games</figcaption></figure> <h2><span>Yeah, with my head spinning ’round</span></h2> <p><span>If you’ve reached this point and are starting to wonder if this is still a </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> game, don’t worry; there’s plenty of shooting, robbing, car theft, murder, and action to be had. But this time around, crime isn’t as easy as it has been in the past, which makes it so much more rewarding and fun. </span></p>
<p><span>Police in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> are not to be underestimated. They can quickly overwhelm Lucia and Jason and will share important details about the couple with each other on the radio, making it harder to evade officers looking for you. It pays to keep a change of clothing in your trunk and store large weapons in there too, unless you want every cop in the area to come calling after people spot an armed person matching a suspected robber’s description. </span></p> <p><span>Even hitting a small gas station is thrilling because of how much smarter police are and how much more challenging </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> can be. </span></p> <p><span>You can walk into a store, pull out a gun, kill the clerk, take his money, and run. But the surveillance camera will record you, people in the area will report your location, and cops will be after you quicker due to the murder and gunfire. </span></p> <p><span>Instead, you should sneak in through the back, put on a mask, destroy any camera recordings, disable the alarm, crack open the safe, and then head out front to force the clerk to hand over the cash. Then bail, leaving him alive, and enter your car, which you smartly parked further away from the scene. Once you reach a safe spot, you can change your clothes if you have any heat, and then drive by the cops rushing to the scene without any of them suspecting a thing. </span></p>
<p><span>Pulling off perfect crimes like that, even on a small scale, is so rewarding. </span></p> <p><span>Even the simple act of stealing a car in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> is more engaging and fun than it’s ever been in past games. Some cars are easy to just pop the lock on, hop in, and hotwire. But other cars demand you use a slim jim to shimmy open the lock via a simple, quick mini-game. And still other, fancier cars require Jason and Lucia to use a key cloner to gain access, which is also pulled off via a mini-game. Those cars can’t even be hotwired, meaning for a large chunk of the game you have to avoid the coolest cars in Vice City and settle for rust buckets and unlocked sedans. And even some of those cars have trackers that make it easier for police to find you later, and harder to modify and sell them to shops around the city. </span></p> <p><span>But like the slimmer roadways and more punishing crashes, this change to the </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> formula didn’t frustrate me. On the contrary, I found it so much more exciting to steal cars in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>. I became skilled at spotting cars that would likely be easier to steal than others without needing to use the car-scanning app included on your in-game phone. (There’s also an Instagram-like social media app on there, and you can text and call other characters, including your partner.) </span></p> <p><span>And if you just can’t help yourself, well, you can always steal any car driving around the map by yanking its driver out if you </span><i><span>really </span></i><span>want that badass Banshee. Jason and Lucia can also drive boats, planes, helicopters, dirt bikes, semi-trucks, airboats, buses, bulldozers, jet skis, and—if you’re brave enough to enter the game’s military base—even a jet or tank. Good luck with that. </span></p> <div> <div> <div> <ul> <li> <p>BACK-OF-THE-BOX QUOTE</p> <p>"Welcome to Vice City. Enjoy spending your next few years exploring every inch of this place!" </p> </li> <li> <p>TYPE OF GAME</p> <p>Third-person open-world action game with two playable characters and some RPG-like systems, cover-based shooting, and lots of customization options.</p> </li> <li> <p>LIKED</p> <p>Jason and Lucia, the open world, challenging police, skillful driving, mission variety, multiple endings, and the music.</p> </li> <li> <p>DISLIKED</p> <p>Some clunky controls, performance issues in big fights, and a back half that loses some narrative oomph. </p> </li> </ul><ul> <li> <p>DEVELOPER</p> <p>Rockstar Games</p> </li> <li> <p>PLATFORMS</p> <p>PS5 / PS5 Pro (Played), Xbox Series X/S</p> </li> <li> <p>RELEASE DATE</p> <p>November 19, 2026</p> </li> <li> <p>PLAYED</p> <p>152 hours. Finished main story campaign, saw two different endings, tried out most side activities, completed most side missions, and explored nearly the entire map.</p> </li> </ul> </div> </div> </div> <p><span>Gunplay, like other parts of the </span><i><span>GTA </span></i><span>formula, is also more challenging, but better, than ever in </span><i><span>GTA 6. </span></i><span>That’s largely because Rockstar has moved away from the easy auto-aim nonsense of </span><i><span>GTA V</span></i><span>. Now, combat plays out more like it did in </span><i><span>Max Payne 3</span></i><span>, with a focus on manual aiming and landing headshots or, if you want to avoid killing people and being labeled a psycho by the in-game criminal profile system, shooting people in the kneecaps. (That profile system can change how characters react and how aggressive police will be when chasing you down.) </span></p>
<p><span>Guns also feel louder and more powerful. When a robbery goes wrong and bullets start flying, it’s scary and tense, and I always wanted to get out of there as soon as possible. You can’t take too much damage before it becomes a problem, so shootouts are risky, but not impossible, to survive. Sometimes, I’d just wing a cop or gang member in the arm, and they’d realistically fall back, giving me enough time to slip away before they got back up and started shooting again. </span></p> <p><span>Alongside all these moving parts and systems, a classic Rockstar problem is back in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>: clunky controls. </span></p> <p><span>Sometimes I’d want to loot a body, but instead Jason would try to enter a nearby car. Other times I’d want to reload my weapon, but I’d kick someone instead. Getting objects out of trunks can be a real pain in the ass as context-sensitive button prompts appear and disappear based on where your character is looking.</span></p> <p><span>I also occasionally found the controls a bit hard to keep straight. Every button on the gamepad does something; oftentimes, multiple actions are mapped to one button or a submenu. I still sometimes forget which d-pad button to hold to swap between Lucia and Jason during the heat of combat. </span></p> <p><span>Still, anytime I had issues with the story or some clunky controls, I’d turn a corner and discover a whole new section of this incredibly detailed world, quickly forgetting about those minor issues. As I said, crappy cup holders don’t make a Bugatti any less fun to drive or impressive to sit in. And like those hyper-expensive and perfectly tuned supercars, </span><i><span>GTA 6 </span></i><span>feels like it was crafted by masters who’ve been making these kinds of open-world games for a long time and are better at it than any other studio by a very wide margin. </span></p> <figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000757855"><img alt="" decoding="async" height="1080" loading="lazy" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/Grand-Theft-Auto-VI_20261026150619.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games / Kotaku</figcaption></figure> <h2><span>Some things are never enough</span><span> </span></h2> <p><span>Adding to the feeling that <em>Grand Theft Auto 6</em> is a masterclass in open-world game design are the missions. Yes, there are still the classic </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> missions that have you driving somewhere, shooting or stealing stuff, and then driving back to wrap it up. But so many missions in </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> are very different from that usual formula.</span></p> <p><span>A very early mission doesn’t even involve guns or crime. Instead, Jason and his buddy Cal just clean up the living room, move a TV, get high, drink beer, and play video games together. We learn a lot about these two and how they care for each other in that moment, and nary a gun was fired or a murder committed. </span></p> <p><span>But there are still plenty of big action-packed missions, and these, too, offer up experiences that aren’t the usual </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> fare. A later mission involving a heist that spins out of control had my heart racing as the tension ramped up, and when all hell broke loose, </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span>’s incredible world and improved combat were on full display. </span></p> <p><span>Another mission involved a massive shootout that started on the street and worked its way through a building and onto a highway. During that whole fight, I was able to find my own way up, and when the game asked me to escape, I was free to do so in any car I wanted. </span></p> <p><span>There’s a lot more flexibility in <em>GTA 6’</em>s missions, too. At one point, I was told to chase down a drug runner in a boat. The game asked me to hop on a dirt bike and follow it via a service road. Instead, I hopped into a small speedboat and the game just… rolled on, even commenting on my change of plans and changing up the objective on the fly. The person I was chasing even seemed to have different voice lines based on my choice. </span></p> <p><span><em>GTA 6</em> is constantly reacting to your choices and actions, both in missions and when just out in the open world. It makes everything you do feel more meaningful and important, and it makes <em>GTA 6</em> feel less restrictive than<em> GTA IV</em> and<em> GTA V. </em></span></p> <p><span>I also appreciated how experimental and creative </span><i><span>GTA 6</span></i><span> can be in some of its missions. During one mission, Jason gets drugged and has to fight off tons of armed thugs. But as you run around and shoot, the world and the lights in it pulse with the music. Another mission involves Jason and Lucia robbing a bunch of tiny stores, and this is presented as a playable montage that hops from moment to moment seamlessly as a classic rock song that Rockstar’s embargo forbids me from naming plays in the background. </span></p> <p><span>The licensed soundtrack is incredible, with a massive list of music across the game’s various radio stations. And this time around, you can also listen to multiple in-game podcasts, either in your car or on foot via headphones. One is a true-crime podcast hosted by two terrible people that includes an episode dedicated to the serial killer side character in </span><i><span>GTA IV. </span></i></p> <figure aria-describedby="caption-attachment-2000755940"><img alt="" decoding="async" height="1080" loading="lazy" sizes="(max-width: 1279px) calc(100vw - 2rem), 1280px" src="https://kotaku.com/app/uploads/2026/11/race-chase7.jpg" width="1920"/><figcaption>©Rockstar Games / Kotaku</figcaption></figure> <p><span>Just like in past </span><i><span>GTA</span></i><span> games, there’s a ton of content to watch on the TV, too, including multiple episodes of animated TV shows parodying American culture, politics, and police. There’s also a game show about people identifying men by looking at just their penises. Some of these shows advertise in the world or on social media, too. And characters will talk about shows and other parts of the world and post about them as the game goes on. It’s just one more thing that makes the world of Vice City feel less like a static playground and more like a real place that reacts to my actions and crimes.</span></p> <p><span>During one random event, I encountered a polar bear being transported to a zoo.
