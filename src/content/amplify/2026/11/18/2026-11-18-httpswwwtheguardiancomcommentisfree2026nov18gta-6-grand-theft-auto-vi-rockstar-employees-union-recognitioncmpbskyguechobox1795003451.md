<h2 data-gu-name="standfirst">After the release of Grand Theft Auto VI this week, Rockstar employees like me can make history again. We need union recognition to safeguard our jobs</h2>
<p>When I first got the call offering me a job at Rockstar, I was thrilled. I thought back to being a child and sneaking into my brother’s room to grab the PlayStation Portable so I could secretly play Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories. The same kid who fell in love with Rockstar’s games back then now gets to spend every day helping to create those worlds as an animation programmer at the studio in Edinburgh. This week, we will celebrate the culmination of more than a decade of effort: the release of the most anticipated game of all time, <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/games/ng-interactive/2026/nov/15/grand-theft-auto-vi-inside-rockstar-games">Grand Theft Auto VI</a>.</p>
<p>But our team has the opportunity to make history a second time this month. Right now, the ballot is open for Rockstar employees to decide whether we want the union we have built with our colleagues to get recognition. As someone who cares deeply about our team, cares about what we do, and wants us to be able to keep delivering world-class games into the future, I’m voting yes.</p>
<p>Winning union recognition would be a huge step forward in securing a meaningful say over our conditions at Rockstar, but its significance would reach far beyond that. As one of the biggest and most influential game studios in the world, what happens here could open the door for other workers across the UK to imagine an alternative future for the industry – one in which workers’ voices are taken into account more.</p>

<p>Sometimes it’s hard to see the impact of these games when you’re working so hard on them. It’s only at times like this that the staggering scale of GTA’s success hits you. GTA V <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&amp;source=web&amp;rct=j&amp;opi=89978449&amp;url=https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-grand-theft-auto-6-making-of/&amp;ved=2ahUKEwirnpOw246YAxXJQkEAHYKXEq0QFnoECCMQAQ&amp;usg=AOvVaw1k01wMYJU8AF3hTDtT48xt">has sold over 230m copies,</a> generating more than $10bn (£7.6bn) in worldwide revenue, surpassing <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.telegraph.co.uk/gaming/news/grand-theft-auto-v-has-made-money-film-history/">any film</a> in Hollywood’s history. This latest instalment is expected to break those records. Behind these numbers are thousands of people whose talent and hard work make the games possible. With a recognised union, we can ensure everyone, across every department, sees a fair share of the success we contributed to. Our games are industry-leading. Our wages, conditions and bonuses should be too.</p>
<p>While the highs of a game release like this can be incredible, game workers have also come to expect other, less welcome side effects: intense overtime in the buildup and mass redundancies in the aftermath. In previous years, Rockstar has faced <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://kotaku.com/inside-rockstar-games-culture-of-crunch-1829936466">allegations of “crunch culture”</a>, where game workers are expected to do unhealthy amounts of overtime in the run-up to a launch. With a strong union we could make sure there is no return to this system. We have already seen the introduction of some overtime pay, and now we want to keep on pushing to ensure that every hour worked in our industry is an hour paid.</p>
<p>After the big day, we have had no indication that layoffs are coming, but it’s hard not to worry about what the future holds. Studios across the industry have been slashing huge portions of their workforces, with an estimated <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://gamesbeat.com/gaming-layoffs-slowed-from-15631-in-2024-to-9053-in-2025-amir-satvat/">24,000 roles axed globally</a> over the last two years. It says a lot about the industry that even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off. But the truth is that workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organised.</p>
<p>There’s another reason this is a pivotal moment for the UK’s biggest game studio to secure union recognition. The rapid emergence of AI is transforming how studios work and raising fundamental questions about the future of our industry. Across the sector, the skills that workers spend years developing risk being devalued as their creative work is replaced by AI. Rockstar has said it is committed to preserving jobs and it has been clear that our games will continue to be made by people. But with recognition we’d have a seat at the table to manage this industry-wide transition together.</p>

<p>This vote will be many people’s first introduction to unions. It’s only over the past decade that game workers have begun to unionise, realising that to tackle the industry’s mounting challenges we need to come together. The IWGB Game Workers Union – which was first founded by Rockstar workers among others in 2018 – now represents thousands of members, across 300 UK studios. Encountering unions for the first time, people have a lot of questions. We’ve heard from colleagues who are nervous that the union is an outside troublemaking force that will threaten the company. But we’re not outsiders. We are hundreds of studio employees who live for Rockstar, its output, and the players. We want to see it succeed.</p>
<p>The vote will also coincide with the closing submissions of an ongoing employment tribunal over alleged union-busting at Rockstar, after the <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/money/2026/sep/11/man-sacked-grand-theft-auto-rockstar-games-watch-list">sudden firings of 31 of our union members</a> last October. Rockstar insists the sackings were for gross misconduct unconnected with union activities. The company <a data-link-name="in body link" href="https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cly8x4dlxg5o">has said</a> it makes “the best games possible by giving our talented teams world-class work environments and ongoing career opportunities” and that employee retention is above the industry standard.</p>
<p>No matter the result of the tribunal over the firings, the union at Rockstar has gone from strength to strength. We now just want employees across all departments to have a hand in shaping those “world-class work environments” Rockstar says we should have.</p>
<p>Whatever happens in this ballot, our union will continue fighting for a studio that works for every single person at Rockstar, and for workers across the industry. Unionising shouldn’t be something we simply admire from afar when dockers, miners or teachers do it. It’s something we can, and must, embrace for ourselves. The future of the industry shouldn’t be a game played over our heads.</p>
