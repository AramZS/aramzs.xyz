<p>This month, the European Commission is marking <a href="https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/european-ai-innovation-month">European AI Innovation Month</a>, bringing together policymakers, industry and researchers to showcase how AI can strengthen Europe’s competitiveness across industries and public services. Europe says it needs more data centers to power those ambitions, yet their rapid expansion is raising questions about energy, water, labor conditions and who gets a say in where they are built.</p>
<p>To learn more, I spoke first with three of the organizers behind <a href="https://stopdirtydatacentres.eu/">this weekend’s planned protests</a> across 15 European countries, where more than 100 groups are calling for a moratorium on new projects: <strong>Owen Espley </strong>of Global Action Plan (UK), <strong>Aurora Gómez </strong>of Tu Nube Seca Mi Río (Spain) and <strong>Rosi Leonard </strong>of Friends of the Earth Ireland.</p>
<p>I then turned to two journalists investigating the industry: <strong>Mei-Ling McNamara </strong>of Investigate Europe, whose reporting with Tech Policy Press <a href="https://www.techpolicy.press/electrocutions-intimidation-and-wage-theft-haunt-europes-data-center-workers/">exposed labor abuses</a> on construction sites, and <strong>Daniel Howden</strong> of Lighthouse Reports, which has filed a <a href="https://www.lighthousereports.com/methodology/data-centre-silence/">legal challenge</a> under the Aarhus Convention over EU secrecy about data centers’ environmental costs. We talked about who gets to shape Europe’s digital future, who pays for it and who holds the industry accountable.</p>
