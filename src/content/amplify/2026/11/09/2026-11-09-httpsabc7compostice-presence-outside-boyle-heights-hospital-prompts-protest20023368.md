<p>BOYLE HEIGHTS, LOS ANGELES (KABC) -- Community members and advocates gathered outside Adventist Health White Memorial Medical Center in Boyle Heights on Saturday afternoon to protest the presence of ICE agents at the hospital.</p>
<p>Federal agents were at the medical center seeking medical treatment for a person in their custody around midnight, according to the Los Angeles Police Department.</p>
<p>Video from the scene showed a teenage girl being assisted by neighbors after she was pepper-sprayed during the demonstration.</p>
<p>"Don't tilt your head back, don't tilt your head back," one person is heard telling the teen.</p>
<p>"Don't touch it, don't touch it," another person says.</p>
<p>Video also showed tense confrontations outside the hospital between community members and federal agents. As ICE agents left the area, Los Angeles Police Department officers followed closely behind.</p>
<p>Community member Derek Mejia criticized the police response.</p>
<p>"LAPD wasn't concerned with the people that were arrested- brutalized by ICE or the two minors who were maced by ICE but they were concerned with keeping their buddies in ICE safe," Mejia said.</p>
<p>The protest drew community advocates and Mayor-elect Nithya Raman, who condemned the federal presence at the hospital.</p>
<p>"What happened last night at this hospital was an affront to the wishes of the American people and to the people of Los Angeles," Raman said.</p>
<p>During the demonstration, protesters entered hospital grounds and gathered around Adventist Health White Memorial President Daniel Fontoura, urging him to address the crowd.</p>

<p>"Give him the Mic! Give him the Mic," demonstrators shouted.</p>
<p>Fontoura said federal agents entered the hospital with a person in need of medical care. He also said hospital officials pushed back when agents sought access during a physician-patient consultation.</p>
<p>"There was a time when ICE actually wanted to break protocol and be in with the physician in that sacred physician patient encounter and we said no," Fontoura said.</p>
<p>Fontoura also explained why the hospital contacted the LAPD during the protest.</p>
<p>"When they lined up to prevent access to EMS trucks and ambulances to our facility that was not done with our approval," he said.</p>
<p>"So when those things happen we called LAPD and said help us with this predicament."</p>
<p>The demonstration continued Saturday afternoon as community members, advocates and hospital officials responded to the overnight arrival of ICE agents at the medical center.</p>
