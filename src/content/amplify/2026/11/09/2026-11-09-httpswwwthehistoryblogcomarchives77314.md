title: '5,000-year-old bread loaves go on display in Turkey'

<p>Eight loaves of bread that were baked 5,000 years ago found at Seyitömer Mound in Turkey’s northwestern province of Kütahya <a href="https://www.turkiyetoday.com/culture/archaeologists-unearth-5000-year-old-burned-bread-in-turkiye-3231780?s=1">have gone on display</a> to the public for the first time. The bread barely even looks stale, preserved in excellent condition from have been carbonized by the fire that ravaged the settlement in the Early Bronze Age.</p>

<p>The loaves were discovered last year in a room with a furnace and the remains of shelves on its walls. Eight bread loaves were found between the shelf supports. Raw materials — the remains of wheat and lentils — were also present. This suggests the shelf collapsed under pressure from the fire, dumping the bread and what had been sacks of wheat and lentil, which then opened, spilling the contents and allowing them to mix together.</p>

<p>Seyitömer Mound is 85 feet high today, 460 feet wide and 490 feet long. It lies within the operating area of Çelikler Seyitömer Electricity Generation Inc. who mine the lignite deep underneath it. Rescue excavations have been ongoing at the mound since 1989. More than 17,000 artifacts have been recovered. A selection of the finds is on display at the Kütahya Archaeology and Mining Museum. The loaves, wheat and lentils have now joined the exhibits from the mound at the museum.</p>
<blockquote><p>Noting that the artifacts are among the most popular finds in the museum, [lead archaeologist Sevgi] Gurdal said Seyitomer Mound “actually makes up a large part of the artifacts exhibited in our museums.”</p> <p>She added: “The Seyitomer Mound artifacts offer various data on the daily and social life, artistic understanding and eating habits of their period.”</p></blockquote>
