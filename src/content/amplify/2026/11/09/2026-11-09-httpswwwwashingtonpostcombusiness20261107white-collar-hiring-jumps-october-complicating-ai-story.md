<p>Democrats on Sunday spoke out against the elevation of Stephen Miller to White House chief of staff, saying it comes after Americans showed at the ballot box that they wanted a shift away from the Trump administration’s most divisive policies and ideas.</p>
<p>House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-New York), speaking Sunday on ABC News’s “This Week,” called Miller’s promotion on Friday by President Donald Trump an “irresponsible” and “awful choice,” and said he would not work with the new appointee.</p>
<p>Jonathan Karl of “This Week” noted that Jeffries occasionally communicated with Miller’s predecessor, Susie Wiles, something that Jeffries said won’t continue.</p>
<p>“It’s not going to happen with this new chief of staff to the extent that [Miller] lasts, because he clearly represents, along with Pete Hegseth and a few others, a really toxic brand of political engagement of politics and of governance,” Jeffries said. “And the American people just voted for that kind of MAGA extremism to be eradicated.”</p>
<p>Miller, 41, recently served as White House deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser and is <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/07/what-know-about-stephen-miller-new-white-house-chief-staff/">the architect of several controversial policies</a>, including the immigration crackdown, strikes on alleged drug boats in the Caribbean, the raid to capture Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, and Trump’s efforts to exert control over Greenland.</p>
<p>Sen.-elect Adam Hamilton (D-Kansas) told CNN that Miller’s promotion seems “really odd” and “like a doubling down” on the first two years of the Trump administration, even after an election that appeared to be a repudiation of it.</p>
<p>Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic senator-elect from Michigan, told ABC News that Miller’s promotion represents the Trump administration’s “incoherent messaging.”</p>
<p>“I don’t know what Stephen Miller has actually contributed to our culture, our society in any real way, except for to make us more angry, to stoke the worst kind of fear and vitriol, to weaponize ICE in our streets, to take American lives,” he said.</p>

<p>Lots of webpages could stand to go on a digital diet: They clutter your view with such extraneous content as sign-up boxes for newsletters, space-hogging ads and battery-draining widgets.</p>
<p>You don’t, however, have to put up with that. All of the four major browsers offer a “reader” or “reading” mode that displays only the core text and sometimes the principal image on a page. Your tip that it’s available — sites can block or limit these modes, and browsers don’t always respond identically — should come in the browser’s address bar.</p>
<p>Apple’s Safari and Google’s Chrome are more obvious in their desktop versions. The <a href="https://support.apple.com/guide/safari/hide-distractions-when-reading-sfri32632/27.0/mac/27">former shows “Reader Available”</a> right after a page loads, after which you click an icon with three horizontal lines at the left of the bar; the latter puts <a href="https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/14218344?hl=en&amp;co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&amp;ai1hc=1">a “Reading mode” button</a> with a book icon at the right of the bar, which you can just click or tap. To use Microsoft Edge’s <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/use-immersive-reader-in-microsoft-edge">Reading mode</a> and Mozilla Firefox’s <a href="https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/firefox-reader-view-clutter-free-web-pages">Reader View</a>, look for a page icon at the right of the address bar and click that.</p>
<p>The cramped interfaces of the mobile versions of these browsers make their reader options a little less obvious; in Safari and Chrome, you’ll have to open a menu to access them, while Firefox and Edge show tiny page icons.</p>

<p>Economists have been asking whether AI is starting to take white-collar jobs. But the latest numbers out this week point the other way.</p>
<p>Employers in professional and business services, the part of the economy that includes lawyers, accountants and consultants, added 70,000 jobs last month, the Labor Department said Friday. That’s the biggest gain in several years.</p>
<p>“Despite the effects of AI on the job market, some white-collar industries are still adding jobs at a solid clip,” said Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist at Fifth Third Commercial Bank.</p>
<figure><a href="https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/yQl0A/?dark=false"><img alt="Professional and business services hiring jumped in October" src="https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/yQl0A/full.png"/></a></figure>
<p>The picture remains murky, though. The sector also counts lower-paid workers like temps, janitors and security guards. Consulting firms added about 17,000 jobs, and temp agencies added about 19,000.</p>
<p>Some office work is shrinking. Firms that design computer systems for other businesses employ about 26,000 fewer people than they did a year ago. Much of that is outsourced programming, Adams said, where companies “can do the same work with fewer people in many cases because of AI.”</p>
<p>And the bump last month could be “a head fake” caused by the election, as campaigns poured money into consultants, ads and temp workers, said Brian Bethune, an economist at Boston College. He expects agencies to shed about 20,000 jobs in November as that work dries up.</p>

<p>The United States announced that it is offering a reward of up to $20 million for information about Iran’s nuclear program.</p>
<p>The State Department, through its Rewards for Justice program, is also offering “possible relocation” for informants, the U.S. Central Command <a href="https://x.com/CENTCOM/status/2118856351858430019">said on X</a>. The program, established in 1984, offers financial rewards for information that helps counterterrorism.</p>
<p>President Donald Trump has repeatedly said his primary objective in the war with Iran is to prevent the country from obtaining a nuclear weapon. He has argued that Iran was close to achieving that capability, despite claiming last year that Tehran’s key nuclear enrichment facilities had been “completely and totally obliterated.”</p>
<p>Centcom said the State Department is seeking information on Iranian “facilities, equipment, and associated personnel, such as those in the scientific community, or the movement of any of the above.”</p>
<p>The reward posting came weeks after Secretary of State Marco Rubio called on Cubans to make discreet contact with the CIA, <a href="https://x.com/SecRubio/status/2113731069518434519">in a social media post</a> that was mocked by Cuban officials.</p>

<p>A South Carolina man has been arrested and accused of leaving his 6-year-old daughter asleep outside a Target store overnight to hold his place in line for Pokémon cards.</p>
<p>John-Michael Dooley, 35, had been waiting with his daughter outside the store Friday in Charleston’s West Ashley district, where a crowd had gathered overnight in hopes of buying a newly released Pokémon card set.</p>
<p>Police arrived at the store around 2:30 a.m. after receiving reports of an unattended child, <a href="https://www.charleston-sc.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/1761">according to a news release</a> from the Charleston Police Department. Witnesses told officers that Dooley gave his daughter a pillow and blanket sometime after 1 a.m. before leaving her asleep in line. He failed to return after an hour, which prompted them to contact police.</p>
<p>Dooley returned to the store and was arrested. During his police interview, he admitted he had gone home and left his daughter unattended so that he could keep his place in line, according to authorities.</p>
<p>Dooley, of Johns Island, South Carolina, was charged with unlawful conduct toward a child. Police said the girl was unharmed and placed in emergency protective custody.</p>

<p>President Donald Trump continues to assert that incumbent Dan S. Sullivan (R-Alaska) is “leading substantially” in the state’s Senate race — despite figures showing an edge for Democrat Mary Peltola.</p>
<p>Basing his math on claims of fraudulent candidates, he said Saturday on social media the race “is OVER.”</p>
<p>Trump has expressed frustration about the ballot’s including a candidate with a nearly identical name as the senator’s, Dan J. Sullivan Jr. Republicans say it confused voters and could propel Peltola to an unfair victory.</p>
<p>“Sullivan is leading substantially in the Alaska Senatorial Race. When you add Daniel (3,600, 1.68%) into Dan, and Gerald Heikes (4,846, 2.27%), Senator Dan Sullivan is up by thousands of Votes,” Trump <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117576912671985823">wrote</a>.</p>

<p>More than a billion people are celebrating Diwali on Sunday with lights, fireworks, prayers and a lot of sweet dishes.</p>
<p>Hindus, Jains, Sikhs and some Buddhists will be celebrating with lit candles, while others will create colorful rangoli designs showing lotus flowers, peacocks and religious symbols.</p>
<p>Diwali, which is also known as Deepavali, falls on the darkest night of the Hindu calendar when the moon can’t be seen in the sky. The festival signifies the triumph of light over darkness, good over evil and knowledge over ignorance.</p>
<p>For Hindus, the festival celebrates god Lord Krishna’s victory over the demon Narakasura, but also the return home of long-exiled Lord Rama, his wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana after they defeated Ravana, a demon you’d never want to meet in any circumstance.</p>
<p>For Sikhs, it’s time to mark Bandi Chhor Divas, which commemorates the release of revered sixth guru — Guru Hargobind from an unjust imprisonment. Jains see Diwali as a time to celebrate Lord Mahavira’s attainment of moksha — the path of liberation away from the cycle of reincarnation.</p>
<p>For all it’s a time to welcome prosperity, remove negative energy and celebrate family. We even have a beautiful <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/home/interactive/2024/prep-your-home-for-diwali/">illustrated guide from the archives</a> if you want to join in all the fun.</p>
