<p>In March, legacy content farm <em>BuzzFeed </em><a href="https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/reaction-buzzfeed-new-ai-apps">conceded</a> that there was “substantial doubt” about its “ability to continue” doing business. At the time, the company was reporting a net loss of $57.3 million for 2025, suggesting former CEO Jonah Peretti’s <a href="https://futurism.com/the-byte/buzzfeed-announces-openai-content">controversial decision</a> to double down on AI content had officially blown up in his face.</p>
<p>Months later, it’s looking like <em>BuzzFeed</em>’s fears are coming true. A brief glimpse at its stock price this morning paints a horrifying picture [nice] after its grim Q3 earnings report: its stock price fell off a cliff, sinking to $0.57 from a high of $2.09 earlier this year — a nausea-inducing 38 percent drop in a single morning.</p>
<p>The company’s earnings per share (EPS) of negative $0.22, 13 cents worse than analysts’ already-gloomy expectations, clearly had investors spooked. That’s despite drumming up some muted enthusiasm back in May, after TV personality Byron Allen’s family affiliate company <a href="https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/byron-allen-buying-buzzfeed-ceo-1236745206/">bought a majority stake in <em>BuzzFeed</em></a>. Peretti, who has for years been singing the praises of AI and <a href="https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/reaction-buzzfeed-new-ai-apps">announced a slew of ill-conceived apps</a> powered by the tech, came away with the flashy new title of “president of <em>BuzzFeed</em> AI.”</p>
<p>“Our vision is to build on the iconic foundation of <em>BuzzFeed</em> and HuffPost by expanding into free-streaming video, audio and user-generated content,” Allen, who owns 13 network affiliate TV stations, proudly announced at the time. “As of this moment, with the power of AI, <em>BuzzFeed</em> is officially chasing YouTube to become another premiere free video streaming service.”</p>
<p>Establishing a free video streaming service at a time when even Google is <a href="https://www.firstpost.com/tech/ai-search-is-eating-into-googles-traffic-as-metas-ad-revenue-soars-what-has-changed-14056969.html">admitting that ad revenues are plummeting thanks to AI</a> takes some chutzpah, to say the least. But considering that <em>BuzzFeed </em>continues to hemorrhage money — it <a href="https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/buzzfeed-layoffs-pivot-ai">laid off 33 percent of its remaining staff</a> roughly two months after Allen took over — it feels more like <em>BuzzFeed</em> is chasing MySpace, straight into the abyss.</p>
<p><em>BuzzFeed </em>first started embracing AI back in 2023 after the launch of OpenAI’s ChatGPT, using the tech to roll out <a href="https://futurism.com/buzzfeed-cnet-ai-differences">forgettable quizzes</a> and strange <a href="https://futurism.com/buzzfeed-publishing-articles-by-ai">AI-generated travel guides</a> that caught a world of flak. Since then, there’s never been a moment when the gamble hasn’t felt like a major unforced error.</p>
<p>In May, shortly after Byron’s acquisition, Peretti <a href="https://www.theverge.com/podcast/932154/peretti-allen-buzzfeed-ai-slop-social-media">sat down with <em>The Verge</em></a> for an interview in which he enthused that AI was the thing he was “most passionate about.” He claimed that there was a “shift” in digital media “where AI is going to start creating a new medium for content that wasn’t really possible before.”</p>
<p>Needless to say, we haven’t seen any evidence of that yet.</p>
