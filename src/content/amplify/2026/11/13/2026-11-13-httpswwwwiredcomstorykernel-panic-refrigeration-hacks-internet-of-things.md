<div><div class="BaseText-fEwdHD SplitScreenContentHeaderDek-juwmcJ hhRWTn bFhTNF">Escalating hacks affecting blood labs and grocery stores show the very real risks to critical infrastructure that come from basic security lapses involving internet-connected devices.</div><figure><img alt="The FridgeHacking Spree Is an InternetofThings WakeUp Call" class="ResponsiveImageContainer-dkeESL cQPiWi responsive-image__image" src="https://media.wired.com/photos/6aecfb1b276d9711716c8ef8/master/w_2560%2Cc_limit/KernelPanic_10312026_v1-ezgif.com-video-to-gif-converter.gif"/><figcaption>Photo-Illustration: WIRED STAFF; Getty Images</figcaption></figure><div class="body__inner-container"><p><em><span class="body lead-in-text">Welcome to </span><a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/newsletter/exclusive/kernel-panic">Kernel Panic</a>,</em> <em>a weekly newsletter by <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/author/lily-hay-newman/">Lily Hay Newman</a> and <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/author/matt-burgess/">Matt Burgess</a> from inside the new world of privacy and digital security. To receive this newsletter in your inbox each week, <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/newsletter/exclusive/kernel-panic">sign up here</a>.</em></p><p class="paywall"><span class="body lead-in-text">Amid an onslaught</span> of <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/kernel-panic-ai-vulnerability-explosion/">AI vulnerability discovery</a> and <a class="text link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/ai-tools-are-helping-mediocre-north-korean-hackers-steal-millions/">AI-assisted hacking</a>, some of the most consequential critical infrastructure attacks this year may have little to do with the technology at all—instead providing a stark reminder that cybersecurity fundamentals are still key to digital defense.</p><div></div><div></div><p class="paywall">Three sites in France that handle blood and plasma bags were briefly impacted this week by widespread attacks on vulnerable internet-connected refrigerators. On Wednesday, the Établissement Français du Sang (EFS) <a class="external-link text link" href="https://presse.efs.sante.fr/actualites/l-efs-confirme-l-incident-de-cybersecurite-impactant-un-de-ces-prestataires-d-equipement-frigorifique-2c07c-08a0a.html">confirmed</a> that cold rooms storing blood were “raised to very high temperatures” on November 2. The blood samples, thankfully, weren’t altered or damaged, the EFS said, because the organization’s alarm facilities and <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.cyberattaque.org/cyberattaque-contre-lefs-des-hackers-sattaquent-aux-reserves-de-plasma-sanguin/">staff</a> quickly detected the changes and implemented fail-safe measures.</p><div class="Container-ioqmpI jzsvml"></div><p class="paywall">The incident, however, comes amid a wave of cyberattacks in recent days affecting supermarket chains in at least 11 European countries that also drastically increased the temperatures of their refrigerators. Pictures from across the continent show <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/02/supermarket-freezers-across-europe-hit-by-second-suspected-cyber-attack">huge volumes of food being trashed</a> following the spikes. Similar fridge attacks in October affected hundreds of French stores that were forced to throw away food. And in August, roughly six commissaries and grocery stores on United States military bases suffered similar incidents resulting in spoiled food. The Pentagon is investigating the incidents and is collaborating with French law enforcement, Reuters <a class="text link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/pentagon-treating-military-base-freezer-malfunctions-suspected-cyberattack-2026-10-29/">reported</a> last week.</p><p class="paywall">“It's been true for more than a decade now that our dependence on connected technology is growing faster than our collective will to protect it, which has exposed us to accidents, adversaries, and now potentially agents,” says longtime critical infrastructure researcher Josh Corman, who is currently the executive in residence for public safety and resilience at the nonprofit Institute for Security and Technology. “These fragile cold-chain systems are too critical to fail, but we keep experiencing hard lessons.”</p><p class="paywall">It is still unclear who is behind the incidents and whether the attacks are all connected or being carried out by multiple actors. The first recent wave of attacks against French supermarkets last month appears to have been caused by attackers exploiting <a class="external-link text link" href="https://cert.cyber.gouv.fr/alerte/CERTFR-2026-ALE-012/">unpatched</a> refrigeration controllers exposed to the internet—including software versions that <a class="external-link text link" href="https://claroty.com/team82/research/freeze-the-controller-defrost-the-food-uncovering-vulnerabilities-in-danfoss-refrigeration-controllers">allegedly</a> used weak passwords formulated based on the date.</p><p class="paywall">Many of the hacks stem from attackers exploiting vulnerabilities in temperature controllers made by the Danish firm Danfoss, and researchers say the attacks would not have been particularly difficult to pull off. Danfoss did not return WIRED’s request for comment.</p></div><div class="body__inner-container"><p class="paywall">In other statements, Danfoss <a class="text link" href="https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/french-supermarket-chain-intermarche-hit-by-cyberattack-150-stories-targeted-2026-10-12/">said</a> impacted systems “were running outdated firmware” and urged customers to keep products updated. In an <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/us-government-partners-urge-hardening-industrial-refrigeration-and-cold-chain-monitoring-systems">alert</a> on Thursday addressing the rash of incidents, the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency said attacks could exploit flaws in cold storage systems via authentication bypasses, arbitrary code injections, and weak configurations. Experts have long warned about the dire threats to critical infrastructure posed by these types of weaknesses in so-called internet-of-things devices.</p><p class="paywall">Russian hacktivists tried to <a class="text link" href="https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c8vgxyvz6y9ro">claim responsibility</a> for some of the attacks but did not provide credible evidence. Still, some researchers <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.thetimes.com/world/europe/article/cyberattack-civilian-target-trigger-article-5-nato-sr9j723cn">see signs</a> that Russia-based actors may have been involved in at least <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.flyingpenguin.com/french-food-chains-hacked-with-freezer-bug-following-us-military-base-case/">some</a> of the incidents. The Kremlin this week <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1127149">denied</a> involvement. In other cases, researchers have found <a class="text link" href="https://www.npr.org/2026/11/03/nx-s1-6026676/cold-war-hackers-fridges">potential ties to Iran-linked</a> actors. NextGov/FCW <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/officials-suspect-iran-series-cyberattacks-military-base-freezers/416934/">reported</a> last week that unnamed US officials suspect Iran-linked attackers may have been behind the freezer attacks on US military bases, though they noted that the investigation is still ongoing.</p><p class="paywall">This type of refrigerator/freezer attack is not new. Back in 2022, 16 supermarkets in Russia were targeted at the start of the country’s full-scale war with Ukraine. That year, 40 stores in Israel were similarly attacked, <a class="external-link text link" href="https://assets.danfoss.com/documents/latest/533772/AO540160316124en-000101.pdf">according</a> to a 2025 report by Danfoss.</p><p class="paywall">“Many businesses use older technology while the systems become increasingly connected,” Sweden’s National Cyber Security Center <a class="external-link text link" href="https://www.ncsc.se/sv/aktuellt/cyberangrepp-mot-kylsystem">pointed out</a> in a recent advisory about the freezer attacks, noting that companies should inventory which of their devices are connected to the internet, disconnect them whenever possible, and make sure they are kept access-controlled, patched, and monitored if they must be connected.</p></div></div>
