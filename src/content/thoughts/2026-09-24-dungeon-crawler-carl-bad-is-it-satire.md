Many people like Dungeon Crawler Carl. I don't. I'm curious why people do? So I've been investigating the positive comments and the defenses raised against criticisms. I want to write more about DCC, but to start I want to address this defense: the books are satire. *There will be spoilers for the whole series.*

I recently finished reading the 8 currently available and I saw this response a bunch, but by the end of book 8 I was more and more sure that the claims to satire were nonsense. I want to try and steel man them here and get a sense of what the possible arguments are and put into writing why I disagree. 

To start, I want to put my bias on the table: I don't take the claims of satire very seriously. Perhaps this could be legitimetly argued for book one. By the end of book 8 though, it just is impossible to me to believe that's a read anyone is taking into the series. 

Here's the thing. I don't think people read the Dungeon Crawler Carl series and are like 'ah yes, what excellent biting satire'. I think the claim of satire in this case is almost always defensively. People hear someone air moral objections to the books & think: 

> 'This person says the book is bad, and I like the book; therefore they're saying I'm bad. But I'm not bad, so the book must be good. The bad things must be satire' 

I feel sure, reading review reactions, this is often the thought process, perhaps not always consciously. I need people (especially you, reading this post) to realize that just because someone doesn't like something you like, it doesn't mean they're judging you personally. 

Also, I encourage you to take an opportunity to think more deeply about these books. It's also ok to genuinely listen to criticism and rethink your feelings towards a piece of media. You are not bad for liking a bad book. You are not good for liking only good books. You are absolutely not bad for liking a bad book and then rethinking it. 

Ok, let's go into our numbered list. 

1. Dungeon Crawler Carl isn't misogynistic / sexist, it's a satire of misogyny / sexism 

This particular satire defense is usually paired with others but I want to take in on in isolation because I think the most common criticism I see of DCC is its bad treatment of women and how they are described. 

**The argument for this is something like**: *DCC is not genuinely sexist in how it is constantly talking about, describing, or portraying women. The game is a framework by bad people and it is doing bad things. We should see the book's choice to have the game portray women badly as a *judgement* on that sexism, not support of it.*

Oh boy does this argument only get worse over time. I think this is... an argument one could make about book 1. It was sort of my assumption in that book which is why I didn't really think much of the sexism in that book. That's before I got to Dead Hooker Mysteries in book 2, where women are game objects and this is merely portrayed by the game, and no one thinks much of it. This is only the first of many pretty thoughtless sex worker references that absolutely undercut the idea that the book is satirizing sexism.

The real thing that shows that the books are just sexist and substantially cruel about women in a non-satirical way is the way they treat women out of the game and in it. 

No female characters in the game, except Juicebox (and her name is Juicebox ok) and .... the *talking sex doll head*, is not about to betray Carl. I don't just mean trying to kill him like the rest of the NPCs do. Male NPCs try to kill Carl, but--starting with Signet--the female characters manipulate him. Usually while the book describes their breasts or sexualizes them. Bad! 

Far more damning is the book's portrayal of player characters and other female beings. Notably the male outworld competitors mostly come straight on at Carl but the female ones all manipulate, lie, mislead, betray and often do so cruelly. Galactic princesses, space snakes and more from outside the game are often not just evil to Carl, but perform evil in specifically female coded ways that allows them to be portrayed as just like Carl's ex - liars and traitors. 

The same is often true of female crawlers. There is a horny old lady (who the book also takes pains to have an out-of-dungeon fan sexualize), and the cat. But these are outnumbered by the many women crawlers who use subterfuge to attack and abuse poor put-upon Carl. At one point in reading the series it was clear it had fully undermined the books' attempts to build plot, because as soon as a new female crawler appeared I could point at her and say 'this lady is going to betray Carl'. None of that flows from the game!

The most undermining to the idea that the sexism in these books is satirical is what they do with the one unalloyed good female character that is not overtly sexualized. Katia is poorly fleshed out for the most part, but she is given more character development than almost every character in the series. Perhaps more than Carl. Except it turns out her tragic backstory is that she can't have children. I thought we'd left this shitty sexist trope behind a decade ago? This character flaw is only resolved by **magical space pregnancy** with the spawn of the dungeon's "good guy" which allows her to leave the dungeon so **she can take care of the world's poor orphaned children**. If you can't see how it is sexist that this character is defined by her tragedy of not being able to have kids and how it is even more sexist to have that character "flaw" resolved by having a fake god **literally put a baby in her and turn her into an uber-mother figure** I don't know what to tell you. Maybe stop reading here.

2. Dungeon Crawler Carl is satirizing gaming culture

What game? There is only one game that has a whole bunch of wildly different and weird gods that players can worship in the way portrayed in these books. That's Dungeon and Dragons! What does DCC have to say about D&D? I don't think it's anything critical.
