Many people like Dungeon Crawler Carl. I don't. I'm curious why people do? So I've been investigating the positive comments and the defenses raised against criticisms. I want to write more about DCC, but to start I want to address this defense: the books are satire. 

*There will be spoilers for the whole series.*

I recently finished reading the 8 currently available and I saw this response a bunch, but by the end of book 8 I was more and more sure that the claims to satire were nonsense. I want to try and steel man them here and get a sense of what the possible arguments are and put into writing why I disagree. 

To start, I want to put my bias on the table: I don't take the claims of satire very seriously. Perhaps this could be legitimetly argued for book one. By the end of book 8 though, it just is impossible to me to believe that's a read anyone is taking into the series. 

Here's the thing. I don't think people read the Dungeon Crawler Carl series and are like 'ah yes, what excellent biting satire'. I think the claim of satire in this case is almost always defensively. People hear someone air moral objections to the books & think: 

> 'This person says the book is bad, and I like the book; therefore they're saying I'm bad. But I'm not bad, so the book must be good. The bad things must be satire' 

I feel sure, reading review reactions, this is often the thought process, perhaps not always consciously. I need people (especially you, reading this post) to realize that just because someone doesn't like something you like, it doesn't mean they're judging you personally. 

Also, I encourage you to take an opportunity to think more deeply about these books. It's also ok to genuinely listen to criticism and rethink your feelings towards a piece of media. You are not bad for liking a bad book. You are not good for liking only good books. You are absolutely not bad for liking a bad book and then rethinking it. 

Ok, let's go into our numbered list. 

## 1. Dungeon Crawler Carl isn't misogynistic / sexist, it's a satire of misogyny / sexism 

This particular satire defense is usually paired with others but I want to take in on in isolation because I think the most common criticism I see of DCC is its bad treatment of women and how they are described. 

**The argument for this is something like**: *DCC is not genuinely sexist in how it is constantly talking about, describing, or portraying women. The game is a framework by bad people and it is doing bad things. We should see the book's choice to have the game portray women badly as a *judgement* on that sexism, not support of it.*

Oh boy does this argument only get worse over time. I think this is... an argument one could make about book 1. It is unclear in book 1 that the series are from Carl's point of view. A lot of the bad behavior towards women in early books can be construed as by the game's creators without Carl's participation. The creepy portrayal and betrayal of Tsarina Signet early in book 2 is clearly coming from the game.

It was sort of my assumption in the first book which is why I didn't really think much of the sexism there. That's before I got to Dead Hooker Mysteries in book 2, where women are game objects and this is merely portrayed by the game, and no one thinks much of it. All to the end of another evil controlling woman who betrays Carl. This is only the first of many pretty thoughtless sex worker references that absolutely undercut the idea that the book is satirizing sexism.

By the time we get to the end of the Hekla arc the far stronger argument is that a major theme of these books is that women cannot be trusted. No female character is given any real agency in these books (I am not counting Carl's cat). The story of these books is, in a substantial way, Carl navigating the intersecting untrustworthy agendas of multiple flatly written repetitively evil women. This dynamic remains a major part of almost every major plot line in the series. 

In fact women are often so focused on betrayal that it makes them unable to accomplish their goals. Traitorous woman are increasingly predictably the plot of multiple "boss" fights including: Hekla, Psamathe, Ines Quiteria and Huanxin. Huanxin and Hekla are both women from *outside the game* who are presumably experts and holding substantial organizational power, but undercut themselves through their insistence on being ludicrously untrustworthy. This theme is so constant and repetitive that it even makes predictable the result of two power hungry woman determined to mess with Carl while performing generationally significant spells: Miss Quill and Queen Imogen. One of the earliest female crawlers portrayed with any competency is Maggie My, so cruel and manipulative she gets her daughter killed and turns herself into a worm all to get vengeance, handily frustrated by Carl. 

Together with the context of Carl's ex and her regularly being brought up by the books for a substantial chunk of the series there's no getting around the idea. "Women are untrustworthy" is a theme of the DCC series. 

The real thing that shows that the books are just sexist and substantially cruel about women in a non-satirical way is the way they treat women out of the game and in it. 

Almost all of the female characters in the game are determined  to betray Carl. I don't just mean trying to kill him like the rest of the NPCs do. Male NPCs try to kill Carl, but--starting with Signet--the female characters manipulate him. Usually while the book describes their breasts or sexualizes them. Bad! 

Far more damning is the book's portrayal of player characters and other female beings. Notably the male outworld competitors mostly come straight on at Carl but the female ones all manipulate, lie, mislead, betray and often do so cruelly. Galactic princesses, space snakes and more from outside the game are often not just evil to Carl, but perform evil in extremely specific ways that allows them to be portrayed as just like Carl's ex - liars and traitors, all falling into some of the nastiest stereotypes about women. 

The same is often true of female crawlers. There is a "horny old lady" (who the book also takes pains to have an out-of-dungeon fan sexualize), and the cat. But these are outnumbered by the many women crawlers who use subterfuge to attack and abuse poor put-upon Carl. At one point in reading the series it was clear it had fully undermined the books' attempts to build plot, because as soon as a new female crawler appeared I could point at her and say 'this lady is going to betray Carl'. None of that flows from the game!

The most undermining to the idea that the sexism in these books is satirical is what they do with the one unalloyed good female character that is not overtly sexualized. Katia is poorly fleshed out for the most part, but she is given more character development than almost every character in the series. Perhaps more than Carl. Except it turns out her tragic backstory is that she can't have children. I thought we'd left this shitty sexist trope behind a decade ago? This character flaw is only resolved by **magical space pregnancy** with the spawn of the dungeon's "good guy" which allows her to leave the dungeon so **she can take care of the world's poor orphaned children**. Katia's character flaw, it turns out, is corrected through a situation that forces impregnation on her. If you can't see how it is sexist that this character is defined by her tragedy of not being able to have kids and how it is even more sexist to have that character "flaw" resolved by having a fake god **literally put a baby in her and turn her into an uber-mother figure** I don't know what to tell you. Maybe stop reading here.

The general defense I read of these is that all of that plot is a commentary on what the game forces people to do and that the game itself is not supposed to be a good thing in the books. That simply cannot fly. The way the books describe women is clearly from Carl's point of view and we're on his side in the fiction. Even more damming are examples like Hekla, D'nadia and Huanxin. To have one, even two, women act this way might be an interesting choice. But to have so many do the same dance of doomed betrayal, over and over, to the point where it is incredibly predictable is bad writing; and it speaks volumes to how this series positions its female characters. 

## 2. Dungeon Crawler Carl is satirizing gaming culture

This argument usually goes something like this: Dungeon Crawler Carl is about a game where the normal violence we see all the time in video games is made gross and real and given serious consequences. This is a framework that inherently calls into mind comparisons to video game violence and asks us to think more critically about it. 

This is an extremely defensive argument because I would assume most DCC readers are also video game players and I gotta ask: did this book series ever make you think more critically about mowing down hundreds in Call of Duty? I doubt it. 

The series does not really have a criticism in mind for games themselves. Satire has to have a target and the only real thing that DCC has to say about games is that 'the people in the game are people and killing them for entertainment is morally repugnant.' There are two things that can be drawn out of that. DCC thinks video games are morally repugnant which is like.... I don't think so? Do I need to argue this? The books are so worshipful of game mechanics it is very clearly a fan of gaming. Beyond that, this is not really a relevant critique. The characters inside your game are not real. They do not have real lives and they do not really exist. I don't really think that Dinniman considers himself aligned with Jack Thompson. If there's one thing that is sure about the DCC series it is pro-obscenity. 

Furthermore, there is the question of what game? There is only one game with this type of leveling system and that has a whole bunch of wildly different and weird gods that players can worship in the way portrayed in these books. That's Dungeon and Dragons! Is DCC a criticism of Dungeons and Dragons? Considering most D&D players I know are big fans, I don't think so. I don't see anything here except a loving reproduction of the ephemera of a D&D setting and play. 

You could argue that the game is heavily *cough* borrowing from World of Warcraft. It certainly is. But is it doing so to make a comment on WoW? I have to note that the one thing the story takes on entirely uncritically, perhaps the only force of good in the game system, is the D&D/WoW style leveling system. It has no criticism of that. 

So is DCC critical of gaming *culture*? Well, I don't think you can really claim any series this sexist can be taken seriously as a critique of the extraordinarily sexist world of bad gaming culture. I play a lot of games, I played a lot of MMORPGs and I lived through GamerGate having recently been a gaming journalist. All the worst tropes and humor of the type of internet user who grew up in WoW Barrons chat and never learned better is certainly in these books. The series' humor, from the incredibly dumb foot-fetish-ism to the T-rex in a tutu, is faithfully reproduced out of shit-gamer forums and the type of 2009 internet culture that [Steve Bannon looked at and thought](https://www.thewrap.com/how-world-of-warcraft-propelled-steve-bannon-to-the-white-house/) "Wow it would be easy to turn these kids into Nazis."

Humor is personal I guess. Maybe you find child-abuse-victim-t-rex-with-tutu-and-wand-child-soldier funny? I don't get it but go you I guess. There's no disputing that this type of humor is *constant* throughout the books. The argument around this usually is that the humor is inserted into the game by the outside aliens and AI as a way of mocking humanity. The author appears to have thought that he might need to make that excuse and has added some stuff in what feels like an afterthought to support this, like the odd tangent about Carl's time as a documentary narrator. 

Except, the shitty humor **is intended to be jokes for the reader**. This is not a satire of gaming culture, it's just replicating it and then putting an occasional lampshade on it. Not even particularly good ones. Everyone who reads and recommends these paints them as feel-good easy reading books. You are not supposed to deep read the humor here as a triple entendre. 

## 3. Dungeon Crawler Carl is satirizing reality TV

DCC absolutely is interested in being a text in conversation with the idea of reality TV. What does it accomplish in that regard is less clear. It is trying to say that reality TV is bad for making entertainment out of the violence it incourages people to inflict on each other. But just like with gaming culture it does plenty to replicate the tropes of reality TV, but does not use them critically. Carl's various monologues and TV appearances make reality TV meat out of his time on the game and they are often framed as big glorious victories for Carl. 

Indeed, the use of leveling, Carl's interactions with the ultra wealthy, and how he can manipulate people on the TV programs, are all positive statements about the way reality TV levels the playing field for Carl vs the more politically or monetarily powerful. 

The most complex thing that the series could possibly be constructed to say about reality TV is that you are a bad person for watching it which is just a shitty framework to talk about what other people like; you will notice I am avoiding that same framework here. 

## 4. Dungeon Crawler Carl is satirizing imperialism

The argument here is that there is a pretty direct imperialism allegory in the game. One day a more powerful alien force lands on your planet, crushes everything to dust and then proceeds to mine it for resources while impoverishing the natives. How could one not compare that to the very similar process of imperialistic colonization? This then is a criticism of imperialism that people can learn from. 

There are a number of problems with this argument. If the author is intending a direct comparison to imperialism then it is the imperialism of the history book "New World" kind, not particularly true to modern day imperialism. 

I could go very deep into this but instead I'm just going to note that Carl's "solution" is to slaughter the **slaves** of his "colonizer" in the thousands. He and the book celebrate this. The end of book 7 throws him a party for it. Book 8 attempts to walk that celebration back slightly, but it is in the text as an afterthought and not realized in the story in any way. 

DCC's only comment on imperialism is "kill, kill, kill." I am not impressed. 

## 5. Dungeon Crawler Carl is satirizing capitalism

Of course capitalism will always attempt to capture its critics, but I just want to note at the top of this section that for a person who supposedly wants to criticize capitalism Matt Dinniman is certainly leaning into it, complete with an official publisher and selling the filming rights to his series. Not to mention all his selling of the stories themselves. 

Putting that aside. 

This defense goes something like this: look at how this universe rotates around terrible corporatocracy and greed. The addiction to the endlessly growing capital generation by the powers-that-be and the game itself doesn't just force them to be incompetent but dooms them by locking them into a cycle of over-leveraging and risk; to the point that it actually dooms their leadership in the game itself. 

There is so little of the galactic economy in these books that it is hard to see where the books could generate any sort of real criticism of capitalism from. If a theme is intended here it is clearly less of a priority than traitorous women and shitty internet humor. Taking what we have though there is some fundamental misunderstandings of how capitalism works in play that would weaken any criticism. Carl's ability to singlehandedly instigate the destruction of multiple corporate entities is... unbelievable. The book simply doesn not establish enough to make his impact on the wider world make sense. I think even Dinniman agrees with this considering how he's shifted the problem over to the AI by book 8. 

To the extent he seems to have anything directly to say in these books it is that it is ok to target and kill the most unfortunote and unwilling victims of capitalism if it means taking down the heads of these organizations and that taking down the heads of the organizations is a goal with real impact. The idea that corporations can survive the death of their executives doesn't really seem to occur to Dinniman. The only construct that the author makes available to handle capitalism is violence and not particularly organized or well-thought-out violence either. 

I admire that DCC seems to want to say 'capitalism is bad'. I agree. But that isn't satire. It isn't executed particularly well either.
