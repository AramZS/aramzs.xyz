> That said, clarity is not synonymous with accuracy. John K. Wilson [notes](https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/columns/debatable-ideas/2026/06/09/academic-authoritarianism-new-attack-humanities-scholars) the lack of direct evidence warranting the field-based scrutiny recommended in the report. Agustín Fuentes [argues](https://www.chronicle.com/article/are-leftist-professors-really-destroying-the-academys-credibility) that the report’s depiction of his field, anthropology, is misleading. Nicholas Dirks and Brian Leiter [separately](https://www.chronicle.com/article/what-a-new-report-about-the-humanities-gets-right-and-wrong) [note](https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/letters/how-strong-is-the-intellectual-case-against-relativism) the report’s imprecise definition of “relativism,” despite their sympathies with its conclusions, while both Leiter and Richard Moran [critique](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/the-wrong-way-to-criticize-the-humanities/) the irregularities in the constitution of its committee. Jason Stanley [questions](https://jasonstanleyantifascist.substack.com/p/on-the-philosophical-muddle-that) the committee’s breadth of understanding in analytic philosophy, the tiny sliver of the humanities that is the home discipline of nearly half of the committee. To their credit, the writers of the report [have responded](https://www.chronicle.com/article/inside-the-report-thats-divided-the-academy) to these and other charges with surprising candor.
