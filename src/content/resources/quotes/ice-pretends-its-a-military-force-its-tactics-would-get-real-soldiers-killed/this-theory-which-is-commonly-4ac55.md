> This theory, which is commonly seen in authoritarian states like the People’s Republic of China or Russia, encompasses many realms of irregular conflict: media warfare, psychological warfare, and legal warfare (sometimes called “lawfare”), to name a few. While strategies of control can be used against external enemies, they can also be used internally. A strategy of control uses terror tactics to create a violent resistance to justify its brutal subjugation and as an excuse to change laws enlarging the definition of [terrorism](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ice-tells-legal-observer-nice-202357894.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAELPMSI_4oUL1GfTPqSjU0GUOYM2ELLZhqj3cMWSBkXkSFxE8X5fmt-pqYyqRmou2F1daQB_HH2AFKsocpTYQ647iRqPN7a-ficvEHm3g71nGQPt5F12vK8RIsQ07d6e23E15-cx3GP71O5_UAV_aN9_XQOcH8KOa6HZKJAQX960) and treason. Regimes then use the surrounding chaos and noise to continue its other initiatives. It is, roughly speaking, conducting policy by crisis.
