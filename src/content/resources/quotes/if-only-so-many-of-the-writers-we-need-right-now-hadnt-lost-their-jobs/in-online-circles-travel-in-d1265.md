> In the online circles I travel in, [some](https://bsky.app/profile/ositanwanevu.com/post/3lynnrbq5fk27) [journalists](https://bsky.app/profile/swin24.bsky.social/post/3lynlyhsir225) have pointed out how this moment highlights the need for reporters and desks dedicated to video games and online culture. It’s a call that hits particularly close to home for me, who, along with Nathan, staffed The Washington Post’s gaming desk until it was [shut down](https://kotaku.com/washington-post-launcher-layoffs-giant-bomb-games-media-1850025471) in 2023. In the time before and since, we’ve seen multiple gaming outlets [closed](https://aftermath.site/game-informer-has-been-shut-down), [sold](https://aftermath.site/polygon-layoffs-sale), or [gutted](https://kotaku.com/fanbyte-website-layoffs-tencent-staff-g4tv-future-1849540893), with many that remain being forced to [churn out](https://aftermath.site/the-gamurs-group-games-journalism-escapist-dot-esports) high volume, SEO-friendly copy for numbers-focused [owners](https://www.thewrap.com/valnet-labor-lawsuit-hassan-youssef-digital-media-sweat-shop/) instead of the journalism and cultural analysis that were once, at least for a brief moment, common. The remaining journalists best suited to meet this moment, to help readers flooded with divisive rhetoric and misinformation, are facing an uphill battle to do that work.
